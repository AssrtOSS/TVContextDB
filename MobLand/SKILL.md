## Series Overview

**MobLand** (Chinese title: 黑幫領地) is a British-American crime drama television series created by Ronan Bennett for Paramount+. It premiered on 30 March 2025. The series is set in London's criminal underworld and follows the escalating conflict between two rival crime families.

- **Broadcaster:** Paramount+
- **First air date:** 30 March 2025
- **Seasons:** 2 (Season 1: 10 episodes, 30 Mar – 1 Jun 2025; Season 2: 10 episodes, 18 Sep – 20 Nov 2026)
- **Season 3:** Renewed September 2026
- **Genre:** Crime drama
- **Showrunner:** Jez Butterworth
- **Directors:** Guy Ritchie, Anthony Byrne, Daniel Syrkin, Lawrence Gough
- **Episode runtime:** 41–59 minutes

## Family Relationships & World Setting

The series is set in **London's criminal underworld**, centred on two rival families:

**Harrigan Family** — a powerful London crime dynasty:
- Conrad (patriarch) and Maeve (matriarch) lead the family
- Sons: Brendan (eldest, killed S1E7), Kevin (second son, married to Bella)
- Daughter: Seraphina (Conrad's daughter from another relationship, Maeve's stepdaughter)
- Eddie appears to be Kevin and Bella's son, but is actually Conrad and Bella's biological child — a secret revealed in S1E10
- Harry Da Souza is the family's fixer — not a blood member but deeply embedded

**Stevenson Gang** — south London rivals:
- Richie Stevenson leads the gang
- Wife: Vron; Son: Tommy
- Connected to the Mexican cartel through Jaime Lopez

**External Powers:**
- Kat McAllister — international cartel operative; Harry owes her increasingly dangerous favours
- Jaime Lopez — Mexican cartel leader controlling fentanyl production
- Colin Tattersall — corrupt retired DCI working for Richie inside the police

**Police:**
- DS Ivan Fisk and DC Yvonne Mukasa investigate the Harrigans (both killed S1E8)
- Alice Barnes / Nicola — undercover officer placed near Jan (S1E9)

## Overall Plot Premise

The Harrigans, a London crime family, find themselves at odds with the Stevenson gang after Eddie Harrigan kills Tommy Stevenson during a drug-fuelled night out. Harry Da Souza, a street-smart fixer, is tasked with managing the fallout and preventing all-out gang war. As tensions escalate, Harry navigates betrayals from within both families, corrupt police, international cartel interests, and his own increasingly complicated debts to Kat McAllister. The first season culminates in the destruction of the Stevenson gang, the arrest of Conrad and Maeve, and Harry being stabbed by his own wife Jan. Season 2 begins with Harry recovering and Kevin attempting to take control of the family.

## Speaking Styles

- **Harry Da Souza:** Measured, calculated, professional. Speaks in short, precise sentences. Uses underworld jargon naturally. Switches between calm negotiation and sudden violence.
- **Conrad Harrigan:** Authoritative, patriarchal. Formal British English with occasional Irish inflections. Commands through tone rather than volume.
- **Maeve Harrigan:** Controlled, manipulative. Polished speech masking ruthlessness. Uses emotional leverage and family loyalty as weapons.
- **Kevin Harrigan:** Tense, conflicted. More colloquial than his father. Shows vulnerability in private moments.
- **Richie Stevenson:** Aggressive, volatile. South London working-class dialect. Direct threats and bluster.
- **Eddie Harrigan:** Reckless, youthful slang. Drug-influenced speech patterns.
- **Bella Harrigan:** Upper-class diction, strategic politeness. Conceals manipulation behind courtesy.
- **Kat McAllister:** Cold, transactional. International business language. Every word is a calculated move.

## Cultural Context

The series draws on Guy Ritchie's established style of London gangster fiction (Lock, Stock and Two Smoking Barrels; Snatch; The Gentlemen). Key cultural elements include:

- **British class system:** The Harrigans represent old-money criminal aristocracy; Stevensons are more working-class south London
- **London geography:** East London, the Cotswolds, and international locations (Antwerp, Amsterdam) are significant
- **Fentanyl crisis:** The drug trade plotline reflects real-world concerns about synthetic opioids in the UK
- **Family loyalty vs. self-preservation:** Central tension throughout; characters repeatedly choose between family duty and personal survival
- **Police corruption:** Colin Tattersall's betrayal reflects anxieties about institutional corruption in British law enforcement
- **Irish heritage:** The Harrigan surname and Conrad's characterization suggest Irish criminal diaspora roots in London
