# The Tudors with Rob Rinder and Ruth Goodman — Subtitle Translation Background

## Series Identity
- **Exact title**: *The Tudors with Rob Rinder and Ruth Goodman*  
- **Parent series**: *Rich Lives, Poor Lives* (Season 2, Episode 4)  
- **Format**: 90-minute documentary special (history / immersive factual)  
- **First air date**: 25 August 2026, 21:00 BST  
- **Broadcaster**: Channel 5 (UK)  
- **Production**: Lion Television & WPP Media Motion Entertainment (All3Media International distribution)  
- **Commissioning editor**: Lucy Willis (Channel 5)  
- **Executive producers**: Sarah Sarkhel (Lion TV), Melanie Darlaston (WPP), Daniel Pearl  
- **Series producer**: Lauren Bennie  
- **Directors**: Stuart Elliott, Stuart Powell  

## Premise & Structure
*Rich Lives, Poor Lives* is a talent-led history strand where barrister/broadcaster **Rob Rinder** and social historian **Ruth Goodman** explore pivotal British eras through the contrasting lived experiences of the rich and the poor. Season 2 (2026) comprises four 90-minute specials: **Roman**, **Tudor**, **Georgian**, **Victorian**. Each episode follows real historical individuals from both strata, blending on-location immersion, expert commentary, and dramatised reconstruction.

**This episode** focuses on **summer 1588** — the Spanish Armada crisis — tracing how the threat of invasion upended domestic life across the social spectrum.

## Relationships & Dynamics
- **Rinder ↔ Goodman**: Professional partners; warm, lightly bantering rapport. Rinder approaches topics with legal/analytical curiosity (“What were the contractual obligations?”); Goodman supplies material-culture detail (“What did they eat, wear, sleep on?”).  
- **Rinder → Cordell**: Rinder “shadows” Cordell’s decisions — legal risk, asset seizure, patriotism vs profit.  
- **Goodman → Browne family**: Goodman walks the domestic spaces Margaret inhabits; emphasis on food, fuel, childcare, information vacuum.  
- **Cordell ↔ Elizabeth I**: Subject-monarch; Cordell’s ships commandeered under royal prerogative.  
- **Parry ↔ Elizabeth I**: Intimate trust; access without appointment; political significance in a succession-anxious court.  
- **William ↔ Margaret Browne**: Separated by war; letterless communication; survival economy.

## World Setting
- **Time**: July–August 1588 (Armada sighted off Plymouth 19 July O.S.; running fights to Gravelines; dispersal northwards).  
- **Place**: London (Cheapside, St Paul’s environs, riverside wharves), Portsmouth / Plymouth naval stations, English Channel, domestic interiors (merchant’s house, sailor’s tenement).  
- **Society**: Late-Elizabethan England — Protestant settlement, maritime trade expansion, privy-council government, nascent impressment system, no standing navy (privateers + royal ships).  
- **Material culture**: Currants (Cordell’s monopoly import), salted fish, ship’s biscuit, wool/linen clothing, rush lights, pewter, heraldic display vs subsistence diet.

## Speaking Styles
- **Rob Rinder**: Articulate, slightly legalistic cadence; rhetorical questions (“So what would *you* do if the Queen commandeered your livelihood?”); direct-to-camera asides; occasional dry wit. Subtitle tone: polished, conversational, precise.  
- **Ruth Goodman**: Warm, tactile vocabulary (“smell of tar”, “weight of the shift”); present-tense immersion (“I’m lighting the rushlight now”); explains *how* not just *what*. Subtitle tone: vivid, sensory, accessible.  
- **Narration (if any)**: Third-person past tense; formal documentary register.  
- **On-screen experts / re-enactors**: Period-appropriate diction in reconstructions; modern English in interviews. Keep distinction clear.

## Cultural Context for Translation
- **UK factual TV conventions**: Presenter-led, “immersive history” genre (cf. *Victorian Farm*, *Tudor Monastery Farm*). Audience expects accuracy + narrative drive.  
- **Class contrast** is the structural spine: every scene pairs a privileged perspective with a labouring one. Subtitles must preserve that parallelism.  
- **Historical sensitivity**: 1588 is a foundation myth (“Protestant wind”, national deliverance). The episode *complicates* it — showing requisition, impressment, family rupture. Avoid triumphalist language unless spoken by a character.  
- **Food & trade details** (currants, salt, cloth) are recurring Goodman motifs; render precisely.  
- **Legal terminology** (prerogative, impressment warrant, charter-party) appears in Rinder segments; use standard Chinese legal-historical equivalents.  
- **Names**: Retain English order (Thomas Cordell → 托马斯·科德尔); titles precede name (Queen Elizabeth I → 伊丽莎白一世).
