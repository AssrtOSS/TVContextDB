## Synopsis
After 33 years of marriage, Leanne Morgan's husband Bill abruptly leaves her for another woman via email. The series follows Leanne — a warm, faith-rooted Tennessee grandmother — as she navigates sudden single life, family chaos, and dating again, all while her opinionated mother, free-spirited sister, adult children, and meddling church community hover nearby. Classic Chuck Lorre multi-cam rhythm with Southern gentility and sharp observational humor.

## Relationships
- **Leanne ↔ Bill**: 33-year marriage ending in separation/divorce; complicated residual affection
- **Leanne ↔ Carol**: Sisters; Carol moves in to support Leanne; bickering but deeply loyal
- **Leanne ↔ Mama Margaret / Daddy John**: Adult daughter caring for aging parents; parents initially idolize Bill
- **Leanne ↔ Tyler & Josie**: Adult children; Tyler responsible, Josie chaotic; both processing parents' split
- **Leanne ↔ Andrew**: Tentative romance; both divorce-wary; kiss on cheek in S1E6
- **Carol ↔ Dylan**: Brief romance; Dylan vanishes when FBI reveals his warrant
- **Bill ↔ Faye**: Affair partner; pregnant; Bill claims he's miserable, not in love

## World Setting
- **Primary location**: Knoxville, Tennessee — suburban homes, local bars, church, hospital, RV Emporium
- **Time**: Present day (2025+); contemporary tech (email breakup, Life Alert alerts on phones)
- **Social milieu**: Southern Baptist-adjacent church community; multi-generational family; small-town gossip network
- **Economic texture**: Middle-class — RV dealership money, divorce settlement (large), Cadillac Escalade purchase

## Overall Plot Premise
A late-life coming-of-age: Leanne, defined for decades as wife/mother/grandmother, must rebuild identity after betrayal. The sitcom mines comedy from the gap between her polite Southern facade and raw anger, the family's terrible secret-keeping, and the absurdity of dating at 60+ in a town where everyone knows your business.

## Speaking Styles
- **Leanne**: Southern lilt, self-deprecating, observational, clean but sharp; "bless your heart" energy with bite; stand-up cadence in monologues
- **Carol**: Faster, cynical, Chicago-hardened; swears mildly ("crap," "hell"); protective big-sister vibe despite being younger
- **Mama Margaret**: Frail but manipulative; guilt-trips via fainting; Southern matriarch passive-aggression
- **Daddy John**: Gruff, pragmatic, old-school gender roles; "back in my day" stories; suspended license = recurring gag
- **Bill**: Deflective, entitled, tries to stay "good guy"; RV salesman patter
- **Josie**: Millennial chaos; wellness-speak mixed with edibles; "manifesting" vocabulary
- **Andrew**: Dry, procedural FBI speak softening into awkward flirtation; "I've been divorced twice" as pickup line
- **Mary**: Church-lady performative concern; gossip disguised as prayer requests

## Cultural Context for Translation
- **Southern hospitality masking judgment**: "Bless your heart" = contempt; translate tone not literal
- **Church as social hub**: Potlucks, fellowship hall, prayer chain = information network
- **Divorce stigma at 60+ in Bible Belt**: Leanne's shame vs. Carol's "twice-divorced" badge
- **RV culture**: Dealership as family business; "Emporium" grandeur vs. reality
- **Multi-cam sitcom conventions**: Live audience laughter beats; setup-punch rhythm; "button" lines before scene ends
- **Chuck Lorre fingerprints**: Broken people forming found family; redemption arcs; heart under cynicism
- **Leanne Morgan's real persona**: "Grandmama from Tennessee" stand-up brand; authenticity sells; translation must preserve her specific voice
