## S02E05

S02E05 — Leanne

## Plot

The episode braids three threads. Carol, worn down from being her mother's constant escort ("Mama's date"), begs Leanne to use her new boyfriend Clint to source a date. Leanne hesitates about "owing" Clint, then agrees; she asks her ex Bill, who only knows the candidate's name: Dutch. Meanwhile Bill coaches Daddy John through Tennessee's written driving exam, where John answers "live nude girls" for flashing-yellow school-bus lights and "live nude girls" again for the meaning of flashing yellow — Mama Margaret openly roots for the State of Tennessee to fail him.

Mama Margaret preps the sisters for the dinner: thongs a size too small, shaved legs, ankles crossed not knees. At the restaurant Clint shows up with Dutch, an arrogant Pittsburgh transplant who sells "designer" urinal cakes (corporate logos, aromatherapy, designer colors), boasts high-six-figures "sniffing seven," and bowls a 260. Dutch arrives unsure which sister is his date — he was told he'd meet "Luanne" — and is game for either. Carol discovers Clint is a father (nine-year-old Taylor) and that Dutch also has a kid Taylor; the "dog named Taylor" Carol had been swooning over on the phone turns out to be a child. The dinner turns combative: Dutch is rude about the waiter's spilled steak, insists on family-style portions, and provokes Carol until she snaps. Midway through, Dutch cracks and admits he's driven away everyone he's ever loved; Carol hugs him and admits the date "went well" because she finally spoke her mind.

In the B-plot, Mama Margaret's regular stylist Kimi is out of town, so Cricket fills in and gives Margaret a style no one asked for. Leanne makes a public scene demanding Cricket redo it, parroting Dutch's "chop chop, quick like a bunny" line. Leanne justifies the outburst as standing up for yourself; Mama counters that Southern restraint is its own kind of power. Daddy John finally takes the road test — fails on the first try, then drives himself home "to prove them wrong," earning Margaret's gratitude that the State did her dirty work.

## Important Character Actions

- Carol sets a "pickle" code word with her date for phone calls around the family, then begs Leanne for help escaping her "Mama's date" obligation.
- Leanne agrees to owe Clint a favor, gives Clint/Bill only a vague brief ("Any Christian with strong arms, blue eyes, cozy cabin in the Smoky Mountains. Anyone will do."), and ends up third-wheeling the dinner.
- Dutch bullies the waiter over a dropped rib eye, then confesses to driving away every loved one and accepts a hug from Carol.
- Leanne publicly confronts Cricket at the salon, demanding Margaret's hair be redone; later lectures Mama on "speaking up for yourself."
- Daddy John fails both written and road portions of his license test but drives home anyway.
- Mama Margaret refuses to intervene with John's driving ("a lady picks her battles") and celebrates his failure.

## Newly Established Names / Terms

- Clint — Leanne's new boyfriend, a father.
- Dutch — Carol's setup date, urinal-cake entrepreneur, raised in the south-side flats of Pittsburgh, now lives north-side.
- Taylor — Dutch's nine-year-old child (Carol initially believed Taylor was a dog).
- Cricket — substitute stylist covering for Kimi.
- Bill (continuing role) — now sourcing blind-date leads; familiar with Dutch.
- "Pickle" — phone code word for "they're listening."
- "Designer urinal cakes" / "urinal cake game" — Dutch's line of work, marketed with aromatherapy.
- "Bowling a 260" — Dutch's brag metric.
- "Chop chop, quick like a bunny" — Dutch's catchphrase, adopted by Leanne at the salon.
- "Three degrees of Southern Women" — closing framing device: too warm, too cold, just right.

## Continuity-Relevant Facts

- Leanne is now publicly dating Clint; Andrew (the FBI agent / prior love interest) is referenced only in passing — Carol says she "dipped her toe over the Mason-Dixon Line for 48 hours," suggesting a brief Northern trip is over.
- Daddy John's license is suspended/expired and his attempt to reinstate it fails; he still drives.
- Mama Margaret attends Zumba-adjacent fitness outings and is socially active enough to set up Carol via a hardware-store contact.
- Kimi (Margaret's regular stylist) is established as away, making Cricket the recurring sub.
- Carol is single and accepting setups; Leanne is coupled and third-wheeling.
- Margaret's moral philosophy is reaffirmed: restraint over scene-making.

## Jokes and References Useful for Later Episodes

- "Pickle" as a recurring code word for covert phone calls.
- Daddy John's running gag of misreading traffic signage as adult content.
- Dutch's "chop chop, quick like a bunny" line being recycled by Leanne — possible callback.
- The "three degrees of Southern Women" taxonomy that closes the episode — Leanne-as-Goldilocks framing.
- Carol's stock insult "arrogant pie wagon" for Dutch.
- "Nutty Buddy Creme Pies" and the breakup-kit motif echo from earlier S2 context.
- Dutch's urinal-cake branding details (Pittsburgh roots, designer colors, aromatherapy) — reusable quirks.
- "Marry me / Not in a million years" banter between Carol and Dutch — registers Dutch as a viable recurring presence.

## Unresolved Threads

- Carol and Dutch's relationship status after the date: she enjoyed being herself with him and would see him again, but no commitment is stated.
- Clint's exact relationship to Taylor — kitchen-gossip framing implies Clint is the father, but dialogue also has Dutch claim a child named Taylor; clarification needed in a later episode.
- Whether Leanne's salon outburst has social fallout with Cricket or the salon itself.
- Daddy John will presumably retake the road test.
- Leanne's "I can draw from the Southern woman handbook when I need it" hints at ongoing tension between her instinct to make scenes and Margaret's restraint — likely to recur.

## S02E01

# Episode S02E01

Plot
- The cold open is set on Leanne's birthday meal: she reveals she is turning 59, about to start a "lose a pound a week" campaign. She hopes for a facelift before 60 but admits Knoxville is only known for "diabetic shoes."
- Mama Margaret arrives at Leanne's house announcing Daddy John is missing. Landline has been dead for six years; Margaret is repeatedly confused (she "gave" her social security number to the wrong person earlier, but won't say when it actually disappeared).
- The women search the post office, the dump, Costco, and the VFW (where Margaret claims she is "an 11" and Leanne has to do a slow dance to escape the men). Daddy John is found at a dollar store buying a giant plastic margarita glass.
- Fire trucks arrive: Daddy and Mama's house is on fire. The cause is beans left on the stove (which Margaret claims Daddy left on, then blames herself). The kitchen and all appliances are destroyed.
- Leanne and Carol take the parents in, putting them in Carol's room (Carol takes it on the chin: "disappointment is her happy place").
- Leanne reads the disaster as a "cue" to research independent assisted living. Carol pushes back, calling the plan selfish; the sisters agree on "divide and conquer" — Leanne takes Mama, Carol takes Daddy, and each leverages the other's ambivalence.
- Leanne pitches Mama by appealing to her secret "city girl" identity (apartments with three dining halls, 24-hour medical care, beauty parlor, near downtown Knoxville). Mama reveals she has a secret bus pass and agrees eagerly, but warns Leanne will never move Daddy.
- Carol pitches Daddy with a romantic/vanity angle: Mama is "losing a step," he sees his wife "forever young and spry as a colt," and the move would be "for her." Daddy agrees.
- Driving to the facility at 15 mph, the parents balk in the parking lot. Mama says "I just can't" (distracted by a guy selling "gulf shrimp" out of a cooler on the roadside). Leanne backs out, promising to protect them under her own roof instead.
- On the porch that night, Carol makes "I-wanna-forget-today-ever-happened tea" and concedes the parents may now be a permanent fixture. Leanne laments her 60th birthday got buried, but reveals both kids called and Andrew sent flowers.
- Tag: Leanne and Carol sneak out to the VFW ("Fort Dixson 1700"). Leanne dances with a veteran named Gus, who jokes she is "the woman I leave my pension to." "I might, Gus. I might."

Important character actions
- Leanne turns 60 (or near it; the cold open says 59 tomorrow, then later lines frame her as 60). Birthdays with her kids are emotionally loaded — both called, none know the milestone number.
- Daddy John's wandering escalates, and he caused a house fire from forgetting beans on the stove. He admits he "may have turned a blind eye" to Mama's decline.
- Mama Margaret performs the assisted-living tour eagerly (cosmopolitan appeal) but physically cannot commit in the moment; the beans house fire is presented as evidence of her slipping.
- Leanne and Carol reconcile over Carol's smaller room being given up, with a bittersweet birthday toast on the porch.
- Andrew is off-screen but still in Leanne's life (sends flowers).

Newly established names, places, terms
- "Subaru Strangler" — Andrew's current case, located in the Pacific Northwest (Carol misreads as a "lesbian serial killer," Andrew jokes "women can do anything men can do").
- Gus — VFW regular flirting with Leanne in the tag, future potential recurring character.
- Fort Dixson VFW #1700 — specific club established as Leanne's social/romantic hangout.
- "Independent assisted living" — form of senior housing the sisters are pushing; amenities shown: three dining halls, beauty parlor, weekly excursions to the Smoky Mountain Speedway, dipping pool (the "tea-bag" dunking chair), chair yoga, low-sodium lunch ($11), a 12-to-1 women-to-men ratio, square dancing, a former Tennessee Vols tackle on staff for fall recovery.
- Busch Light — Daddy's surprise praise for the beer at the VFW.
- "Lumberjack special" — Carol's name for Daddy's enormous breakfast.
- Pennysaver — the free classifieds paper Daddy was supposedly reading when he "disappeared."

Continuity-relevant facts
- Leanne's exact age shifts in the cold open: she is turning 59, then "the big hmm-hmm" (60) next year. By the porch scene the number 60 is openly named and she jokes "I'm 60 and back living with my parents."
- Leanne's mother has been missing objects for six years (the dead landline predates her decline); social-security-number confusion flagged earlier by the family.
- Tyler's lawyer (not Tyler himself) visited Margaret and had her sign "some stuff" — likely estate planning, kept ambiguous for later plot.
- Andrew and Leanne are still coupled-adjacent; he is currently working out of state.
- Carol remains the live-in sister at Leanne's house.
- Daddy John: license still suspended, still can't operate self-checkout, still uses a hose to wash his hair, still day-drinks at the VFW despite promising Margaret.
- Margaret knows her social security number but faints/uses helplessness actively. She has a hidden bus pass she never told Daddy about — a small rebellion the writers may lean on later.
- Daddy's romantic frame for Margaret ("22, forever young, spry as a colt") is established as something he genuinely believes — useful later if her decline forces a reckoning.
- Leanne's plastic-surgeon search is open-ended; the Knoxville/LA/New York joke (NY facelifts, LA breasts, Knoxville diabetic shoes) can recur.

Jokes and references useful for later episodes
- "Pencil it in for me" type running gags established this episode: Daddy can't self-checkout (calls the machine a "robot whore," "skunky"), Daddy has the "hips of a 10-year-old" making pants sag with a phone, Carol's seat got "lowered" on the drive, Carol's bedroom nightstand may contain Kleenex (an in-joke about her sex life / lack thereof — "we gotta start praying harder for that girl").
- "Disappointment is her happy place" — Carol's defining line, memorable callback if she ever gets good news.
- The 12-to-1 women/men ratio at the assisted-living facility is a jokes goldmine.
- VFW as the brothel-romance playground for Leanne's 60+ arc (Gus the pensioner suitor; the "11" energy; the slow-dance exit fee).
- The "Pacific Northwest Subaru Strangler" is a background Andrew arc — when he returns, that case is his reason for absence.
- Leanne's self-pity birthday line "where did the time go" / Candy Crush is filed for future birthday-episode callbacks.
- Daddy's road-shrimp obsession (shrimp from a roadside cooler) is now canon.
- "We did good" porch ritual between Leanne and Carol at night, ending with sneaking out.

Unresolved threads
- The assisted-living push: parents agreed, then bailed at the curb. Is the tour rescheduled, or has Leanne conceded to permanent in-home care?
- Was the fire truly Mama's fault or Daddy's? The cloud of blame is being dodged by both — likely a future conflict about who is actually declining.
- Daddy now has an indoor phone rule ("It pulls my pants down," "I got the hips of a 10-year-old") that Margaret actively prevented; the unlocked-self-checkout vs. pants-on-the-floor dynamic is a "three strikes" baseline for future interference.
- Tyler's lawyer and the documents Margaret signed — possibly trust, possibly real estate, possibly a will.
- Andrew's case (Subaru Strangler) is off-screen and unresolved.
- Daddy's "five VFW slow dances" and ongoing flirting at the club; Gus may recur.
- Leanne's plastic-surgery search is on hold.
- The "three-strike" rule the sisters have for parental decline — Margaret's wandering and the beans fire may both be strikes, and a third strike is being implicitly awaited.
- Margaret's secret bus pass hints at a covert outings subplot for her.
- Leanne was about to flip the thermostat to 75 — Carol's menopause vs. Daddy-in-the-cold, an every-episode temperature war now codified.
