## Premise & Format

Six stand-up comedians split into two teams of three. Host **Dara Ó Briain** poses questions drawn from the past week’s news (politics, celebrity, royal family, international affairs, viral stories). The quiz element is deliberately sidelined; the focus is **topical satire, stand-up routines, and improvisation**.

**Typical episode structure (BBC era):**
- Four rounds selected from a pool filmed per episode
- No formal team captains; no persistent scoreboard
- Dara awards round wins at his discretion
- Unaired material → compilation / “Too Hot for TV” specials

**Revival (TLC, Series 22+):** hour-long episodes, new round “The Paper Review”, sole regular panellist **Rhys James** alongside rotating guests.

## Relationships & Dynamics

- **Dara Ó Briain ↔ Hugh Dennis**: anchor duo; Dara chairs, Hugh is the “everyman” foil.
- **Frankie Boyle vs. Rory Bremner (S1–2)**: “aggressive” vs. “impressionist” captain dynamic; Rory left citing discomfort with competitive atmosphere.
- **Andy Parsons ↔ Russell Howard (S4–9)**: younger, high-energy regulars balancing Hugh’s dryness.
- **Chris Addison (S10–12)**: “posh nerd” persona; often targeted for class jokes.
- **Guest panellists**: rotate weekly; no fixed teams after S2. Chemistry varies — some guests (Jo Brand, John Oliver, Milton Jones) become semi-regulars by sheer frequency.
- **TLC revival**: Rhys James as sole regular; guests include newer UK circuit comics (e.g. Sophie Duker, Kiri Pritchard-McLean, Ahir Shah).

## World Setting & Cultural Context

- **Contemporary UK**: each episode reflects the **news cycle of the week before filming** — politics (Brexit, PM changes, elections), royals, NHS, cost-of-living, celebrity scandals, viral internet moments.
- **British panel-show tradition**: descendant of *Have I Got News for You*, *QI*, *Would I Lie to You?* — but **edgier, stand-up–centric, less reverent**.
- **Class & regional accents**: humour often mines North/South divide, posh vs. working-class, Scottish/Welsh/Irish/English stereotypes.
- **Profanity & “Ofcom” line**: BBC version pushed broadcast limits (Frankie Boyle era drew formal complaints); TLC version post-watershed, freer language.
- **Compilation episodes**: “Best of”, Christmas specials, “Too Hot for TV” DVDs — recurrent source of callback jokes.

## Speaking Styles & Translation Notes

| Persona | Style | Subtitle Strategy |
|---|---|---|
| **Dara Ó Briain** | Articulate, quick-witted, occasional Irishisms (“grand”, “feck”), meta-commentary on format | Keep crisp; preserve Irish flavour sparingly; translate format explanations literally |
| **Hugh Dennis** | Dry, self-deprecating, “baffled everyman”, puns | Preserve deadpan; puns → functional equivalents or translator’s note |
| **Frankie Boyle** | Dark, offensive, surreal, Scottish accent | **Do not sanitise**; use equally sharp Chinese vulgarity; flag cultural references (e.g. Rebecca Adlington) |
| **Rory Bremner** | Impressions (Blair, Brown, Trump, etc.), political satire | Identify target in brackets first occurrence; mimic register |
| **Andy Parsons** | Left-wing, rhetorical, “angry young man” | Keep political edge; translate “Tory”, “Labour”, “Lib Dem” directly |
| **Russell Howard** | High-energy, observational, physical, Bristol accent | Capture enthusiasm; accent → neutral unless plot-relevant |
| **Chris Addison** | Posh, intellectual, self-mocking | Use slightly formal register; “public school” references → 精英/贵族学校 |
| **Rhys James** | Millennial/Gen-Z, meta, queer perspective | Contemporary slang; LGBTQ+ references naturalised |
| **Guests (varied)** | One-liners (Milton), storytelling (Ed Byrne), character (Al Murray) | Match each comic’s voice; recurring guests → consistent rendering |

**Recurring translation challenges:**
- **Topical references** (MP names, UK towns, tabloid headlines) → add brief inline gloss or TN.
- **Puns / wordplay** → prioritise humour over literalness; use “TN: pun on X” if untranslatable.
- **Censored words** (bleeped on BBC) → ★/○ or Chinese equivalent; TLC uncensored → translate fully.
- **Running gag: round-title changes** → translate each episode’s silly title (e.g. “Ashley Cole Just Sent Me a Picture of His Mock” → “阿什利·科尔刚发了张他的鸟照”).
