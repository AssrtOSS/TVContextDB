## S01E01

## Rian na Fola — Episode Summary (Single Documentary)

### Episode Plot
Former Kerry footballer and broadcaster **Dara Ó Cinnéide** investigates the 1930 murder of **Patrick M. Foley**, a West Kerry writer, historian, and Irish-language activist who emigrated to the US in 1909 and was brutally killed in Sallisaw, Oklahoma. The documentary traces Foley’s life from the Corca Dhuibhne Gaeltacht through Depression-era America — Boston, Texas, Cherokee Territory, Oklahoma — uncovering archival material, family testimony, and forensic insight to restore Foley to West Kerry’s cultural record.

---

### Important Character Actions
- **Dara Ó Cinnéide** (presenter): narrates in Irish and English; travels from Dún Chaoin cliffs to Boston, Fort Worth, and Sallisaw; interviews historians, forensic experts, and Foley’s granddaughters.
- **Mary McGillick** & **Mairéad Bolger** (granddaughters): share family lore, reveal Foley’s trunk of manuscripts/photos sent home after his death, describe their mother’s reverence for a father she barely knew.
- **Dr. Muiris MacGiollabhuí** (Purdue): contextualises Foley’s movements alongside Éamon de Valera’s US fundraising tours; links Foley to Irish nationalist circles (Tom Ashe, Austin Stack).
- **Boston historian**: details Foley’s arrest records (1924 assault on a policeman; 1927 drunkenness commitment to Boston Psychopathic Hospital for two months); reveals “knockout drops” used by Gustin Gang.
- **Oklahoma local (Uriah Grass / Eagle Grass)**: guides Dara to Badger Lee Mountain area; describes 1930s Sallisaw as lawless — outlaws, moonshiners, railroad, river traffic.
- **Forensic / newspaper analysis**: death certificate lists “assassination”; body found with $1.26 in pocket (undermining robbery motive); black bag Foley carried vanished; funeral home burgled post-mortem for documents from Foley’s body.

### Continuity-Relevant Facts
- Foley active in **Conradh na Gaeilge** and **Conradh na Talún** (Land League) in West Kerry pre-emigration.
- Lived with **John Kennedy** in Boston (Dara jokes: “Ó Cinnéide!”).
- Travelled extensively 1910s–1920s: San Francisco, Cuba, Canada, Philadelphia, Niagara Falls — expensive for the era; not explained by carpentry or book sales.
- Arrested 1924 for assault on Patrolman Edward Kelly; 1927 committed to psychopathic hospital after found undressed in February Boston streets — possible drugging by Gustin Gang.
- 1920 Fort Worth census: coincides with de Valera’s US tour; Foley in same cities as de Valera.
- **Anti-bootlegging stance** documented in writings; KKK in 1920s Oklahoma targeted Catholics/immigrants and enforced Prohibition.
- Final days: visited Fort Smith police station, introduced himself, promised no trouble; met Jack Adams Fri/Sat/Mon before death, claimed **New York gangsters** pursuing him, said he’d been drugged with narcotics and held a year in Boston hospital.
- Death certificate: **“assassination”** (not gunshot wound).
- Post-mortem: funeral home broken into; documents from Foley’s body stolen.
- Family lore (granddaughters): **KKK execution** theory; local Oklahoma expert rates KKK motive lower than organised crime.

### Unresolved Threads
1. **Source of Foley’s travel money** — no legitimate income explains coast-to-coast movement.
2. **Black bag contents** — vanished at murder site; likely contained incriminating documents.
3. **Funeral home burglary** — who ordered the targeted document theft?
4. **“New York gangsters”** — Foley’s specific claim to Jack Adams; never identified.
5. **Boston hospital year-long stay** — Foley claimed drugged by enemies; records incomplete.
6. **KKK vs. organised crime** — both plausible; documentary leaves open.
7. **Manuscripts sent home after death** — unknown sender; granddaughters donated to National Library of Ireland.
8. **Foley’s exact role in Irish nationalist networks** — proximity to de Valera, Ashe, Stack suggested but not proven.
