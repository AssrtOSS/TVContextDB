#绅士们 / The Gentlemen — Subtitle Translation Background

## Verification

This background covers the Netflix television series titled exactly **The Gentlemen** (no subtitle), created by Guy Ritchie for Netflix as a spin-off of his 2019 film of the same name.

- Format: Television series (action comedy / black comedy / crime drama); not the 2019 film, not the 2016 Singaporean drama, not any other homonym.
- Country / language: United Kingdom & United States co-production, original language English.
- Broadcaster: Netflix (global streaming).
- First air date: March 7, 2024 (Season 1).
- Renewals: Season 2 premiered September 3, 2026; Season 3 was ordered in August 2026 ahead of Season 2's debut.
- Episode structure: 2 seasons, 16 episodes total (8 per season). Episode runtimes 41–67 minutes.
- Production: Moonage Pictures and Miramax Television; filmed in London and at Badminton House (November 2022 – June 2023).

## Overall Plot Premise

When the 12th Duke of Halstead dies, his second son — former British Army captain and UN peacekeeper Edward "Eddie" Horniman — unexpectedly inherits the dukedom, the crumbling Halstead Manor estate and, with it, an enormous underground cannabis empire secretly operated beneath the grounds by the Glass criminal dynasty. A reluctant aristocrat is pulled into the British underworld alongside Susie Glass, the sharp-witted de facto head of the Glass syndicate while her father Bobby is serving time in a luxury open-air prison. Across the two seasons the Horniman brothers, the Glasses and a rotating gallery of rival gangs, travellers, billionaires and ex-military fixers fight, scheme and double-cross each other for control of the trade — with Eddie gradually evolving from reluctant bystander into a fully fledged criminal operator.

## Key Relationships

- Eddie and Freddy Horniman: brothers; Eddie inherits the dukedom and lands, Freddy — older but irresponsible — is bypassed and is in hock to the Scouse cocaine family for £8 million.
- Eddie and Susie Glass: reluctant partners who marry at the end of Season 1; together they eventually inherit Bobby's empire.
- Eddie and Bobby Glass: Eddie negotiates directly with the imprisoned patriarch; Bobby ultimately tests Eddie andSusie as his successors.
- Geoff Seacombe and Sabrina Horniman: former lovers; Geoff is revealed to be the biological father of Charly.
- Freddy and Susie: Freddy proposes having Eddie killed so he can take over the estate; Susie refuses on family grounds.
- Henry Collins and Jack Glass: ex-army boxing promoter manages Jack as a boxer and ultimately uses the ring to attack the Glasses.
- Stanley Johnston and the Glass empire: Stanley tries to acquire Halstead Manor and run rival cocaine / meth operations; he is eventually arrested and joins Bobby in the luxury prison.
- The Hornimans and the 12 aristocratic "host" landowners: a network of country estates secretly hosting Glass cannabis operations, with Lord "Tibsy" Whitecroft as the former Glass associate who knows them all.
- John "The Gospel" Dixon and Tommy Dixon: brothers, rival heirs of the Liverpool cocaine family.

## World Setting

- Halstead Manor: fictional English aristocratic estate (filmed at Badminton House, Gloucestershire) whose grounds conceal a vast underground cannabis farm; seat of the Dukes of Halstead.
- Halstead Estate / Estate: as used by the characters, refers to both the house and the surrounding land; in dialogue "the estate" usually means the Halstead property itself.
- The luxury open-air prison: a fictional high-security prison for wealthy offenders where Bobby Glass and later Stanley Johnston are held; rooftop pigeon-keeping is a recurring motif.
- London filming locations: Longcross Studios, West London Film Studios, Princess Victoria pub in Shepherd's Bush, Tate Modern, Printworks London, Hackney and Islington streets, Stoke Court in Stoke Poges (Buckinghamshire) doubling as the prison, and a property on West End Lane as Chucky's mansion.
- British social landscape that frames the story: titled aristocracy on the edge of ruin, big country houses, the shooting / hunting set (gamekeeper, pheasants), upper-crust manners layered over criminal enterprise, plus the Liverpudlian (Scouse) cocaine trade, Irish Traveller families, Kosovan-Albanian gangs, Colombian and Belgian drug pipelines, and an American billionaire class.

## Recurring Terminology and Chinese Renderings

Estate / Manor / Grounds — 庄园, 领地, 宅邸. "Halstead Estate" and "the estate" in dialogue almost always refer to Halstead Manor and its lands; render consistently as 霍斯特德庄园 (house) vs 霍斯特德领地 / 霍斯特德的土地 (the wider grounds including the cannabis fields). "Manor" by itself is usually 庄园或宅邸.

The Dukedom / the dukedom / the title — 公爵爵位. "The dukedom" is the peerage itself; "the title" is shorthand for it; "the estate" usually follows.

Duke / Duchess / Dowager Duchess / Lord — 公爵 / 公爵夫人 / 遗孀公爵夫人 / 勋爵. Apply titles before the name on first reference, then drop or keep the surname-only form thereafter (e.g. 霍尼曼公爵 → 霍尼曼).

Gamekeeper — 猎场看守人 / 猎场主. Geoff's role; an old English countryside profession tied to poaching, hunting and country-house life.

Cannabis / weed / marijuana / grow / the farm / the operation — 大麻. Render "weed farm" as 大麻种植场 or 大麻农场, "the operation" as (格拉斯家族的)生意 / 业务 /贩毒生意. "Grow" as a noun = 大麻种植场; as a verb = 种植大麻.

Cleaner (in crime sense) — 清理工, "清道夫"; Felix's job is to dispose of bodies and crime scenes, so in dialogue translate as 「清道夫」or 「现场清理工」.

Money laundering / to wash money — 洗钱. "Wash" used as a verb (Thick Rick "washes" money through boxing bets) = 洗钱.

Crystal meth / meth — 冰毒 / 甲基苯丙胺. Stanley's empire.

Cocaine — 可卡因. The Scouse / Dixon family's trade.

Distributor / distribution network — 分销人 / 分销网络 / 运毒渠道. Florian de Groot and later JP Ward are both described as "distributors".

Travellers (Irish Travellers) — 「旅行人」社区 / 「流浪者」族群. A distinct ethnic/cultural group in the British Isles; do not translate as 流浪汉 (which would mean homeless vagrants). Standard Chinese coverage tends to keep 「旅行人」or游民家族.

Scouse — Liverpool / 默西塞德; "Scouse family" = 利物浦可卡因家族 / 默西塞德家族. "Scouser" = 利物浦人 / 默西塞德人.

The Glass empire / Glass dynasty — 格拉斯家族 / 格拉斯贩毒集团. Refers to the whole criminal organization, not just the family.

Bobby's "retirement" / the "test" — 鲍比的"退位" / "考验". In Season 1 finale Bobby's announced retirement turns out to be a test of Eddie andSusie's suitability to inherit the empire.

"Gentleman" — 绅士 / 老爷. The show's title and recurring nickname for senior male criminals held at the luxury prison (Bobby is the original "gentleman"; Stanley is later described as a new "gentleman" when he joins him there).

The Halstead wine cellar / the wine collection — 霍斯特德酒窖 / 酒庄藏品. Eddie raises £4 million by selling it to Stanley.

The "Halstead dozen" / the 12 landowners — 霍斯特德十二庄园主 / 十二位地主. The other aristocratic estates secretly hosting Glass farms.

## Speaking Styles and Cultural Context

- Tone: Ritchie's signature British crime caper — rapid, dry, darkly comic, laced with cockney / aristocratic / Liverpudlian / Irish-Traveller / American-inflected dialogue. Translators should preserve the contrast between upper-crust politeness ("quite", "rather", "old chap", "I say") and blunt criminal slang.
- Aristocratic register: drawn-out vowels, hedging, formal address. Characters like Lady Sabrina, Lord Tibsy, Lord Bassington and Lord Freddie speak in clipped, mannered English; subtitles should keep an equivalent register (e.g. "I shall", "do shut up", "rather indelicate") rather than colloquial speech.
- Cockney / criminal slang: Susie, Felix, Chucky, Keith, Blanket — short, sharp, often single-word or noun-phrase sentences. Use 国内犯罪片常见口语 rather than literal translation. "Boss", "guv'nor", "love", "duck" → 老板 / 大佬 / 亲爱的 / 妹子 as appropriate.
- Scouse (Liverpool): John "The Gospel" Dixon speaks with Liverpudlian cadence, religious-evangelical turns of phrase ("the Lord provides", "hallelujah") — render with a northern-English flavour in Chinese, e.g. 「主啊」「感谢上帝」.
- Irish Traveller: the Ward family uses Traveller cant and insults; keep the rough, conspiratorial tone. JP is pragmatic, Kellie Ann more impulsive.
- American: Stanley Johnston and Emory Stevens use clipped American-business English with a faintly menacing politeness; Giancarlo Esposito's character especially favours pauses and formal diction.
- Eddie Horniman: mid-Atlantic ex-military English — measured, occasionally clipped Army cadence ("at once", "understood", "stand down"), breaking into sarcasm or dark humour as he adapts to the underworld.
- Freddy Horniman: louche, hedonistic, often cocaine-fuelled; lines frequently trail off or escalate into rages — convey impulsiveness.
- Cultural context for translators: keep English place-names, peerage titles, and idioms intact in the first reference (Halstead, Halstead Manor, Duke of Halstead, Lord Bassington Smythe, Zeebrugge, Princess Rosanne). For British criminal argot ("job", "job lot", "firm", "the bill", "bent", "grass", "bent copper"), prefer established organised-crime vocabulary (差事 / 一票 / 团伙 / 警方 / 腐败的 / 告密者 / 警局内线) over literal glosses. Do not soften the violence, drug references or racism inherent to several scenes (the Hitler-testicle plot, Max Bassington's secret, Stanley's meth operation); they are part of the black-comedy tone.
