## Series Overview

**Format**: Television series (scripted comedy)  
**First-air date**: November 6, 2026

**Season/Episode structure**: Season 1 — 6 episodes (~28–30 min each)  
**Creator/Writer**: Jared Keeso  
**Directors**: Sean Skene (eps 1–3), Dan Skene (eps 4–6)  
**Production**: New Metric Media / Play Fun Games Pictures  
**Country of origin**: Canada  
**Language**: English (with Franco-Ontarian French elements)  
**Filming location**: Sudbury, Ontario

## Synopsis

A fourth-generation Franco-Ontarian family of bear wranglers for film sets decides, after 100 years in the business, to make their final film. The Thibaults — kind, well-intentioned, and bush-isolated — suddenly find themselves navigating a Hollywood production world they don't fully understand. The series juxtaposes their unconventional, hands-on bear-wrangling methods against "New Hollywood" PR machinery, animal-welfare scrutiny, and the family's own legacy.

## World Setting

- **Primary locale**: Northern Ontario bush (family base) + film sets (Hollywood productions shooting in Canada).
- **Time**: Contemporary (2026).
- **Industry context**: Animal-wrangling for film is under scrutiny; "New Hollywood" means sensitivity readers, PR teams, insurance mandates, CGI pressure.
- **Cultural texture**: Franco-Ontarian identity — French first names, English-dominant dialogue peppered with Québecois/French-Canadian idioms, Catholic-school references, bush self-reliance mythos. The Thibaults are *not* Québécois; they are Franco-Ontarian (distinct minority within Ontario).

## Speaking Styles

- **Gabby (Keeso)**: Deadpan, economical, Letterkenny-rhythm — short sentences, repetitive cadence for comic timing, occasional French interjections ("tabarnak", "câlisse" — mild, contextual).
- **Andrée**: Sharp, verbally quick, feminist-aware, code-switches French/English fluidly.
- **Olivier (GSP)**: Soft-spoken, physically expressive, philosophical in a simple way; "the bear needs calm" delivered like a mantra. French accent noticeable but English fluent.
- **Cédric (Torrens)**: High-energy, absurd, meta, breaks fourth wall lightly; J-Roc-adjacent energy but distinct.
- **Hollywood characters (Timothy, Rose, Edward, Kathy, Bob)**: Corporate-speak, HR language, "safe" euphemisms, passive-aggressive politeness — contrast with Thibault directness.
- **Son of Grizzly Adams (Kroeger)**: Gravelly, rock-star mythic, half-joking legend-building.

**French insertions**: Expect *tabarnak, câlisse, ostie, sacrament, esti* as emotional punctuation (mild Quebec swearing). Render as-is or with functional equivalents (e.g., "他妈的", "操") — **do not sanitize**; they are character voice. Formal French lines (Andrée especially) may appear; translate meaning, keep flavor.

## Cultural Context for Translation

1. **Franco-Ontarian ≠ Québécois**: The Thibaults are a minority within a minority. Do not use Québécois-specific slang (e.g., *dépanneur, cégep, tabarnak* is shared but *ciboire* less so). Use neutral Canadian French or context-appropriate Ontario French.
2. **Letterkenny/Shoresy universe**: Same creator, overlapping cast, shared comedic DNA (rhythmic dialogue, recurring bits, "puppers" beer). Translators familiar with Letterkenny Chinese subs (信使镇 / 小镇大法官) should maintain tonal consistency.
3. **Indigenous presence**: Episode 103–104 guest Andrew Antsanen (Inuk actor) as "Man from Nunavut"; respectful portrayal expected. Avoid stereotypes.
4. **Animal welfare / CGI debate**: Real bears used on set; ethical tension is a running thread. Terminology: "live animal" vs "CGI bear" — translate precisely.
5. **Canadian media cameos**: Jay Onrait, George Stroumboulopoulos, Ben Mulroney, Jann Arden, Brent Butt, Kim Coates, Tricia Helfer, Melanie Scrofano, Hannah Simone, Maxim Roy — playing heightened versions of themselves. Chinese audiences may not know them; add brief contextual gloss if plot-relevant.
6. **Brad Pitt / Ed Norton as themselves**: Meta cameos in finale. Translate names standard; keep self-referential jokes intact.
