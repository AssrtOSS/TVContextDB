## S05E05

# The Traitors US — S05E05 Summary

## Plot

The episode opens mid-roundtable from the previous night. Jay lays out a sprawling case against Katie as a Traitor, citing her posture shifting after the blindfold selection and a comment she allegedly made about being a Traitor. Katie fires back that Jay is the real problem: a "crock pot of confusion" whose chaos has derailed the Faithful for the third consecutive round. Sherry, silent the prior night, delivers an impassioned monologue defending herself against Jay's accusation that she privately claimed "no way all three Traitors are women" — she argues she's 65, plays quietly, and never volunteers names without certainty. Other players call out the absurdity of the dynamic. Votes land 5 Jay / 8 Sherry / 1 Michael (Victor voting Michael out of lingering suspicion), and Jay's own vote for Sherry pushes her over the threshold. At the Circle of Truth, Sherry reveals as Faithful, making it three Faithfuls banished in a row ("literally 0 for 3").

Alan then announces a twist: nine players are on the murder short list, and the Traitors are tasked with murdering one of them "in plain sight" by throwing a cursed apple from the castle kitchen — whoever catches an apple dies. Joe, the most recently recruited Traitor, draws the short straw to perform the throw. He first targets Mark (trust-based throw), then Morgan (she doesn't move her hands), panics, and bites the apple himself to play it off. He pivots back to Mark, pelting him with gummy bears, and ultimately completes the throw before the castle clock chimes.

The group is then marched to a graveyard where each short-list member lies down in a coffin bearing their name. Alan asks for eulogies, then calls out the survivors one by one: Tomica, Kriste, Abby L, Morgan, and Victor escape. That leaves Clyde, Niyyah, Joe, and Mark underground — and the episode cuts on Alan beginning to reveal the murder victim.

## Important Character Actions

- **Jay**: Delivers the longest accusation of the episode against Katie; votes for Sherry; fails to write names on his actual ballot (called out post-banishment — "you don't even have the guts to write it down").
- **Sherry**: Breaks silence with a pointed, eloquent defense, mainly aimed at Jay; banished; revealed as Faithful.
- **Katie**: Counter-attacks Jay's behavior as more treacherous than any Traitor's; votes for Sherry.
- **Victor**: Holds to a gut vote for Michael despite no conviction from anyone else tonight.
- **Joe (Traitor)**: Chosen to perform the apple throw; visibly guilt-ridden; botches first attempt at Morgan; eventually succeeds against Mark (implied).
- **Tomica (likely Traitor)**: Offers apples around the group; freaks out when Michael notices.
- **Michael**: Notices Joe eating fruit out of character and Tomica's suspicious apple-offering — a real behavioral tell that survives the night.
- **Mark**: Identified by Traitors as a high-trust target because he'll instinctively catch thrown items; pelted with gummy bears as a setup.
- **Niyyah**: Emotional throughout; delivers a heartfelt eulogy for her fellow "coffin" players; her fart in the coffin is a notable beat.
- **Victor**: Names "buried alive" as his biggest fear; visibly shaken seeing his own tombstone.

## Newly Established Names / Terms

- **Murder in plain sight**: New mechanic — Traitors must kill a named short-list member while other players watch, using cursed apples.
- **Cursed apples**: Apples hidden in the castle kitchen; whoever catches one is murdered.
- **Wake of the living dead**: The themed event framing the murder — a funeral for someone not yet dead.
- **Circle of Truth**: The reveal stage where banished players disclose Faithful or Traitor (Sherry uses it here).
- **Subterranean darlings**: Alan's flourish for the players lying in coffins underground.

## Continuity-Relevant Facts

- Faithful have now banished three Faithfuls in a row; the group openly acknowledges this failure.
- Joe is confirmed as a Traitor and explicitly identified as the "newest" / recruited one, drawing the murder assignment.
- Tomica is strongly implicated as a Traitor (in the planning huddle with Joe, actively offering apples, saying "he's onto us" about Michael).
- Confirmed or near-confirmed suspect pool after this episode: Joe, Tomica, plus either Katie or Jay as the third Traitor (the original trio suspected by the house — Katie, Jay, Kriste — gets narrowed by the roundtable but never resolved).
- Nine players enter the short list; eight emerge alive; one is murdered by episode's end (cliffhanger).
- The "no way all three Traitors are women" line becomes a flashpoint — Sherry's denial of saying it doesn't fully convince everyone.
- Jay's refusal to write names on paper (only verbal accusations) is now a known pattern other players can weaponize.
- The "blindfold" moment in the first roundtable remains unexplained context for Jay's posture-based theory.

## Jokes / References Useful for Later Episodes

- "Worn me slap out" — Sherri's Southern exhaustion phrase aimed at Jay.
- "Crock pot of confusion" — Katie's nickname for Jay's gameplay style.
- "Ripping the Band-Aid off" — Sherry explaining why she finally spoke.

- Joe's metaphor: "driving an 18-wheeler down a narrow highway at 90 miles per hour in a blinding snowstorm."
- "I forget whose side I'm on" — Joe (Traitor) admitting he gets lost in his Faithful performance.
- Niyyah's coffin fart — likely to be replayed/ referenced.

## Unresolved Threads

- Who was murdered? (Cliffhanger among Clyde, Niyyah, Joe, Mark — Joe cannot be the victim since he threw the apple, narrowing it to Clyde, Niyyah, or Mark.)
- Who are the three (or more) Traitors? — Joe and Tomica appear confirmed; the third remains unproven, with Katie, Jay, and Kriste all still under suspicion.
- The "blindfold" first-roundtable event — referenced but never explained.
- Jay's pattern of accusing without writing names — will be a flashpoint at the next roundtable.

- Michael's suspicion of Joe and Tomica — a planted seed that could grow next episode.
- Whether Kriste is a Traitor or simply paranoid — her accusation that she "couldn't make eye contact with Jay" was never resolved.

## S05E07

## Episode Summary: The Traitors US S05E07

### Episode Plot
The episode opens with the aftermath of Niyyah's public "murder" (the "Fruitgate" incident). The Traitors meet in the turret and decide to target **Mark** — a universally trusted, "absolute Faithful" — to sow maximum chaos. Mark is murdered that night, devastating the group (he's affectionately called "the goldendoodle").

A scarecrow-field mission follows. Players race to rip open scarecrows for gold bags (adding $15,300 to the pot, now $112,300) while avoiding **black hearts** — four hidden traps that eliminate a player from the mission and the chance to win a new **game-changing power**. Shane uses a black heart on Victor; another player hits Morgan; Abby L hits Katie; Ben uses the last on Katie. **Joe** collects the most gold and wins the **Power of the Pardon** — the ability to overturn tonight's banishment.

At the Round Table, **Victor** aggressively pushes his Fruitgate theory (Tomica gave Niyyah fruit → Niyyah died). **Abby L** comes under fire for having directed votes toward multiple Faithfuls (Arisa, Sherry, Michael). **Tomica** is accused of a name slip (saying "Katie" instead of "Kriste"). Votes split: Victor 5, Tomica 3, Wyatt 2, Abby L 1, Morgan 1, Katie 1. **Victor is banished** — but Alan reveals the Power of the Pardon before he leaves. **Joe** holds it and can save Victor or let the banishment stand. The episode ends on this cliffhanger.

## S05E09

## The Traitors US S05E09 Summary

### Episode Plot
The episode opens with the players celebrating Tomica's banishment as a Traitor at a Highland feast hosted by Alan Cumming. After dinner, Alan introduces a **Poisoned Chalice** twist: the Traitors secretly select one player to receive poison; everyone must drink but may swap glasses. If a Traitor drinks the poison, no murder occurs. During the frantic two-minute swap window, **Victor** aggressively tries to trade (revealing he believes he's targeted), **Joe** refuses to swap with Victor but swaps with **Shane**, and **Kriste** snatches her glass suspiciously fast. **Morgan** ends up drinking the poison and is murdered.

The next day, the surviving players undertake a haunted-cabin mission ("Keep your spirits up" = ghosts). They summon spirits, identify cherished items (spicy homebrew, white heels, wedding dress), and earn **$18,000** for the prize pot (now at $18k this episode). **Abbey B** memorably dives headfirst into a toilet to retrieve the final item. No one finds the **Shield** offered during the mission.

### Important Character Actions
- **Victor**: Dominates the glass-swap chaos, demands trades, frames himself as a target to deflect suspicion; later pushes hard for Joe's banishment.
- **Joe**: Refuses to swap with Victor (claiming Victor wouldn't poison himself), swaps with Shane instead; becomes the primary target of Victor's faction.
- **Shane**: Trusts Joe, swaps glasses with him; survives but is shaken.
- **Kriste**: Grabs her glass instantly during the swap, stays eerily calm ("whatever will be will be"), drawing heavy suspicion from multiple players who theorize she was recruited after the first Round Table.
- **Abbey B**: Mission MVP — retrieves the final item from a toilet, earning praise and $18k for the pot.
- **Morgan**: Murdered via poisoned chalice; her exit yields no strategic information ("told me nothing").

### Newly Established Names/Terms
- **Poisoned Chalice** — new murder mechanic: Traitors assign poison to one glass; players may swap; Traitor drinking poison = no murder.
- **"Fruitgate"** — running joke/reference to Victor's earlier grape/pocket-fruit moment, used to mock his suspicion of Joe.

### Continuity-Relevant Facts
- **Tomica** (Traitor) was banished in the previous episode (E08).
- **Abby L** (murdered earlier) had targeted **Wyatt** before death; her "dying wish" keeps Wyatt under suspicion.
- **Victor** and **Wyatt** identified as ringleaders of a "boys' club" alliance.
- **Joe** vs. **Victor** is now the central conflict; **Kriste** and **Wyatt** are alternate banishment targets.
- No Shield is in play entering the next Round Table.

### Jokes / References for Later Episodes
- **"Keep your spirits up"** — Alan's pun hinting at the ghost/spirit mission.
- **Victor's grape/pocket-fruit moment** — becomes "Fruitgate," a meme used to undermine his credibility.
- **Abbey's toilet dive** — "worth going down a toilet headfirst for" / "I've smelled worse in 13 years as a nurse."
- **Alan's "Guten morgen... because Morgan is no more"** — signature dark humor.

### Unresolved Threads
1. **Who are the remaining Traitors?** — Joe, Victor, Kriste, and Wyatt are the main suspects; the group is split between voting Joe or Kriste.
2. **Was Kriste recruited after the first Round Table?** — Multiple players theorize her behavior shift (aggressive → meek) signals recruitment.
3. **Will Victor's aggression backfire?** — His controlling behavior during the swap and at breakfast makes him a target for the Faithful.
4. **No Shield in play** — Next murder will be unpreventable; Traitors have a free kill.
5. **Prize pot** — Currently increased by $18k this episode; total not stated but accumulating.

## S05E03

# The Traitors US — S05E03 Summary

## Episode Plot
The episode opens with the resolution of the "Accomplices" twist from E02: Katie and Xavier competed head-to-head to retrieve "death warrants." Katie succeeded; Xavier failed. The existing Traitor trio (Joe, Tomica, and one unseen) chose Katie to become a full Traitor; Xavier was **murdered**.  
Morning arrives: players discover Xavier's absence. Alan Cumming introduces the day's mission — a **battering ram challenge** across the castle grounds. Three ancient crypts hold gold (up to $30,000 total) plus hidden twists (shields, "top secret Traitor information"). The group collects only $15,000 (prize pot: $50k → $65k).  
Crucially, Michael and Ben chose a **clue over $10k extra** in the third crypt. The clue reads: *"Last night, two players battled in plain sight to become a Traitor"* and describes a **"recruitment in plain sight"** — a hand-holding "truth test" between Arisa and Victor in the billiards room.  
At the **Round Table**, Niyyah claims she saw Arisa pocket a shield in the first crypt; Michael and Ben reveal the clue; a player outs themselves as a **body language expert** and asks if anyone's demeanor shifted post-recruitment.

## S05E04

## Episode plot
- The episode picks up at a Round Table where suspicion centers on Victor (tied to the “recruitment in plain sight” clue involving Arisa) and Arisa (the hand-holding incident). Michael is also questioned for being too calm and surviving a murder. In the vote, Arisa receives the most votes and is banished; at the Circle of Truth she reveals she is a Faithful, marking another failed banishment for the group.
- That night in the turret, the original Traitors welcome newly recruited Katie. They discuss shifting heat and consider Sherry after Abby B. reports that Sherry said, “There’s no way all three Traitors are women,” raising suspicion about how Sherry knows there are three Traitors. For the murder, they debate Logan, Abby L., and Mark, and let Katie (the “teeny Traitor”) make the final choice.
- At breakfast, Logan does not arrive and is revealed to have been murdered. Players connect his murder to his organized, analytical presence at the Round Table and note he had mentioned Xavier bringing up Katie’s name.
- The mission is a timed “death parade” challenge: players must move carts of gold ($24,000 total) through gates while being pursued. To open gates, some players must stay behind in cages and are placed on a shortlist for that night’s murder. The group solves the gates, carries barrels to the end, and lights a fire to stop the parade, banking $12,000. Ten players are left in cages/shortlisted: Victor, Morgan, Niyyah, Mark, Tomica, Kriste, Sherry, Clyde, Abby L., and Joe. Alan announces a funeral for one of them later that night.
- After the mission, tensions spike around Jay’s chaotic gameplay and his refusal to share names before the Round Table. Jay spreads suspicion toward Katie (and also mentions Victor/Kriste), claiming the Traitors reveal themselves through his “chaos.” The episode ends at the next Round Table as Jay begins making a formal case that Katie is a Traitor.

## Important character actions
- **Arisa**: Defends herself against the hand-holding accusation; votes Michael; is banished and revealed as a Faithful.
- **Victor**: Remains under suspicion from the clue/Arisa incident; survives the vote; volunteers to stay behind at the first mission gate to prove loyalty.
- **Michael**: Presses Victor and frames his own survival as strategic; is defended by Joe as honorable and analytical; later becomes mildly suspicious to others.
- **Katie**: Newly recruited Traitor; participates in turret planning; is protected by Joe; becomes Jay’s primary target by episode’s end.
- **Joe (Traitor)**: Publicly vouches for Michael; shares a personal moment with Michael after the Round Table; in the turret, relays intel about Sherry’s “three Traitors” comment and steers murder discussion.
- **Tomica (Traitor)**: Part of the turret trio; supports recruiting/murder strategy and helps evaluate targets.
- **Logan**: Speaks up about Xavier mentioning Katie; is murdered overnight; his absence is noted as a loss of a calm, fact-based voice.
- **Sherry**: Quiet at the Round Table; tells Abby B. they should target men and that there likely aren’t “three female Traitors,” which Abby B. reports as suspicious.
- **Abby B.**: Notices and shares Sherry’s “three Traitors” slip as potential evidence.
- **Jay**: Causes friction by withholding names, demanding to light the mission fire in exchange for revealing a suspect, and antagonizing others; names Kriste during the mission scramble and accuses Katie at the Round Table.
- **Mark, Niyyah, Morgan, Abby L.**: Among those who volunteer/stay behind during mission gates and end up on the murder shortlist.
- **Xavier** (referenced): Previously murdered; his mention of Katie’s name becomes a thread linking to Logan’s murder.
