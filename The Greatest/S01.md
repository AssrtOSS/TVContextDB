## S01E01



### Plot
- Framed by a narrated Rome sightseeing film (Colosseum: "one million bricks," Titus in the center box, gladiators fighting for life not prize), intercut with the Olympic gold-medal final, Rome, summer 1960.
- Cassius, 18, under Coach Stoner, faces Ziggy Pietrzykowski. Round one easy; a big left hook in the third shakes him; he recovers, uses the shuffle, wins by unanimous decision. The "Who are you?" ritual with his father is the episode's spine: "I'm the greatest… now, make it true."
- Homecoming: airport press, Louisville parade, Bird, Rudy, a grudgingly proud Cash Sr. ("I have to eat my words"). On the private charter, Stoner warns: "everything in your life is about to change."
- Reynolds' party at Chukasaw Park: Louisville's who's who, the push to turn pro, William Faversham Jr.'s counter-offer, a $5,000 signing bonus, the "too much riffraff" argument, and Reynolds' pitch that Cassius needs "the right management" and a manager in the family.
- Parallel thread: Cash Sr. misses the parade, drinks at the Derby Tavern, plays dominoes, gets fetched by Rudy.
- Home: Bird's rebuke ("the proudest day of your life, and where were you?"), the morning talk about waiting to sign, then the confrontation where Cassius orders his father to stop hitting Bird — "You might have won them Olympics, but I'm still the champ in my house."
- The Blue Boar runs a "Gold Medal Burger" in his honor; the dining room refuses to serve him — "that gold medal don't make you white."
- Radio flash: Cassius Clay will turn pro. A closing montage runs the clock forward: knockouts, Patterson, Sonny Liston, the promoter who kills the title fight unless Cassius denounces the Black Muslims, and Malcolm X's verdict — "Every time you stepped into the ring, you're fighting for the people. That's what it means to be great."

### Character actions
- Cassius / Gee: wins the final; refuses to sign until his father is present; refuses to eat at the Blue Boar rather than be served outside; promises Bird the money will change the family's life; tells Cash he will not hit Bird; commits publicly to turning pro; aims at "the youngest heavyweight champion ever"; says he wants to help "my community, my race."
- Bird / Odessa / Mama Bird: takes the collect call from Rome; phones the Derby when Cash won't come home; stays with her husband ("there's no such thing as perfect… it's just a question of what you can put up with"); says Cassius was right to wait; will stop Cash herself if Cassius can't; names the one thing she can't excuse — that Cash takes his anger out on her.
- Cash Sr. / Pops: admits he doubted his son's work ethic; warns against pro at 22 ("if you push him, Cash, you can get him hurt"); distrusts Reynolds ("all this ain't free… that man got plans"); misses the parade, gets drunk, comes home violent; "the champ in my house."
- Rudy: takes the watch gag, teases his brother about needing a dance partner, is sent to pull Cash out of the bar, tells his mother "Mama, he ain't GeeGee no more. You got to call him 'Champ' now."
- Coach Stoner: coaches the gold ("keep sticking him with the jab," "don't worry about the score"); holds Cassius together after the third-round scare; dresses him for the cameras.
- William Reynolds: benefactor and promoter ("I've been backing your son since he was in high school"); presses the pro decision; proposes "the right management" and a family share; offers Cash a city painting job with pension ("he'll be the first Negro in that position. Another Clay making history"); holds the $5,000 bonus.
- William Faversham Jr.: warns Cassius off "Billy" Reynolds, counters with "select friends," and issues the "next Floyd Patterson or the next Jim Boyd" warning.
- Joe Martin: the childhood coach whose grip loosens; the father concedes "Stoner did that."
- Malcolm X: sets the condition — the message can only go to the world once Cassius is heavyweight champion.
- McDonald: the promoter who cancels the title fight unless Cassius publicly denounces the Black Muslims.

- William Reynolds (威廉·雷诺兹) — white promoter/manager, party host, keeper of the signing bonus.
- Locations: Chukasaw Park (契卡索公园), Saks Fifth Avenue (萨克斯第五大道百货商场), The Blue Boar (蓝野猪餐厅) and its "Gold Medal Burger" (金牌汉堡), Derby Tavern (德比酒馆, founded 1928, white-only door), Smoketown (烟城), plus the airport's white-only and colored waiting rooms.

- The move out of Smoketown is seeded: house first, Cadillac only with a driveway, "it ain't gonna be in this neighborhood — too much riffraff."

## S01E02

Episode 2 opens on WLOU radio in Louisville in late October 1960, roughly two weeks after Rome, and the whole episode turns on one question: who gets to manage Cassius Clay's money and his career. Threads run in parallel — a ten-year management contract with the white-backed Louisville Sponsoring Group, a housing protest against urban renewal that the family gets pulled into, a celebrity romance with Wilma Rudolph, the family's own money anxiety, and finally the six-round pro debut at Freedom Hall. The closing scene introduces Sonny Liston as something darker than a rival.

## Plot

- Radio framing device: WLOU 1350 AM, "River City," October 22. A promo teases Clay's pro debut against Tunney Hunsaker "one week from today," with local color (Portland pumpkin festival, Mrs. Tucker's blue-ribbon pie).
- The contract lands without warning: at Mrs. Colette's tea party, Mrs. Faversham reveals her husband William Faversham Jr. chairs the Louisville Sponsoring Group, which has signed Clay to a management contract. Cash shook hands with "the white boys" the previous day and sent the paperwork out that morning; nobody in the house was told.
- Money and protest collide: outside the Food Fair store downtown, a peaceful demonstration against urban renewal ("Urban renewal means Negro removal!") enters its fifth day. Black attorney Alberta Jones is fighting the City Planning Commission with injunctions; the Chas are inside the white tea-party network that could fund or obstruct the movement.
- Legal thread: LSG's contract is "thick as a Bible" and full of land mines. Bird wants a lawyer; Alberta refuses twice on community grounds; the family settles for Johnny Reneau, the father's less rigorous attorney. At the signing, Alberta arrives late, and renegotiates clause 2C, the earnings split, royalties, and page ten.
- Wilma Rudolph, four Olympic golds and "the fastest woman in the world," comes to promote the fight (proceeds to Kosair Hospital). The radio presses Cassius about a kiss; he deflects ("a gentleman doesn't kiss and tell"). They race in the street; Ali loses to a fluffed shoelace, gives her his Olympic medal, and receives her relay gold.
- Family pressure: Cash buys a drop-top Cadillac for Bird; she refuses to drive it to Miss Colette's because the family arrived by scrubbing floors, sews Cassius a robe in his favorite color, and admits she is scared, not angry, about a contract that governs the next ten years of his life.
- The fight: Freedom Hall, Louisville, 6,000 spectators, six rounds. Clay's left eye closes; Hunsaker goes to roughhouse tactics and lands his best punch (left hook to the body) in the sixth; Clay fights out of the clinch, wins by unanimous decision, and announces he is coming for Floyd Patterson and intends to be the youngest heavyweight champion.
- Final scene: Sonny Liston introduced through violence — repeated shouts of "Sonny!" over a man beaten down, ending with "Enough! He's done."

## Character actions

- Cassius / Gee: signs despite not understanding the contract; declares himself "today's champion" against trainer Joe Martin's "tomorrow's champion" caution; donates the gate to Kosair; leaves his Olympic medal at home so it cannot "conk some poor kid on the head"; gifts Bird the Cadillac; predicts a career of "whup Hunsaker… until I whup Floyd Patterson."
- Odessa / Bird / Mama Bird: learns of the deal from a stranger before her family; confronts Cash ("This family ain't in a rush. Family been waiting. We been owed since the beginning"); defends Cassius to Alberta ("you were wrong"); refuses the Cadillac ("you can't ask me to like it"); sews the robe; attends the fight calling out "Babe."
- Cassius Sr. / Cash / Pops: signed first, without consultation, because LSG offered more; has his own business tie to the Favershams (his wife's continued employment depends on the deal); recommends Reneau ("this seems like a real generous deal"); knows the stakes ("Ten years could be his whole career"); drives with Rudy's help after a DUI suspension.
- Rudy: mocks the contract's thickness, jokes about the Cadillac impressing Wilma, organizes neighborhood spectators for the race, and defends his brother publicly ("Ain't nobody getting in my brother's way").
- Alberta Jones: community lawyer; refuses the paying client twice, warns about the city's razing schedule and Black Wall Street, is moved by Cassius's community ties, then takes the contract over and renegotiates it anyway; asks only that he "make a difference" once he gets where he is going.
- Joe Martin: Cassius's coach since childhood, blamed for slow-rolling the career — effectively finished ("Joe Martin's about to be toast").
- The Miami trainer (Fifth Street Gym, per series context Angelo Dundee): gets Jimmy onto the Franco–Dixon undercard, tracks Frank Azzunio (12-0 southpaw out of Queens, KO'd by Clay in the amateurs), explains Martin's hold-back, and calls the lawyer ("Call that lawyer gal"). Corner maxims delivered here and at the fight: "get in the pocket" and "Ref can't make him respect you — you got to do that all yourself."
- Wilma Rudolph: promotes the fight and the hospital, shares the childhood polio story and leg-brace scar, gives Ali a medal, wins the race.
- Johnny Reneau: father's attorney, defends the contract's trust and guardianship clauses, recedes once Alberta arrives.
- Mrs. Faversham (Sheila), Mrs. Colette, Tristan (neighborhood kid accused of helping Wilma cheat), Frank Azzunio, Jimmy, "Angie" (Dundee's wife / voice on the phone).

- Family register: Gee / GeeGee (Cassius), Bird / Mama Bird (Odessa), Pops (Cash), Junior (Cassius), Peaches, Babe, Rudy, and a third brother Johnny.

## Continuity facts

- Timeline: radio broadcast October 22; fight one week later at Freedom Hall, Louisville, 6,000 in attendance, six rounds, unanimous decision. Pre-conversion era: still "Cassius Clay," no "I am the greatest" declaration, no Nation of Islam on screen, no Liston fight, Patterson is the named target (then champion).

- Trainers: Martin's role ends with the LSG signing; Dundee's Miami/Fifth Street Gym presence is established in the same episode. Note the corner voice at the Louisville fight is never named — if it is Dundee, he is already the cornerman immediately after the LSG deal, which needs reconciling with Martin's status and the Miami setup.

- Protest arc: day1 at the radio, day 5 by the end of the episode; the City Planning Commission is razing businesses; Alberta has filed an injunction; her fear is Louisville becoming the next Black Wall Street.
- Fight detail: Ali's left eye nearly shut, Hunsaker's best punch a body-shot left hook in round six, Ali's answer a stinging right out of the clinch; post-fight "Tell Patterson I'm coming."

## S01E05 Summary

### Episode Plot
The episode spans 1955–1963, centering on Cassius Clay's deepening involvement with the Nation of Islam and the fallout threatening his boxing career. It opens with a 1955 flashback (Emmett Till's murder) that planted the seed of Cassius's anger at injustice. In 1962 Detroit, Malcolm X delivers a fiery speech on revolution and self-defense. By 1963, Cassius and Rudy return to Louisville for their mother Odessa's Pastor's Aid Committee presidency ceremony. At the church, a Malcolm X record is discovered in the house; Cash Sr. warns against "false prophets," and the pastor prays for the boys' protection from such influence. Cassius confronts his father on Christianity vs. Black liberation, then travels with Rudy to meet Elijah Muhammad ("the Prophet"), where they take shahada and receive new names: **Cassius X** and **Rahaman**. Back in Miami, manager Chris Dundee reveals reporter Marvin Geller has a photo of the brothers at a Nation temple — enough to jeopardize the upcoming Henry Cooper fight in London and the title shot against Sonny Liston. Chris brokers a deal: Geller sits on the photo in exchange for an exclusive post-Cooper interview.

### Important Character Actions
- **Cassius Clay**: declares he wants to be a Nation spokesman "like you" to Malcolm X; insists "temptation don't mean nothing to me"; takes shahada, becomes Cassius X; tells Dundee "ain't nothing getting in my way" of the title.
- **Rudy / Rahaman**: reveals *he* recruited Cassius into the Nation, not vice versa; stands up to Cash Sr. ("Things are changing, Pop").
- **Malcolm X**: tests Cassius's motives; reveals the Prophet sent him to turn Cassius down; later presents the brothers to Elijah Muhammad.
- **Cash Sr.**: denounces the Nation as "ravening wolves"; later admits life is unfair but resists change ("Ain't nothing changing… All they're gonna see is a monkey in a suit").
- **Odessa "Bird" Clay**: honored as Pastor's Aid president; privately worries "I never thought I'd have to worry about you leaving Jesus."
- **Angelo Dundee**: secures the Cooper fight in London; warns the commission sold Cassius as an "All-American Boy Scout" — the photo blows that cover.
- **Chris Dundee**: cuts deal with Marvin Geller to suppress the photo.

## S01E03

# The Greatest — S01E03 Summary

## Episode Plot
The episode interweaves **Sonny Liston's origin and redemption** with **Cassius Clay's search for the right trainer**, culminating in both men arriving at pivotal crossroads.

**Liston thread (1952→1961):**  
- 1952 St. Louis: Mob associate Frank recruits teen Liston from reformatory, offering a boxing career in exchange for off-book collections work. Liston lies about his age (claims 20).  
- Montage establishes Liston's destructive rise: cannonball fists, string of KOs, media polarized between "disgrace" and "the future."  
- Liston meets Geraldine (sharecropper's daughter, typist) at a diner; she reads him a *Sports Illustrated* piece — first time he hears anything positive about himself.  
- Kefauver Commission arrests Carbo, Palermo; Vitale imminent. Commissioner pressures Liston to testify; he refuses ("can't say nothing about people I don't know").  
- Pennsylvania Athletic Commission hearing: Liston testifies raw — mule-plowed from age 7–13, no education, juvie, prison. "Boxing made me a winner. Winning made me feel human." License reinstated.  
- Return fight vs. Howard "Honeyboy" King: quick KO. Newspaper still vilifies him ("shredded warrants as confetti").

**Cassius thread (1960→1961):**  
- Winter 1960: Cassius arrives at Archie Moore's "Salt Mine" (Ramona, CA). Archie's regimen: 4-mi hill runs (no water till top), wood-chopping, "sharpen spirits" philosophy — humility as path to greatness.

# S01E08 — The Greatest (Miami Beach, February 25, 1964)

## Plot
Cold open on fight night: the "champ is here" walk-around, denim jackets, the bear-hunting-season motif, and a failed publicity beat (Liston skipped the staged entrance; "You wasted my time"). The episode then intercuts three strands around the 10:00 p.m. title fight at the Miami Beach Convention Hall.

1. **Weigh-in (earlier that day at the Convention Center).** Clay insists on the "champ is here" chant, is fined $2,500 for "conduct unbecoming" ("fine him for that ugly face… donate the money to the Bureau of Wildlife"), taunts Liston ("you big ugly bear… scared to death!"), and predicts a round-eight KO. Liston, whose blood pressure is 120-and-rising, is nearly barred by the doctor until he gets one hour to calm down. Liston cuts the prediction to two rounds and threatens "I predict somebody's gonna die tonight!" The corners prepare two water buckets because of what happened to Cleveland Williams' eyes.
2. **Backstage pressure.** Liston dismisses his own corner ("Don't matter if it's true as long as I'm putting money in your pocket"). Clay admits fear: "What if I let them all down?" Manager Chris is exposed as having placed an illegal wager on the fight; a Nation of Islam figure warns that the Nation already knows and will hold him personally responsible if Cassius falls. Chris insists Cassius will win and demands a share. Bird visits; Malcolm X blesses Cassius and warns him to keep God's voice louder than any other.
3. **The fight.** Clay dances, outboxes Liston, and rips him under the left eye. After round four Clay cannot see — "They're cheating me, Angie" — and his corner flushes his eye; he nearly goes blind in round five ("This is destiny. How you gonna win against God?"), then rallies. Liston stays on his stool at the start of round seven; "Clay has won." The arena erupts. Clay claims the heavyweight title, "I am the greatest! I shook up the world!", is introduced as "Cassius Marcellus Clay Jr.", reconciles with his parents, receives a house from the Dundees (funded by the bet), shops for ice cream and rehearses his next trash-talk, and finally tells reporter Marvin Geller his new name: "My name is Muhammad Ali."

## Important character actions

- **Malcolm X:** tells Clay he is "holding the world's biggest megaphone," reminds him that promises made will be tested ("easy to say, but maybe harder to live with"), warns that "they find ways to come for you" and that "even trusted friends" will try to lead him astray; counsels him to listen to God first.
