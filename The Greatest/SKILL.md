# The Greatest (2026) — Subtitle Translation Background

## Series Identity
- **English title**: The Greatest
- **Chinese title (official Prime Video)**: 《传奇永驻》
- **Format**: Limited biographical sports drama series
- **First air**: November 4, 2026 (all 8 episodes)
- **Broadcaster**: Amazon Prime Video (global, 240+ territories)
- **Production**: Amazon MGM Studios, Outlier Society, Blue Monday, Authentic Studios, Roc Nation, PolyGram Entertainment, Grace: A Storytelling Company
- **Creator / Showrunner**: Ben Watkins
- **Executive producers**: Ben Watkins, Josh Wakely, Michael B. Jordan, Elizabeth Raposo, Lonnie Ali, Michele Anthony, Boyd Muir, Corey Salter, Marc Rosen, Matthew Gross, David Blackman

## Premise
Authorized biographical drama chronicling Muhammad Ali’s life — from young Cassius Clay in Louisville through Olympic gold, conversion to Islam, refusal of the Vietnam draft, exile from boxing, the Rumble in the Jungle, the Thrilla in Manila, and his later years with Parkinson’s. The series emphasizes private, off-stage moments: husband, father, brother, son — not only the public icon.

## World Setting
- **Primary era**: 1950s–1980s United States (Louisville, Miami, Chicago, New York, Kinshasa/Zaire, Manila, Las Vegas)
- **Key locales**: Clay family home (Louisville), Columbia Gym (where Clay’s bike was stolen), Miami’s Fifth Street Gym, Nation of Islam mosques, Zaire 1974, Philippines 1975, training camps (Deer Lake, PA), hospital rooms in later life
- **Cultural backdrop**: Jim Crow South, Civil Rights Movement, Black Power, anti-war movement, Nation of Islam under Elijah Muhammad and Malcolm X, Cold War sports diplomacy, media transformation of athlete into global celebrity

## Relationships
- **Ali ↔ Father**: Tension — Cash’s drinking, high expectations, pride in son’s Olympic medal
- **Ali ↔ Mother**: Gentle anchor; “Bird” nickname; religious (Baptist) contrast to Ali’s Islam
- **Ali ↔ Brother (Rudy/Rahaman)**: Protective older brother; Rahaman follows into boxing, lives in shadow
- **Ali ↔ Malcolm X**: Spiritual father → betrayal after Malcolm’s split from Nation of Islam; profound regret
- **Ali ↔ Angelo Dundee**: Trusting, technical partnership; Dundee manages Ali’s style, not his politics
- **Ali ↔ Bundini Brown**: Hype man, cornerman, cultural translator; “float like a butterfly” origin
- **Ali ↔ Sonny Liston**: Antagonist → reluctant respect; two fights (1964, 1965) define early reign
- **Ali ↔ Dr. Pacheco**: Medical conscience; eventually urges retirement as skills erode

## Speaking Styles
- **Muhammad Ali**: Rhythmic, poetic, rhyming boasts (“I’m so mean I make medicine sick”); code-switches between Louisville vernacular, Nation of Islam cadence, media-savvy soundbites; later years — slower, softer, trembling voice
- **Cassius Clay Sr.**: Gruff, proud, colloquial Kentucky; sign-painter metaphors
- **Odessa Clay**: Gentle, Baptist cadence, maternal worry
- **Malcolm X**: Oratorical, precise, intellectually dense; shifts to warmer register with Ali privately
- **Angelo Dundee**: Practical, gravelly, boxing vernacular (“keep your hands up,” “move your head”); Yiddish-inflected humor
- **Bundini Brown**: Street-poetic, hype-man energy, rhyming cornerman talk
- **Sonny Liston**: Minimal, menacing, monosyllabic; few words carry weight
- **Dr. Pacheco**: Clinical, measured, reluctantly candid about damage
- **Howard Cosell (if appears)**: Arrogant, polysyllabic, “telling it like it is” broadcaster persona

## Cultural Context for Translation
1. **Nation of Islam terminology** — Honorifics (The Honorable, Minister, Brother, Sister) must be preserved; “Black Muslim” is period usage but now considered dated/pejorative — use “Nation of Islam member” or “Muslim” per speaker stance
2. **Racial slurs / period language** — “Negro,” “colored,” “white devil” appear in historical dialogue; translate faithfully with register markers, not softened
3. **Vietnam War discourse** — “Draft dodger,” “unpatriotic,” “conscientious objector” carry loaded political weight; preserve ambiguity
4. **Boxing argot** — “Orthodox/southpaw,” “jab/cross/hook/uppercut,” “canvas,” “neutral corner,” “saved by the bell,” “cut man,” “swell” — use standard Chinese boxing terms
5. **Media landscape** — Closed-circuit theater broadcasts, Howard Cosell’s ABC commentary, Sports Illustrated covers — reflect era-specific distribution
6. **Islamic phrases** — “As-salaam-alaikum,” “Alhamdulillah,” “Inshallah” — keep Arabic transliteration + Chinese gloss where context demands
7. **Ali’s poetry** — Rhyme and meter are character-defining; prioritize rhythmic equivalence over literal meaning in taunts (“Your hands can’t hit what your eyes can’t see” → 你的拳打不到眼看不见的地方)
