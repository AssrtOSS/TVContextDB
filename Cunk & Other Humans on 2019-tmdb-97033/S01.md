## S01E01

Cunk & Other Humans — S01E01 (2019)

## Episode plot
Philomena Cunk stands in the English countryside and frames 2019 as a year of man-made global disaster: climate change. She ridicules David Attenborough's Climate Change - The Facts — "you can't change the facts," a "snuff movie starring everyone on the planet," ants included — then turns to Greta Thunberg, whom she dubs "Greta Thunderberg," arriving at the Davos summit with her "our house is on fire" warning. Cunk feigns confusion about the science ("half the scientists say one thing, the other half completely agrees, so it's all just opinion"), mocks school climate strikers ("a few years ago they wanted a fidget spinner; make up your fucking minds"), and takes aim at Extinction Rebellion for blocking the tube even though the trains are electric. The segment ends with her declaring herself "fully vegan now, apart from the odd bit of cheese and chorizo. And Bovril." The supplied aligned file cuts off at that line, so this summary covers the opening climate segment only.

## Key character actions
- Cunk delivers the piece to camera from the English countryside, adopting a solemn tone and immediately undercutting it.
- She tries to find a way to discredit Thunberg and lands on the fact that Thunberg's mother once took part in Eurovision, making Thunberg "part of the Eurovision elite."
- Seeing child marchers fills her with despair but also makes her "quite broody"; she can't wait to have "a frightened little baby."

The episode centers on two catastrophic British TV moments: Jeffrey Epstein's prison death and Prince Andrew's disastrous *Newsnight* interview with Emily Maitlis, then pivots to HBO's *Chernobyl* — which Cunk reimagines as a bakery disaster.

## S01E03

## Episode plot
Philomena Cunk dissects 2019 as the year everyone caught "polarisation" — a brain disease of constant anger spread by the internet. She argues politicians have stopped pretending to be nice, that the useful trick is to be just dodgy enough to divide people (so they argue with each other instead of you), and that hate has become exhausting. She moves from Britain's Brexit bitterness to America under Donald Trump (impeachment, kids in cages, the wall), then widens out to a planet split everywhere except the poles. She closes by admitting the hatred is fun but tiring, and says she needs a weekend off before ramping back up in 2020.

## S01E05

# Cunk & Other Humans S01E05 Summary

## Episode Plot
The episode chronicles the chaotic state of British politics throughout 2019, centering on the Brexit deadlock. It opens with Theresa May's failed premiership — her inability to pass a withdrawal agreement despite three attempts, her awkward public demeanor, and eventual resignation with a wobbly-voiced farewell speech. The narrative then covers the Conservative leadership contest featuring Michael Gove, Sajid Javid ("the first baby to be Home Secretary"), and the inevitable frontrunner Boris Johnson, depicted as an "overstuffed scarecrow" making "posh buffering noises." Johnson becomes PM, prorogues Parliament by "tricking the Queen," fails to deliver Brexit, then calls a general election after insisting nobody wanted one. Interwoven are Jeremy Corbyn's strategic silence on Brexit (and antisemitism), the brief flash of Change UK, and Nigel Farage's Brexit Party sweeping the European elections.

## Important Character Actions
- **Theresa May**: Repeatedly presents withdrawal agreement; rejected by MPs three times; resigns with emotional farewell.
- **Jeremy Corbyn**: Refuses to take a clear Brexit position to appease both sides; hampered by "centrists" in Labour who defect to form Change UK.
- **Nigel Farage**: Launches Brexit Party; wins European elections with a "no-nonsense name."
- **Boris Johnson**: Wins Tory leadership; prorogues Parliament via the Queen; fails to pass Brexit; U-turns on election pledge.
- **Dominic Cummings** (as "Dominic Cumberbatch"): Portrayed as a genius strategist; film depiction (*Brexit: The Uncivil War*) makes people assume his real-life actions are all "clever strategy."
- **Jacob Rees-Mogg**: Framed as an "honest man of the soil" defending Brexit against the elite.

## Newly Established Names / Terms
- **Change UK** (mocked as "Change 'uck") — the short-lived centrist breakaway group that "altered the face of British politics for an entire afternoon."
- **Brexit Party** — Farage's single-issue vehicle, compared to Anusol cream ("does what it says on the tin").
- **Article 50** — used as a verb: "Article 50 the shit out of herself" (resign).
- **Dominic Cumberbatch** — deliberate misnaming of Dominic Cummings, joking he was played by "his brother, Sherlock" in the HBO film.
- **"Posh buffering noises"** — Cunk's term for Johnson's hesitant verbal tics.

## Continuity-Relevant Facts
- 2019 is framed as a year of total political gridlock ("total shitlock" by April).
- The 52% Leave referendum result is satirically inflated to "100% Leave" via the logic that winners decide what losers *really* wanted.
- The European elections become a proxy referendum; Tories perform poorly, forcing May out.
- Johnson's prorogation ("turning Parliament off and on again") is presented as a constitutional trick.
- The episode ends on Johnson demanding an election he previously claimed nobody wanted — setting up the 2019 general election.

## Jokes / References for Later Episodes

## S01E06

# Cunk & Other Humans on 2019 — S01E06 Summary

## Episode plot
Philomena Cunk reports from Westminster on the UK's first December general election since 1923, billed as "the election of a lifetime" — which she notes they already said a couple of years earlier, so it must refer to a mealworm's lifetime, since mealworms can't vote or hold a pencil. The episode covers the campaign between Jeremy Corbyn (Labour) and Boris Johnson (Conservatives), the Tory landslide victory, Corbyn's resignation, and Johnson's victory speech outside his "palace."

## Important character actions
- Boris Johnson ducked Andrew Neil's interview after seeing Neil dismantle Corbyn; dodged Jeremy Vine on Channel 4; hid in a big fridge/freezer when Good Morning Britain sent Piers Morgan after him; appeared on This Morning, described as a notoriously hostile environment for Amanda Holden; refused to look at an ITV reporter's photo of a boy sleeping on a hospital floor, then "did the decent thing and nicked the reporter's phone."
- Jeremy Corbyn faced Andrew Neil without apologizing once (or twice, or at all) over the Chief Rabbi's accusation of a serious antisemitism problem in Labour; announced he will step down next year and not lead Labour in any future general election.
- The Conservative Party temporarily renamed its Twitter account "factcheckUK" during the leaders' debate to pose as an independent fact-checking service; stuck to the slogan "Get Brexit done."
- Boris Johnson won a landslide majority, took Tory seats in places the party had never heard of or stood in, and stood outside 10 Downing Street urging Leavers and Remainers, "letterboxes or bumboys," to find closure and let healing begin.
