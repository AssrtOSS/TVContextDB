## S02E01

Summary of S02E01 — One Hundred Years of Solitude (based on the aligned EN/ZH subtitles and the series context skill):

## Episode Plot

The episode opens on the frame story: Aureliano Babilonia finally deciphers Melquíades' parchments and reads the epigraph — "The first of the line is tied to a tree, and the last is being eaten by ants."

The main action: Colonel Aureliano Buendía, alone inside the chalk circle only he may enter, rules by terse commands. He executes the captured Conservative general José Raquel Moncada by firing squad despite a protest led by Úrsula ("Release Moncada!"), enforcing a town-wide curfew. The execution is witnessed by the twins José Arcadio Segundo and Aureliano Segundo. Moncada's death drives José Arcadio Segundo to the church, where he becomes Father Antonio Isabel's bell-ringer and cock-handler, while Aureliano Segundo befriends Melquíades' ghost and studies in the house. The colonel is nearly assassinated with strychnine but is saved by Úrsula's folk remedy; he appoints Gerineldo Márquez civil and military leader of Macondo and rides back to war. Aureliano José, hardened by the barracks, tries to force himself on Amaranta, is rejected, and dies in a theater brawl — losing the happiness (Carmelita Montiel, seven children) he was destined for. Months later the colonel returns, learns of his 17 unrecognized sons, and signs the armistice on the government's terms after realizing 20 years of war bought nothing but power. José Arcadio Segundo meanwhile stumbles on great-grandfather José Arcadio Buendía's alchemical projects — a discovery that "will change the fate of Macondo."

## Important Character Actions

- Colonel Aureliano Buendía: executes Moncada; refuses the president's armistice letter ("go straight to hell"); is poisoned; quells his own officers' rebellion with extreme cruelty; signs the armistice anyway, demanding pensions and legal recognition for his army in exchange for total surrender.
- Úrsula: leads the women's protest; saves the colonel with eggs, vinegar and dill; keeps the list of his 17 sons; swears on her parents' bones and José Arcadio Buendía's memory that she will kill Aureliano with her own hands if Gerineldo dies — "the same way I would have done if you'd come into the world with a pig's tail."
- Gerineldo Márquez: runs Macondo, holds twice-weekly telegraph conversations with the colonel; denounces the armistice as treason, is sentenced to the Council of War, then released ("The farce is over, my friend"); vows he'd rather die by firing squad than see Aureliano become a traitor.
- Aureliano José: declares he wants to marry his aunt Amaranta (even told a Liberal colonel, who said that's "the whole reason we're at war with the Church"); is banished, drinks, brawls, and is shot at the theater.
- Amaranta: refuses Aureliano José, reminds him she raised him like a son, threatens suicide if he returns.
- José Arcadio Segundo: traumatized by Moncada's execution, becomes a sacristan, masters "theological tricks" and cockfighting tricks alike.
- Aureliano Segundo: meets Melquíades every afternoon; when mistaken for his brother he does not correct the error ("that day").
- Pilar Ternera: shelters Aureliano José; they become "accomplices in solitude"; buries him ("Rest in peace, my son").
- Remedios the Beauty: wanders naked through the house at 20, "because her nature would not accept any form of convention."

- Father Antonio Isabel (安东尼·伊萨贝尔神父); Colonel Márquez (马尔克斯上校, i.e., Gerineldo).
- Petra Cotes (佩特拉·科特斯) — new arrival, raffles rabbits at the cockfights, "God's political party."
- Carmelita Montiel (卡梅丽达·蒙铁尔) — 20-year-old virgin, bathed in orange blossom water.
- Asunción Centeno (阿松修·森特诺) — brings one of the colonel's sons.
- Catarino's bar (卡塔里诺), fighting cocks Torbellino (旋风) and Pinto (品托); Marconi telegraph; the press ("subversive group leader in the Caribbean"); tombstone "HUMAN VANITIES END HERE" (人类的虚荣心到此为止).
- Three armistice conditions: renounce property-title review, renounce the fight against clerical influence, renounce equal rights for illegitimate children.

## Continuity-Relevant Facts

- All 17 sons are baptized Aureliano with their mother's surname, since law forbids the father's name until recognition — the list Úrsula keeps; these 17 will all be assassinated later.
- The strychnine poisoning and the pension promise ("You have my word") set up the veterans' pension theme.
- Melquíades' manuscripts "are not to be understood until a hundred years had passed" — the 100-year frame.
- Moncada's warning — the colonel has become exactly like the military he despises — and Úrsula's pig's-tail oath echo the family curse.
- Aureliano José's fate is told as destiny denied ("seven children... die in her arms of old age").
- José Arcadio Segundo's discovery of the founder's projects points toward Melquíades' room and the parchments.
- The twins' interchangeable identity (not even their mother can tell them apart) is a running device.

## Unresolved Threads

## S02E06

## Episode Plot

The episode weaves together two storylines: the rise of the banana workers' strike and the Buendía household's private tragedies.

Banana workers rally for Sundays off, real wages instead of commissary vouchers, housing, medicine, and eight-hour days, drafting a petition of demands. Foreman José Arcadio Segundo delivers it to the absent Mr. Brown; company lawyers declare the workers "nonexistent" — day laborers without contracts are not employees. With help from Françoise, a French woman, José Arcadio Segundo tricks Mr. Brown into signing, but the company refuses to negotiate. He resigns as foreman, founds a union (headquartered in his old shipping office), and strikers are jailed en masse. Union leaders flee Macondo, later reappearing to organize protests across the banana zone.

Meanwhile, Fernanda confines Meme for "kissing in a theater" and suspects mechanic Mauricio Babilonia; Meme denies it and begs her father to let her live with him and Petra. Police shoot Mauricio as a "chicken thief" scaling a fence — the bullet lodges in his spine, paralyzing him for life; he dies in solitude, tormented by yellow butterflies. Fernanda then ships Meme to the Saint Teresa of La Colina convent (cloistered nuns); Meme never speaks again. Aureliano Segundo, who never believed the chicken story, lets forged-looking documents ease his conscience and returns to Petra.

Amaranta announces she will die tomorrow: Death told her in advance to weave her own shroud, promising a painless, fearless death. She offers to carry mail to the dead — a box of letters fills the living room, including one "for the colonel." She dies a virgin, "as she came," having wished to rescue Rebeca out of a boundless understanding of loneliness. Fernanda gives birth to a girl — Amaranta Úrsula — and writes to José Arcadio III in Rome about his priesthood.

The government appoints General Carlos Cortés Vargas as civil and military commander; martial law is declared, three regiments arrive and harvest bananas themselves, and a U.S. warship is requested. The strike turns to sabotage (plantations and commissaries burned, rails torn up). Government representatives promised for Friday never arrive. Over 3,000 people crowd the station; a lieutenant reads Decree No. 4 through a gramophone horn declaring the strike illegal, gives five minutes, and fires. The crowd mistakes the machine guns for fireworks and doesn't react. Bodies are loaded onto the longest train José Arcadio Segundo has ever seen — unlit, gliding silently to the sea "like rejected bananas," the same sea where his great-grandfather's dreams had met their ruin. He survives, crouched in the dirt; the child witness will tell the story for years, believed by no one.

## Important character actions

- José Arcadio Segundo: resigns the foremanship, leads the union, defies the company ("You can keep your last minute and go fuck off!"), survives the massacre as the sole witness.
- Fernanda: confines Meme, engineers the disgrace-erasing plan, sends Meme away, lies to her husband, bears Amaranta Úrsula.
- Meme: broken and mute — never speaks again, whether by will or shock.
- Mauricio Babilonia: shot, paralyzed, dies of old age in solitude, cast out as a chicken thief.
- Amaranta: weaves her shroud, dies a virgin, delivers letters to the dead.
- Aureliano Segundo: withdraws into Petra Cotes' company and parties.
- General Cortés Vargas and Major Enrique García Isaza: decree the strike illegal, order the firing.

## New names/terms

- Mr. Brown — banana company manager (absent, "with the French girls")
- United Fruit Company; commissary vouchers; petition of demands
- Françoise — French woman who tricks Brown into signing
- General Carlos Cortés Vargas, Major Enrique García Isaza; Decree No. 4; martial law
- Saint Teresa of La Colina convent (cloistered nuns)
- Ciénaga; Santa Marta Province; the banana zone
- Union chant "Only eight hours!" (八八八) and "The people united will never be defeated" (人民团结永不败)
- Certificate of Sacred Music (clavichord) — Meme's
- Union members named on the rolls: Octavio González, Gregorio Fontal, José Martínez, Antonio Avendaño, Joaquín Fernández

## Continuity-relevant facts

- Amaranta Úrsula is born (baby girl; Úrsula-style spirit: "won't put up with anyone's shit").
- Amaranta is dead; her shroud and the mail-for-the-dead box (the colonel is already dead — letters for him are accepted).
- Meme is gone from Macondo for good; Aureliano Segundo swallows Fernanda's story; the family rift deepens.
- José Arcadio Segundo survives the massacre — he will be the witness when the massacre is denied.
- Circular-time motif made explicit: "just like the days of the colonel's revolution… time keeps going around in circles"; José Arcadio Segundo is told he's "as naive and pathetic as your uncle, the colonel."
- The massacre was foreshadowed for José Arcadio Segundo since the morning he witnessed Moncada's execution.
- Úrsula is still alive, asking when José Arcadio III will take his vows.

## Jokes or references

- Bar tall-tale scene: the war story inflates from 10 men to 25 — "there's a big hole in that story, pal."
- "Maybe the spirit will get a contract" — riff on the Holy Spirit harvesting bananas.
- "Eight hours to rest, and eight to study" — the 八八八 chant.
- Françoise: "Love is not to be interrupted"; "French women can get creative" (Brown's signing trick).
- Machine guns "loaded with pyrotechnic trickery" — the crowd's fatal non-reaction: "not a voice, not even a sigh."
- Fernanda's superstitions: moths bring bad luck; scorpions come out at dusk.

## Unresolved threads

- The massacre's aftermath: bodies "like rejected bananas," official denial to come; José Arcadio Segundo's testimony vs. the town's future forgetfulness.
- Meme's fate in the convent — per the family tree, she will bear Mauricio's son Aureliano Babilonia, to be delivered to the house.
- Fernanda's lies and her erasure of every "vestige of disgrace."
- The strike: the promised Friday representatives never arrived.
- José Arcadio Segundo's whereabouts after the massacre.
- Amaranta's undelivered letters to the dead (including the colonel's).

## S02E07

# S02E07 Summary

## Episode Plot
The episode covers the aftermath of the Banana Massacre and its erasure from history. The government declares "there were no deaths" and enforces this lie through propaganda. José Arcadio Segundo survives the massacre at the train station (3,000+ men, women, and children machine-gunned, bodies dumped in the sea), escapes, and returns to Macondo. He finds the Buendía house under military search but remains unseen—a magical realism manifestation of the regime's willful blindness. Fernanda del Carpio seizes control: she fabricates Meme's death from yellow fever, writes letters to her son José Arcadio (III) in Rome, and erases all traces of Meme. Meme's son, Aureliano Babilonia, is deposited at the house by a nun; Fernanda rejects him as a "bastard" (野种), but Úrsula instinctively recognizes the Aureliano lineage. Five years of ceaseless rain follow. José Arcadio Segundo hides in Melquíades' room, deciphering the prophecies and finding peace in invisibility. He passes the massacre truth to Aureliano (II). The rain finally stops after ~10 years. Aureliano Segundo exhausts himself selling lottery tickets to fund Amaranta Úrsula's education in Brussels. She departs. Aureliano Segundo and Petra Cotes die simultaneously on August 9. Santa Sofía de la Piedad vanishes. Aureliano Babilonia remains in the decaying house, continuing the decipherment.

## Important Character Actions
- **José Arcadio Segundo**: Survives massacre; walks back to Macondo along train tracks; escapes military capture; hides in Melquíades' room for years; deciphers prophecies; transmits massacre truth to Aureliano (II).
- **Fernanda del Carpio**: Orchestrates cover-up of Meme's existence; writes false death letter to son in Rome; brings Aureliano Babilonia to house but rejects him; enforces rigid order.
- **Aureliano Segundo**: Works himself to death selling lottery tickets to send Amaranta Úrsula to Brussels; dies simultaneously with Petra Cotes.
- **Úrsula Iguarán**: Recognizes Aureliano Babilonia by cheekbones and "solitary air of the Aurelianos"; affirms his lineage.
- **Aureliano Babilonia (child)**: Arrives at house; locked in Melquíades' room; begins deciphering prophecies (identified 30 repeating characters).
- **Petra Cotes**: Stays with Aureliano Segundo to the end; dies same moment as him.
- **Amaranta Úrsula**: Sent to Brussels for schooling; promises to write letters.
- **Santa Sofía de la Piedad**: Disappears without trace after decades of raising generations.

- **José Arcadio Segundo's invisibility**: Soldiers look directly at him but do not see him; magical realism = political erasure made literal.

## S02E04

### S02E04

**Plot**
- The train finally arrives in Macondo, with José Arcadio Segundo and Aureliano Triste waving from the locomotive — fulfilling the founder's dream of connecting the town to the world ("Your great-grandfather would be so proud"). Meme drags her brother José Arcadio III into the last car; Petra Cotes finds them and brings them home.
- Gringo Mr. Herbert is invited to lunch at the Buendía house and is astonished by bananas ("papoche," which locals feed to animals), declaring Macondo a paradise; merchants from Ohio are coming to buy every vacant lot.
- Mr. Jack Brown arrives from Prattville, Alabama with engineers, topographers and surveyors, becomes the town's "mayor," and the foreigners move the river, alter rainfall, and found the United Fruit Company; José Arcadio Segundo is hired as plantation foreman. Wages are paid in commissary vouchers; two of Segundo's men die in "an accident" (men fought over Remedios the Beauty at the plantation, machetes drawn), and Brown quietly rewards Segundo with extra coupons.
- The Colonel, disgusted by the gringos, visits Rebeca's house: she is alive, a recluse who burned the land titles (Arcadio had given them to José Arcadio) when her husband died, keeping only the house and a shotgun.
- Fernanda's household war escalates: she sends Aureliano Segundo's trunk to his mistress Petra Cotes' house in broad daylight, locks up the children, and after a prank desecrating her dead father's memory ("a Christian man, not a dog") takes the children away — Meme to be educated by nuns, José Arcadio III to Rome for the priesthood.

- Remedios the Beauty — attacked by townspeople who blame her provocativeness — ascends to heaven wrapped in sheets at four in the afternoon; only Úrsula understands and witnesses it.

## S02E02

## Episode S02E02

### Plot
- The episode opens at the end of a life: the 17 Aurelianos have all been hunted down and killed within a single week, shot through the ash crosses on their foreheads (Centeno and Triste named among the dead; no witnesses anywhere in Macondo). The old Colonel Aureliano Buendía, numb and adrift, sinks into a nostalgia trap on October 11th at 4:10 p.m., reliving the afternoon his father showed him ice; he walks to the chestnut tree, tries to hold onto the memory of the circus while he urinates, loses it, and dies there. An epitaph is raised: "Relentless defender of freedom and justice… his fight for a sovereign nation will live on in the heart of his people." Years later, nobody in Macondo remembers the colonel.
- Banana-company era Macondo: Aureliano Segundo is now fat, violet, and tortoise-like. His niece Meme (Renata Remedios) returns from convent school as a clavichord soloist; Fernanda, pregnant and bedridden, makes Meme swear an oath (decency, modesty, fear of God, home before dark) before letting her play at the company club. There Meme meets Patricia Brown (Mr. Brown's daughter) and Mauricio Babilonia, an apprentice mechanic, who teaches her to drive ("like a piano") — a day she never forgets. Fernanda then forbids her from the compound and from leaving the house alone.
- Workers' grievances mount: chemical spraying makes everyone itch; the company health center hands out identical turquoise pills for malaria, gonorrhea, or constipation (children re-queue and use the pills as bingo markers); pay is docked for doctor visits; the "Yankee" toilet is falling apart. Foreman José Arcadio (Segundo) confronts Mr. Brown and is brushed off; when he compensates workers with double commissary vouchers, exiled Mexican colonel Lorenzo Gavilán rejects them — dignity is worth more than Virginia ham — and the workers begin organizing with or without José Arcadio.
- Meme's love affair ignites: a sweaty, ice-cold handshake with Mauricio at the cinema; a first encounter at the workshops that "mercilessly drags her into an animalistic state" (she later understands it as tenderness); a secret note ("Tomorrow at 7 PM at the matron's place") delivered with flowers ostensibly from Patricia Brown. Pilar Ternera recognizes the Buendía love-sickness, dispels Meme's fears, and lends her the old canvas bed where she once conceived Arcadio (Meme's grandfather) and Aureliano José. Aureliano Segundo, torn between Petra Cotes and Fernanda, makes Meme promise to tell him when she falls in love ("it feels like the earth is shaking"). The episode ends with Fernanda, spying from the shadows at a party, watching Meme kiss a man she cannot see — only catching the tremor of his voice.

### Important character actions
- Colonel Aureliano Buendía: refuses Gerineldo's accusation, insists he will keep asking who killed Triste, then dies at the chestnut tree in solitude.
- Gerineldo (Márquez): blames the company and government for the 17 murders, tells the colonel grieving is all that's left, and ends their exchange with "Leave me alone."
- José Arcadio Segundo (foreman): petitions Mr. Brown about the health center, barracks, and latrines; gets dismissed; faces a workers' ultimatum to choose a side.
- Mr. Brown: dismisses every grievance ("we're not a charitable foundation," "enough, foreman"), accuses José Arcadio of communist ideas.
- Lorenzo Gavilán: refuses the ham vouchers, demands José Arcadio prove his loyalty, pushes the organizing forward without him.
- Fernanda: enforces the religious oath, house-arrests Meme, and catches the kiss (voice only).
- Aureliano Segundo: reunites with Meme, takes her to the cinema and party, visits Petra ("I can't abandon my unborn child, or Meme"), and extracts the love promise.
- Pilar Ternera: lends the canvas bed and the "true love is worth the risk" counsel.
- Úrsula: still the household's heart, asking daily "How's Meme?" and joking that she'll be fine "as long as she doesn't come back a Protestant."

### Newly established names/terms
- The 17 Aurelianos' deaths; the ash crosses on their foreheads; Aureliano Triste and Aureliano Centeno named as victims.
- Gerineldo (Márquez), the Colonel's old war comrade.

## One Hundred Years of Solitude — S02E08 Summary

### Episode Plot
The series finale. **Aureliano Babilonia** (last Buendía) has spent 15+ years confined in Melquíades' laboratory by **Fernanda del Carpio**. He escapes to the **Alley of the Future** to find a Sanskrit primer for deciphering the prophecies. **José Arcadio (III)** returns from Rome (having abandoned the seminary), discovers the family treasure under Úrsula's bed, and forms a drunken, destructive bond with Aureliano.
