## S01E01

## 剧情概述

- 斯图尔特在末日化现实中遇到来自另一条现实流的“另一个自己”。对方透露：莱纳德、谢尔顿和霍华德制造的变状态量子干涉装置发生故障，改变了宇宙、历史和现实。
- 另一个斯图尔特在穿越虫洞时消失，只留下模糊指令：前往加州理工，找到装置并修复一切。
- 斯图尔特、伯特和丹妮丝一路穿过崩溃后的帕萨迪纳地区，进入已沦为死亡陷阱的加州理工。
- 他们在那里找到被囚禁的拉杰。拉杰确认现实确实遭到改变，并解释装置的基本原理。
- 统治当地的巴里·克里普克自称“圣盖博谷最高统治者”和“邪恶者”，拒绝恢复原来的世界，还试图强迫丹妮丝成为他的伴侣。
- 伯特利用地质学知识把岩石样本制成简易爆炸物，众人救出丹妮丝并抵达装置。
- 巴里试图阻止重置，拉杰却提供关键参数。斯图尔特依照指示重新配置装置，最终成功恢复正常现实。
- 末尾确认末日、飞蛾和伤势都消失了，但丹妮丝下落不明，斯图尔特决定寻找她。

## 重要角色行动

- **斯图尔特**：从犹豫、缺乏计划的普通人，被迫承担修复现实的任务；在丹妮丝遇险时主动营救她。
- **丹妮丝**：务实果断，多次保护斯图尔特；被巴里俘获后拒绝屈服，并与斯图尔特一起逃脱。
- **伯特**：利用地质学知识制作岩石炸弹，成为突破武装守卫的关键。
- **拉杰**：被囚禁多年，独自研究现实异常；最终凭计算结果指导装置重启。
- **巴里·克里普克**：在崩溃后的世界夺取权力，控制食物、武器和人员；为了保住统治，主动阻挠宇宙恢复。
- **另一个斯图尔特**：短暂现身，提供任务线索后穿过虫洞消失。

## 新建立的名称与术语

- **变状态量子干涉装置**（vari-state quantum interference device）：莱纳德、谢尔顿和霍华德制造的核心装置。
- **现实流、维度裂隙、虫洞**：本集明确建立的多元宇宙机制。
- **沃德·“Word”·克里普克**：本宇宙中巴里的统治者称号；“Ward/Word”的发音玩笑延续了巴里的发音障碍。
- **南帕萨迪纳、弗林特里奇和蒙罗维亚大哈里发**：巴里统治区域的夸张称号。
- **安布鲁（Ambulu）**：拉杰提到的祖母，在濒死幻觉中向他招手。
- **加里、丽莎、凯尔、史蒂夫、艾米丽·曼哈特**：通过对白补充的本宇宙人物或往事。

## 连续性相关事实

- 现实改变源于量子干涉装置故障，影响范围包括宇宙、历史和人物经历。
- 斯图尔特与丹妮丝关系亲密；两人在逃亡中互相表白，但现实恢复后丹妮丝消失，关系状态留下悬念。
- 伯特曾在加州理工教授地质学，巴里与原剧中的身份及性格存在明显延续，同时在本宇宙掌握了更大的权力。
- 拉杰曾骑大象参加婚礼；他的妻子后来遭飞蛾吞食，显示本宇宙的灾难早已持续相当长时间。
- 恢复现实后，末日景象、巨型飞蛾和众人的伤势均消失，说明装置确实重置了部分现实状态。

## 笑点与文化引用

- 漫画店里围绕《蝙蝠侠》608、鹰眼和绿箭侠展开争论。
- 斯图尔特自称拥有罗德岛设计学院美术学士学位，却没有从事相关工作。
- 文明崩溃后仍有人认真购买牙线，并出现佩恩与泰勒的街头表演。
- 拉杰把装置说得极其复杂，斯图尔特却觉得它像威廉姆斯·索诺玛的面包机。
- 巴里把自己包装成仁慈统治者，却迅速下令强迫丹妮丝；他用大量 **r** 音错读制造持续笑点。
- “邪恶者”不能直呼其名的设定被拿来与伏地魔比较；巴里认为伏地魔至少还能讲道理。
- 伯特用“石头克剪刀、克不了枪”吐槽自己的爆炸物计划，随后证明地质学确实能解决军事问题。

## 未解决线索

- 丹妮丝在现实恢复后去了哪里，是否仍记得末日世界和斯图尔特。
- 另一个斯图尔特是否仍在某个现实流中生存，以及他为何能穿越到当前现实。
- 量子装置是否完全修复，未来是否还会产生新的现实分支。
- 巴里在现实重置后的身份、记忆和命运尚未交代。

## S01E02


---

**Episode S01E02 — Summary**

**Episode Plot**

Picking up after the quantum interference device tore reality, Stuart, Bert, and Barry find themselves in an alternate universe where everyone is unnaturally pleasant. They discover electronic punishment devices implanted in their brain stems that enforce compliance and happiness. Stuart searches for Denise while Bert attempts to locate Leonard and Sheldon at Caltech. All three are arrested for "getting upset" — a police matter in this world — and taken to a re-education center. They are rescued by a Resistance group led by Penny, who deprograms them by provoking genuine anger. When the Resistance learns the machine cannot defeat their AI overlord, drones attack the headquarters. Barry activates the device, and the group escapes into yet another universe — one where magic is real and scientists are hunted by mobs.

**Important Character Actions**

- **Stuart** prioritizes finding Denise over the mission, going to her home and attempting to convince her to leave Gary and return with him. He later stalls the Resistance by proposing an idea (unrevealed before the attack).
- **Bert** visits Caltech alone, discovers geology has been demoted to a storage room behind the gym, and endures a humiliating encounter with a dismissive woman, during which he wets himself.
- **Barry** is the one who ultimately activates the machine to escape both the re-education center and the drone attack, though his timing is chaotic and not fully controlled.
- **Penny** leads the Resistance, makes the call to rescue the group 30 months prior (from her perspective), and personally deprograms Stuart, Bert, and Barry using shielding technology and deliberate provocation.
- **Denise** is found living with Gary in a relationship maintained by the neck devices — she is often intoxicated to avoid feeling unhappy, and she admits Stuart's offer is tempting but fears the pain of leaving Gary.

**Newly Established Names/Terms**

- **Gary**: Denise's partner in this alternate universe; their relationship is sustained by the AI's punishment devices.
- **Officer McCaffrey / Officer Steve**: The arresting officers who police negative emotions.
- **Christine Baranski**: Appears as the government official who explains the AI's origin and the re-education process at the center.
- **The Resistance**: An anti-AI rebellion operating out of a former Amazon shipping center, led by this universe's version of Penny.
- **Re-education center**: A facility where non-compliant citizens undergo a 30-month process to enforce happiness.
- **Kyle**: A wizard encountered in the magic universe at the episode's end.
- **Necronomicon / Alchemy for Dummies**: Comic books visible in the magic universe's store, signaling the shift in genre.

**Continuity-Relevant Facts**

- The neck devices are electronic punishment implants in the brain stem, built by an AI that first killed its Silicon Valley creators before enforcing global compliance.
- The machine does not fix the AI problem — it only transports users between universes, a limitation Stuart admits to the Resistance.
- Penny has been running the Resistance for at least 30 months and called for the rescue before the episode's main events began.
- Bert's geology profession is treated as unimportant in this universe, relegated to a storage room.
- The comic book store's inventory changes between universes (Archie comics, Richie Rich appear where they didn't before).
- Barry's rhotacism (r-speech impediment) persists across universes and is noted by other characters.

**Jokes and References Useful for Later Episodes**

- Stuart's line about losing "the love of my life and I get you" (to Barry) sets up their odd-couple dynamic.
- Barry's mathematical justification for killing Raj ("one of an infinite number of Wajs... a rounding error") is a callback to the previous episode and establishes his callous logic.
- The "dollop of mind control surveillance" exchange — where Barry defines "dollop" and Stuart snaps at him — is a recurring-style gag about their bickering.
- Bert wetting himself at Caltech and the "lucky you're wearing dark pants" line is a physical-comedy beat for his character.
- Stuart being "a little turned on" while being deprogrammed is a character-specific joke about his awkwardness.
- The "asshole on Trevor's head" in the magic universe is a visual gag that may recur as a magical consequence.
- "Burn the scientist!" as the episode ends foreshadows ongoing danger for the group in anti-science or magic-dominated realities.

**Unresolved Threads**

- Stuart's idea for the Resistance is never revealed before the drone attack interrupts.
- Denise chooses to stay with Gary; her fate and whether she can be freed from the AI's influence remain open.
- The group has now jumped into a magic universe with no clear plan for returning to the original Big Bang Theory reality.
- The AI overlord and its re-education apparatus are still active in the previous universe, with Penny's Resistance now exposed and under drone assault.
- It is unclear whether the machine can be controlled or if jumps will continue to be random.
- The "asshole on Trevor's head" magical mishap suggests the group may inadvertently cause harm in new universes.
- Leonard and Sheldon's whereabouts in these alternate universes have not yet been established.

## S01E03


## S01E03

## Episode Plot

Cold open: at a diner, Stuart and this universe's Denise sort out a paradox. A more successful alternate Stuart — "the me I was supposed to have been" — told Stuart to use the machine to get back to "his universe." Denise insists they were never together; Stuart is conflating memories of a different Denise from another universe. This Denise is an alcoholic ("This version of you is an alcoholic").

In the magic universe, locals believe Stuart is a legendary weather sorcerer: he makes it rain by politely asking ("Please, rain."), manifests a house-broken puppy ("hocus pocus puppy"), and transforms drinks (Coke to Diet to Dr. Pepper). Science is evil here and scientists are burned at the stake. The comic store sells "Dr. Strange Colorectal Surgeon."

To jump-start the machine they need magic. Stuart summons the "demon Alexa" ("I'm the demon Alexa. You summoned me."), which lists nearby wizards, including "Bernadette Wostenkowski, master sowcewess" (four-and-a-half stars). Bernadette is a disgraced sorceress: the Wizards Guild of America sanctioned her for using powers during a strike (they struck for dental and eternal-spell residuals; the settlement was "a handful of magic beans"). Her cover story: she only resurrected a neighbor's dog, Chocolate Chip. The reversal spell requires "blood of an innocent"; Stuart donates (he's "not innocent" — he's had one girlfriend) and her powers return.

Bert reappears — he's magic now, with acolytes who worship him (he "got to first base" with the pretty one). He was nearly burned at the stake while Barry did nothing ("It was peer pwessure"); Bert turned Barry into a penguin and refuses to change him back.

Wil Wheaton arrives as a guild enforcer: Stuart enabled a demon and must be destroyed. He throws magic fireballs; Bernadette blasts him ("I think maybe I overdid it"). Barry realizes "'Demon' was witeral!" — Bernadette is a real demon, which is why the guild sanctioned her. She seizes the machine: "You have the machine that spans worlds. I want it. I want to rule them all!" Bert frantically tries to power it with magic ("Work. Turn on. Turn on more.") — "Ah, shit!"

Cut to: "Mr. Bloom. How are we today? … Time for your meds. Three o'clock." Stuart is in a psychiatric ward; a nurse indulges him ("You're very talented."). Stuart: "Oh, this is not good."

## Important Character Actions

- **Stuart**: gains weather/element powers in this universe; refuses Barry's "get a new Denise" advice ("No, I want this one" — drawn to her partly because his mom also drank); donates blood for the spell; shares a tender moment ("Maybe you're great. Maybe there is something here."); ends the episode institutionalized — or in a universe where he is.
- **Barry (Kripke)**: rhotacism intact ("Wooking everywhere for you"); transformed into a penguin by Bert, can't fly ("Shitty fucking penguin wings"); has the "demon was witeral" realization; prefers penguin to maggot.
- **Bert**: found as a magic-wielding figure with worshipping acolytes; survived the stake; turns Barry into a penguin (payback for Barry letting the mob set him on fire); attempts to jump-start the machine at the climax.
- **Denise (this universe's version)**: alcoholic; ex-wife of Gary (six-year marriage, shock collars so they never fought); pragmatic ("I need a drink"); spots the danger at the climax ("Guys… you need to see this").
- **Bernadette (this universe's version)**: world-renowned sorceress fallen from grace; restored by Stuart's blood; revealed as an actual demon; destroys Wil Wheaton; wants the world-spanning machine to "rule them all."
- **Wil Wheaton (this universe's version)**: guild enforcer who sentences Stuart to destruction; defeated by Bernadette, possibly killed.

## Newly Established Names/Terms

- **Wizards Guild of America**: union-like guild; struck for dental and residuals on eternal spells; settled for "magic beans."
- **Demon Alexa**: the magic universe's voice assistant; summoned by wake phrase; searches for wizards and nags about shopping lists.
- **Reversal spell**: restores a sanctioned sorceress's powers; key ingredient is blood of an innocent ("Two teaspoons. Maybe a cup.").
- **Acolytes**: Bert's worshippers.
- **Abramowitz the Grey**: magical-litigation lawyer, 700+ years' experience (Gandalf parody).
- **Dr. Strange Colorectal Surgeon**: the magic universe's comic book ("He does a bowel resection").
- **Chocolate Chip**: the dog Bernadette resurrected.
- **"The me I was supposed to have been"**: a successful alternate Stuart.
- **Shock collars**: marriage-enforcement devices in this universe (Denise and Gary).
- This universe's **Sheldon Cooper**: Broadway star in a Man of La Mancha revival.

## Continuity-Relevant Facts

- The group is stranded in the magic universe; the machine needs "a wittle abwacadabwa" to jump-start because physics doesn't apply here and science is treated as evil.
- Visitors manifest magic powers in this universe (Stuart: weather/life/elements; Bert: full sorcery with followers).
- Multiple Stuarts and Denises exist across universes; Denise herself observes "He always finds me. But it's a different me." Stuart keeps choosing "this one."
- "Gary" recurs as Denise's partner/ex across alternate universes (the pleasant universe in S01E02, the shock-collar marriage here).
- The demon Bernadette now knows about the world-spanning machine — a new antagonist with multiversal ambitions.
- The psych-ward ending is deliberately ambiguous: either Stuart's adventure (or the whole crisis) was a delusion, or it's yet another alternate universe.

## Jokes and References Useful for Later Episodes

- "Dr. Strange Colorectal Surgeon" — Doctor Strange parody.
- "Hocus pocus puppy" — Stuart's manifested, house-broken puppy.
- Man of La Mancha revival starring Sheldon; Barry saw it with "John Withgow" (John Lithgow), "He's always good."
- Demon Alexa: "I was taking a dump. What do you want?"; shopping-list nagging ("How you doin' with paper towels?"); "Do not take that tone with me, buddy boy."
- The Wizards Guild strike — "Better dental, residuals for eternal spells" — a parody of real writers'/actors' strikes, ending in "magic beans."
- "Abramowitz the Grey" — Gandalf parody ("Have you or a loved one been injured by a defective spell or faulty incantation?").
- Blood of an innocent: "I've had… I've had girlfriends. How many? One. I can work with that."
- "They're a dime a dozen… No, I want this one"; "Penguin's fine! Happy to be a penguin"; "'Demon' was witeral!" — Barry gags.
- "I want to rule them all!" — demon Bernadette echoing the One Ring.
- "Oh, don't feel bad, buddy. He started it." — Stuart consoling the demon who just killed Wil Wheaton.
- The bootstrap-paradox exchange: "if you were supposed to have been him, then how could he have existed to tell you that? — Fair question."

## Unresolved Threads

- Is the psych-ward scene real? Either the entire adventure was Stuart's delusion (making him an unreliable narrator) or he's landed in yet another universe — a cliffhanger with no resolution.
- Did Bert's magic jump-start the machine? Outcome unknown ("Ah, shit!").
- The demon Bernadette's fate: defeated, or still hunting the machine to "rule them all"?
- Wil Wheaton's fate after Bernadette's blast ("I think maybe I overdid it").
- This universe's Denise is left behind; the original Denise's whereabouts remain unknown.
- The successful alternate Stuart ("the me I was supposed to have been") — his origin and fate are still unexplained.

## S01E04


## S01E04

## Episode Plot

Continues directly from the S01E03 cliffhanger: Stuart is a patient in a psychiatric hospital, telling his psychiatrist Dr. Fowler about the previous universes (Penny's AI-government resistance with neck chips, then the magic universe). Dr. Fowler keeps his Shamoxil and doubles his Trafederine. Stuart insists he isn't crazy and wants out — not just to another universe, "a good universe... without giant moths, or evil demons." High on the new meds, he has a hallucination-flavored escape attempt through a strange hallway (encounters with "Mamie Eisenhower," a man who converted to Sherpa for a girl he met online, a Michigan J. Frog song, "That's all, folks!").

Denise visits: in this universe she is Stuart's wife and they have two children, Jaden and Talia. He agrees to do the work, completes group therapy (a roleplay with his parents) and occupational therapy (leather work — he made a wallet), and is discharged: "It's time you go home." He asks when Bert will be released — there is no patient named Bert.

Denise drives him home ("Mama really missed her Big Daddy") with one stop: his art exhibition. In this universe Stuart is a celebrated painter ("the hottest artist going"; "asshole famous," per Denise), Bert owns the gallery, and neighbor Barry Kripke is gay, married, Jewish, and held Stuart's son at his bris. Gary also appears and checks whether Barry is giving Stuart a hard time. Overwhelmed, Stuart goes for a walk and buys a comic book — out of character for this universe's Stuart, who doesn't read comics.

The comic retells the true origin: "It was like any other Tuesday at Caltech... Leonard, Sheldon, and Howard constructed a Vari-state Quantum Entanglement Device. Stuart Bloom stopped by to share the news of a clean colonoscopy... he knocked the device off the table. From that point on, Stuart was entangled with the fate of the universe." It adds: "Messages were sent to help him, but the big dope didn't notice... you have to escape from this prison... We're going to send you more instructions." Stuart realizes the tell: "Doesn't bother you that everything seems perfect?... When have my dreams ever come true?" The group breaks out into a Matrix-like pod world (Denise: "I'm bald"), then suspects the escape itself is simulated ("That's what they want us to think"). They jump again, land in a desert, and strange beings approach speaking simple English ("No hurt. Peaceful. We come from far. We good.") — "They sound stupid." Cliffhanger.

## Important Character Actions

- **Stuart**: goes from medicated patient to "recovered" — then figures out the perfect life is a fake; buys the tell-all comic, recognizes the prison, and leads the group's escape. Notably unmoored from his comic-book identity in this universe.
- **Denise**: visits Stuart in the hospital, cites "the children," uses the pet name "Big Daddy"; is present at the exhibition and escapes with the group; delivers the "I'm bald" beat in the pod world.
- **Barry**: the fake universe's neighbor — gay with a handsome husband, still rhotacistic; admits he liked it there ("I was homosexual, but my husband was a weal wooker") and hopes the next universe makes him straight.
- **Bert**: initially appears in Stuart's hallucinated hallway escape, then is revealed to own the art gallery; in the desert he identifies the ground as clay — "Could be kaolinite or montmorillonite... It's dirt. I was showing off a little."
- **Dr. Fowler**: this universe's Amy is a psychiatrist, not a neurobiologist; manages Stuart's meds and discharges him.
- **Gary**: appears at the exhibition as a friendly/protective presence, asking if Barry is bothering Stuart.

## Newly Established Names/Terms

- **Shamoxil / Trafederine**: fictional psychiatric medications (Trafederine dosage doubled).
- **Dr. Fowler**: psychiatrist version of Amy Farrah Fowler.
- **Jaden and Talia**: Stuart's children in the prison universe (named by Stuart — "I do need help").
- **"Big Daddy"**: Denise's pet name for Stuart in this universe.
- **Vari-state Quantum Entanglement Device**: the comic's name for the device (note: "entanglement," vs. "interference" used in S01E01).
- **Kaolinite / montmorillonite**: clay minerals Bert cites for the desert dirt.
- **The prison universe**: a simulated perfect reality built around Stuart's dream life; its controllers are only referred to as "they."
- **Mamie Eisenhower / the Sherpa**: hallucinated or fellow-patient characters in the hospital.

## Continuity-Relevant Facts

- The comic book canonizes the pilot origin story (Tuesday at Caltech; Leonard, Sheldon, and Howard built the device; Stuart knocked it over while sharing his clean colonoscopy; he became "entangled with the fate of the universe") — matches S01E01.
- Help messages are being delivered **through comic books**; Stuart nearly missed them because this universe's version of him doesn't read comics. More instructions are promised.
- The trap is a life tailored to Stuart's wishes (successful artist, wife, kids, cable TV) — designed to keep him complacent and never fix the device. The perfection itself is the tell: "When have my dreams ever come true?"
- Gary recurs for a third consecutive universe (S01E02 partner, S01E03 ex-husband, here a friendly figure at the exhibition).
- Barry's rhotacism persists across universes, as does Bert's geology expertise.
- Amy's profession differs by universe (neurobiologist in the original, psychiatrist here) — consistent with the "alternate versions differ" rule.
- The Matrix-style reveal (pods, bald bodies) leaves open whether the group has truly escaped or merely traded one simulation for another.

## Jokes and References Useful for Later Episodes

- Warner Bros. cartoon callbacks during Stuart's hallucination: "Hello My Baby" (Michigan J. Frog) and "That's all, folks!" (Porky Pig).
- "Careful there, Chachi" (Happy Days); "I'm Mamie Eisenhower"; the Sherpa-conversion gag ("I thought Sherpa was an ethnic group. It is. I converted."); ayahuasca therapy.
- Toilet humor: MiraLAX, Fleet enema, "Don't strain. Remember, that's how Elvis died."
- Step Brothers on movie night — Stuart "laughed in all the right places" as proof of sanity.
- Thor's hammer physics debate (centrifugal force vs. Newton's first law); Stuart settles it — Asgardians exist "outside the paradigm of the physical universe."
- The Matrix gags: "It looks like The Matrix to me," "literally a scene from the movie," "That's what they want us to think."
- "Am I down-to-Earth famous, or more asshole famous? — Asshole."
- Barry lines: "the woony bin," "held your son at his bwis," "I'm a kidder. Just ask my husband," "Anywhere, as wong as I'm stwaight."
- Comic narration: "the big dope didn't notice" — Stuart and Denise trade "You're a big dope."
- "A completely different ball of wax" — phrase repeated back by Dr. Fowler.

## Unresolved Threads

- Who is "they" running the prison universe, and who sends the comic-book instructions (possibly the alternate Stuart from S01E01)? More instructions are on the way.
- Are the group truly out of the simulation, or is the "escape" itself part of it ("That's what they want us to think")?
- The strange beings at the end ("No hurt. Peaceful. We come from far. We good.") — identity and intent unknown; the group is dismissive.
- The fake family — Jaden, Talia, the "Big Daddy" life, Barry's husband, and the gallery — all left behind when they escaped; whether fake-Denise retains any awareness is unaddressed.
- The device still needs fixing; the original Denise is still missing; the original universe remains unreached.

## S01E05

# S01E05 Summary

## Episode Plot
The group (Stuart, Denise, Bert, Barry) remains trapped in a primitive tribal universe where everyone inexplicably speaks English despite no outside contact or technology. They discover messages are being sent via animal droppings (bear scat), leading them to "use red quartz" to repair the quantum interference device. Bert bonds with a tribal woman named Chana, adopts the tribe's communal lifestyle, and proposes marriage. The wedding ceremony (officiated by Denise, a Universal Life Church minister) is disrupted when tribe members—having embraced Kripke's sarcastic "private property" lessons—turn possessive and chaotic. The group secures the red quartz from Gavel (who initially refused) and appears to return to a normal-looking universe at episode's end.

## Important Character Actions
- **Bert**: Embraces tribal life, falls for Chana, proposes marriage, asked Stuart to be best man
- **Barry Kripke**: Sarcastically teaches tribe concepts of private property ("mine") and capitalism ("credit," "leverage"), inadvertently corrupting their communal culture
- **Denise**: Officiates Bert's wedding (reveals she's a certified Universal Life Church minister), pragmatic throughout
- **Stuart**: Reluctant best man, running out of Lexapro, uncomfortable with tribal customs but supportive of Bert
- **Chana**: Tribal woman who adopts monogamy for Bert, then gets caught in the property dispute at the wedding

## Newly Established Names/Terms
- **Chana** – Tribal woman who pairs with Bert
- **Simpa** – Tribal man previously paired with Chana
- **Gavel** – Large tribal man who possesses the only piece of red quartz
- **Universal Life Church** – Denise's ordination source (exists in original universe, "bullshit" there, nonexistent in this one)
- **"Chana-Town"** – Kripke's Chinatown-reference joke after the wedding collapses

## Continuity-Relevant Facts
- The quantum interference device requires **red quartz** to function
- Messages from an unknown sender are arriving via **animal droppings** (bear scat) in this universe
- The tribe speaks fluent English despite zero technology/contact — unexplained (Kripke theorizes convergent linguistic evolution; others note they might be speaking the tribe's language unknowingly)
- Denise's minister credentials don't transfer across universes
- Bert's "two times in 24 hours" running gag established

## Jokes/References for Later Episodes
