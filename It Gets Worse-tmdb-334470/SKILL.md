## Core Premise
Three university best friends — Ethan, Abi, Sam — moved to London together a year ago. They consider themselves soulmates-for-life but are also deeply dysfunctional roommates who have “collectively accomplished nothing.” When their landlord sells the flat, they are evicted and scattered across a city that “seems to want them dead.” The series follows their attempts to keep the friendship alive while navigating humiliating jobs, non-existent love lives, dating disasters, and the general sense that the world is falling apart. The recurring refrain: “Surely it gets better than this? … Spoiler alert! It doesn’t.”

## Key Relationships
- **Ethan–Abi–Sam**: Core triad. “Best friends from uni, soulmates for life, dysfunctional roommates.” Codependent, bickering, deeply loyal. Shared history = shared shorthand, inside jokes, unfinished sentences.
- **Trio vs. London**: The city itself functions as an antagonist — housing crisis, cost of living, indifference.
- **Trio vs. Adulting**: Jobs, dating, bureaucracy, “getting their shit together.”
- **Narrator ↔ Characters**: Zadie Smith’s narration frames, comments, occasionally judges; literary omniscient voice contrasting with characters’ chaotic immediacy.

## World Setting
- **Contemporary London (2026)**: Realistic but heightened. Neighborhoods unspecified; focus on rental precarity, gig/low-level office jobs, pub culture, dating apps.
- **Generational lens**: Cusp Gen Z / younger millennials (mid-20s). Themes: housing insecurity, climate/doom anxiety, performative wellness, irony as defense mechanism.
- **Tone**: Sharp British comedy — cringe, deadpan, meta, warm underneath. A24 sensibility: stylized, slightly surreal edges, emotional honesty beneath absurdity.

## Speaking Styles
- **Ethan (Leo Reich)**: Fast, self-interrupting, hyper-verbal anxiety; meta jokes; second-guessing mid-sentence. Jewish-British cadence possible. Subtitles: tight, preserve rhythm; don’t over-explain jokes.
- **Abi (Emily Fairn)**: Sharper, drier, more pragmatic. Cuts through Ethan’s spirals. Subtitles: concise, punchy.
- **Sam (Olive Gray)**: Softer, observational, sometimes the straight man. Subtitles: naturalistic, slightly longer beats.
- **Narrator (Zadie Smith)**: Literary, measured, omniscient, slyly witty. Formal register vs. characters’ slang. Subtitles: slightly elevated 书面语 tone; maintain irony.
- **Group dialogue**: Overlapping, unfinished sentences, callbacks. Subtitles: use “……” for trailing, “—— ” for interruptions; keep ensemble energy.

## Cultural Context for Translation
- **British comedy codes**: Self-deprecation, class awareness (implicit), rental crisis as shared trauma, pub as social anchor, NHS/GP references possible.
- **Generational signifiers**: Dating apps (Hinge, “the apps”), side hustles, climate dread, therapy speak used ironically, “manifesting” jokes.
- **A24 fingerprint**: Stylized realism, emotional vulnerability under irony, occasional surreal cutaways.
- **Channel 4 legacy**: *Derry Girls*, *Motherland*, *Catastrophe* — female-led, ensemble, domestic chaos. *It Gets Worse* sits in this lineage but younger, more meta.
- **Zadie Smith narration**: Literary prestige marker; her voice signals “this is also a novel about a generation.” Translate narration with novelist’s precision.
