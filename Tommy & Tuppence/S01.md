## S01E02

### Episode Plot

Tommy and Tuppence investigate Tatiana's murder after Bingo's arrest. Armed with the button clue (two costumes, not one), they pressure DI Merriot, who dismisses them. Seeking guidance from Aunt Ada at Sunny Ridge, they infiltrate Gossington House posing as a wedding-couple and recover the second bunny costume from a dumpster. With Bingo's costume now confirmed intact, police re-examine the case. Meanwhile, Tuppence discovers Lina Weaver (Simon's employee) lied about her Uber alibi. Lina reveals she found a password on Tatiana's belongings that unlocked evidence of a secret criminal business between Tatiana and Simon. Tommy's flat is ransacked by a motorbike-helmet intruder (later revealed to be Sebastian). Tracing the costume shop records, they prove Alice rented two costumes under an alias ("Carroll Lewis"), identifiable by her trademark heart-dotted i's. They confront Seb at an abandoned building site, where he confesses the full scheme: he and Alice conspired to kill Tatiana — Seb orchestrated it, Alice wielded the knife. Seb's real motive was seizing Tatiana's data on Simon's drug-smuggling operation as leverage. He tries to kill Tuppence but is caught by police. In the aftermath, Tuppence stays in London, and she and Tommy formalize their partnership as "Blunt's Brilliant Detectives," funded by a reward from Bingo.

### Important Character Actions

- **Tommy**: Builds a colour-coordinated murder wall overnight; tracks Tatiana's phone via read receipts (grey tick); recognises the costume shop's Blanche DuBois display to buy time while Tuppence finds the rental records.
- **Tuppence**: Texts "I know what you did" to Tatiana's phone to flush out the killer (reckless but effective); fakes a health-and-safety inspection to access the dumpster; distracts the costume shop assistant with theatrical fandom; deduces Seb as Motorbike Man; is nearly pushed off a ledge by Seb.
- **Aunt Ada**: Advises them to "find the other bunny" and "talk to the dead"; provides the strategic framework for the whole investigation.
- **Lina Weaver**: Reveals the password, Simon/Tatiana's secret business, and that she's being stalked by Motorbike Man; flees rather than testify.
- **Sebastian (Seb)**: Revealed as the killer and Motorbike Man; partnered with Alice to murder Tatiana; ransacked Tommy's flat to recover the password/data; attempts to kill Tuppence; arrested.
- **Alice Dalvey**: Rented the second bunny costume under alias "Carroll Lewis"; committed the actual murder during the fireworks; was already writing a book about it beforehand; arrested.
- **DI Merriot**: Reluctantly accepts the second costume evidence; arrests Alice based on the costume-shop tip; warns Tommy and Tuppence she'll arrest them for wasting police time if they keep meddling.
- **Draper (constable)**: Fan of Tommy's novels; mentions his character Di Dashford; notes fans have dropped off due to long gap between books.

- **Charlie** — Lives with Constance; unclear exact relationship (partner/husband).
- **Albert** — Tommy's landlady at The Lady Albert pub; gets knocked out by Motorbike Man during the flat break-in.
- **Section 27** — Bluffed by Tuppence as a regulation against dogs in the workplace to intimidate a groundskeeper.

## S01E05 — Summary

### Episode Plot

The episode opens at Ada Blunt's funeral. Tuppence grieves her aunt and searches Ada's belongings for a hidden goodbye message — a childhood game Ada used to play. At the care home (Sunny Ridge), she encounters **Mrs. Jane Lancaster**, a confused elderly resident who recognizes Tuppence by name and cryptically asks about "your poor child in the chimney," adding that no one believed her.

Tommy and Tuppence discover a recent note Ada left inside a medicine box (opened via the Roman numeral code **MCMV = 1905**, found in Ada's papers): Ada was worried Mrs. Lancaster had witnessed a crime and was not safe, and she wanted Tommy and Tuppence to investigate "the painting." When they return to Sunny Ridge the next morning, Mrs. Lancaster has been taken away at midnight by an unknown "phantom" — leaving behind the painting she said she carried everywhere.

Using reverse image search, they identify the painting as **"The Red House"** by an artist named **Boscowan**, located in **Birnham Hollow, Sutton Chancellor**. They drive there (borrowing Constance's car), find the Red House boarded up and empty, and are confronted by **Amos**, the caretaker/gardener employed by the owner, **Sir Philip Starke**.

At the local pub they meet **the landlady** (Hetty's mother), who denies knowing Lancaster but reacts to the painting. She later reveals her younger sister **Susan** disappeared on **Halloween 1989** at age seven while cycling home from a party. Two more children — **Nicholas and Helen** — also vanished. No one was ever charged.

They visit **Boscowan**, who reveals the Red House belonged to **Julia Starke** (Sir Philip's wife), with whom he had an affair. Sir Philip discovered the affair, accused Boscowan of the child disappearances out of revenge, and moved Julia abroad. Boscowan says Julia is now dead.

At the local church, the **vicar** (from Sutton Chancellor, self-described) warns them to leave and not stir up old wounds. Back at the pub, Tommy and Tuppence realize the connecting figure is **Sir Philip Starke** — he owns the Red House, he employed Amos, he accused Boscowan, and he owns a **Rolls-Royce Phantom** — the "phantom" the Sunny Ridge witness described taking Mrs. Lancaster. The episode ends with them breaking into Sir Philip's estate, finding the Phantom in the drive, and moving to rescue Mrs. Lancaster.

### Important Character Actions

- **Tuppence** discovers Ada's hidden note implicating Mrs. Lancaster as a witness; initiates the entire investigation.
- **Tommy** decodes MCMV as 1905 (Roman numerals), unlocks the medicine box.
- **Ada Blunt** (posthumous) set the plot in motion — she believed Mrs. Lancaster's claims and planned to bring Tommy and Tuppence in before her sudden death.
- **Mrs. Lancaster** is taken from Sunny Ridge at midnight by an unknown party; her fate is the immediate mystery.
- **Tommy and Tuppence** have a major emotional confrontation: Tommy accuses Tuppence of stringing him along (paralleling their university history — he loved her, she left without responding, then quickly replaced him). Tuppence admits she was going to say "I love you" back then but Tommy had already moved on. Tommy tells her the indecision is cruel. They agree to finish the case together regardless.
- **Constance** (Tommy's sister) lends her car in exchange for two weeks of babysitting when Tommy and Tuppence go to America.

### Newly Established Names / Terms

- **Ada Blunt** — Tuppence's aunt, recently deceased; former investigator, possibly MI6-connected (nicknamed "Dame Shirley" by an Italian diplomat's handler).
- **Mrs. Jane Lancaster** — elderly Sunny Ridge resident, key witness to a decades-old crime.
- **Sunny Ridge** — the care home where both Ada and Lancaster resided.
- **The Red House** — painting by Boscowan, based on a real house in Birnham Hollow, Sutton Chancellor; key clue.
- **Boscowan** — local artist, former suspect in the child disappearances, had an affair with Julia Starke.
- **Sir Philip Starke** — owner of the Red House; married to Julia; accused Boscowan of the disappearances (possibly to deflect guilt); employs Amos.
- **Julia Starke** (née unknown) — Sir Philip's wife, Boscowan's muse and lover; now deceased.
- **Amos** — large, axe-wielding gardener/caretaker at the Red House; employed by Sir Philip.
- **Sutton Chancellor / Birnham Hollow** — the village and area where the Red House and the disappearances occurred.
- **Hetty** — the landlady's young daughter; Verity and Libs are Constance's children (Tommy's nieces).
- **Draper** — a friend/contact who helps Tommy (texts her about the cold case; also gave Tuppence flowers).
- **Susan** — the landlady's sister, missing since Halloween 1989, age 7.
- **Nicholas and Helen** — two other children who disappeared after Susan.
- **MCMV** — Roman numeral for 1905; the code Ada used on a medicine box to hide her notes.
- **Apothecary roses** — roses at the Red House, given to Tommy and Tuppence by Amos as a parting warning.

### Continuity-Relevant Facts

- **Julius** is Tuppence's partner; they are going to LA in a few days for his film. Tuppence has not yet told Tommy.
- **Tommy and Tuppence kissed** at some point prior (during the Ada mourning period); both acknowledge it. Tommy says he's not sorry about it. Tuppence agrees, then pulls back, saying Tommy "has to stop doing this."
- **University backstory**: Tommy confessed his love; Tuppence was going to reciprocate but he'd already moved on with someone else. He accuses her of "replacing" him quickly; she says that's unfair.
- **Constance's deal**: two weeks of babysitting in exchange for the car — payable when Tommy and Tuppence are in America.
- **Constance directly challenges Tuppence**: she suspects Tuppence is using the case as an excuse to avoid saying goodbye to Tommy.
- **Ada's MI6-adjacent past**: an Italian diplomat case; Ada gathered intel (and possibly slept with the contact); always brought back good wine.
- **The Halloween party at the church** is imminent — it's roughly late October.

### Jokes / References Useful for Later Episodes

- **"Silence of the Lambs" vs. "The Wicker Man"** — Tommy's horror film debate; Tuppence hates being put through it. They watched several horror films the night before, which pays off in the spooky Red House sequence.
- **"Texas Chainsaw Massacre"** — Tuppence's joke about nearly being extras in a remake when Amos appears with an axe.
- **"Dame Shirley"** — Ada's MI6 codename; a running tribute when friends raise a glass to her.
- **"It's very… Hansel and Gretel"** — Tuppence on the painting; thematic foreshadowing of a trail leading into the woods and a dangerous house.
- **Libs dressed as "The Autumn Budget"** — a quick political joke.
- **Verity's witch speech** — "Witches were persecuted women who defied the patriarchal narrative" — a character beat for the kids.
- **Girl Guide badge** — Tuppence only got her Entertainment Badge; Tommy teases her about it, but she insists she'll protect him. Callback potential if they end up in real danger.
- **"Apolothecary roses… they bite"** — Amos's warning delivered with roses; the roses having "their last bloom" is an ominous seasonal metaphor.

### Unresolved Threads

- **Where is Mrs. Lancaster?** Taken at midnight by someone in a Rolls-Royce Phantom — almost certainly Sir Philip. Is she alive? Is she being silenced?
- **Is Sir Philip the child murderer?** All circumstantial so far: he had motive to frame Boscowan, he was present at the time, he now has Lancaster. No direct evidence yet.
- **Boscowan's guilt** — still ambiguous. He was a suspect; he had proximity; his reaction when asked about Lancaster was evasive (denied knowing her, then got defensive). Tommy and Tuppence noted he "can't be ruled out."
- **The vicar** — she is from Sutton Chancellor, remembers the disappearances, actively discouraged the investigation, and appeared to be hiding something. Tuppence flags her as suspicious.
- **What did Mrs. Lancaster actually witness?** Ada's notes say she saw "a crime" — likely connected to the children. But Lancaster also mentioned "your poor child in the chimney," which is unexplained.
- **Julius and the LA move** — Tuppence hasn't told Tommy. The confrontation forced the issue into the open emotionally, but the logistical decision (does she stay or go?) is unresolved heading into the next episode.
- **Ada's sudden death** — described as "just so sudden" and "unexpected"; no cause given. Could be natural, but given the episode's themes, worth noting.
- **Who collected Mrs. Lancaster?** The Sunny Ridge staff gave no name, address, or phone number; confidentiality cited. One neighbor saw it happen and described a "phantom" (the car, not a ghost).
