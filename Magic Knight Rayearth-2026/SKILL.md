# Magic Knight Rayearth — Subtitle Translation Background

## Series Verification
- **Title**: Magic Knight Rayearth (魔法騎士レイアース, *Majikku Naito Reiāsu*)
- **Format**: Television series (anime)
- **First air**: October 17, 1994
- **Final air**: November 27, 1995
- **Broadcaster**: Yomiuri TV (NNS network: YTV, NTV)
- **Studio**: Tokyo Movie Shinsha (TMS Entertainment)
- **Seasons**: 2 (Season 1: eps 1–20, 1994-10-17 to 1995-03-13; Season 2: eps 21–49, 1995-04-10 to 1995-11-27)
- **Episodes**: 49 × 25 min
- **Synopsis**: Three eighth-grade girls—Hikaru Shidou, Umi Ryuuzaki, Fuu Hououji—are summoned from Tokyo Tower to the magical world of Cephiro to become Legendary Magic Knights and rescue Princess Emeraude, the Pillar whose prayers maintain Cephiro. After killing Emeraude (who loved her captor Zagato), they return to Tokyo. A year later they are drawn back: Cephiro is crumbling without a Pillar, three invading nations (Autozam, Fahren, Chizeta) vie for control, and the girls must decide whether to uphold or end the Pillar system.

## Key Relationships
- **Magic Knights trio**: Childhood friends from different schools; Hikaru (fire), Umi (water), Fuu (wind).
- **Hikaru ↔ Lantis**: Mutual romantic feelings; Lantis is Zagato's younger brother, former Captain of Emeraude's Guard.
- **Umi ↔ Clef**: Umi admires/loves Clef (one-sided, unspoken).
- **Fuu ↔ Ferio**: Romantic couple; Ferio is Emeraude's younger brother (prince).
- **Zagato ↔ Emeraude**: Deep love; Zagato kidnaps her to prevent her sacrifice as Pillar.
- **Clef**: Master mage, teacher of Zagato, Lantis, Alcyone, etc.; 745 years old, appears as child.
- **Presea ↔ Clef**: Presea (master smith) has feelings for Clef; Shera (twin sister, anime-only) also loves Clef.
- **Ascot → Umi**: Admiration after Umi lectured him; magically ages himself.
- **Caldina ↔ Lafarga**: Romantic; Caldina from Chizeta, uses Osaka dialect.
- **Eagle ↔ Lantis**: Close friends; Eagle (Autozam commander) wants Pillar power to save his polluted world.
- **Nova**: Born from Hikaru's grief/guilt after killing Emeraude; manipulated by Debonair; eventually reintegrates into Hikaru.
- **Mokona**: Creator god of Cephiro and Earth; guides the Knights; decides Pillar in manga; crown decides in TV.

## World Setting
- **Cephiro (セフィーロ / 錫菲羅 / 瑟費洛)**: Magical world where will/prayer shapes reality. Maintained by the **Pillar (支柱)** — a single person who must pray solely for Cephiro's peace, forsaking personal desires.
- **Tokyo / Earth**: The Knights' home world; connected to Cephiro via Tokyo Tower.
- **Autozam (オートザム / 柯特贊)**: High-tech, polluted world; seeks Pillar system to cleanse air; mecha (NSX, FTO, GTO).
- **Fahren (ファーレン / 法蓮)**: Chinese-style fantasy realm; ruled by child Princess Aska; illusion magic.
- **Chizeta (チゼータ / 奇利達)**: Middle-Eastern/Indian-style realm; sibling princesses Tatra & Tarta; djinn/lamp magic; overpopulated.
- **Mashin / Rune-Gods (魔神)**: Giant mecha bonded to each Knight — Rayearth (fire/lion), Ceres (water/dragon), Windam (wind/phoenix). Combine into **Combined Mashin Rayearth**.
- **Escudo (エスクード / 艾斯庫多)**: Legendary ore from Spring of Eterna; evolves weapons/Mashin.

## Overall Plot Premise
**Season 1 (eps 1–20)**: Summoned to Cephiro → guided by Clef & Mokona → awaken magic & Mashin → defeat Zagato's minions (Alcyone, Ascot, Caldina, etc.) → learn truth: Emeraude loved Zagato, summoned Knights to kill her → Knights kill Emeraude → return to Tokyo, traumatized.
**Season 2 (eps 21–49)**: One year later; summoned back → Cephiro collapsing, only castle remains → three nations invade → Knights defend survivors → Lantis & Eagle seek to end Pillar system → Hikaru passes Pillar test, rejects system → Cephiro reborn without Pillar; fate now in hands of all who love the land → Knights return to Tokyo, visit Cephiro freely.

## Speaking Styles
- **Hikaru**: Energetic, boyish, direct; uses plain/casual speech (ため口); calls herself "Hikaru" (first person); boyish "ore" rare, mostly "watashi"; exclamatory ("火炎の矢!", "みんな!"). Kendo background shows in battle cries.
- **Umi**: Sharp-tongued, tsundere; "watashi"; formal with strangers, blunt with friends; frequent "バカ!" ("idiot"); feminine but assertive; occasionally polite "desu/masu" to elders.
- **Fuu**: Polite, analytical, "watashi"; consistent "desu/masu"; uses honorifics (-san, -sama); strategic explanations; shy/hesitant around Ferio (stutters, ellipses).
- **Clef**: Gentle, formal, archaic/wise tone ("〜じゃ", "〜のだ"); explains patiently; uses "わし" (washi) for self.
- **Zagato**: Low, calm, menacing; "ore"; measured, philosophical; rarely raises voice.
- **Lantis**: Terse, stoic; "ore"; minimal words; "…" pauses; softens only with Hikaru.
- **Ferio**: Light, roguish, flirtatious; "ore"; teasing; switches serious in combat.
- **Alcyone**: Haughty, obsessive; "watashi" / "atashi"; dramatic, jealous rants about Zagato.
- **Ascot**: Childish → mature; early "boku", later "ore"; earnest, emotional.
- **Caldina**: Osaka dialect (関西弁); playful, money-obsessed; "〜やで", "〜やん"; shifts seductive/serious.
- **Mokona**: "Puu~", "Mokona" only; expressive sounds; understood by Hikaru.
- **Nova**: Childlike, singsong, psychotic; "boku" (male pronoun despite female); "Hikaru-chan"; shifts to vulnerable.
- **Debonair**: Regal, cold, philosophical; "watashi"; speaks of fear/despair as power.
- **Eagle**: Frail, polite, determined; "boku"; formal with subordinates; warm with Lantis.
- **Aska**: Childish, selfish, imperious; "atashi"; demands; tantrums.
- **Tatra/Tarta**: Tatra elegant teasing ("ara~"); Tarta brash, competitive.

## Cultural Context
- **CLAMP hallmark**: All-female mangaka group; blends shōjo (magical girl, romance) with mecha/RPG tropes; strong female leads; tragic romance; cyclical fate.
- **Car-name theme**: Nearly every Cephiran character named after a car model (Cefiro, Clef, Zagato, Alcyone, Presea, Ferio, Escudo, Eterna, Ascot, Caldina, Lantis, Primera, Nova, Ceres, Windam, Autozam, FTO, GTO, NSX, Eagle Vision, Geo Metro, Aska, SangYung, ChangAn, Tatra, etc.). Preserve or note in translation notes.
- **RPG structure**: Explicitly modeled on JRPGs (classes, elements, summoned beasts, dungeons, boss progression). Fuu often meta-comments ("like an RPG").
- **Pillar system critique**: Central theme — sacrifice of one for many; parallels to Japanese "gaman" (endurance) and societal pressure; Season 2 deconstructs it.
- **Isekai pioneer**: Early 90s "transported to another world" with female protagonists; predates modern isekai boom.
- **Broadcast context**: Monday 19:30 JST prime-time on YTV; 49 eps + recap specials; sponsor Polydor Records; OP/ED by Naomi Tamura ("Yuzurenai Negai", "Hikari to Kage o Dakishimete").
- **Chinese release history**: Taiwan (Tōhan/Dongli) — "魔法騎士雷亞斯"; HK (ATV) — "魔法少女" (TV), "魔法騎士" (manga); Mainland — "魔法骑士" (common). Multiple term variants exist; unify per project glossary.
- **Mecha + magical girl fusion**: Mashin designs by Kazutaka Miyatake (Studio Nue); cockpit-less to show faces; transformation sequences.
