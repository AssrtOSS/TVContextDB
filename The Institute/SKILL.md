# The Institute (2025–) — Subtitle Translation Background

## Series Verification
- **Title**: *The Institute* (exact match; not a film, not a subtitle-containing title)
- **Type**: American supernatural horror / sci-fi thriller television series
- **First air date**: July 13, 2025 (SXSW preview June 5, 2025)
- **Broadcaster / Platform**: MGM+ (streaming)
- **Season / Episode structure**: 2 seasons × 8 episodes each (16 total, ~54–59 min/ep). Season 1 aired Jul 13 – Aug 24, 2025; Season 2 premiered Nov 8, 2026.
- **Based on**: Stephen King’s 2019 novel *The Institute* (Chinese title: 《异能研究所》, tr. 姚向辉, 湖南文艺出版社 2021)
- **Developed by**: Benjamin Cavell; directed by Jack Bender (lead)
- **Production**: MGM+ Studios, Spyglass Media Group, Sashajo Productions, Nomadicfilm
- **Filming location**: Halifax, Nova Scotia, Canada

## Key Relationships
- **Luke ↔ Kalisha**: closest allies; she reads his mind, he protects her; TP link persists after her “graduation” to Back Half.
- **Luke ↔ Avery**: genius + strongest TP; Avery reads everyone, Luke plans escape; “golden duo.”
- **Luke ↔ Nick / George / Iris / Helen**: Front Half cohort; Nick (TK) helps physically, others provide moral support.
- **Tim ↔ Wendy**: DuPray deputies, partners; warm banter masks Tim’s trauma.
- **Tim ↔ Sheriff Ashworth**: reluctant boss/subordinate; Ashworth tolerates Tim’s odd hours.
- **Sigsby ↔ Stackhouse ↔ Hendricks**: Institute command chain; Sigsby orders, Stackhouse enforces, Hendricks experiments.
- **Sigsby ↔ “The Man on the Phone”**: she reports to him; he represents the larger conspiracy.
- **Luke ↔ Tim**: parallel narratives converge; Tim becomes the outside ally Luke needs.
- **Kalisha ↔ Maureen**: Maureen (housekeeper) secretly aids kids; Kalisha trusts her.

## World Setting
- **Primary location**: The Institute, a covert facility in rural Maine (near fictional town of DuPray). Externally a nondescript campus; internally split into **Front Half** (前半区 / 前区) — children’s dorms, testing rooms, common area — and **Back Half** (后半区 / 后区) — windowless cells where “graduated” kids are drained to death.
- **Secondary location**: DuPray, Maine — a dying logging town where Tim takes a night‑knocker (夜巡员) job at DuPray Storage.
- **Tone**: Cold institutional horror + small‑town noir; King’s signature blend of bureaucratic evil and childhood resilience.
- **Timeframe**: Present day (2025); Season 2 jumps forward months after the escape.

---

## Overall Plot Premise
Teen genius Luke Ellis is abducted from Minneapolis and wakes in the Institute, where children with telekinesis (TK) or telepathy (TP) are tortured to amplify their abilities for a shadowy “greater good” agenda run by Ms. Sigsby. Kids who survive Front Half testing are “graduated” to Back Half and never return. Luke organizes an escape with Kalisha, Avery, Nick, and others. Meanwhile, haunted ex‑cop Tim Jamieson settles in nearby DuPray as a night knocker, gradually uncovering the Institute’s local footprint. Their paths collide as Luke’s breakout exposes the facility and Tim becomes the kids’ only adult ally. Season 2 follows the fugitive children hunting the larger conspiracy while Sigsby, now a fugitive herself, seeks a way back in.

## Speaking Styles
- **Luke**: Precocious, analytical, calm under pressure; uses advanced vocabulary but still a kid (“That’s not how physics works”). Internal monologue → external strategy.
- **Kalisha**: Guarded, sardonic, protective; short sentences, gallows humor (“Great. Another graduation party”).
- **Avery**: Childlike wonder mixed with eerie omniscience; speaks in fragments when overwhelmed (“So many voices… loud”).
- **Nick**: Loyal, pragmatic, brotherly; casual teen slang (“Dude, that’s messed up”).
- **Tim**: World‑weary cop noir; laconic, dry wit, avoids emotional depth (“Just doing my job, ma’am”).
- **Sigsby**: Bureaucratic coldness masking cruelty; polite, precise, corporate-speak (“We’re saving the world, Luke. One child at a time.”).
- **Hendricks**: Clinical detachment with sadistic undertone; medical jargon, calls kids “subjects.”
- **Stackhouse**: Gruff enforcer; minimal words, threats disguised as procedure.
- **Wendy**: Warm, grounded, pragmatic; gentle teasing with Tim (“You’re a terrible liar, Jamieson”).
- **Maureen**: Quiet kindness, coded warnings; maternal tone (“Eat your vegetables, honey”).

## Cultural Context
- **Stephen King DNA**: Small‑town Maine, institutional evil, children as truth‑tellers, “the world has teeth” fatalism.
- **MKUltra / CIA echoes**: Real‑world Cold War mind‑control programs underlie the Institute’s pseudo‑scientific justification (“DEW line,” “drones”).
- **Post‑2020 anxiety**: Surveillance, state overreach, exploitation of the vulnerable resonates with contemporary fears.
- **Genre blend**: Prison‑break thriller + supernatural horror + character drama; comparable to *Stranger Things* (kid ensemble) meets *The X‑Files* (government conspiracy).
- **Chinese audience notes**: “异能” (paranormal ability) is standard genre term; “毕业” as euphemism for death carries dark irony; “夜巡员” fits police procedural tone better than literal “敲门人.”
