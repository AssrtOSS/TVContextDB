## S14E01 Summary

### Episode Plot
Season 14 opens with the team's 14th year on Oak Island. Marty, Rick, and Craig hold a planning meeting in Dan Blankenship's former house, honoring his 50+ year legacy. They outline three primary fronts: the Money Pit (continuing the solution channel theory), Lot 5 (round foundation and newly discovered slipway), and Lot 8 (boulder feature with anomalous silver). The swamp cobblestone road is also slated for excavation once drained.

### Important Character Actions
- **Rick, Marty, Craig**: Meet as "three principals" in Dan's house, commit to solving the mystery as stewards of Dan's work
- **Tom Nolan**: Joins as partner (Fred Nolan's son), continuing the truce Dan brokered
- **Dr. Ian Spooner**: Presents winter lab results on Lot 8 samples — highest silver ever recorded on the island (0.56 ppm, 14× background)
- **Emma (lab)**: CT-scans Lot 5 slipway iron artifacts; confirms hand-forged wrought iron, bloomery smelting (mid-1600s–mid-1700s), near-identical match to William Phips collection from Maine
- **Terry, Scott, Kyle**: Drill first hole of season (L.25-11 near RP-1); recover large cut wood at 214 ft in solution channel — shows knife/adze marks, sinks in water (indicating centuries-old timber), possibly from original Money Pit collapse
- **Billy, Gary, Laird, Alex**: Excavate Lot 5 slipway; recover hand-forged cribbing spike and square nails, linking slipway activity to Lot 5 round foundation period
- **Team consensus**: Approve drilling through Lot 8 cradle beneath the 20-ton boulder

### Newly Established Names / Terms
- **Solution channel**: Natural water/sludge-filled cavity >200 ft deep where Money Pit contents allegedly fell during 1861 collapse
- **Shaft Six**: 1861 search shaft 18 ft west of Money Pit, 118 ft deep; tunneling toward Money Pit from below triggered catastrophic collapse
- **RP-1 Shaft**: Caisson near Shaft Six area; yielded wood with high gold values at tunnel depth
- **Lot 5 round feature**: Undocumented rounded stone foundation near shoreline; artifacts dated 12th–16th century, possible Knights Templar link
- **Slipway / ship's wharf**: Sloped stone ramp for docking boats between Lots 4–5; excavation yields period iron fasteners
- **Cradle**: Mortar-encased rock structure beneath Lot 8 boulder; wood in mortar dated 1694–1726
- **Bloomery iron**: Pre-blast-furnace smelting product; dates artifacts to 1600s–1700s
- **Sir William Phips**: 1687 salvager of *Concepción* (100+ tons silver); knighted; worked with Freemason Capt. Andrew Belcher (Nova Scotia ties); theory: hid unreported treasure on Oak Island
- **Andrew Belcher**: High-ranking Freemason, Phips's partner, Nova Scotia connections

### Continuity-Relevant Facts

- Unexplained high gold/silver traces in deep Money Pit area water/soil samples (established prior seasons)

## S14E02

# The Curse of Oak Island — S14E02 Summary

## Episode plot
The team splits its efforts between Lot 8 and the Money Pit area. At Lot 8, they drill core samples under the 40,000-pound boulder after Dr. Ian Spooner detected high silver there and carbon-dated embedded wood to the late 1600s — a timeframe matching Sir William Phips's recovery of over 30 tons of silver from the Spanish wreck *Concepción*. The cores show normal soil, bedrock at 13 feet, and blue-gray, mortar-like till similar to the material at the center of the stone cradle, suggesting material was brought up from deep underground. Fiona Leach argues this implies human construction below or beside the cradle — possibly a tunnel. Drilling ends without hitting silver or a void, and the rig returns to the Money Pit.

In the war room, lidar expert Pete Kelsey presents SLAM (terrestrial) lidar data for Lots 5–8. It reveals a possible ancient path running from Lot 5 through Lots 6 and 7 and ending at Lot 8, almost directly at the boulder, plus a deep ditch on Lot 5, a berm on Lot 6, walls on Lot 7, and walls near the boulder site on Lot 8. The team then metal-detects the Lot 5 path: Gary Drayton finds a single-loop button (est. 1750–1850) and a heavy square-shank rosehead spike, a hand-forged iron fastener used in 16th–early 18th-century mining shafts — suggesting a cart carried something along the route.

Later, after a heavy rainstorm, Fiona films bubbles erupting from the Lot 8 drill hole while pumping. Hydrogeologist Terry Matheson says it could just be trapped air forced out by the earlier drilling (a Garden Shaft bubble incident in 2022 preceded the discovery of a possible 17th-century tunnel), but an air-filled chamber or void below cannot be ruled out. The team resolves to keep monitoring.

At the Money Pit, borehole M.75-10.3 drills near Shaft Six and RP-1 — the 7-foot shaft where wood with high gold traces was found two years ago, from a tunnel believed flooded by an 1861 booby trap that collapsed the original Money Pit into the solution channel ~200 feet down. At roughly 137–148 feet, the team pulls up in-situ wood followed by 7–8 feet of loose, soupy material in an open void — an undocumented tunnel, 30 feet deeper than Shaft Six and deeper than the original pit's recorded 113 feet. Rick Lagina and Doug Crowell come to inspect; the wood is rushed to lab for metals and gold analysis.

In the war room, the carbon-dating report on wood from over 200 feet in the solution channel returns ranges mostly predating the 1795 discovery of the Money Pit (29.7%: 1759–1789; 21.9%: 1660–1684; 17.7%: 1733–1757). Dr. Spooner runs a portable XRF on the wood and finds extremely high lead and copper, consistent with earlier high lead/tin/copper/zinc readings in the area — possibly treasure-related contamination absorbed by the wood. Full lab chemistry is pending.

## Continuity-relevant facts
- The Money Pit mystery dates to 1795 (231 years); six men have died, and legend says one more must die before the treasure is found.
- The Lot 8 boulder is 40,000 pounds, man-made, and sits several hundred yards west of the Money Pit area.
- Sir William Phips, English sea captain, recovered 30+ tons of silver from the *Concepción* — the timeframe match for the Lot 8 wood.
- Shaft Six tunnel was flooded by a suspected booby trap in 1861, collapsing the original Money Pit into the solution channel roughly 200 feet underground.
- No record exists of the original Money Pit going deeper than 113 feet.
- Wood from RP-1 (~110 feet, two years ago) tested high in gold.
