## Premise
Ben and Lil, a young couple and recent graduates, move from Leeds to London for Lil’s new job at an architecture firm. On moving day, they break up — but their six-month tenancy agreement contains a break clause that cannot be exercised until the fixed term ends. Forced to remain flatmates, they navigate the messy, often painful aftermath of their first love while sharing the same confined space. The series blends romantic comedy tropes with the very real pressures of the UK cost-of-living and housing crises, mining both humour and pathos from the “exes stuck together” setup.

---

## World Setting
- **Primary Setting:** A London flat (exteriors and most interiors filmed in Ashton-Under-Lyne, Greater Manchester). The flat feels lived-in and authentic — a character in itself.
- **Timeframe:** Episodes are titled by month (August → February), tracking the six-month lease period in real time.
- **Social Context:** Post-university transition, precarious rental market, graduate employment anxiety, first serious relationship breakup. The “break clause” is a real UK tenancy term allowing early termination only at a fixed point — here, the dramatic ticking clock.
- **Tone:** Warm, bittersweet, verbally sharp. Inspired by 1980s/90s rom-coms (*When Harry Met Sally*, *Notting Hill*, *While You Were Sleeping*) — colour palettes, visual style, and emotional beats consciously echo those classics while grounding the story in contemporary British millennial/Gen-Z reality.

## Speaking Styles & Translation Guidelines

1. **Ben (本):** Gentle, hesitant, internally monologuing aloud. Avoid overly polished Mandarin; use filled pauses (呃、那个、就是) and self-corrections. Northern English vowels → subtle Beijing/Northern Mandarin flavour optional but not required; do not overdo dialect.
2. **Lil (莉):** Rapid, articulate, layered with sarcasm. Spanish insertions (mi amor, cariño, pendejo) — keep in Spanish with Chinese subtitle gloss or translate inline if tone demands. Emotional shifts are sudden; subtitles must pivot fast.
3. **Alan (艾伦):** Dad jokes, malapropisms, warmth. Translate humour for sense not literal wordplay; preserve the “trying too hard” energy.
4. **Jos (乔):** Motor-mouth, anxiety-driven tangents. High reading speed required; consider condensation without losing jokes.
5. **Frances (弗朗西丝):** Deadpan one-liners. Timing is everything — keep subtitle duration generous for punchlines.
6. **Owen (欧文):** Camp, theatrical, literary references. Preserve cultural references (Shakespeare, pop culture) with brief gloss if obscure.
7. **General:** British slang (mate, shag, knobhead, minging,Sorted, gutted) — translate to natural Mandarin colloquialisms (哥们、上床、傻逼、恶心、搞定、心碎) rather than literal equivalents. Swearing level: moderate (Channel 4 post-watershed); mirror register in Chinese (操、他妈的、去你的) where appropriate, but calibrate to platform standards.

## Cultural Context for Translators
- **Housing Crisis:** The break clause is not a contrivance — it reflects Generation Rent reality. Lines about deposit, guarantors, viewings, “no pets/no DSS” carry real weight.
- **Class & Regional Identity:** Ben (Leeds, working-class adjacent) vs. Lil (middle-class, internationally mobile) — subtle in accent, vocabulary, family dynamics. Alan’s Northern dad energy vs. Deb’s pragmatic immigrant-mother vibe.
- **Mexican-American Heritage:** Lil’s background informs specific beats (Thanksgiving, Spanish, family expectations). Ricote is Mexican-American; the character mirrors this. Do not flatten to generic “Latina”.
- **Rom-Com Literacy:** The show knowingly deploys and subverts tropes (meet-cute, grand gesture, airport chase, fake dating). Subtitles should signal awareness — e.g., when characters lampshade a trope, the Chinese should land the meta-joke.
- **Mental Health / Grief:** Ricote filmed while grieving a real breakup; the show treats heartbreak as physical, messy, non-linear. Subtitles must honour the rawness without melodrama.
- **British Comedy Timing:** Pauses, awkward silences, reaction shots carry narrative weight. Subtitle in/out points must breathe with the edit.
