## S22E14

# Episode S22E14 — Plot Summary

## Episode Plot

Victoria Coren Mitchell hosts a second-round heat between two returning teams, both fresh from opening-match wins.

### Teams and Introductions

- **Frenemies** (captain Nia Roberts, with Karen Wooding and Jason Butler) — formed from former online-quizzing rivals who finally played together live for the first time; they reveal they live on opposite sides of the UK, haven't met up since, and "tolerated each other" more than bonded. Nia is from Anglesey and makes her own sloe gin; Karen has never been to a pop concert; Jason appeared in a film as a zombie.
- **Sinistrals** (captain Millar McGroarty, with Mia Eaglesham and Geena Baird) — three left-handers now living in Glasgow who almost missed the recording when Glasgow station closed after their previous heat. Millar knows all the lyrics to "Scatman"; Geena once was chased by a peacock; Mia has never played Scrabble.

The Sinistrals win the toss and pick first across the rounds.

### Round 1 — Connections

| Choice | Team | Connection | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| Horned Viper | Sinistrals | Characters/items wearing pyjamas or nightgowns: Arthur Dent (Hitchhiker's Guide wakes in pyjamas), Bananas in Pyjamas, Wee Willie Winkie (in his nightgown) | Solved |
| Twisted Flax | Frenemies | Algospeak — social-media censorship euphemisms: THEY CONFER, Unalive, Pan**demic** ("panorama"), **Ac**countant (sex worker), Seggs | Failed; bonus to Sinistrals |
| Lion | Sinistrals | Human-shaped holes: Japanese horror manga (people drawn through), Hole In The Wall game show, Michael Jackson music video with a body silhouette through a wall, a Chicago pavement rat-print (red herring) | Failed; bonus offered (Frenemies declined) |
| Eye of Horus | Frenemies | Broadway musicals shown as city images: *Chicago*, *42nd Street*, *Oklahoma!*, *South Pacific* | Solved |
| Two Reeds (music) | Sinistrals | Singers whose names contain ground substances: Sandie Shaw, Dusty Springfield, Muddy Waters, Eartha Kitt — Sinistrals crack it via the side route "they're all covered in sand or dust … unclean" prompting audience laughter | Solved with colourful language |
| Water | Frenemies | "Armadillo" in different languages: Tupi, Nahuatl, German (Gürteltier = "belt animal"), Spanish | Solved after two clues |

Score at end of Round 1: Frenemies 4 – Sinistrals 4.

### Round 2 — Sequences

| Choice | Team | Pattern | Answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Horned Viper | Sinistrals | First four winners of the Best Animated Picture Oscar (intro'd 2002): Shrek (2002), Spirited Away, Finding Nemo, The Incredibles | Shrek (confirmed as the first-ever winner) |
| Water | Frenemies | Islands of the Irish Sea ranked by land area, largest to smallest: Anglesey, Isle of Man, Walney, Holy Island | Anglesey / Isle of Man; bonus variant: by population, Anglesey and the Isle of Man swap places |
| Lion | Frenemies | Parts of a dart, front-to-back: flight, shaft, barrel, tip | The tip / the point |
| Eye of Horus | Sinistrals | Words where removing W, X, Y, Z yields another word: now→no, apex→ape, copy→cop, answer needs a Z removal | "Zoo" → "ooh!" (accepted, prompting Victoria laughter) |
| Two Reeds | Frenemies | The number 4 written in descending bases: Base 5 = 4, Base 4 = 10, Base 3 = 11, Base 2 = 100 | 100 in binary |

Score at end of Round 2: Frenemies 6 – Sinistrals 12.

### Round 3 — Connecting Walls

**Frenemies on the Lion Wall** — solved cleanly in one pass with all four groups:

- Pink Floyd albums (Ummagumma, Animals, …).
- Synonyms for "nosy": Snoop, Pry, Poke, Intrude.
- Vintage British high-street cinema chains: Ritz, Empire, Odeon, Plaza.
- Words that follow "Roman": Candle, Alphabet, Nose, Road.

They take the full 10 points (4 groups + bonus connection).

**Sinistrals on the Water Wall** — found two groups before time expired:

- Words ending in "site": Composite, Requisite, Parasite, Opposite.
- Surnames of Liberal Democrat leaders — a six-clue group spanning Ashdown, Clegg, Campbell, Cable, Swinson, Farron.

The two groups they didn't crack were nautical distance units (Cable, Shackle, League, Mile) and *Last of the Summer Wine* characters (Foggy, Marina, Truly, Clegg), with "Compo" planted as the red herring; Victoria notes Clegg is doubly baited (real Lib Dem leader and a Co-Op/clever-clever overlap with the sitcom). Sinistrals score 5 points.

### Round 4 — Missing Vowels

Categories were Mars-themed in a triple sense:

- Things on Mars: Olympus Mons, polar ice cap, Curiosity rover, iron oxide.
- Bruno Mars songs: Locked Out of Heaven, The Lazy Song, When I Was Your Man, Grenade.
- Mars Inc. brands: Whiskas, Dolmio, Hotel Chocolat, Wrigley's Extra.
- Final category appeared to be canonical books (Das Kapital, The Art of War); Sinistrals' "Karl Mars" gaffe is waved off and they recover with Art of War.

Sinistrals dominate the buzzer almost end-to-end.

## Key Character Actions

- Nia Roberts carries the Anglesey bit from Round 2 into the closing beats — Victoria openly fears she has made the island "unforgivable" by sorting it fourth-largest-by-land.
- Millar nearly missing the taping due to Glasgow station's closure is revisited verbally but never graphically; Sinistrals joke their rivals back home will hate them after broadcast.
- Sinistrals deliver the moment of the episode by solving "substances in the ground" via "unclean / covered in sand or dust," which Victoria openly relishes.
- Sinistrals convert the Eye of Horus sequence with a deliberately lateral answer ("animal enclosure to a sound of enthusiasm") that Victoria accepts over laughter.

## Newly Established Names / Terms

- **Sinistrals** — three left-handed Glaswegians, advancing directly.
- **Frenemies** — Roberts / Wooding / Butler trio, kept alive via repechage.
- Anglesey origin for Nia Roberts — flagged for any future Anglesey-themed questions and jokes.
- "Algospeak" used as a colloquium term for platform-censorship euphemisms.

## Continuity-Relevant Facts

- Both teams are confirmed as having won their opening matches, so neither can be eliminated in this heat — Sinistrals go "straight through to the next round," while Frenemies are explicitly told they are "not out" and will return later in the competition.
- Victoria's "whatever that means, I certainly don't know" line about the next-round format implies the Series 22 knockout structure has not been (or has only just been) reformed; future episodes should be checked for whether the structure has clarified by then.
- The "Hole In The Wall" / "Bring on the wall" gag explicitly links Wall-reveal language to the old game show — worth remembering if the show revisits that phrase.

## Jokes and References Useful for Later Episodes

- Opening "Coronation Street is the longest-running TV series" claim is undercut in the closing runner by a fictional "The News," framed as having "started the same day as television itself"; viewers will "find it all on iPlayer." This is the show's standard self-referential "longest programme" canon item.
- "Anglesey is very wonderful" exchange becomes a small in-show catchphrase candidate for the Frenemies arc.
- "Millar knows all the lyrics to Scatman" and "Geena chased by a peacock" remain stock-facts about this trio.
- Sinistrals are tagged with the scoreline 27 and a clean buzzer-round — likely to be the team to beat later in the bracket.
- The "Karl Mars" / Mars-the-corporation threading of three Missing-Vowels categories may recur as a callback reference.

## Unresolved Threads

- What "straight through to the next round" and the missing-repechage pairing actually entail under Series 22's revised bracket.
- Whether Frenemies return later in the same series and against whom (the missing Anglesey/I.O.M./Lib Dem/Last of the Summer Wine categories are all available for re-use).
- The "Glasgow station closed after the last heat" running thread is dropped without follow-up; only worth tagging if a Glasgow-based team reappears.

## S22E15

## Episode Plot

A Series 22 heat with a twist: because both teams had already won their opening matches, neither could be knocked out, so Victoria frames the game as a "warm-up" with no stakes. The Colonial Geese (Reuben Bennett, Sharon Lam, captain Henry Schofield — an Australian/Kiwi trio of medics and a designer) face the returning Knitwits (Dom Hewitt, Charlotte Stokes, captain Sam Hosegood). The Knitwits dominate from Round 1, solve both Connecting Walls cleanly (maximum 10 points each), and crush the Missing Vowels round, winning 39–15. Victoria calls their 39 "absolutely incredible" and notes 27 was the second-highest score ever seen at that point in a match, with 28 the record to beat.

## Important Character Actions

- Henry explains the team name comes from an old Only Connect question about food named after something it isn't: the colonial goose, a New Zealand dish of mutton shaped into a gooseneck. He has a Twisted Flax hieroglyph tattoo, and picks Twisted Flax/Twisted Flex as their first question code.
- Knitwits reveal they've been learning to knit since their first appearance: Sam made "a terrible scarf," Charlotte made "lovely penguins." Victoria insists there's a huge qualitative difference between a scarf and a penguin.
- Knitwits solve the Water Wall in seconds; Geese struggle on pictures (Bill Hader/Barry, hay) but buzz in with the correct "places in Wales" (Hay, Cardigan, Fishguard) for the bonus point.
- Geese nearly crack the "Fin + land = Finland" puzzle and the "B + ananas = bananas" sequence but run out of time on the latter; Knitwits steal both bonuses.
- Victoria refuses to dance Gangnam Style, saying she only knows it from Ed Balls on Strictly.
- Sharon misreads "Lara Croft" as "Laura Croft" in Missing Vowels, costing Geese a point.

## Newly Established Names / Terms

- Colonial Geese — Southern Hemisphere team (residents in paediatrics/anaesthetics, architectural design)
- Knitwits — librarian/cricket/quality-management/machine-learning team, now genuine knitters
- Jordan's grandma — the only other known consumer of colonial goose, aged 93
- Debbie Clifford — someone from Victoria's school, invoked as a benchmark of hardness (she once fought a horse)

## Continuity-Relevant Facts

- Both teams are safe; nobody was eliminated this week.
- Knitwits scored 39, one off the series record of 28 set by a previous team (as cited by Victoria) — a record-breaking chase is live.
- Victoria tells the Geese they're "possibly mine" favourite team this series and expects to see them again.
- Knitwits are characterised as "the gambling Knitwits" — they go for broke and it pays off.

## Jokes / References Useful Later

- The Forth Bridge opening gag: painting it last finished December 2011, next due 2031 — a running length-of-series joke tied to the "extended series."
- Victoria's highlight-of-the-series moment: "Is that Keith Richards?" / "Oh, no, it's the other one" (Mick Jagger) — she says other contestants will struggle to beat it.
- Ed Balls dancing Gangnam Style on Strictly Come Dancing, used as a British culture touchstone the Geese hadn't absorbed.
- Outer Mongolia is real, used idiomatically for somewhere far away.
- The chimpanzee/monkey distinction in the Robbie Williams biopic Better Man — "robot or monkey, I chose monkey."
- "That grizzly force" — the Geese's controversial guess of US immigration officers (ICE) for Iceland.
- Balamory reboot announced 2024, "bigger than the Oasis reunion."
- Victoria's sign-off: "the show that's harder than Debbie Clifford from my school... she once had a fight with a horse."

## Unresolved Threads

- The 39-point score sits just above the existing record benchmark; whether anyone breaks it this series is open.
- Whether the Knitwits' all-or-nothing style survives a tougher draw.
- The Colonial Geese are explicitly promised a return — expect them in a later round, still beloved by the host.
- Debbie Clifford is now established as a recurring yardstick; future sign-offs may escalate her feats.

# Only Connect S22E18 Summary

## Episode Overview
Series 22, Episode 18 of the BBC quiz show *Only Connect* (《神准配对》). Two teams of three compete across four rounds: Connections, Sequences, Connecting Wall, and Missing Vowels. Host: Victoria Coren Mitchell.

## Teams
| Team | Members | Notable Details |

## Round 1 — Connections (Egyptian hieroglyph clues)
- **Makeweights (Horned Viper)**: Image editing tools — *crop, shadow, blur, glow* (pictures from *Glow*, cropped, blurred).
- **Ad Astra (Lion)**: Foot bones mapping to Italian geography — calcaneus (heel/Brindisi), talus, phalanges, metatarsals.
- **Makeweights (Twisted Flax)**: Add "NO" to second word → first word (*nook→ook, nous→us, notable→table, nomad→mad*).
- **Ad Astra (Two Reeds)**: Song titles meaning "a small part" — *Portions For Foxes* (Rilo Kiley), *Doll Parts* (Hole), *Piece Of My Heart* (Erma Franklin), *Ooh Aah... Just A Little Bit* (Gina G).
- **Makeweights (Eye of Horus)**: "Ladies' versions" of gender-neutral products — Virginia Slims, pastel Bic pens, Ms Monopoly, ePad Femme (pink tablet with "Women's Log" app).
- **Ad Astra (Water)**: Things called "killers" — *curiosity killed the cat*, *video killed the radio star*, *fear is the mind-killer* (*Dune*), *beauty killed the beast* (*King Kong* 1933).

**Round 1 score**: Makeweights 6, Ad Astra 4.
