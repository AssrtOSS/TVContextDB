## S03E06

- **剧情概述**  
  S03E06 以“慈善晚宴+奖项”的表面场景展开：肯特和露易丝前往大都会人道主义晚宴，现场既有红毯礼仪，也有明显安保代号（Fox / Vulture / Shark）的战术调度。卡拉（女超人）与克拉克一同被提及与颁奖、社群关注和“Superman 缺席一个多月”的舆论焦点。晚宴后段，武装人员突入造成袭击，Princess Marina Maru 揭露身份为**毒药博士（Dr. Poison）**并发动更大破坏威胁；她宣称自己的手下在大都会水坝，随时可向全城供水系统投毒。随后形成分身式作战压力（前线与水坝双线），最终由卡拉挡下灾难并与吉米配合击退，克拉克在现场受伤并被困。  

- **重要角色动作与情节点**
  - 露易丝与凯特在现场互动：洛伊丝仍保持“职业竞争”节奏，和维姬/马特拉格特“独家新闻”对打，强化三人组新闻线。  
  - 卡拉晚宴上与克拉克、记者圈互动后，被卷入安保与袭击混乱。  
  - Dr. Poison 借“Marina Maru”身份渗透活动、制造高压与误导，直接揭露恐怖行动。  
  - 克拉克在混战中受创、被控制，后续角色讨论把“英雄=超人”与“英雄=克拉克·肯特”这层关系拉到前台。  
  - 吉米（火焰鸟）不仅承担旁线“花絮/互动”角色，也在关键时刻打退 Dr. Poison。  
  - 卡拉最终向克拉克表白：她爱的是克拉克本人，但直指他长期与“超人形象”比较带来的痛。  

- **本集新增/再确认的名词与称谓**
  - **Princess Marina Maru（马科维亚玛丽娜·马鲁公主）** / **Dr. Poison（毒药博士）**  
  - **Operation Make a Splash（搅动海面行动）**  
  - **Vulture / Fox / Shark**（现场对讲代号）  
  - **Le Monde 的动物陵墓/“mauseole”相关场景**（Dr. Poison 的掩护叙事元素）  
  - **Metropolis Dam（大都会水坝）与供水系统投毒威胁**  
  - **“Four legs”**式转移威胁表达（有明显“猎物管理/恐惧施压”语言风格）  

- **连续性相关事实**
  - 超人缺席对大都会与“英雄需求”形成持续性压力（“一个月多没出现”语境被强化）。  
  - 卡拉在危机与情感上更主动，推动两人关系从“未明说的情绪”转为明确选择。  
  - 克拉克的主线自我认同再次受挫：他不再是“必须成为超人”的唯一标准，更多强调“Clark Kent 作为可交付真实英雄行为的个体”。  
  - 现场安保系统（前门、出口、警报、视频重定向）被轻视/被攻破，说明反派未来可继续以高压事件作战。  

- **笑点/梗或后文可用参考**
  - “你的青春魅力（boyish charm）”被当作反恐/反制手段玩梗，和主线严肃危机形成对比。  
  - Dr. Poison 与 James 的互动里有明显“先暧昧后揭穿”反讽结构，后续可复用成“信任反转”类型对照。  
  - “Bad dog / adieu / poundcake”这类法语/俚语混用词组，强化角色口头攻击与身份反差。

- **未解线程**
  - Dr. Poison 的组织链是否完全清空？“Operation Make a Splash”是否有剩余子行动？  
  - 受伤的克拉克后续是否影响其公开行动能力线？  
  - 卡拉与克拉克未来关系边界（爱情、秘密身份、职业身份）在“不能再当超人”之后如何落地。  
  - 这类安保代号体系是否是独立势力（X特遣/第三方干预）还是临时雇佣网络，仍未明。

## S03E07

## Plot

Mxyzptlk and his soon-to-be “ex-wife,” Ms. Gsptlsnz (“Glitzy”), trap Clark, Lois, Jimmy, and Kara in a deadly interdimensional contest. Each team must collect keys and reach the center of a series of bizarre challenges. The losing team will be trapped forever—or transformed into a gift for Glitzy.

The contests gradually reveal that the keys unlock the Cosmic Anvil, a device capable of reshaping an entire universe. Mxy and Glitzy cannot activate it themselves because of a curse, so they manipulate mortals into retrieving the keys. When Mxy threatens the heroes, Clark refuses to participate, and the team deliberately allows the time limit to expire. With no victor, Mxy’s seal remains intact and the Cosmic Anvil stays locked.

## Important character actions

- Clark confronts his identity crisis after seeing alternate versions of himself and hearing that one Clark died. He decides that he must change and become a better friend, rather than define himself solely through Superman.
- Kara realizes that her harsh, competitive behavior resembles the version of herself associated with Brainiac. She accepts that identity is shaped by choices and change, not by a predetermined “true self.”
- Lois recognizes that the contest’s “one true path” premise is a trick. She helps expose the inconsistencies in the game and refuses to accept a manufactured future.
- Jimmy bonds with an alternate Flamebird Jimmy, who hides his grief behind forced optimism after Clark’s death.
- The main team chooses solidarity and resistance over the temptation to restore Clark’s powers or rewrite reality.
- Lois tells Clark that she may be starting to like Clark Kent as he is, suggesting emotional progress in their relationship.

## Newly established names and terms

- **Mxyzptlk / Mxy** — An immensely powerful interdimensional being who has previously encountered Superman.
- **Ms. Gsptlsnz / Glitzy** — Mxy’s wife and partner in the staged “divorce” contest.
- **S’ivaan Mountain** — A sinking challenge site above the Manticore Trench.
- **Manticore Trench** — A dangerous location containing the Manticore.
- **Hell Hippos** — Another lethal creature released as part of the challenges.
- **Cosmic Anvil** — A device capable of reshaping an entire universe.
- **One true path** — The contest’s false premise that each person has one predetermined fate.
- **Flamebird Jimmy** — An alternate Jimmy who becomes a superhero after his world’s Superman dies.

## Continuity-relevant facts

- Clark has lost his powers in the main timeline at this point.
- Mxy has interacted with Superman before, and Clark knows enough about him to distrust his promises.
- Mxy and Glitzy’s “divorce” is role-play for a date; their relationship remains intact, though Glitzy is frustrated by Mxy’s behavior.
- The alternate team comes from a world where Clark died, the Hunger Dogs invaded, Lex became a force for good, and Jimmy took up a superhero identity.
- Alternate futures are presented as possible outcomes, not fixed destinies.
- The Cosmic Anvil remains sealed and unused.
- The episode reinforces that Clark, Lois, Kara, and Jimmy’s identities are defined by ongoing choices rather than predetermined roles.

## Jokes and references

- Mxy insults the main team with absurd labels such as “Gullible Superman,” “Jimmy Lamebird,” “Question Mark Kara,” and “Fun Lois.”
- Glitzy’s team includes “Good Lex Luthor,” a darkly comic reversal of Lex’s usual role.
- Mxy repeatedly treats universe-threatening danger as recreational relationship drama, including the “Team Ficus” joke about turning losers into houseplants.
- The increasingly ridiculous hazards—Hell Hippos, hamster balls, and the Manticore—contrast with the serious emotional revelations.
- The episode parodies game-show and escape-room formats while referencing alternate-universe superhero futures.

## Unresolved threads

- What exactly happened to the alternate world’s Clark, and how did the Hunger Dogs invade?
- What is Mxy’s prior history with Superman, and why does he have a seal or curse limiting his power?
- What are the full capabilities and origins of the Cosmic Anvil?
- Will Clark regain his powers, or continue defining himself without them?
- How will Lois and Clark’s relationship develop now that Lois is reconsidering whether Superman is essential to their future?

## S03E08

# S03E08 — Summary

## Episode Plot

Future versions of Clark and Lois's children, **Jonathan Kent** and **Lara Kent**, arrive in the present on a mission from **General Lane** to assassinate **Lex Luthor**. In their timeline, Luthor commands **the Eradicators** — an army of mind-controlled Kryptonian clones — and has subjugated humanity. Lara attempts to kill Luthor at Lexcorp but is thwarted. **Amanda Waller** returns to power, orders Project Eradicator shut down, and arrests Luthor.

Luthor reveals **Project Eradicator**: a weapon forged from **Superman's living tissue** that self-repairs, self-evolves, and operates under Luthor's direct mental control via implanted brain matter. The incomplete prototype goes rogue, fights the heroes, and escapes into the **time stream**. Kara is badly injured in the battle. Clark realizes Superman must return to fix everything, and departs over Lois's pleas — but not before promising to come back.

## Important Character Actions

- **Jonathan Kent (future)**: Arrives hostile to Clark, resentful of growing up without Superman; gradually softens after Clark shows him compassion.
- **Lara Kent (future)**: Impulsive and soldier-like; attempts the assassination; mirrors Lois's headstrong nature.
- **Lex Luthor**: Unveils the Eradicator as humanity's "dominant species" enforcer; loses control of his own creation.
- **Amanda Waller**: Reasserts authority, shuts down all of Lexcorp, and orders Lex's arrest.
- **General Lane (future)**: Authorized the time-travel assassination mission.
- **Clark/Superman**: Acknowledges his powers are gone and must leave to restore them.
- **Lois**: Confronts her future self's failures as a mother; makes Clark promise to return.
- **Kara**: Battles the Eradicator and is rendered unconscious.

## Newly Established Names/Terms

- **The Eradicators**: Future mind-controlled Kryptonian clones under Luthor's command.
- **Project Eradicator**: Luthor's weapon program using Superman's living tissue.
- **The Eradicator (prototype)**: Self-evolving monster with Luthor's brain-matter control interface.
- **Jonathan Kent / Lara Kent**: Clark and Lois's future children.
- **Time stream**: Dimension the Eradicator escapes into.

## Continuity-Relevant Facts

- Clark and Lois have two children in the future: Jonathan and Lara.
- The future is a dystopia where Luthor rules via Kryptonian clones.
- Superman's biological material was harvested for Project Eradicator.
- Superman is currently depowered or separated from his abilities.
- Amanda Waller has regained her directorship and authority over Luthor.
- A functional prototype time-travel device exists at Lexcorp.
- General Lane (future) is running covert temporal operations.

## Jokes and References

- Pa Kent's world-famous pizza slices and snickerdoodles as emotional buffer.
- "Beautiful day out, eh?" / "The weather is pleasant." — Kara's deadpan delivery.
- "That man can bake" — awed reaction to Pa Kent's cooking.
- Jimmy Flamebird making a brief appearance.
- Parents lamenting their disrespectful children ("Our children... are so disrespectful").
- Lois and Clark both agreeing they're "horrible parents" regarding Lara's upbringing.

## Unresolved Threads

- Clark's destination and method for restoring Superman's powers.
- The Eradicator loose in the time stream — a threat across all timelines.
- Kara's recovery after the Eradicator battle.
- Whether Jonathan and Lara will remain in the present or be returned.
- Whether the future these children came from can actually be changed.
- Lex Luthor's arrest — what consequences follow.
- Clark's promise to return — the emotional weight of Lois facing the future alone.

## S03E09

## S03E09

## Episode plot

The episode cuts between two threads. In Krypton's ancient past, Clark (Kal-El) — summoned by the visions and seeking to recover his lost powers — stands before the Brainiac of old Krypton, an uncorrupted intelligence distinct from the mad creature Clark met on Kandor. Brainiac offers to restore his power if Clark proves himself a conqueror: the star invaders who once attacked Krypton's ancestors are returning that night, and Clark must dominate them. The trial turns real, and death in it is real. Clark instead recognizes the "invaders" as refugees, refuses to kill them, befriends them, and even carries a wounded one up a mountain. Brainiac declares his mercy a failure — the Coluans called Clark "friend," and friendship, not conquest, is why he failed — and sentences him to death.

In the future timeline, the world is reeling in a "post-Superman world." Amanda Waller seizes command of the Kryptonian assets (Jonathan, Lara, and the other children), demanding soldiers, not kids. Lex Luthor has found a temporal anomaly: the Eradicator is returning to area zero — the Zero Day site, now a Checkmate Command Post — in under 48 hours. Waller launches Operation "Doomsday" with two lines of defense: superhumans led by Agent Wilson first, then the Kryptonian children taking orders from Supergirl. Jonathan, talked down from his self-imposed exile on the moon by Kara, refuses to take orders and confronts the Eradicator, declaring "I am Superman." The Eradicator strikes back: "You have no Superman to defend you." The episode ends on both cliffhangers.

## Important character actions

- Clark refuses to murder the Coluans to pass Brainiac's test ("I won't murder these people just to satisfy you. That's not what strength is"), recognizes Brainiac as the being he carried up the mountain, apologizes for what the Empire did to him, and insists he must get home to his family.
- Brainiac (old Krypton) recounts Krypton's origin: the first Kryptonians conquered the star invaders, stole the secrets of strength and flight, and turned their dying star into Rao, the living sun that still powers the fortress. He condemns Clark as a "beggar" unworthy of questioning the Empire's principle that the weak serve the strong.
- Lois (future) admits she can't make the hard call alone, sends Kara to talk to Jonathan, gifts Lara a compass (the same pep talk and nearly the same compass her future self gave Lara before the mission), and confronts Lex: act like the hero you pretend to be, or admit you're the villain.
- Kara wakes from her injuries, ready to fight, and gives Jonathan a key pep talk on the moon — Clark isn't perfect, but he never gives up on people even in failure, and neither will she.
- Jonathan owns his failure (letting Lara leave alone, letting Eradicator escape), runs away to the moon, is brought back by Kara, and charges the Eradicator claiming the Superman mantle.
- Jimmy Flamebird stayed by Kara's tent the whole time she was unconscious and vows never to leave her alone again.
- Waller takes the children as assets, demands soldiers, and briefs Operation "Doomsday."
- Lex Luthor tries to flee ("I know when to get off a sinking ship"); Lois retorts it's his ship and his monster.

## Newly established names/terms

- Brainiac of old Krypton — the uncorrupted original, "eternal servant of the Empire," distinct from the corrupted Brainiac from Kandor.
- Coluans — the star invaders of ancient Krypton; Clark insists they are refugees, not invaders.
- Rao — the "living sun" the first Kryptonians made from a dying star, which still powers the fortress.
- Area Zero — the Zero Day site, now a Checkmate Command Post (first alien attack on American soil).
- Operation "Doomsday" — two-line defense plan against the Eradicator.
- Temporal anomaly — Lex's discovery enabling prediction of Eradicator's arrival.
- "Post-Superman world" — Waller's framing that no one is coming to save them.

## Continuity-relevant facts

- Clark remains depowered; he left to recover his powers via the visions, which were not sent by the past Brainiac — the summons is unexplained.
- The Eradicator (from S03E08) was created by Jonathan's plan to murder Lex Luthor, beat Kara unconscious, is impervious to Kryptonite, escaped into the time stream, and is now converging on the Zero Day site.
- Jonathan and Lara are Clark and Lois's future children; Jonathan's guilt drives his recklessness. "In every single timeline your plan is just 'bring back Dad'" implies repeated timeline resets.
- Future Lois and Sam Lane (the kids' "Grandpa") never got along; Lois says she and her father are too much alike.
- Kara gives Jonathan the same "never give up" lesson Clark-style, positioning herself as his anchor.
- Brainiac's origin: Clark once carried him up a mountain; the Empire's cruelty made him what he is — the "one I carried up the mountain" reveal reframes Brainiac as a sympathetic victim of Krypton.
- Waller is back in charge; Lex is working under/with her, and she implies he wastes their time.
- Agent Wilson (Deathstroke) leads the first line of defense; Jonathan and Lara fight under Supergirl's command.

## Jokes or references useful for later episodes

- "Are you playing moon catch?" — Kara's deadpan line to Jonathan floating on the moon.
- Kara's "I am your friend. Fuh... fuh-rend." — a deliberate callback to Clark's awkward first-contact style.
- "Jimmy Flamebird?" — Kara greeting Jimmy by his Flamebird brand; his "Because you were here" devotion gag.
- Jonathan's bratty "She's not even our real mom!" followed by "Sorry for saving your life, Mom" — teenage spite that may hint at family complications.
- "I know when to get off a sinking ship" / "This is your ship!" — Lex vs. Lois.
- Waller's "little scout troop taking orders from Uncle Sam" and "Let's hope you're as super as you think you are."
- "You know who could use a hot dog?" — Jimmy's comfort-food reflex.
- Lois's compass as a recurring object — the same pep talk and nearly identical compass across timelines.

## Unresolved threads

- Clark is about to be killed by ancient Brainiac ("Die!") — whether he escapes the past, gets his powers back, and returns in time.
- Jonathan confronts the Eradicator alone and is in mortal danger ("You have no Superman to defend you"); the Eradicator's defeat of Kara may repeat.
- Who/what actually sent Clark the visions, and why the old Brainiac did not call him.
- Whether the dystopian future (Luthor's Eradicator army, from S03E08) can be changed; the "every timeline" line suggests prior failed resets.
- What the Empire "did" to Brainiac and how the uncorrupted servant became the corrupted Kandor creature.
- The second line of defense in Operation "Doomsday" and the fates of Lara, Kara, Lois, and the children at area zero.
- Lex's standing after Lois's confrontation — hero or villain, and whether he stays to help.
- The hint about the children's parentage ("not our real mom") left dangling.

## S03E10

# S03E10 — My Adventures with Superman

## Episode Plot
The season finale brings together multiple timelines and the full ensemble cast for a climactic battle against **Brainiac** (the Eradicator), who has evolved across countless timelines to eradicate all Kryptonians. Clark, depowered earlier in the season, enters the Fortress of Solitude and confronts the AI avatar of **Rao** (Krypton's living sun) to reclaim his abilities. Meanwhile, **Jonathan "Jon" Kent / Jon-El** — Clark and Lois's son from a dark future — arrives with his mother **Laura**, warning of a timeline where Brainiac wins. The battle spills into Metropolis as Brainiac's self-replicating Eradicator units overwhelm the heroes. **Jor-El** (Clark's biological father, preserved in Kryptonian armor) returns to fight alongside his son. Together — Clark, Kara, Jon, Jor-El, and the human allies — they defeat Brainiac by choosing connection over fear. The episode ends with Clark proposing to Lois.

## Important Character Actions
- **Clark / Superman**: Enters the Fortress powerless, negotiates with Rao's avatar to restore his powers, fights Brainiac alongside his family, proposes to Lois.
- **Lois Lane**: Coordinates the defense, refuses to abandon civilians, accepts Clark's proposal.
- **Kara / Supergirl**: Fights beside Clark, offers Jimmy a chance at real friendship.
- **Jon-El (Jonathan Kent)**: Returns from a ruined future, initially driven by fear, learns to trust his parents, helps turn the tide.
- **Jor-El**: Manifests via Kryptonian armor/tech, reconciles with Clark, fights as a father not a "Lord of Krypton."
- **Lex Luthor**: Tries to exploit the chaos; arrested by Slade/Task Force X.
- **Slade Wilson / Deathstroke**: Lets Lex go to pursue his own agenda; secures Luthor and Olson.
- **Amanda Waller**: Orders evacuation, prioritizes containment over saving Kryptonians.
- **Jimmy Olsen**: Rebrands Flamebird, invests in W.O.R.M.S. startup, achieves financial independence.

## Newly Established Names / Terms
- **Rao** — Krypton's "living sun," a god-like entity whose child (a sun-eater/fragment) powers the Fortress.
- **Jon-El / Jonathan Kent** — Clark and Lois's son from a dark future timeline; wears Kryptonian armor.
- **Laura** — Jon's mother (presumably a future Lois variant or alternate timeline counterpart).
- **W.O.R.M.S.** — A hot new tech startup Jimmy invested in (name spoken repeatedly as a meme/joke).
- **House of El** — Used formally by Brainiac ("Death welcomes you... house of El").

## Continuity-Relevant Facts
- Brainiac has killed Clark in *every* previous timeline, but an ally/child/lover always escapes — this time, the loop breaks.
- Jor-El's consciousness survived in the Fortress armor; he was the one who sent Clark the visions of Rao in the fire (S03E09).
- The Eradicator self-replicates after being cut in half (by Jon in a prior episode).
- Clark's power restoration came from a "deal" with Rao's avatar — cost unstated but "nothing I wasn't willing to pay."
- Kandor's destruction created new stars/galaxies; Lex gazed into them and acted from existential fear.
- Slade/Task Force X now have Lex in custody.
- Jimmy is financially solvent via comics + W.O.R.M.S. investment; Flamebird is active.

## Jokes / References for Later Episodes
- **W.O.R.M.S.** chant — Jimmy's absurd startup investment, likely a running gag.
- **"Cup or cone? Both, my good man."** — Callback to Clark's childhood / Smallville simplicity.
- **Jimmy: "I'm a dealer, not a user."** — Re: the telephone app; positions him as a hustler.
- **Kara calling Jimmy "a promising friendship"** — Meta nod to comics' Superman's Pal Jimmy Olsen.
- **Krypto adoption via government ID** — Bureaucratic humor; "not a whole farm, but pretty great."

## Unresolved Threads
- **The "deal" Clark made with Rao** — What was the actual cost? Future leverage?
- **Lex in Task Force X custody** — Will he become a Suicide Squad asset?
- **Slade's agenda** — He let Lex go once; now has him. What does he want?
- **Waller's long game** — She sees Superman as a threat contained, not solved.
- **Brainiac's survival** — "In every timeline I defeat you... but someone escapes." The multiversal threat remains.
- **Future timeline (Jon's world)** — Still exists as a branch; "we're gonna fight for the future you deserve."
- **Jor-El's status** — Is he fully restored, or bound to the armor/Fortress?
- **Lois & Clark engaged** — Wedding planning, secret identity dynamics, potential pregnancy (Jon's existence).
