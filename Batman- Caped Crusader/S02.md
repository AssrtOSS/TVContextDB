## S02E01

## Episode Plot

**S02E01** centers on the trial of crime boss Rupert Thorne, whose accountant Julian Gregory (nicknamed "Calendar Man" for his eidetic memory with dates) is the prosecution's last, best witness. From prison, Thorne learns of Gregory via a broadcast by Hattie Tetch (Mad Hatter) and orders his lieutenant Eddie to kill the accountant. Thorne reveals he already has a mole inside GCPD who leaked Gregory's location.

Batman investigates, pressing Barbara Gordon for information. She reluctantly reveals that Commissioner Gordon has used Moldoff Airfield in the past. Alfred identifies three possible safe houses; Gordon settles on Chandler Arms, where he assigns Detective Renée Montoya as lead with Flass as her partner—a humiliating demotion for the corrupt detective.

Meanwhile, Eddie visits "Professor" Strange (Edward Nygma/Riddler), who provides a super-strength serum for a "human trial" against Batman. The serum is used to enhance a thug who attacks Chandler Arms. Flass and Montoya fight back while Gregory flees, but the subtitles cut away before the rescue is shown. Gregory survives to testify, quoting Thorne as saying, "The law is whatever I decide it is." Despite this, Thorne's defense argues GCPD corruption tainted the evidence, and the judge dismisses all charges. Thorne walks free, publicly declaring victory while Eddie tells his son, "We didn't beat them, son. We own them," then laughs hysterically—unsettling the boy.

---

## Important Character Actions

- **Batman** actively investigates to protect Gregory, demonstrating detective work and reaching out to Barbara, but does not appear in the on-screen attack at Chandler Arms (his intervention is implied).
- **Barbara Gordon** is caught between loyalty to her father and her alliance with Batman; she provides partial information but refuses to spy on Jim, insisting Batman find Gregory "without using me."
- **James Gordon** is weary but principled, recognizing the case is shaky yet determined to protect Gregory; he pairs Montoya with Flass, asserting Montoya as lead.
- **Renée Montoya** takes command of the safe-house detail, calling for backup during the attack and coordinating with Flass.
- **Arnold Flass** is humiliated—demoted to partner under Montoya, a consequence of losing his shield after the Dent shooting.
- **Rupert Thorne** remains confidently menacing from prison, revealing he owns the judge and can find Gregory "anytime, anywhere."
- **Eddie** is incompetent but dangerous, willing to deploy an untested super-soldier serum to assassinate a witness.
- **Hattie Tetch** (Mad Hatter) uses her TV platform to expose the weakness of the case and name Gregory publicly, acting as an unwitting catalyst for the assassination attempt.

## S02E02

**S02E02 – Batman: Caped Crusader**

## Episode Plot

Triad Industries (三联工业) has developed a revolutionary power source called **Ultrium** (超能元素) — a clean, inexhaustible element that generates near-unlimited energy from even the smallest amounts — along with rocket technology intended to launch humanity into a space-faring future. The genius behind the work is **Dr. Roxy Sutton** (罗茜·萨顿博士), who was reported killed in a lab explosion six months ago.

Bruce Wayne becomes intrigued by the project after learning that Triad’s CEOs (Warren Lawford, Armand Lydecker, and Sal Hardwick) had previously been turned away by Wayne Enterprises for funding. He decides to revisit the investment, and Lucius Fox arranges a meeting. At Triad, Bruce is shown a working rocket prototype powered by a single brick-sized cube of Ultrium, and is invited to a live demonstration that weekend.

Meanwhile, Batman has been tracking a string of attacks on Triad facilities — five in total over the past year, including the train carrying Ultrium and the company’s records library. The explosions are caused by small, precise blasts consistent with Sutton’s own **rocket pistol** (火箭手枪). Batman deduces that Sutton is alive, that she faked her own death, and that she has been waging a one-woman vendetta to destroy everything Triad stole from her.

Batman tracks Sutton to the final Triad testing facility, where she plans to sabotage the rocket launch and bankrupt the company. He confronts her and learns the truth: Sutton discovered that the CEOs were planning to sell her designs to foreign military powers — essentially turning her work into weapons of perpetual war. When she confronted them, they tried to kill her. She survived and has been methodically destroying Triad ever since.

At the demonstration, Lawford orders the rocket launched early despite Sutton’s interference. One CEO, Lydecker, shows a flicker of conscience and refuses to have blood on his hands, but Lawford overrules him. The launch proceeds.

In the aftermath, Bruce tells Alfred he is taking possession of Sutton’s work, believing it could be transformative in the right hands.

## Important Character Actions

- **Bruce Wayne** takes an unusually active role in Wayne Enterprises, initiating the Triad review and attending the meeting personally.
- **Batman** investigates the Triad attacks with Alfred, correctly deduces Sutton’s survival, and tracks her to the final target. He attempts to stop her non-lethally and hears her side of the story before the confrontation escalates.
- **Dr. Roxy Sutton** is revealed as a driven inventor whose idealism was betrayed; she uses her own weapons to systematically dismantle Triad’s infrastructure.
- **Lucius Fox** acts as the gatekeeper of Wayne assets and facilitates the Triad connection, showing his characteristic dry professionalism.
- **Alfred** provides both logistical support (remote detective work) and moral commentary, questioning Bruce’s decision to take control of Sutton’s legacy.

## Newly Established Names and Terms

- **Ultrium (超能元素)** — the fictional miracle fuel that powers the rocket; clean, reusable, and nearly limitless.
- **Triad Industries (三联工业)** — the defense/energy corporation run by Lawford, Lydecker, and Hardwick.
- **Dr. Roxy Sutton (罗茜·萨顿博士)** — the brilliant inventor whose work forms the backbone of Triad’s rocket program.
- **Rocket Pistol (火箭手枪)** — Sutton’s signature weapon, capable of small, shaped explosions.
- **Gunther (冈瑟)** — Triad’s head of security, responsible for protecting the Ultrium transport and the test site.

## Continuity-Relevant Facts

- Bruce is shown moving beyond his usual playboy detachment and engaging directly with Wayne Enterprises’ business decisions, signaling growth in his civilian identity.
- The episode reinforces the series’ theme of institutional corruption: Triad’s CEOs are willing to sell dangerous technology to foreign powers, and the line between legitimate business and criminal enterprise is deliberately blurred.
- Batman’s methods remain non-lethal; he seeks to bring Sutton to justice rather than kill her, even when she is clearly dangerous.
- Sutton’s vendetta is framed as morally complex — her cause is just, but her methods are extreme, echoing the show’s recurring question of whether vigilantism can ever be righteous.
- The fate of the Ultrium technology is left in Bruce’s hands, potentially setting up future storylines involving the weaponization or weaponization-prevention of advanced energy sources.

## Jokes and References

- Alfred’s dry observation about the Triad explosion: *“That is the definition of a rum do.”*
- Bruce suggesting they screen Triad’s pitch reel in Wayne Manor’s theater, with Alfred making popcorn.
- The opening cold-open banter about homemade donuts — a small, humanizing detail in a noir world.
- Alfred’s deadpan responses throughout (“My notepad is at the ready,” “And so I gather, sir.”)

## Unresolved Threads

- **What happens to the rocket launch?** Lawford orders it to proceed early, but it is unclear whether Sutton’s sabotage or Batman’s intervention altered the outcome.
- **The fate of the Triad CEOs** — particularly Lawford and Lydecker — is left open. Lydecker’s moment of conscience may have consequences.
- **Who will control Ultrium?** Bruce claims it should be in the right hands, but Alfred’s pointed question (*“And whose hands would that be?”*) underscores the moral ambiguity. The technology could become a powerful asset or a dangerous liability in future episodes.
- **Sutton’s status** — whether she escapes, is captured, or dies in the aftermath is not resolved in the available subtitles.

## S02E05

# S02E05 — Summary

## Plot

Corrupt cop Jim Corrigan—who previously tried to murder Barbara Gordon for money—volunteers for Dr. James Peterson's "Osiris Project" at Blackgate Penitentiary, a longevity experiment that stops and restarts the heart to achieve resurrection and life extension. Bruce Wayne attends as a potential investor. The experiment appears to fail: Corrigan flatlines on the table, triggering a prison riot in which inmates believe they're being slaughtered like lab animals.

Corrigan reanimates as a decayed, zombie-like figure and begins systematically hunting down criminals he arrested whose cases fell apart due to insufficient evidence. He kills Vinny DeLeon (con artist who stole dozens of life savings), Roger Marshall (wife-murderer), and Freddy Borke (drug dealer), uttering only "Vengeance" to each. Batman pieces together the pattern with Barbara's help: all were Corrigan's arrests that ended in acquittal.

Batman warns Barbara she's also on the list—she "got away" when Corrigan's attempt to kill her failed. Corrigan corners Barbara but instead of killing her, he asks for forgiveness. He collapses and dies permanently before she can respond. Barbara is left shaken, uncertain whether she wanted revenge, closure, or something else entirely.

## Important Character Actions

- **Barbara Gordon** confronts her trauma from Corrigan's betrayal; refuses protection and faces him alone.
- **Renee Montoya** shields Barbara during the prison riot and tries to get her to safety.
- **Bruce Wayne** observes the Osiris Project firsthand, tempted by its potential.
- **Alfred** reminds Bruce of his mother while baking holiday biscuits; later muses on the ethics of life extension with Bruce.
- **Gordon** investigates the zombie-like killings and fields chaotic Christmas Eve dispatches.
- **Batman** tracks the connection between the victims and identifies Barbara as the next target.
- **Corrigan** completes his "unfinished business" as a reanimated corpse, then seeks the one thing vengeance couldn't give him.

## Newly Established Names / Terms

| Name / Term | Role |
|---|---|
| Dr. James Peterson | Head of the Osiris Project at Blackgate |
| Osiris Project | Longevity experiment: stop/restart heart to extend life by decades |
| Vinny DeLeon | Con artist who bilked dozens of people out of life savings |

## Continuity-Relevant Facts

- Corrigan's attempted murder of Barbara for money is established as past season/series canon.
- The Osiris resurrection technology exists in Gotham and works—at least temporarily.
- Bruce Wayne is actively considering investing in life-extension science.
- Alfred still calls Bruce "Master Bruce" (少爷) and recalls his mother warmly.
- Gordon's eggnog is a running family joke: terrible, but tradition.
- Barbara and Renee's protective dynamic continues; Renee is the one Barbara turns to after trauma.

## Jokes and References

- *A Christmas Carol* by Charles Dickens is read on-air by Houseman Welch—a direct thematic parallel to Corrigan's redemption arc.
- "Not tonight, Jacob Marley" — Welch cuts the broadcast short, acknowledging Corrigan is Gotham's own haunted ghost story.
- Gordon: "Christmas Eve's just like a full moon in this town. All the loonies come out."
- Barbara to Gordon: "Your eggnog is awful, Dad." / "And that's part of the tradition."
- Corrigan's first victim dies of sheer fright upon seeing Corrigan's face—"I can understand why Vinny croaked like that. That Corrigan guy didn't seem human."

## Unresolved Threads

- **The Osiris Project**: The technology works. Dr. Peterson's fate and whether the project continues are unknown. Other parties (criminals, corrupt officials, Bruce himself) may seek to exploit it.
- **Barbara's emotional state**: She ends the episode saying she's "not okay but thinks I will be." Her path to processing Corrigan's final plea—and her own conflicted relief at his death—remains open.
- **Redemption vs. justice**: Corrigan killed innocents and guilty alike to settle scores. Whether his death absolves him or simply ends the story is left ambiguous.
- **Bruce's interest in longevity**: Alfred warned against it, but Bruce's "who decides how to use that?" suggests he hasn't ruled it out.
- **Corrigan's unfinished list**: He was cut down mid-mission. Whether there are other names on his mental list is unknown.

## S02E06

**S02E06 — "The Fear" (Scarecrow debut)**

Five Gotham University students have died in five weeks, each hallucinating invisible threats—spiders, rats, clowns—before dying in a panic. Batman identifies the pattern as a controlled field test: limited pool (students), consistent age range (19–25), identical symptoms. Someone is testing a fear-inducing chemical weapon on campus.

**Key plot points:**
- Barbara Gordon asks Renée Montoya to find a psychiatrist for a client; Renée laments the loss of Harleen Quinzel (presumed dead) and complains about being saddled with the corrupt Detective Flass as a partner.
- Bruce Wayne attends a Gotham U endowment photo op while the GCPD keeps the deaths out of the press.
- Montoya and Flass investigate victim Thomas Dalton, who died screaming about spiders that weren't there. The morgue is later broken into—Dalton's body stolen.
- Batman tracks down Harleen, who has been hiding in a secret apartment with Renée's protection. He brings her into the case.
- The victims all share one connection: Professor Charles Burton (chemistry). His student files include photos of the dead and research titled "alkaline-based phobic instigator agent."
- A female student, Jane Overstreet, is targeted next after rebuffing Burton's sexual advances. Jane hallucinates fire in her sorority house and dies jumping into a lake.
- Harleen is also exposed to the gas at the scene. Batman pulls her back by insisting her visions aren't real. Harleen remembers that milk neutralizes alkaline compounds.
- Flass deduces the killer uses a gas weapon and a protective costume.
- Burton's teaching assistant is revealed to be **Jonathan Crane, Jr.** (son of Harleen's own psychology professor). Crane helped develop the fear chemical but Burton aborted the project on ethical grounds. Feeling abandoned and unmoored, Crane continued the field test alone.
- Batman confronts Crane at Gotham U. Crane tries to identify Batman's phobia—guessing agoraphobia, heliophobia, chiroptophobia (fear of bats). Batman overpowers him with "No... fear!" and exposes Crane to his own gas.
- After the case closes, Harleen leaves Renée a note and departs: "Keeping my secret is drowning you... Time to wake up."

**New names/terms:**
- **Jonathan Crane, Jr.** — debut of **Scarecrow** (稻草人), a teaching assistant and son of Harleen's psychology professor
- **Professor Charles Burton** — chemistry professor who co-developed then abandoned the fear agent
- **"Alkaline-based phobic instigator agent"** — the fear gas formula
- **Jane Overstreet** — sorority student targeted after rejecting Burton
- Phobia terms used as dialogue texture: *gelotophobia* (fear of ridicule), *autophobia* (fear of isolation), *chiroptophobia* (fear of bats)
- Milk as an emergency antidote for the alkaline compound

**Continuity-relevant facts:**
- Harleen Quinzel remains legally dead and in hiding; Renée is actively harboring her
- Montoya is now forced to work with Flass, deepening her frustration with GCPD corruption
- Barbara Gordon is a public defender who still needs psychiatric expert referrals
- Bruce uses Wayne Foundation public appearances as cover for case-related travel
- Crane's father (Jonathan Crane Sr.) was Harleen's psychology teacher—linking Scarecrow to Harley's pre-villain academic past
- The GCPD is suppressing press coverage of the student deaths on the university's behalf

**Jokes / references:**
- Harleen's therapy-booth asides: "Overeating won't solve your marital issues, Herman" / "If there's one thing I'm an expert at... it's hiding."
- Flass: "Talkin' to these kids is like watchin' grass grow."
- Batman's "Fear is never in control!" is a direct rebuttal to Crane's entire worldview—classic Batman willpower trumping chemical fear.
- Crane guessing *chiroptophobia* as Batman's deepest fear lands with heavy irony given the cape.

**Unresolved threads:**
- Where does Harleen go? ("My ride's here"—who is picking her up, and is she leaving Gotham?)
- Does Crane survive his own gas, or is he remanded to Arkham?
- Is Burton arrested as an accomplice, or does he walk?
- Montoya's forced partnership with Flass remains a simmering friction point with Commissioner Gordon.

## S02E08


## Episode Plot

Rupert Thorne is murdered by a professional hitman who disposes of his body in Gotham Lake, following a client's order to make it look like an accident with "no holes, no cuts, no marks." Bruce Wayne and Alfred note that the Riddler's death should not end Thorne's vendetta against Gotham — suggesting Thorne is either dead or kidnapped. GCPD recovers Thorne's empty sedan from the lake with no sign of violence.

Meanwhile, ten-year-old Carrie Kelly — a rebellious orphan living with her kind foster parents, the Carsons — is repeatedly caught breaking curfew. She idolizes the Gray Ghost (a fictional pulp hero) and has adopted the vigilante persona "Crime Smasher." She sneaks out to investigate a delivery truck that wrecked a kid's bike, tracking it to a hideout on Wallace Wood Drive through Old Gotham.

Batman is independently investigating the same truck. He finds Carrie at the hideout and orders her to leave, dismissing her desire to be his partner. Before she can go, **Mr. Zero** (Victor Fries / Mr. Freeze) arrives with henchmen. He speaks through an electronic voice and is an artiste obsessed with creating perfect frozen specimens. He sees Batman as his "masterpiece" because Batman's body has no holes or scars.

Batman and Carrie are captured and taken to Mr. Zero's lair — a cold storage warehouse filled with frozen human "statues" (real people preserved in his diorama). Mr. Zero plans to freeze Batman in liquid nitrogen, then shatter him. Carrie and Batman work together to escape; Carrie uses Batman's utility belt (Batarang) to save him, and they destroy Mr. Zero's collection in the process. Mr. Zero vows revenge, screaming that Batman will "pay for this."

Carrie escapes and sneaks back home just as Bullock arrives searching for her; the Carsons cover for her. The episode ends with Batman interrogating the hitman who killed Thorne. The hitman reveals the client hired "Mr. Zero" to make Thorne into "art" — but before he can name the client, the subtitles cut off.

## Important Character Actions

- **Batman** investigates the Thorne disappearance with Alfred, narrows down delivery companies to find the truck, refuses Carrie's help initially, and later accepts it during the escape. He interrogates the hitman at the end.
- **Carrie Kelly** is established as a foster child who breaks curfew to play detective. She has a "Crime Smasher" alter ego inspired by the Gray Ghost. She saves Batman's life during the Mr. Zero escape, proving her resourcefulness despite her age.
- **Alfred** assists with research (flight manifests, delivery company logistics) and maintains his dry wit.
- **Mr. Zero / Victor Fries** debuts as a cold, theatrical villain who freezes people and displays them as art. He views Batman as the ultimate specimen.
- **Officer Bullock** harasses Carrie for breaking curfew, showing his disdain for orphans. He later questions the Carsons.
- **The Carsons** (Mr. and Mrs. Carrie) are caring foster parents who are unaware of Carrie's nighttime activities.

| Mr. Zero / Mr. Freeze | Victor Fries; speaks through an electronic voice; freezes people as "art" for his diorama |
| Carrie Kelly | Ten-year-old orphan, foster child of the Carsons; first female Robin |

| The Gray Ghost | Fictional pulp hero whose movies Carrie watches; quotes "The road of life takes many turns, but crime is a dead end" |

## Continuity-Relevant Facts

- Rupert Thorne is confirmed dead (murdered, not kidnapped). The hitman cleaned up the body per a client's orders.
- Mr. Zero is revealed as the artist who froze Thorne's body — "Mr. Zero makes his art" — connecting the Thorne murder to a new villain.
- Carrie Kelly is introduced as a potential Robin figure; she has already met Batman before ("I saved your life").
- The Carsons are established as Carrie's foster family; she lies to them about her nighttime activities.
- Batman's resistance to a partner is shown to be weakening — he accepts Carrie's help when it matters.
- The client who hired Thorne's murder remains unknown, creating a conspiracy thread.

## Jokes and References

- The hitman complains about "no holes, no cuts, no marks" while his partner points out that "you air a guy out, there's gonna be holes" — dark comedy about the cleanup job.
- Carrie mocks Bullock by calling him "Mr. Bolluck" (misspelling his name) and says "I'll show you juvie, Bullock!"
- Carrie uses Gray Ghost catchphrases while playing detective: "The road of life takes many turns, but crime is a dead end."
- Mr. Zero's theatrical self-image as an "artiste" contrasts with his henchmen's crude behavior; they call him "boss" while he demands artistic respect.
- Carrie calls herself "Crime Smasher" with complete sincerity; Batman is unimpressed.
- Bullock's line about orphans: "These orphans, they're all half-criminals" / "See if that orphanage has a return policy."

## Unresolved Threads

- **Who hired the hitman to kill Rupert Thorne?** The hitman is about to reveal the client's name when the subtitles cut off. This is the episode's cliffhanger and ties into the season's larger conspiracy.
- **Mr. Zero's fate:** He escapes after his lair is destroyed and vows revenge. His whereabouts are unknown.
- **Carrie's future as Crime Smasher:** Batman has not accepted her as a partner, but she has proven herself. Her continued vigilantism will likely bring her back into Batman's orbit.
- **The GCPD mole:** Who leaked information about Thorne's location or movements to the hitman? This connects to the ongoing GCPD corruption arc.
- **Thorne's power vacuum:** With Thorne dead, who fills the criminal power gap in Gotham? This likely drives future episodes.

## S02E09

## S02E09 — Summary

## Plot

The episode opens with Hattie Tetch (Mad Hatter) hosting her live television talk show, *Hattie's Tea Party*, enthusiastically promoting Gotham's upcoming World's Fair. Mid-broadcast, Tetch suffers a sudden seizure-like attack and collapses, dying on air with a horrible grin frozen on her face.

The Joker hijacks the television signal and issues an ultimatum to Gotham: Mayor Jessop will die at midnight, and all citizens will perish before dawn. It is revealed the Joker has already murdered Tetch's entire production staff. Batman responds to the threat while Commissioner Gordon organizes protective custody for the mayor, planning to move him to the Tricorner safe house under Detective Montoya's lead.

Meanwhile, Barbara Gordon identifies a submerged body as Barry Vance, a client missing for over six months — found with the same ghastly grin as Tetch. This confirms the killer has been operating in Gotham for months.

Mayor Jessop overrules Gordon's security plan, demanding the corrupt Detective Flass handle his protection instead of Montoya. Flass takes Jessop to an undisclosed, off-the-books location. The Joker's men attack the site, leading to a shootout. Despite being in custody, Jessop dies at the exact minute the Joker predicted — poisoned beforehand with a toxin of extraordinary precision.

Batman investigates and discovers the Joker had been using the Riddler's leftover crew as muscle before murdering them all — sixteen bodies are found at Stumper's nightclub on Stark Street. Alfred performs autopsies on Tetch and the others, finding injection marks. The episode ends with a forensic discovery: something found under a victim's fingernails, shouted as a potential identity clue.

## Important Character Actions

- **Batman** — Investigates the murders, identifies the connection to the Riddler's crew, tracks the Joker's movements, and works with Alfred to analyze the crime scenes.
- **Alfred Pennyworth** — Conducts the autopsies, discovers injection marks on the victims, mentions Mr. Zero (Victor Fries) froze Rupert Thorne's remains, and makes the final discovery under the fingernails.
- **Barbara Gordon** — Identifies Barry Vance's body, deduces the killer has been in Gotham for months, and reports her findings to her father.
- **James Gordon** — Attempts to organize the mayor's protection but is overruled by Jessop; frustrated by GCPD corruption and Flass's interference.
- **Renée Montoya** — Is pushed aside from the mayor's security detail when Jessop insists on Flass.
- **Arnold Flass** — Takes over Jessop's protection, moves him off the books, and is present when Jessop dies.
- **Mayor Jessop** — Rejects Gordon's security plan, trusts Flass, and pays with his life.
- **The Joker** — Murders Tetch on live television, hijacks the broadcast, issues an ultimatum, eliminates Riddler's henchmen, and executes Jessop with a precisely timed toxin.

## S02E04

## S02E04 – The Cult of Barbatos

### Episode Plot

A small-time thief is found murdered in a Gotham museum—beaten by multiple attackers and stabbed, with a bat symbol drawn in his blood. The killing is staged to frame Batman. Hattie Tetch, a popular TV talk-show host, seizes the moment on *Hattie's Tea Party* to publicly denounce Batman as a murderer and excoriate the GCPD for tolerating a vigilante.

Commissioner Gordon agrees to appear on Hattie's program to defend the department. Before the taping, a fanatical cult devoted to Barbatos—a demon bat god said to slumber beneath Gotham—kidnaps both Gordon and Hattie from Monoscope Pictures. The cult, led by a figure known as the Mad Monk, believes Batman is Barbatos's prophesied avatar and intends the pair as the final sacrifices needed to summon the entity.

Batman traces the cult to its lair in St. Bartholomew's Church, fights through the zealots, and rescues Gordon and Hattie. In the confrontation, the Mad Monk is unmasked as a cynical con artist who never believed in Barbatos himself; he exploited the cult's faith to accumulate wealth and power. He offers Batman a cut of the operation. Batman declines. The Mad Monk is taken into custody.

The episode closes with Hattie back on her show, publicly thancing Gordon and Batman for the rescue while simultaneously blaming Batman for inspiring the cult in the first place.

### Important Character Actions

- **Batman** — Investigates the museum murder, identifies the cult connection, tracks the kidnappers to St. Bartholomew's Church, and rescues Gordon and Hattie. Confirms his no-kill rule when confronting the Mad Monk.
- **James Gordon** — Voluntarily subjects himself to a hostile TV interview to defend the GCPD's integrity. Survives the kidnapping and fights alongside Batman.
- **Hattie Tetch** — Uses her media platform to attack Batman and the police. After her rescue, she immediately returns to the airwaves, reframing the narrative to suit her agenda.
- **Mad Monk** — Leads the Barbatos cult, orchestrates the kidnapping, and is ultimately exposed as a fraud exploiting religious fervor. Captured by Batman.
- **Renée Montoya** — Coordinates the police response and is present at the church for the takedown.

### Newly Established Names / Terms

- **Barbatos (巴巴托斯)** — A demon bat god from occult myth; the object of the cult's worship. Believed to rise when sufficient treasure and blood are sacrificed.
- **Mad Monk (疯僧)** — The cult's charismatic leader; a con artist posing as a holy man. Gender-bent reinterpretation of the comic-book villain.
- **St. Bartholomew's Church (圣巴塞洛缪教堂)** — The cult's lair and the site of the climactic rescue.
- **Monoscope Pictures (单镜影业)** — The film studio where *Hattie's Tea Party* is taped; the location of the kidnapping.
- **"Avatar" (化身)** — The cult's term for the prophesied champion of Barbatos; they identify Batman as this figure.

### Continuity-Relevant Facts

- Hattie Tetch is this series' gender-bent version of the Mad Hatter, reimagined as a 1940s-style TV host rather than a hat-obsessed criminal.
- Batman explicitly states he does not kill, reinforcing a core character rule.
- The supernatural elements of the series are played ambiguously: Barbatos is treated as a myth by Linton Midnite and by the Mad Monk, yet the cult's devotion is real and lethal.
- Gotham's media landscape is a weapon; Hattie demonstrates how easily public opinion can be manipulated through television.

### Jokes and References

- "Hattie's Tea Party" is a direct nod to the Mad Hatter's tea-party mythology.
- Hattie's line about "mindless sheep who want what I tell them to want" is a self-aware jab at media manipulation.
- The Mad Monk's line "Alas, cultists tend to get a bit zealous" is delivered with dry, self-deprecating charm.
- Montoya's deadpan "Why am I not surprised?" when the Mad Monk tries to recruit Batman.

### Unresolved Threads

- The Barbatos cult is not fully dismantled; several cultists are still chanting at the episode's end.
- Hattie Tetch continues her media crusade, now arguing that Batman's existence breeds the very lunacy he fights.
- The ontological status of Barbatos remains ambiguous—whether it is a real supernatural entity or purely a myth exploited by grifters is left open.
- The Mad Monk's arrest removes the immediate threat, but the cult's underground network and remaining true believers are unaccounted for.

## S02E03

# S02E03 — 深度潜伏 (Deep Undercover)

## Episode Plot

Bruce Wayne goes undercover in Blackgate Penitentiary as "Match" (火柴马龙), a small-time Detroit wheel-man, to befriend captured gangster Mark Desmond and find out who has taken over Rupert Thorne's criminal empire. Inside, Desmond survives four assassination attempts — which Match helps him escape — and the two bond. Desmond reveals he once ran with a partner from juvie who became the Riddler (Edward Nygma), whom he later introduced to Thorne; the Riddler is now nearly the #2 in the organization. Match engineers a jailbreak, and Desmond brings him to Eddie, the new boss. Eddie is planning a jewelry-store heist and recruits them. At the job, Eddie reveals he has deduced that Match is Batman, then reveals his own identity: Edward Nygma, the Riddler. Batman escapes, and later tells Alfred the situation is chaotic but may simplify once Thorne returns to power.

## Important Character Actions

- **Bruce Wayne / Batman**: Creates a false identity (Detroit driver, record of robbery/fraud/theft), gets himself transferred to Blackgate, cultivates Desmond, breaks out, infiltrates the gang, and is unmasked by Eddie.
- **Mark Desmond**: The only living gang member captured since Thorne's trial; refuses to inform; trusts Match after repeated life-saving favors; vouches for him with Eddie.
- **Eddie / Edward Nygma / Riddler**: Now running Thorne's operation; impulsive, violent, and unpredictable; dismisses the idea that someone wants Desmond dead; figures out Batman's identity mid-heist.
- **Alfred Pennyworth**: Prepares the fake identity papers and transfer from Wayne Manor to Blackgate; debriefs Bruce afterward.
- **Gordon, Montoya, Flass**: Appear in the cold open arguing over Desmond's silence and the tripling of gang murders since Thorne's arrest.

## Newly Established Names / Terms

- **Match / 火柴马龙** — Bruce Wayne's undercover alias; a Detroit wheel-guy with a robbery record.
- **Mark Desmond** — Old-school gangster, old friend of the Riddler, the only live catch from Thorne's crew.
- **Edward Nygma / Eddie / Riddler (谜语人)** — New gang boss; violent, erratic, impulsive; not the cerebral puzzle-villain of other canons.
- **Rocco** — Dead gang member whose killing sparked unrest in the ranks.
- **Tony, Vic, Johnny** — Rank-and-file gang members under Eddie.

## Continuity-Relevant Facts

- Rupert Thorne is missing/on the run; his absence has tripled gang murders and sparked two bombings and a foiled kidnapping.
- The Riddler is introduced as a gangster, not a puzzle-obsessed mastermind — consistent with the series background note that he is a "violent gangster reinterpretation."
- Eddie is described as impulsive, reckless, and "extremely unpredictable" — a sharp contrast to Thorne's calculated control.
- Batman notes the chaos is temporary; things may get simpler when Thorne returns — implying Thorne is still alive and expected back.
- Barbara Gordon appears briefly; Flass is confirmed as a corrupt cop on the gang's side.
- The Riddler was introduced to Thorne by Desmond and rose to nearly #2 in the organization.

## Jokes / References

- **Opening gag**: "When is a pineapple not a pineapple? When it's a grenade." — a throwaway joke before the grenade goes off.
- **"Match is a stupid name"** — Eddie's first comment on the alias.
- **Leaky-boat metaphor** — Eddie tells his crew the way to fix a leaky boat is to plug the hole (kill the weak link).
- **Necklace gift** — Eddie promises his girlfriend a new necklace, a small humanizing detail before the heist.
- **Dramatic irony** — Eddie says "my best friend turns out to be the goddamn Batman," not realizing how literally true it is.

## Unresolved Threads

- Where is Rupert Thorne, and when will he return?
- Will Eddie seek revenge on Batman for the infiltration?
- What is the fallout of the jewelry-store heist — did Eddie escape, and what is his next move?
- Who ordered the hits on Desmond inside Blackgate, and why?
- Will the gang's escalating violence (bombings, kidnappings) continue to spiral under Eddie?
- How will Batman use the intelligence that Eddie is the new boss?
