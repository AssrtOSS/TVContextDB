## S15E07

## 剧情概述

汉克和戴尔为解决鸡蛋短缺买来十只母鸡，并在后院搭建鸡舍。布姆豪尔带着儿子小卢克参与其中，原本被过度保护、缺少朋友的卢克逐渐投入养鸡和FFA活动。

其中一只母鸡伯特鲁德出现罕见的公鸡性状。卢克因其他孩子质疑伯特鲁德的身份而与人打架，遭到停学。布姆豪尔带他参加公鸡打鸣比赛，汉克则据理力争，最终卢克获准继续留在父亲身边，也可以养伯特鲁德。

与此同时，鲍比帮助康妮适应大学篮球队的吉祥物工作。康妮因害怕失败而逃避上场，鲍比多次替她表演，后来指出她一直在利用自己。两人和解后，鲍比鼓励康妮亲自上场，并接受表现不佳的可能性。

## 重要人物行动

- 汉克负责设计、建造鸡舍，并把养鸡变成卢克的FFA项目。
- 戴尔擅自买来十只母鸡，持续从希尔家拿鸡蛋制作蛋白奶昔。
- 布姆豪尔努力与小卢克培养感情，最终带他参加公鸡打鸣比赛。
- 小卢克首次主动参与劳动、交友和公开竞争，也为伯特鲁德与人争执。
- 鲍比先后替康妮完成吉祥物表演，后来拒绝继续被利用，并帮助她克服恐惧。
- 康妮承认自己不擅长吉祥物工作，最终亲自上场并学会面对失败。
- 旺达原本用“禁止清单”限制卢克的活动，结尾允许他继续与布姆豪尔相处并保留伯特鲁德。

## 新出现或需统一的名称、术语

- 小卢克（Luke Jr.）：布姆豪尔的儿子，本集主要儿童角色。
- 旺达（Wanda）：小卢克的母亲，制定严格“禁止清单”。
- 伯特鲁德（Bertrude）：原被当作母鸡饲养、后来显现公鸡特征的鸡；布姆豪尔改称其为“伯特”。
- 彗星特莫克（Temoc the Comet）：UT达拉斯篮球队吉祥物。
- FFA：美国未来农民协会（Future Farmers of America）。
- 灰熊加斯（Gus the Grizzly）：鲍比提到的另一名吉祥物，其“双竖大拇指”是标志动作。
- “禁止清单”（No-No list）：旺达为保护卢克制定的活动限制清单。

## S15E09

## 剧情概述

- 鲍比参加真人秀《烧烤之王》，与巴黎厨艺学校毕业生克莱尔搭档。两人在“百宝箱”挑战中用随机食材即兴烹饪，以日式“石烤”（ishiyaki）赢得首轮比赛。
- 节目制作人伊莎贝尔发现鲍比和克莱尔很有“化学反应”，要求他们整季炒作“成不成”的暧昧关系。克莱尔逐渐对鲍比产生好感，还在拍摄中亲吻并触摸他。
- 鲍比的女友康妮前来探班，目睹节目组把两人的互动包装成“美食色情片”，深感受辱并离开。鲍比最终在节目最后一场挑战中公开宣布自己真正爱的是康妮，并请求她搬来同住。康妮接受，两人正式迈向同居。
- 与此同时，戴尔开始磁铁钓鱼，和汉克、布姆豪尔在河里捞出一门南北战争时期的大炮。三人将大炮清理并准备捐给阿伦历史学会，却在戴尔怂恿下偷偷试射。
- 炮弹误中附近的德克萨斯巨人雕像，摧毁其手指，使雕像变成对全德州竖中指。三人连夜修复雕像，最终恢复原状，并决定让这段意外留在历史之中。

## 重要人物行动

- 鲍比展现出临场 improvisation 和烹饪才能，凭借石烤技巧赢下第一轮；面对节目炒作，最终选择公开维护康妮。
- 康妮原本只是陪鲍比参赛，却因目睹暧昧表演而离开；收到鲍比的公开告白后接受同居请求。
- 克莱尔把鲍比视为竞争对手后转为暧昧搭档，推动了节目中的恋爱炒作。
- 伊莎贝尔和节目组刻意制造“鲍比—克莱尔”恋爱叙事，以换取更多镜头和收视率。
- 汉克负责大炮的清理、炮架制作和事故善后，最后承认戴尔不适合单独做决定。

- 汉克、戴尔、布姆豪尔的后巷友情因“兄弟阋墙”和事故短暂紧张，最终重新团结。

## S15E05

# S15E05 — Summary

## Plot

Two parallel storylines unfold: Bobby's pursuit of a television cooking show, and Hank and Peggy's experience at a church marriage retreat.

### Bobby's TV Audition

Bobby Hill learns that the producers of a cooking show called *King of the Grill* were impressed by his initial presentation and are sending a crew to film an audition tape. Hank boasts that money is no problem and plans an extravagant meal featuring matsutake mushrooms, red tuna belly, and premium wagyu beef.

At the restaurant Robata Chane, where Bobby works, the audition is filmed. Bobby attempts to cook robata-style dishes but comes across as dull and flat. Producer Isabel tells him he is boring and needs to be more entertaining if the tape is to impress the investors in Burbank.

Bobby returns the next day and, during the shoot, loses his temper — screaming at his staff in a furious rant. The producers are thrilled. Isabel says America loves a villain and offers him a spot on the show as "Bobby the Damn" (Bobby el Maldito). However, Bobby realizes he genuinely cares about his employees and does not want to become a cruel person just for television fame. He declines the offer.

The producers then reframe the pitch: Bobby the Kid — a 21-year-old running his own successful restaurant. Bobby accepts this version and returns to work, relieved to be himself again.

### The Marriage Retreat

Hank and Peggy attend a church marriage retreat where they hope to serve as mentors, with Peggy boasting of her professional teaching background and perfect marriage. Instead, the reverend assigns them menial tasks: Hank does light carpentry and maintenance, while Peggy is asked to help prepare and serve food.

Dale and Nancy Gribble lead the marriage ministry. Dale uses a live rat named "Problemas de Dinero" (Money Problems) to illustrate his theory of the "five pests of marriage." Peggy openly criticizes their qualifications, revealing to the reverend that Nancy has been cheating on Dale for twenty years — a fact everyone in the neighborhood knows except Dale himself.

Shocked, Reverend Stroup expels Dale and Nancy from the retreat and forces Hank and Peggy to take over as marriage mentors. During the sessions, Hank and Peggy discover their own communication skills are lacking. Peggy admits she sometimes wishes she had gone to college and pursued teaching full-time, while Hank acknowledges he never considered how gender norms affected Peggy's choices.

The retreat culminates in a canoe exercise on a lake, where couples must work together to paddle and repair a leaking canoe. One canoe — representing Dale and Nancy's marriage — begins taking on water. Hank guides everyone to work together, using clothes to make a sail and towing the failing canoe back to shore.

The episode ends with Dale and Nancy reinstated as marriage mentors (now "Methodist marriage gurus"), while Hank sanders and varnishes benches as punishment. Nancy visits John Redcorn for a massage, and her headaches disappear; Dale notices nothing.

## Important Character Actions

- **Peggy** reveals Nancy's long-term affair to the reverend, showing her blunt, judgmental side
- **Dale** remains completely unaware of his wife's infidelity while teaching marriage counseling
- **Nancy** casually confesses her affair is common knowledge to everyone except Dale
- **John Redcorn** appears briefly — his relationship with Nancy continues unchanged

## S15E04

# S15E04 Summary

## Plot

Two parallel storylines unfold: Bobby's food truck venture at a sci-fi convention, and Peggy's poetry workshop that becomes an unexpected partnership with Minh.

### Bobby's Sci-Fi Convention

Bobby Hill rents a food truck called **Robata Chane**—a Japanese-German fusion concept featuring dishes like miso-wurst and yakatori schnitzel. He secures the last vendor spot at the **Dallas Sci-Fi Convention**, hoping to earn back the $3,000 rental cost. His father Hank, skeptical of sci-fi fandom, offers to help run the truck, boasting that customer relations are his "superpower." Hank purchases a $300 "professional pass" that Bobby claims grants full access to all convention areas.

Business is slow. A rival vendor—the Zulu Martians—draws away foot traffic. Hank attempts to drum up customers by dressing in a graphic T-shirt and handing off flyers, but he's awkward and out of place. When he tries to enter a restricted area called **Sala F**, his professional pass is stolen by a cosplayer dressed as a **Zolomarian Imperial Guard**. Hank attempts to retrieve it, first by confronting the cosplayer in broken "alien" speak (replacing profanity with silly nonsense words), then by rallying other attendees against the con for being "weirdos" and mocking them for having families who share his disdain. The crowd turns on Hank, chanting "Shame!" and Bobby loses any chance of attracting customers. Bobby tells Hank to go home.

Meanwhile, Dale attends the convention disguised as a "bug alien," claiming it's the perfect hiding place for a real alien to blend in. He says he's been collecting DNA samples from attendees. When Hank's pass is stolen, Dale tries to intervene but is restrained by event security, who search him while he protests the inconsistency.

Hank later returns bearing a peace offering: a "sacred battle axe of Lerio" he built in his garage. The axe is remarkably detailed—complete with blaster burns from the "Battle of Flyon 15" and what Hank calls Zolomarian hieroglyphics (actually Pavronian, the Zolomarians' ancestors). The convention-goers are impressed, and Bobby's business picks up as fans line up to take photos with the axe. Bobby and Hank reconcile, and Hank ends the day geeking out over the upcoming prequel trilogy midnight premiere.

### Peggy's Poetry Workshop

Peggy collects her old newspaper columns—**"Peggy's Musings"**—to submit to a poetry workshop at **Arlen Community College**, taught by **Marina Butler**, who edits a Texas poetry journal called **"Susurros" (Whispers)** and makes greeting cards. Peggy assumes she'll dominate the class, but Minh enrolls as well, seeking a creative outlet while going through her divorce from Kahn. Minh insults Peggy, saying if "a hick like her" can write poetry, anyone can.

The class style involves snapping fingers instead of clapping. Marina's exercises ask students to write about "people, places, and things" and to let life inspire them. Minh's work is dark—she writes about death and revenge, comparing despair to a soufflé made of misery without flour. Peggy's work is lighter but equally shallow—"Hope is like looking at stained glass; it costs nothing" and "The higher the hair, the closer to God; well, my truck has a lift kit, so hallelujah!"

Marina recognizes their opposing styles as complementary: "You bring the light, you bring the shadow. Together, you forge 'shadow light.'" The two begin collaborating. Peggy takes full credit when Marina suggests her journal could publish them, claiming Minh's near-publication is thanks to her. Minh storms out, furious at being used. She later reveals she had a date at **Capital Shrimp and Steak**—her first in years—but it went badly after a humiliating fight with Kahn that Peggy had inadvertently caused.

Minh returns to class and reads a poem about betrayal that wins the group's admiration. Marina publishes it for $20. Peggy and Minh argue over splitting the money—Peggy insists on dividing it evenly; Minh demands half of Peggy's half. The episode ends with them bickering but still together, while Kahn jealously plots to kill Minh's mystery date and Bill obliviously enjoys Kahn's soup parties.

## S15E01

# S15E01 Summary

## Plot

Two parallel storylines unfold: the mystery of an electric pickup truck parked on Rainey Street, and Bobby's romantic rivalry with his restaurant owner for Connie's affection.

### The Electric Truck Mystery

An electric pickup truck parks in front of Hank's house overnight, blocking the house number on the curb. Hank is mortified — not just by the aesthetic offense, but because neighbors might think he, a propane man, owns an electric vehicle.
