# Below — S01E01 Summary

## Episode Plot

The episode opens on a traumatic flashback: a young Calvin Penney watches in horror as the towline snags, his father "Leo" calls out in panic ("Don't lean out so far," "Cut the towline!"), and Leo is dragged into the water along with the family house. Voices cry for the line to be cut before it pulls them under — the trauma that has haunted Calvin ever since.

The action jumps to the present. Calvin scrapes a living harvesting small icebergs for the Crockers' premium iceberg-vodka brand. Stan Crocker rejects today's "growler" — a salinity test reads 1,190 ppm, "bad ice," and the berg Calvin hauled has a strange slime and an ammonia stench. Stan refuses to buy; Calvin argues for a discount.

The core civic conflict is laid out at a Snooks Arm town meeting: the province is offering a one-time $140,000 (potentially negotiated up to $150,000) relocation fee per resident to vote in favor of resettlement to Gander, Lewisporte, or elsewhere. Ruth Penney, speaking for the old guard, resists: the Penneys were already displaced once from Little Pot Rock, and their grandfather drowned saving the family house. The vote needs 90% of the cove in favor — and the province is lowering the threshold to 75%.

Calvin drives to the Ocean Science Center at Seal Cove to consult Dr. Fonda Howander about the bad ice. Fonda is clinical, outsider, and pressed for time: she is currently puzzling over a flamingo that landed on Maggie Toup's roof at Logy Bay (Toup shot it). She examines Calvin's sample — the iceberg contains a "frozen mucus membrane" loaded with organic material, a possible protective coating from "something big and old." She notes the unusual weather is changing migration patterns and ocean currents, and a major Greenland glacial collapse is imminent.

Meanwhile, Wade has converted the family home into an Airbnb hosting mainland guests Keith and Candice-Marie. A "Come Home Year" (the province-wide family reunion) brings Calvin's adult children home: Mary arrives with her partner Noah from Montreal, and after a Calgary layover drinking with the Hibernia rig crew, Jason "Jay" Penney — Calvin's high-earning offshore son — finally shows up. Jay announces the current resettlement offer is up to $140,000.

Mary and Noah, taking a late-night walk, find Calvin brooding in his shed. They gently turn him down when he pitches his big idea: he wants to apply for a commercial ice-harvesting license, scrap his dependence on the Crockers, get a bigger boat, maybe one day open a brewery, and keep Snooks Arm "on the map." It would require the whole family working together. Mary has just signed a lease in Montreal for Noah's music career; Jay is making too much money on the rigs to give it up. Calvin retreats alone to his shed with his "Blue Stars."

That night, Fonda visits Wade's place to ask Calvin about a long-buried detail: in the original coast-guard report on his father's accident, the boy Calvin said he saw something in the water — shadows. Calvin deflects — "friggin' kelp, fata morgana" — but Fonda tells him she is gathering reports of strange incidents in these waters.

The climax: at the harbor, Jay and Noah take a borrowed boat out with a deckhand named Jerry to harvest from a particularly massive iceberg. While Jay chases Noah's clout-chasing shots at the bow, something enormous catches on their cable. The winch screams, the line wraps around the boat, and the crew frantically scrambles for the kill switch. The episode freezes on them fighting the cable in panic as the boat is pulled toward the berg.

## Plot

The episode cuts between four interlocked threads while the town braces for the creature.

Town hall. A local "Steve" restores order and hands the floor to Dr. Howander (Fonda), who briefs Snook's Arm on the recent losses: the sinking of the Santa Cristina off Fogo, Jerry Biles's death in Snooks Arm, and two tourists killed in Deep Bay. She floats Architeuthis dux — North Atlantic giant squid — as the working hypothesis and orders everyone at least 50 feet back from the shore. Calvin Penney publicly rejects her, points out that it was "you crowd of scientists" who told the government to shut down the cod fishery in '92, insists he saw the thing rip Jerry Biles in two and nearly take his son Wade, and announces he'll "do it meself."

The conservation ship. Calvin — still recovering from a recent dunking, scar on his arm, classic chronic-wasting symptoms (splitting headache, no sleep, bone-deep thirst, "the hag" visions) — is being dried off aboard a 120-ft, 300-ton, 5,000-nm-endurance vessel bristling with sonar and satellite gear. The crew (Steve and a partner) insist they're "conservationists" running a climate-awareness mission. Calvin retells the Jerry Biles run — pot-shotting at harbor "ice," a bump from something enormous, blackout, Biles and boat "ripped in two." Fonda's colleague explains the working theory: a previously undetected cephalopod whose gelatinous ink acts like chronic-wasting prions (caribou drown themselves seeking water) and carries a psychoactive component producing the hag visions — "like the toads in the Amazon." Calvin confirms he's swallowed some of it. They ask him to find a local hire; Calvin steers them to Wade. Calvin phones Air Canada — 45- to 55-minute waits — and mentions a missing heirloom diamond ring from his grandmother.

Calvin vs. Ruth. Mary confronts Calvin about going out alone and offers to come; he insists this is solo work and tells her to go home to Noah. He then storms into Ruth's office hunting his engine coil. She refuses to hand it over and threatens to impound his boat. He accuses the creature of being "the same one that took the house, the same one that took Dad" — confirming the family has been haunted by it for generations. Mary sides with Ruth and tells Calvin he's losing it; he fires back that the pieces are "finally starting to make a bit of sense." Ruth reveals she is voting to leave with Phonse and Noelle and tells him to keep the coil and "fuck off. Forever." Calvin's last local option, Paddy at the bar, also refuses — won't cross the Department of Fisheries or Calvin's sister.

Mary and Noah. At Linda's, Mary and her partner Noah commiserate. He can't wait to leave; she feels the strain goes past the creature. Jan greets Mary warmly, and Mary slips out ("gotta scoot back home"). Gossip surfaces that Mary is sleeping with Jay — and that she's "trying way too hard."

Wade gets hired behind Calvin's back. Wade has signed on as the crew's local navigator; Steve warns him to keep Calvin sober for tomorrow. Calvin arrives at the dock, sees the ballistic harpoon system, and calls the mission what it is: "you fellas are out here hunting squid." The conservationists insist non-lethal. Calvin demands they take him instead of his boy, dropping the Titanic line about these waters being full of "fine crews" that didn't come home. The crew accepts. Wade explodes at Calvin, who tells him he's not well, "clammy in the face," and that these people "are not to be trusted."

The Officer. A conversation with the "resettlement person" reveals the official $60K payout for leaving, an attempted name to add to the list, and a quiet bribe — pieces of mail with a cove address — in exchange for being looked the other way. The official's parting shot: "Don't ever leave me waitin' in the dark again."

Fonda meets Dr. Sayed. Fonda corners her idol, Dr. Sayed — externally funded, runs a private-sector operation — aboard his research ship. She brings her iceberg-contaminant theory connecting the secretions to the cephalopod; he agrees, then reframes the whole case. This isn't Architeuthis dux. It's "Species A" — far older, far more dangerous, native to the deep Arctic. Warming oceans, thawing permafrost, and melting glaciers have pushed her south down the Labrador Current every few years, but this time she is staying and using icebergs as territorial markers. Toxicity depends on dose and route of exposure. He closes with the 1742 Low Arctic log: British sailors stranded in a blizzard took shelter inside a dead kraken carcass on the ice, then went mad, fought over tools, and tried to dig through the ice to drown themselves in the seawater below. One survivor sketched it. Fonda's reaction: "Fuck." She still says she's getting on a flight tonight.

Wade at Linda's / Jay's debt. At Linda's bar, Wade soaks up apologies to Mary — "the family's suffered enough" — and confirms he understands why she wants out. In a back booth, Jay is cornered by Fort Mac (Fort McMurray) heavies to whom he owes $80,000. He tries to stall with high-school transcripts and GST stubs as "proof of residency," claiming "a girl on the inside" will produce the money in six to eight weeks. The thugs rough him up, Calvin intervenes, and the crew warns "This isn't over, Jay." Calvin learns the full $80K figure and that Jay thought he was "making money hand over fist out there."

Final minutes. Linda tells Wade that Steve and his shipmate left a while ago — no tab, never had a drink, "what sailor comes to shore and doesn't have a drink?" Wade bolts. He reaches the vessel calling for "Barry," who is screaming "Water! Water! Water!" and being held back. The episode cuts on the chaos.

## S01E04

Episode plot:

The RSV Sir John Franklin continues hunting Species A near Snooks Arm. Wade secretly reaches the ship in a Zodiac, angering Calvin when he learns Ruth coerced the crew into bringing both father and son by threatening to impound their vessel. Rats flee just before the creature attacks, forcing Calvin to take the helm and guide the surviving crew. They manage to shoot and tag it, but the ship takes on water, loses fuel, and loses two crewmen.

Calvin resists withdrawing, while Fonda pushes Dr. Sayed and Steve toward a dive for definitive footage. Wade eventually joins Fonda and Sayed in a purpose-built submersible. They lose the tracker signal, discover the creature has bitten off its own arm to remove the tag, and conclude that it is aware of them and may be playing with them. The creature captures the submersible during ascent and opens its hatch, prompting an unanswered mayday.

In Snooks Arm, contaminated iceberg ice causes worsening breathing difficulties, paranoia, aggression, and compulsive water-seeking among people who drank from it. Fonda treats the outbreak as possibly bacterial or viral, orders everyone indoors, and distributes bottled water. The Coast Guard assigns top medical priority but remains delayed by multiple maritime incidents. Amid the crisis, Mary proposes to Noah without her missing engagement ring.

A Lieutenant meets Jim Crocker, patriarch of the Crocker Group, who offers his resources and mentions his daughter Janet. They review the previous night: after attempts to force the creature from the harbor, it went on a rampage, tore up the marina, and injured five coast-guard personnel plus two of Crocker's people — one in a coma, the other losing both legs.

Wade and Calvin return to town (Wade in unfamiliar gear, "You like it?"). Crockers are clearing everyone out. Calvin mocks: the Crockers "have been lying for years," and even the giant squid rumor is "the last thing we needs to worry about."

Wade introduces Calvin formally to Dr. Fonda Howander, marine researcher at the Ocean Science Center in Seal Cove. Crocker asks what they're dealing with. Fonda: a deep-water cephalopod, highly intelligent, unmatched in taxonomic records — a female, with her egg sac frozen in the harbor's iceberg. She is aggressive because the water is too warm for her eggs.

Crocker pivots to a strike: the egg is "a soft spot," strike package "hot and standing by." Fonda argues the creature may be the last of her kind and deserves study, not bombing. Crocker compromises: Fonda can have the remains for exclusive research after elimination. She accepts under protest.

In a gossip huddle, locals recall Jan's chalky lemon squares — store-bought. Jan has been in town five years, moved in just after the last resettlement vote, and "turns out she's been a Crocker the whole friggin' time," manipulating the vote. The Crockers don't care about the people, they care about what's left behind: mining rights. Snooks Arm sits on a fortune — the residents were the only obstacle.

Calvin has the long-delayed realization: there is no Penney curse — "there's only the Crockers. They've had this Jesus thing rigged from the get-go."

Wade confronts Fonda over a ring — Wade claims it's his grandmother's and he was going to propose with it; Fonda counters that her missus gave her hers. Wade: "Missus? What friggin' missus?" — establishing Fonda has a same-sex partner.

When the mandatory evacuation order comes, Calvin refuses: "I love all of ya, but I can't just roll over. I just needs to stay and see this thing through!" Wade stays. Calvin argues Wade is "his own man." Mary first plans to evacuate, then changes her mind; Ruth also decides to stay. On a count of three, the Penneys split from the evacuation. Rendezvous if separated: the old packing plant.

Fonda lays out a different plan: the iceberg IS the egg sac — the creature is nesting and will not abandon her eggs. The iceberg weighs 100,000–150,000 tons but still floats. They can tow her (and the nest) out to deeper, cooler waters before the bombs fall. Wade starts cutting the iceberg into manageable chunks using "loops and knots." Calvin grudgingly admits: "your bad b'y's got a little bayman in him, after all."

Cut to Jim Crocker: Calvin has slipped the evacuation zone. Crockers call the creature "providence… manna from heaven" — "Thanks to her, we have the cover we need to clear this place without a fight." Calvin is "a liability." Crockers identify a "weak spot" and exchange something — "Wasn't meant for me, anyway."

The air strike is codenamed Raven Flight. Ground command orders Calvin's boat to stop. He refuses. Crockers/Lt. demand he be stopped. Fonda has the radio and refuses to call Calvin off. Crocker threatens: "You wouldn't be here if it wasn't for my family. We built your institute. We created the endowment that pays your salary." Fonda: "Good. I don't want anything to do with your money." They fight over the radio; Fonda is thrown to the deck.

In deep water — about 2,000 meters down — Calvin talks quietly to the creature. "Most of my life, you had me scared half to death. But I dare say we're more alike than I ever expected, you and me."

Then: "What the Jesus fuck?" — Calvin is now inside the strike zone. The Lt. is asked to authorize a strike on a civilian vessel. Crockers push for it. The Lt. refuses and orders Raven Flight to stand down: "Thanks to that fisherman, the harbor is no longer under threat. Residents can return home."

Fonda then narrates cephalopod biology: most cephalopods mate once and die; the mother stops hunting and eating, gives all her bodily nutrients to the eggs, then uses her last oxygen to blow the hatchlings out of the den. Her long ordeal is finally over. Calvin: "So none of them made it?" Fonda: "Oh no. Water's too warm." The mother is dead, and so are the eggs. "And it's just getting warmer. Then who knows what's next."

In an epilogue, the Penney family (now living away from town) is visited. Crockers are pushing for another resettlement vote despite the previous one being tossed; lawyers are fighting them next week. Someone is congratulated on "a foolish bit of ice" — Calvin's iceberg plan has become a celebrated story, and the legal/financial proceeds are "the only thing keeping the lights on now."

Officer Noseworthy and Paddy are out fishing — "Cod stocks are up again this year, and they're big as dogs." The closing credits roll over the Newfoundland folk song "Squid Jiggin' Ground."

## Plot

The episode picks up immediately after the harbor incident. Calvin, Ruth, and other townsfolk are searching the harbor for wreckage from Jerry Biles' vessel; the harbormaster urges everyone to stay back from the ice. The town is split between postponing the dory race (Jerry's body is still missing) and keeping the Garden Party and "Come Home Year" events on schedule.

Calvin is quietly pushed out of the search — Ruth tells him his involvement would look "improper" since Wade was on Jerry's boat. Calvin nonetheless pulls a half-digested moose carcass from the water and is quipped at about his moose license. He then delivers condolences to Mrs. Biles, who asks "what did this?" — a question no one can answer.

At the Penney house, Fonda (staying over after she and Wade returned "in a state") is making pour-over coffee; Wade emerges hungover, chugs a gallon of water, and goes back to bed. Calvin presses Wade about what happened: Wade remembers shooting at ice in the harbor and the ice flipping, then nothing. He admits to mushrooms and five or six Blue Star beers. Calvin coaches him to claim he wasn't driving the boat to dodge a BUI (boating under the influence) charge.

A news segment interrupts — strange events are wiping out entire fish stocks along the coast; Fonda's contact Anik describes the "total collapse of the Atlantic Gulf Stream" and the tipping point. Fonda tells Jan not to log the iceberg samples he's analyzing — put them in the other fridge.

Mary and Ruth discuss the relocation vote: most residents will vote yes; payouts are 140,000 per household and 60,000 per resident. They worry Calvin will refuse to leave and try to survive alone.

Wade wakes up changed. He says he can finally "see the bigger picture" and that Wade Penney won't be a burden anymore. Then Calvin sees something on Wade's body — strange marks — and recoils in horror.

Fonda returns from the Ocean Science Center, sees Wade's markings, and shows Calvin her reference: Architeuthis dux, a preserved giant squid. Calvin argues real squid hide, don't attack, even the big ones; the town never did figure out what sank the Penney family house decades ago — they blamed his father. Fonda reports that two of the largest North Atlantic ecological reserves are empty, six fish farms have lost 1.3 million in seven days, and the incidents track from Greenland south through "Iceberg Alley." She has no data on Pittman's unlicensed farm.

A news bulletin releases the final distress call from the Portuguese vessel Santa Christina — "Mayday. Mayday!" — something in the water struck them. Ruth orders Calvin off the search after 20 hours; DFO won't press overtime. Jan later shows Calvin that Pittman's farm is wrecked too — the fish pens are gone and Pittman himself is missing.

Wade has a nightmare of a drowned woman standing over him pinning him down. Jan calls it the "old hag" — stress-induced sleep paralysis from his Fort Mac days — and offers his couch out west as an escape hatch. At the Airbnb campsite, the guests' belongings are shredded.

A debt collector named Connor and his crew catch up with Wade and demand money; Wade pleads for time, citing the government payout, and gets 24 hours.

The Garden Party gets underway. Mary finds something missing from Wade's bag and reacts with alarm. Panic erupts on the shore — someone screams "there's something in the water!" and everyone is herded back. The episode closes on a radio call: the coast guard is calling off the search for the Santa Christina; the five Portuguese nationals are presumed dead, over a faint Portuguese prayer.
