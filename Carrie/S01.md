## S01E08

# S01E08 《AP 物理》/《大学预科物理》 — 概要

## 剧情主线苏·斯内尔（唯一幸存的关键证人）由一位名为凯特琳的女性调查员采访，逐帧还原毕业舞会之夜。两段主线交替推进：凯特琳的访谈 / 档案追溯，以及 埃玛琳·韦斯特 护送 嘉莉·怀特 离开张伯伦镇的公路逃亡。

- 凯特琳提示苏："有一件事，没人理解。" 嘉莉事件被官方数字定格为 200 人死亡、整座镇被摧毁，但"我们当时还是孩子"。
- 苏补完一系列未解警讯：金属探测器里的初见、教室里的"格格不入"的善良、毕业舞会之夜 9:25 抵达目击猪血淋头、9:27 体育馆信号与机械表集体停摆、维多利亚·吉布斯直播流（最后一张截图被当作"停电前最后影像"）。
- 苏主动修正叙事："并不是只有嘉莉一个人。还有别人。""嘉莉身后有一个影子"—— 这点是卡莉其实不是源头，所有人没问对问题。
- 尾声：玛格丽特 死在自己卧室，嘉莉被埃玛琳从救护现场救出并驶向未知地点。

## 关键人物动作

- 苏·斯内尔：配合访谈，透露自己从未真正亲眼看到"其他人"，被凯特琳反复暗示是 PTSD错位；但坚持自己的证词。
- 汤米·罗斯：被一名陌生女声叫住"汤米！"——出现短暂校园封锁/射击警报（"他们把我们锁在里面！""他们在朝我们开枪！"），生死未明。
- 克里斯·哈根森 与 比利·诺兰：驾车逃出镇路上，第一次意识到"是她"。比利回嘴："他们活该……该死的恶有恶报。""该死的怪胎！去死吧！撞死你。"——以车祸/撞人未遂的暴力狂欢收尾。
- 嘉莉·怀特：被赋予"我存在"宣告；在停尸房式回忆中与母亲相对，点头说"我原谅你，妈妈"；被埃玛琳救出后，退烧、肩臂受伤；最后站在方向盘前接受"这就是你的世界"。
- 玛格丽特·怀特：直升机掠过时为嘉莉唱《薰衣草是蓝的》（Dilly dilly）；回忆嘉莉出生的第一晚清理血污，"用的是同一种海绵"；请求嘉莉原谅自己、被嘉莉宽恕；之后独自领受埃玛琳的临终礼物，心跳停止。
- 埃玛琳·韦斯特：在嘉莉濒死时显形为实体；催眠玛格丽特（"想象你的心脏"），命其心脏停跳；从凯特琳口中被确认为"星体投射者"，被一位母亲形容为"对我女儿是威胁"。

## 新建立的名词与术语

- 念力基因（TK gene）：剧集对念动力（念力移物）能力的遗传学命名。隐性、隔代遗传、应激触发。
- 星体投射者（Astral Projector / A.P.）：埃玛琳所属的另一类超常人种，可在精神层面干预/操纵他人。凯特琳事先在"餐厅遇到那个女人"之前已经读过相关资料。
- A.P. 与 TK 被明确划分为两条不同的"通灵/超能"血脉。
- 凯特琳（调查员/采访者）：嘉莉事件后的联邦或类似层级调查主导者，持有一个黄色名为"凯特琳"的录音助理。
- 维多利亚·吉布斯（Vicky / 维姬）：毕业舞会现场直播者，尸体"缠在屋顶横梁上"被发现，录像带成为关键物证。
- 杜克大学：克里斯与苏正等候录取通知的学校，作为她们"频临崩溃"的时间锚点。

## 与剧集前情相关的连续性事实

- 苏从未亲眼见过嘉莉，却凭借一张视频、一辆比利·诺兰的车，独自冲回学校——延续她"还来得及吗"的赎罪主线。
- 嘉莉在浴室的生理期风波、毕业舞会猪血桶恶作剧等被苏以证词形式重新照亮。
- 玛格丽特被塑造为"挚爱、护犊、虔诚"的母亲形象——她对嘉莉的禁闭曾以"妈妈生你第一晚清理血污"作回应，与 SKILL 注明的"护犊而非暴君"基调一致。
- 调查者凯特琳提到了一个细节：她调查过"餐厅遇到那个女人"之前发生过的事——指向埃玛琳更早就被列入监控名单。
- 苏谈到"嘉莉身后有影子，影子是实体能的"——与本季片段 #107 嘉莉跪地面对"影子"插曲呼应。

## S01E06

# S01E06 — 《家政》

## Plot

The episode opens on a flashback to Carrie's childhood (around five years old) when Margaret brings her to "Damon Nostra" (Greg McMurray), a phone/television psychic. Margaret is desperate — Carrie has been seeing and saying disturbing things. Nostra turns out to be a fraud; he calls police and is later unmasked. Years later, that history still haunts Margaret, who insists there is "no such thing as mental illness" — only "weakness of spirit" — and forbids doctors, vaccines, and mainstream medicine.

In the present, Carrie discovers hidden cameras Margaret has installed throughout the house. A furious argument leaves Carrie shouting that Margaret was spying on her. Carrie is shaken when a vision of an unseen "Emaline" — possibly a hallucination of her dead childhood figure — appears in her room and asks her to "stop pretending to be one of them." Carrie tries to scream the presence out.

At Ewen High, Mr. Peist is missing. Tommy privately confronts Sue: he has heard she took a pregnancy test after a party. Sue says it was negative but admits she kept it from him; Tommy is hurt that she shut him out when he works two jobs and deals with his father's poor health.

A newscast then detonates the episode's central crisis: high-school teacher Seth Peist has committed suicide at home after a student-run gossip account (@CarrieRed) publicly accused him of having sexual relations with students. The accusations turn out to be true — Peist had a relationship with fifteen-year-old Brooklyn, and had done this at least three times at his previous school. John Hargensen appears on camera claiming his daughter Chris is being scapegoated and should be "treated like a hero" for exposing a predator. Parents are publicly siding with Margaret White; a physical fight between two mothers even breaks out at Claire's hair salon on Main Street over Carrie Red posts.

Principal Henry Grayle pulls Chris's file and confronts John Hargensen in his office. By Grayle's count, @CarrieRed has 1,655 posts attacking 522 students and teachers. John is unmoved: he threatens lawsuits, claims Chris's account was a free-speech outlet "for the community," and warns he will "take this school, take you, to court" and walk out with Grayle's job. He also rehashes Carrie's "first period" bullying as grounds for a countersuit. Grayle counters with Chris's disciplinary history: 24 assigned detentions in four years, six for bullying, and an expunged middle-school suspension for putting a firecracker in a girl's shoe (she nearly lost two toes). John is shaken; he didn't know.

Grayle suspends Chris and offers her a path back: (1) admit the voice on Sue Snell's recording is hers, (2) take accountability, and (3) do community outreach. John demands mediation. Mrs. Hargensen (Madison) leaves furious with John, calling him "an unfit father" for covering up the middle-school incident. A young woman in the Hargensen household (Billy Nolan's girlfriend, fed up) tells Chris flatly: "Whatever gets me to graduation… Chris, if you think that this is ending any way other than expulsion, you're dreaming."

At home, Carrie timidly asks Margaret about family mental illness. Margaret erupts in a paranoid monologue about demonic academics, vaccines, drinking-water poison, satellite weaponry, and weather events. Carrie escapes into the rain to visit Estelle Horan, her neighbor. Margaret catches her, drags her home by the arm, and forces her into the praying closet for breaking the rule of seeing Horan. Margaret also rants that Horan is "immoral" for entertaining boys in her bathing suit.

The next morning, the mediation between Carrie and Chris is held in an empty classroom, facilitated by Peter Morton, with parents and lawyers present, plus a news crew John secretly invited (Channel 4). The Hargensens argue that Carrie Red was a community submission account — Chris just curated it, others sent the posts. Margaret counters by accusing John of embezzlement. Outside, students begin chanting "Expel Hargensen!" and walking out, soon joined by parent-rights activists. Sue leads the student body down the hallway chanting "Walk out!"

John pulls Margaret aside and offers her a deal in the parking lot: Chris will publicly apologize and take civil-suit blame; Margaret will use her new "Maggie Red" platform to publicly accept the apology and de-escalate. Margaret agrees, calling it "Mother to mother."

Back inside the mediation, Morton pushes Carrie to describe how she felt finding out about Carrie Red. Margaret intervenes to protect her, and John pivots to defending Chris as a conduit for community speech. Carrie finally speaks directly to Chris. She asks: "Why me? I never did anything to you." She offers forgiveness: "The worst part… is that I really could have been your friend." Chris, cornered and harried by her father to say "two words," loses her nerve and snaps — "Fuck off. Freak." — a four-word refusal that amounts to a public rejection.

Chaos erupts. The hall chants "Death to Hargensen!" Chris is punched in the face (a stone or bottle from the crowd — the wound is treated by a man in the parking lot who says "Keep your head back"). Margaret uses Carrie's bloody nose as a campaign prop on live television, positioning herself as a national parent-rights leader: "This is not about one town. This is about America." She introduces her "Maggie Red" social-media account as a national platform against "institutionalized endangerment, corrupt practices, terrorists, bullies."

Carrie breaks from Margaret on camera, calling her "my bully" and shouting, "I wish you died instead of him [Daddy]!" Margaret, shaken but unshaken, tells Carrie she must "repent" and "get right with God… because things are gonna change." Episode ends on the Stephen King adaptation card.

## Important Character Actions

- Carrie White: discovers the hidden cameras, shouts at Margaret, hallucinates "Emaline," visits Estelle Horan against Margaret's orders, gets dragged back and put in the closet, speaks honestly and vulnerably at the mediation, asks Chris "why me?", gets hit in the face during the chaos, and on live TV calls Margaret "my bully" and says she wishes her father had died instead of Margaret.
- Margaret White: doubles down on anti-psychiatry, anti-vax, anti-"establishment" conspiracy thinking, forbids Carrie from seeing Horan, physically forces her into the praying closet, engineers the Maggie Red platform, accepts John's deal to swap a public apology for a public acceptance, and uses Carrie's wounded face as a campaign image.
- Chris Hargensen: refuses to apologize publicly despite her father's coaching, lashes out with "Fuck off. Freak." on camera, gets physically attacked by a student/protester in the hallway, and ends the episode bloodied and shaken.
- John Hargensen: threatens the school with lawsuits, privately admits Chris is a "conduit" not the author of the posts, offers to pay settlements to graduate her, secretly tips off Channel 4, and accepts the deal with Margaret.
- Mrs. Hargensen (Madison): discovers John covered up the middle-school firecracker incident, calls him an unfit father in front of him, and walks out on him.
- Henry Grayle: pushes the path-to-graduation deal, accepts the mediation (against his own instinct), and is publicly embarrassed when the walk-out happens during school hours.
- Sue Snell: revealed to have recorded Chris ("like a spy movie") earlier; admits to Tommy she once took a pregnancy test after a party and hid it from him, straining their relationship; later leads the student walk-out down the hallway.

- Peter Morton: chosen to facilitate the mediation; visibly out of his depth ("marshmallow," as Margaret later puts it).
- Estelle Horan: receives Carrie's visit; gets dragged into Margaret's morality rant (boys coming and going, "flaunting" in a bathing suit).
- Billy Nolan (girlfriend, name not yet confirmed): at the Hargensen house, tells Chris bluntly that expulsion is coming.
- Seth Peist: dies by suicide off-screen between episodes; revealed to have actually been a predator with three prior incidents at a previous school.

- Brooklyn: the fifteen-year-old student Peist was involved with; her interview with police triggers the suicide storyline.

- "Emaline" / 艾玛琳: the recurring hallucination / imaginary friend figure from earlier episodes; appears again in Carrie's room here asking her to "stop pretending to be one of them" and "go rogue." Listed in the series-context skill as new to this adaptation (not in King's 1974 novel).
- Channel 4: the local TV station John tips off; covers the protest as "Death to Hargensen" / "Free speech" dueling chants.

## Continuity-Relevant Facts

- Carrie's powers remain dormant in this episode: no telekinetic outbursts, but the hallucinations (Emaline, voices in the room) continue. Margaret's flashback shows Carrie has had visions since age five.
- Margaret now publicly leads a national movement, not just a local parent group. Her "Maggie Red" branding mirrors Chris's "Carrie Red" — both are publicly humiliating their own daughters.
- John Hargensen has covered up Chris's disciplinary record before (the expunged firecracker incident). Mrs. Hargensen did not know.
- Sue Snell's recording of Chris ("like a spy movie") is the evidence that pinned the @CarrieRed account on Chris in the prior episode and now anchors the school's case for suspension.
- Carrie Red posts included teacher contributors, not just students — a fact John uses to argue Chris should not take sole blame.
- Tommy's home situation (two jobs, sick father) is established for the first time, foreshadowing his limited bandwidth later in the season.
- The school-subject title pun holds: this is "Home Economics" — Margaret's all-domestic sphere, and the episode's domestic drama between Carrie and Margaret frames the entire school-side crisis.
- Prom remains a few weeks away. Chris's reinstatement is now conditioned on a public apology she refuses to give; expulsion is the default path.

## Jokes and References Useful for Later Episodes

- "True psychics don't prosper. They suffer." — Damon Nostra's parting line. The episode bookends itself with this: Margaret, the failed-psychic's most devoted believer, ends the episode as a movement leader with her own "Maggie Red" hotline-by-platform.
- Chris's "two words" — the Hargensen coaching gag. John demands "two words." Carrie's forgiveness scene is built around this countdown. Chris delivers "Fuck off. Freak." — Margaret in voiceover says, "That's three words" — then the student "Bitch" lands as a fourth, capping the joke.
- "Expel Hargensen!" / "Death to Hargensen!" / "Free speech!" — the protest's three-way chant, replayed on the news cycle.
- Margaret's Conspiracy Bingo: vaccines, drinking water, weather modification / cloud seeding, drone tests, satellite weaponry, New Mexico military base. This is the established Margaret vocabulary for later episodes.
- Margaret uses "Carietta" / "嘉莉埃塔" as the full given name for Carrie — a King-novel staple, and an escalation of "Momma" / "Girl" / "Carietta" used in prior episodes.
- Grayle's "hall of fame numbers" line about Chris's 24 detentions — a future callback line if Chris's behavior escalates.
- Sue leading the walk-out chant — establishes her as the de facto student leader, a role that will compound with her "Is it too late?" motif later in the season.
- The "Claire's on Main" hair-salon parent fight is a small-town-color beat that seeds how deeply the Carrie Red wars have reached adult spaces, not just student ones.
- "Because things are gonna change." — Margaret's closing line to Carrie. Sets up the apocalyptic domestic arc running into the prom episodes.

- Margaret's movement now uses Carrie as a prop (the bloody nose on camera). Carrie has publicly broken from Margaret on live TV — the mother-daughter rupture is live and unhealed.
- Carrie's mental state: Emaline hallucinations, the open question of family "mental illness," Margaret's refusal of any medical help, and Carrie's wish that her father had died instead — all active.
- The Hargensen marriage: Madison has openly called John an unfit father. John's embezzlement accusation surfaces publicly via Margaret.
- Sue and Tommy's relationship: bruised by the pregnancy-test secret; they have not yet resolved whether they're "breaking up" or just strained.
- The Peist suicide has hardened the Carrie Red defenders ("she exposed a predator") and hardened the Hargensen legal posture. Both narratives are now public and competing.
- Carrie's telekinesis: still dormant, but the Emaline vision and Margaret's flashback imply the awakening arc is approaching — the prom episode titles suggest the build to S01E07 《浪漫文学》 and S01E08 《AP 物理》.

## S01E03

# 《社会研究》 (Social Studies)

## Plot

The episode opens on the viral aftermath of the gym-shower incident: classmates mock Carrie as "Bloody Carrie Red," and a "Carrie Red" private social-media account is already circulating content. Miss Desjardin excuses Carrie and makes the others run. Emaline, eating with her at lunch, slips Carrie a quiet mantra: "You have all the power. They can only hurt you if you let them." Carrie has been researching the historical record of telekinesis (Cottin, Palladino, Tomczyk, Schaberl, Kulagina) for a school project; she tells Emaline the pattern is "extraordinary claim, accusation of fraud. Often, by a man."

Chris Hargensen and Tina Blake corner Carrie at school with a stage-managed apology for the shower incident, then invite her to a "Spring Road" open-house party at a vacant McMansion, coaching her to sneak out while Margaret works nights. Sue, present, tells Carrie not to do anything she's uncomfortable with. At home, Margaret installs a smart-camera system because Carrie is "being targeted." A certified-mail notice arrives: Ralph's ashes remain unclaimed and will be "humanely disposed of" within 30 days unless Margaret picks them up. Margaret leaves for her overnight shift at the big-box store.

At school, guidance counselor Peter Morton tells Chris she got into Duke but missed the financial-aid cutoff; Chris lashes out, and Morton tells her to drop the attitude.

Carrie sneaks out to the party with her neighbor Stella Horan. Sue gives her a tour, brands her "under my protection tonight," and announces a beer-pong game. Tina gives Carrie a vodka lemonade; Carrie is soon a little drunk. Billy Nolan — Chris's boyfriend, a slacker drug dealer who works on a farm ("stringing up pigs") — sells weed, deflects Carrie's Molly request, and flirts with both girls. He eventually opens up to Carrie about being "weird" and finding his "people"; Carrie reciprocates with her own story of loneliness and the death of her father. Billy calls Carrie his friend. Tina privately tells Sue she only invited Carrie "because I thought it'd be funny"; Sue calls Carrie "homework for my real friend" and a "replacement."

A physical confrontation erupts between Chris and Brooklyn — Brooklyn's father is having an affair with Chris's mother, who has a history of these "phases." Chris mocks Brooklyn ("you and your daddy are just a phase"), a slap lands, and Chris's mother (Mrs. Hargensen) arrives and pulls Brooklyn out. Chris gets blackout drunk.

Sue drives a passed-out Chris and a tipsy Carrie home. In the dark, Carrie recalls her father's instruction to find her way home by the stars, name-drops Mars and The Martian Chronicles, and quotes: "Earth men have a talent for ruining big and beautiful things."

The next morning, Margaret quizzes Carrie about a headache and a suspiciously good night. The "Carrie Red" private account shares a photo of Carrie at the party that makes her look stoned. At school, someone takes Carrie's phone; Chris and Tina get the clip.
