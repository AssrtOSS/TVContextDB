# Marshals (TV series) — Translation Background

## 1. Series identification
- English title: Marshals (originally developed as Y: Marshals; the "Y:" was dropped from the title in January 2026 but kept internally as silent branding).
- Chinese title (mainstream mainland rendering): 《法警小队》. A fan-press rendering 《执法者》 appears in some articles but the dominant term is 《法警小队》; prefer 《法警小队》 unless a specific publisher uses otherwise.
- Format: live-action drama, neo-Western / police procedural, 42–43 minutes per episode.
- Creator/showrunner: Spencer Hudnut (also showrunner of CBS's SEAL Team); based on characters created by Taylor Sheridan and John Linson. Sheridan is executive producer but not the day-to-day showrunner.
- Broadcaster / network: CBS in the United States (first series in the Yellowstone franchise to air on a broadcast linear network). Streams on Paramount+. Distributed internationally by Paramount Global Content Distribution.

## 2. Premise (whole-series, not single-episode)
Marshals is a sequel and spin-off of Yellowstone (2018–2024), and the fourth live-action series in the Yellowstone franchise. It picks up about 15 months after the Yellowstone finale. Former U.S. Navy SEAL and current rancher Kayce Dutton (the youngest son of the late John Dutton) has sold the bulk of the Yellowstone Dutton Ranch to the neighboring Broken Rock Indian Reservation, keeping a small parcel called East Camp for himself and his teenage son Tate. His wife, Monica Long Dutton, has died off-screen of cancer linked to toxic dumping on the reservation.

Grieving and adrift, Kayce is recruited by his former SEAL team leader Pete "Cal" Calvin to join an elite U.S. Marshals task force operating in Montana. The team balances case-of-the-week operations (fugitive hunts, anti-government militia, domestic terrorism, narcotics, trafficking, witness protection) with a serialized arc about the Duttons' legacy, the political and economic pressure on Broken Rock, the question of whether Kayce can be a "protector" instead of a "chip off the old block," and — beginning in Season 1's finale — a land-grab threat to East Camp from cattleman Tom Weaver.

Season 2's arc follows the kidnapping of Tate by Tom Weaver and the exposure of Weaver as the mastermind behind Season 1's string of attacks; Cal's battle with cancer; the partial changeover of leadership inside the Marshals unit; and the integration of Jimmy Hurdstrom (a returning Yellowstone character) into the Marshals world.

## 4. World setting
- State: Montana (蒙大拿州), primarily around the fictional Paradise Valley and the (also fictional) Broken Rock Indian Reservation.
- Reservation: Broken Rock Indian Reservation (断岩印第安保留地 / 破碎岩印第安保留地). The "断岩" rendering is from the zh-Wikipedia Yellowstone entry and is the established translation; "破碎岩" appears in mainland press. Both are acceptable, but prefer 破碎岩 to match the show's deliberate "broken rock" naming and avoid confusion with the unrelated Chinese word 断岩.
- Landmarks:
  - Yellowstone Dutton Ranch (黄石达顿牧场). The original ranch sold to Broken Rock in the Yellowstone finale; the remaining parcel Kayce still owns is East Camp (东营 / 东营地).
  - Painted Horse Casino (彩马赌场) and Grey Wolf Peak Casino (灰狼峰赌场) — both Rainwater's, on Broken Rock.
  - 6666 Ranch in Texas — "四六六牧场" (player's preferred rendering in Yellowstone subs); Jimmy is posted there.
- Real-world law-enforcement backdrop: the U.S. Marshals Service (USMS, 美国法警局 / 美国法警总署), a federal agency under the Department of Justice. Title holders in the show: Special Deputy U.S. Marshal (特别副执法官 / 特别副法警), Supervisory Deputy U.S. Marshal (督察副法警), Chief Deputy U.S. Marshal (首席副法警 / 局长). For non-U.S. readers: explain in a translator note that the U.S. Marshals Service is a federal warrant/enforcement bureau, not a city police force — it differs from a "sheriff" (县治安官/警长) and from a "deputy" in a sheriff's office.
- Geographic and historical hook (drawn from the Yellowstone universe): Crow Indian Reservation in southeast Montana stood in as Broken Rock for filming; do NOT confuse the show's fictional Broken Rock with the real Crow reservation.
- Cultural-philosophical hook: cowboy code / 牛仔信条 (unspoken code of honor, sticking to one's word, hard work, respect for the land). Kayce invokes this directly when confronting Tom Weaver in S2E1.
- Lakota / Native American linguistic and ceremonial elements recur in the show, especially in episodes set on or near Broken Rock. Lakota phrases are kept in original with English subtitles in the broadcast — when translating, preserve the Lakota transliteration first, then a Chinese gloss in parentheses, e.g. "Piya Wiconi" (派亚·维科尼, 新开始).

## 5. Recurring terminology and Chinese renderings
- U.S. Marshals / Marshals Service → 美国法警局 / 联邦法警; team shorthand 法警小队. Lower-case "marshals" in dialogue can stay as 法警 (no need to add 美国 each time after first mention).
- Special Deputy U.S. Marshal → 特别副执法官; U.S. Marshal → 联邦执法官. Use 副法警 only in informal team banter.
- NAVY SEAL → 海豹突击队 (海豹突击队员 = SEAL operator / frogman).
- Montana → 蒙大拿 (full name 蒙大拿州 only on first reference).
- Broken Rock Indian Reservation → 破碎岩印第安保留地. The tribal body is the Confederated Tribes of Broken Rock (破碎岩联合部落).
- High Chief / Chairman → 酋长 (in ceremonial sense) / 主席 (in political/business sense). Rainwater's preferred self-description uses 主席.
- Dutton family legacy / Dutton ranch dynasty → 达顿家族 / 达顿家业. The "Dutton name" as a brand is itself a plot element — translate 名字 (Kayce's "my name" as a Dutton) literally as 达顿这个姓氏 to preserve that double meaning.
- Anti-government militia / domestic extremist group → 反政府民兵 / 国内极端组织. "Trail Keepers" → 护径者 (militia name; keep capitalization).
- Reservation police → 保留地警局 / 部落警局.
- ATF (Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives) → 烟酒枪炮及爆炸物管理局 (full first time) → ATF / 美国烟酒枪炮局 thereafter. Belle's background is as a former ATF agent → 前 ATF 特工 / 前烟酒枪炮局探员.
- East Camp → 东营 / 东营地. The "last piece of the Yellowstone ranch" framing should stay literal.
- Range justice / cowboy justice / 牧场正义 → 牧场式正义 / 边境正义. The official tagline uses "range justice" — translate as 牧场正义 in show-summary contexts and as 边境正义 in dialogue.
- Piya Wiconi (Lakota: "new beginning") → 派亚·维科尼. First occurrence: 派亚·维科尼（拉科塔语，意为"新开始"）; subsequent occurrences: 派亚·维科尼.
- The lone wolf (a recurring Yellowstone-universe symbol for Kayce's wild self) → 孤狼 (singular, with symbolic weight; not "独狼" which carries a different political connotation).
- Cowboy code / code of honor → 牛仔信条 / 牛仔之间的信条.
- Land grab / 抢地 → 土地掠夺 / 强占土地. "East Camp land grab" is Season 2's central conflict — render as 东营土地争夺 / 抢夺东营.

## 6. Speaking styles and tone
- Register: terse, laconic, frontier-modern. Long pauses, short sentences. Kayce often answers a question with one word.
- Western idiom: "Howdy" → 你好 / 嘿 (rare in this show); "ma'am" → 女士 / 夫人 (rare, mostly when addressing Andrea or female federal judges); "y'all" → 你们 / 大家.
- Military idiom: SEALs call each other by surname or callsign, not first name. Garrett's callsign is "Double G" → 双G. Cal's team calls him "Cal" (not Pete) — preserve that.
- Lakota / Native characters: Mo, Thomas Rainwater, Miles Kittle frequently code-switch between English and occasional Lakota phrases. Use 拉科塔语 to gloss the language; reserve 保留地话 for casual reference.
- Federal/military jargon: "lock it down" → 封锁现场; "we have a positive ID" → 目标已确认; "secure the perimeter" → 控制外围; "go loud" → 强攻 / 开火突击; "clear" → 清理完毕 / 安全.
- Series-specific tone: the show mixes straight-faced CBS-procedural dialogue ("door-kicker," "range justice," "team lead") with Sheridan's signature Western fatalism ("My life's been defined by losing the things I love"). Avoid flowery classical-Chinese phrasing; the show deliberately sounds clipped and modern even when characters invoke the past.
- Songs and music: when country artists diegetically play in-scene (Riley Green appears as Garrett and may sing in-episode), translate song titles literally if shown on screen; do NOT add Chinese lyrics translation beyond glosses.

## 7. Cultural context for translators
- Federal vs. tribal jurisdiction. The Marshals operate as federal law enforcement on land adjacent to a sovereign tribal nation. Keep the legal distinction clear — "federal land" / "联邦管辖土地" vs. "reservation land" / "保留地" — because the show runs much of its conflict on this jurisdictional boundary.
- The historical trauma of resource extraction on reservations is a recurring subtext. Mining, fracking, illegal dumping of toxic waste — these are not generic "pollution" but reference real documented harms on Montana reservations. Treat as serious subject matter; do NOT soften language around cancer linked to toxic dumping.
- The Lakota language belongs to the Siouan family. Render Lakota phrases in italics in source-script transliteration first; do not attempt a character-by-character Chinese phonetic rendering. Example: "Piya Wiconi" (派亚·维科尼). "Wakan Tanka" if it appears = 瓦坎·坦卡 (神祇/大神).
- Cowboy culture: roundups, branding, cutting horses, "East Camp" working ranch hands. Avoid modern "horse farm" vocabulary; prefer 牧场 / 牛群 / 给牛烙印 / 套马.
- The show's family-drama legacy is from Yellowstone, where the Dutton family controlled the largest contiguous ranch in Montana; new viewers may not have watched Yellowstone. Use one-line recap in on-screen text only when needed; do NOT over-explain in subs.
- Memorial / grief language: Monica's death is referenced obliquely in early S1 episodes and directly later. Subtle phrasing — Kayce says "the best of me went with you" at her grave (S1E1 closing). Preserve the restraint; do not melodramatize.
- On-screen profanity: the show airs TV-14 on CBS — strong profanity is rare and almost always contextually meaningful (e.g., Kayce's "chip off the old block"). Render faithfully without embellishment.
