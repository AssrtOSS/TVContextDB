## S04E04

# S04E04 — Summary

## Plot

The episode intercuts three timelines. In the present, Gerry Cliff runs a three-day Basic Public Order course at Kent Marsh Training Camp under Inspector Tennent. A standout trainee — Sandra Adelola, a London (Dagenham) constable — excels through every exercise: she throws a live Molotov, advances under mock hostile-crowd conditions, rescues a "badly injured" protestor, and walks through a fire lane while singing Chaka Khan's "Ain't Nobody." Flirtation develops between instructor and trainee; by the final night Gerry sings "Ain't Nobody" at the cohort's send-off and gives Sandra his number "if you're ever in Dagenham."

The second thread: Halloween night back at Blackthorn. Gerry and his probationer partner "Sully" are assigned 7-4 (south perimeter) watching Carrick View. Gerry spots a Dublin-plated metallic-grey VW Polo idling outside Carrick View and, ignoring Inspector Johnston's repeated "discontinue pursuit" orders (Carrick View is OOB), chases it in. He crashes, his partner is bottled by local kids, and the suspect escapes.

Third thread: weeks later, Gerry and Sandra are now married and living together in Belfast. While dining out, she spots the same VW Polo driver and, off-duty, arrests him (resisting arrest, dangerous driving). Constable Jen Robinson processes the booking — but Inspector Johnston releases the man on bail without even recording a name. "Colly" Collins (C3) reveals the man was an MI5 surveillance target and warns Gerry off. Sandra transfers in as Blackthorn's new custody sergeant, replacing Barney (who quit to become a 999 call handler). The episode closes with Sandra reading Gerry's old Special Branch informant file on James McIntyre, locking it away, and agreeing: "we're not going to think about the past any more."

## Important Character Actions

- Gerry: instructs the PO course; pursues into OOB Carrick View against Jonty's orders; reveals his 25-year freeze as Response constable (refused to recruit a 16-year-old tout in Special Branch); confronts Colly about McIntyre; marries Sandra; hides the agent file for years before showing her.
- Sandra (Adelola → Cliff): throws the Molotov, walks the fire lane singing, becomes "Queen of Fire"; arrests the VW Polo suspect off-duty; accepts the Blackthorn custody sergeant post; reads the McIntyre file and seals it.
- Inspector Jonty Johnston: orders pursuit discontinued, releases the suspect on bail, told by Colly to "cool your jets"; revealed to covet the Chief Superintendent post and to currently run all station decisions.
- Colly Collins (DCS, C3): confirms the Halloween incident was an MI5/Box op; orders Gerry off the case; name-drops "7428 — James McIntyre."
- Sully (Gerry's probationer partner): pulled from the crashed car to guard the suspect, gets bottled by Carrick View kids; bristles when called "scumbags"; receives Gerry's "hard place to police / hard place to live" lecture.
- Jen Robinson: custody/booking officer on the off-duty arrest; hands the suspect over to Jonty.
- Barney: departing Blackthorn custody sergeant (10 years), leaving for a 999 call-handler job — triggers Sandra's transfer.

## S04E01

# S04E01 — Summary

## Plot

The episode opens with Stevie Neil (acting sergeant / Bronze Commander on her first major public-order command) overseeing a peaceful protest at the Matchhill Street Migrant Centre, whose chairperson Amira Saeed refuses to evacuate the building because, as she tells Annie Conlon, "we've been running all our lives — there's nowhere left to go." Stevie negotiates the delay long enough to set up an evacuation route.

Reports surface of an organised group of Republicans travelling north from Dublin carrying Irish tricolours and a right-wing nationalist group also mobilising; once their flags appear together near a Loyalist area, C3 Intelligence's DCI "Colly" Collins identifies them as the Anti-Fascist Initiative. Helen McNally (now Chief Inspector / Silver Commander) requests TSG (Tactical Support Group) support, but the two TSG units are tied up on "Op Harvest" under Colly's authority. A standoff escalates: Stevie performs CPR on a male protester who collapses and stops breathing while missiles are thrown, while Annie (with Shane Bradley as her partner) is unable to evacuate Amira from inside the migrant centre. Eventually Helen authorises Stevie to "let them pass," and the Anti-Fascist Initiative is allowed through; the masked men then attack the Loyalist protest from behind.

Helen forces Colly to stand down Op Harvest tasking "on the authority of DCI Collins" so TSGs can deploy; Stevie falls back into the migrant centre's courtyard to release the uniformed officers under fire and form a new cordon, putting the TSGs in a vulnerable forward position. The riot is eventually brought under control by "Reds" (red-capped TSGs) once they arrive. One officer (McCloskey) is injured.

In the aftermath, Helen meets privately with Nicola Robinson (Chief Superintendent), who praises Stevie as "superb" but warns that a manipulated social-media narrative will spin the incident as deliberate police bottling-in of protesters. Nicola then reveals she is being lined up to apply for Assistant Chief Constable as part of a major PSNI restructure, realignment of districts, and consolidation of assets. She asks Helen to be her right-hand at headquarters. Helen declines, citing her need to stay at Blackthorn. Nicola counters that Blackthorn may be closed entirely because quarterly crime stats have it bottom of the table.

Sandra Cliff, now Station Sergeant managing uniform output, is told by Helen to push stop-and-search, drink-driving, and volume-crime clearance rates before the next quarterly report. Helen also orders a temporary hold on all transfers and secondments — then rescinds Grace Ellis's transfer to CID Child Protection, blocking her promotion indefinitely on "operational" grounds. Aisling Byrne, working late, breaks up with Tommy Foster: she had earlier applied for a transfer back to Derry (rejected as Blackthorn was short-staffed), has decided she cannot sustain a relationship with him because the trauma from S03 is "too close," and says, "I don't have room for the emotions." She tells Tommy "you did the right thing" but ends it, leaving him devastated.

The episode intercuts Tina McIntyre's household, where a High Court ruling has just been issued allowing MI5 surveillance footage of Gerry Cliff's murder to be played at the upcoming trial of Maurice McIntyre (Tina's father-in-law James's father) and Anthony Donovan. James — now out on bail, drinking despite a no-alcohol order — argues with Tina over a card from his father telling them to keep quiet; he physically shoves her after she calls him a tout.

Annisling praises Stevie's work and says "you saved a man's life," and hints at plans for the evening. Later, at Stevie's celebration (held informally because of the Blackthorn "never celebrate" rule), the song "Brewing Up a Storm" by The Stunning plays. Aisling tells Stevie she "can't do it any more" with Tommy and asks for space; Stevie, after a brief chat, goes outside and takes Amira's panicked call about people surrounding her house on Breslin Street. Stevie and Annie race there, find a hostile crowd smashing windows and shouting anti-migrant abuse, take cover behind the door as the mob tries to break it down, and are told someone has a gun. The episode ends on a gunshot outside the door.

## Plot

The Gerry Cliff murder trial of Maurice McIntyre and Anthony Donovan begins in the High Court. Both defendants are charged with common enterprise murder; the prosecution's case rests on MI5 surveillance footage of the shooting that killed Gerry in an area marked OOB (out of bounds) for Response police. Jennifer Robinson — Nicola Robinson's daughter, now a barrister — prosecutes and gives emotional evidence about Gerry's death and the way Oisin Sullivan killed him "in cold blood." Happy Kelly testifies that Gerry visited him the day he died and that he told Gerry about Dublin number plates spotted near the Palace; his brother and father died in a Troubles bomb, which is why he remembers plates. The case effectively collapses when Mo McIntyre has a public meltdown in court, shouting that "they're lying in there." Nicola reveals to Helen that the case exists only because she struck a deal: MI5 surrendered the footage in exchange for permanently burying the fact that James McIntyre spent decades as their agent under the codename "Farset." Mo's collapse threatens to expose everything, so a back-channel pressure group (Tina McIntyre, Lee Thompson, and an angry Dublin-connected contact) forces Nicola's hand. The Director of Public Prosecutions arrives at the courthouse unannounced; the Crown withdraws its evidence and the judge directs the jury to find both defendants not guilty.

On the street side, a recording of Grace's hospital conversation with anti-migrant influencer Katie Close is posted online, exposing Grace's service number and home address and sparking threats against both women. Social media immediately blames the migrant centre; the team defuses a near-riot at Amira Saeed's hostel and a hostile doorstep at Katie's house. Grace and Annie pursue a car with false plates (Indigo Sierra Golf 3101) seen circling the migrant centre. Tommy, stressed by the looming trial and his breakup with her, is put on a "file day" by Helen after mouthing off. Shane is reassigned to him as observer; the pair notice Lee Thompson has resurfaced after nearly two years, now driving a bus. They theorise Lee is using ex-military contacts on the route to move drugs on a larger scale. Shane flags a brake-light defect on Lee's vehicle (registration with a Dublin ex-squaddie called "Fergy"); Lee denies it. Annie presses C3 for intel and is told Lee is a has-been. ## S04E06

S04E06 begins with Stevie being told Blackthorn Station will close in roughly six weeks. A cold open at a closing-down announcement later in the episode confirms the timeline. Nicola Robinson eventually tells the whole station in a briefing that Blackthorn is shutting, promotions and transfer freezes are lifted, and that she and Helen have been removed from duty pending the Ombudsman process. The team is told they can apply for posts elsewhere.

Two overlapping threats dominate the hour. The first is a bomb at 16 Drumlin Street. The PSNI callsign 7-2 calls "officer down"; Annie Conlon arrives with Grace Ellis, McCloskey (Blue 3) and Shane Bradley. Annie survives because her flak jacket absorbed the blast, but a young woman in her twenties, Katie Close, is hit by a ball bearing and develops a sucking chest wound. Grace improvises a chest seal with a plastic bag and tape and gets her to hospital; Katie survives surgery and ends up in ICU. Later, Annie visits Katie in hospital and they discuss the case. Grace tells Katie the attackers were not migrants or a local community group but a drugs cartel trying to provoke both sides so the police would focus on community unrest instead of their shipments, and suggests Katie meet Amira Saeed (the migrant centre attack victim) since both know what it is like when something terrible comes to your front door.

The second threat comes from the Ombudsman announcing a major investigation into state collusion by a long-running republican informant codenamed "Farset," widely understood to be James McIntyre. Tina McIntyre and her son Mo are identified as potential targets. John O'Boyle, whose teenage son Ciaran was killed in an earlier season (Aisling Byrne was present with Ciaran when he died), breaks down watching the news and drives armed to the McIntyre house. Tommy Foster and Aisling are already guarding the property; armed police and McCloskey surround it. Aisling talks John down by repeating Ciaran's last words — "Tell my mammy and daddy I love them" — and he is taken without shots fired. The O'Boyle subplot was run as "Op Gulliver."

A CID strand runs through Helen McNally. She is revealed to be the officer who passed material to the Ombudsman. Earlier in the season, she took a witness statement from Geraldine, a former Special Branch/RUC officer who recruited "Farset" during the Troubles and handed him to MI5; Geraldine defends the "line" she stood on. Helen also tips off Colly (Paul Collins, C3 detective chief superintendent) that a Ginley cartel spanning Mexico, Trinidad, Ireland and the UK is behind the shipments — they know who is behind it but cannot prove it.
