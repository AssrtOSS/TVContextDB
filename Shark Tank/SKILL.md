# Shark Tank — Background

## Series identification

- English title: Shark Tank
- Chinese titles: 《创智赢家》 (mainland standard; used by YYeTS/人人影视 and zh.wikipedia), 《鲨鱼坦克》 (Baidu Baike alternate), 《创业鲨鱼帮》 (Taiwan/Hong Kong rendering)
- Format: American business reality television series (竞赛/发明真人秀)
- Network: ABC (美国广播公司); later syndicated on CNBC
- Premiere: August 9, 2009
- Created by: Mark Burnett (executive producer of Survivor, The Apprentice)
- Format origin: US adaptation of UK Dragons' Den, itself based on Japanese マネーの虎 (The Tigers of Money, 2001–2004, Nippon TV)
- Seasons/episodes: 17 seasons aired as of 2025–2026; Season 18 announced for fall 2026; 376+ episodes
- Episode length: ~42 minutes; each episode typically features 3–4 entrepreneur pitches edited down from ~45-minute sessions
- Production: Mark Burnett Productions → One Three Media → United Artists Media Group → MGM Television (with Sony Pictures Television throughout)
- Awards: Primetime Emmy for Outstanding Structured Reality Program (2014–2017, 2024)

## Premise

Aspiring entrepreneurs pitch their business ideas, products or inventions to a panel of self-made millionaire and billionaire investors called the "Sharks." Each Shark hears the pitch, asks probing questions, identifies weaknesses in product, business model or valuation, then either makes an investment offer (typically in exchange for equity) or opts out. A successful offer is a "handshake deal" subject to due diligence; an entrepreneur who draws no offer leaves empty-handed ("I'm out"). The series dramatizes the negotiation, the clash of personalities, and the business reasoning behind each Shark's decision.

## Speaking styles and tones (subtitle guidance)

- Sharks speak in direct, transactional, often combative business English: short imperatives ("I'm out." "Give me a number." "Why are you so cheap?" "That's the worst valuation I've ever heard.").
- Frequent high-frequency phrases to render naturally into Chinese:
  - "I'm out." → "我不投了" / "我退出"
  - "I'm in." → "我投了" / "我加入"
  - "For what?" → "凭什么？" / "为什么是这个价？"
  - "Let me ask you this..." → "我问你一个问题"
  - "Here's the deal." → "条件是这样的"
  - "I'll give you X for Y%." → "我给你X，换Y%的股份"
  - "You're dead to me." → "你出局了" (Shark disqualifying another Shark's competing offer)
  - "Royalty deal." → "版税方案"
- Entrepreneurs often begin with a one-line pitch (the "ask"): "I'm seeking $200,000 for 15% of my company."
- On-screen graphics ("On-screen text:") show each entrepreneur's "ask" at the start of a pitch; preserve as on-screen text in subtitle.
- Phil Crowley's voice-over introductions: "The following pitch has been edited for television" / "The Sharks are paid as cast members of this show, but the money they're investing is their own." Render these disclaimer lines as neutral narration.
- Tone register: business-formal but reality-TV blunt; Sharks use sarcasm and confrontation freely. Do not soften Kevin O'Leary's "Mr. Wonderful" sarcasm or Mark Cuban's bluntness into polite Chinese — keep the bite.

## Cultural and cross-format context

- The show is the US franchise in the global Dragons' Den family: UK 《龙穴》(Dragons' Den), Japan マネーの虎 / 《金钱之虎》, Australia Shark Tank (Robert Herjavec dual role), Germany 《创业鲨鱼》(Höhle der Löwen), France 《龙之眼》, Denmark 《狮子洞》, etc. When Sharks reference the show's "family," subtitle can briefly clarify with 国际同类型节目 / 全球同系列节目.
- Mainland China's licensed version is 《合伙中国人》(2016, Tencent / Tianjin TV). The phrase "中国版Shark Tank" is acceptable shorthand.
- Cultural rendering notes: American units (dollars, patents via USPTO, QVC, Amazon, Costco) should stay in original terms; do not localize currency. "QVC" is widely understood in Chinese media and need not be translated.
