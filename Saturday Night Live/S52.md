## S52E02

The Cold Open is a campaign-ad shoot where the GOP, facing "bleak" midterm polls, films spots: Marco Rubio does a vague bilingual ad, J.D. Vance is told to fetch people and accidentally keeps launching into "Live from New York" before being cleared, Pete Hegseth hijacks the set with a Bero-beer ad ("no weak, no woke, no DEI, no guy on guy," "balls gone," "put God back in war," "Hegseth out!"), and Trump finally walks on to "approve this ramble," admit his $5,000 bribe is one he "have no intention of following through" on, urge viewers to use "imagination," riff "Imagination makes a MAGA nation," insult Ken Paxton's eyes, and record both a win and a loss — ending with the proper "Live from New York, it's Saturday Night!"

Dakota Johnson's monologue leans into her birthday, repeats she's still promoting Madame Web because "the movie hasn't made any money yet," jokes about "two very public, very lame breakups with two very awesome musicians. Looking for a third," introduces her "literal therapist" who just shows up weekly with an empty wine bottle and says "Damn, that's crazy," then claims dating musicians is a public service because she is "saving you a lot of money on Zoloft."

In "Press Your Luck," contestant Sherri wins a million dollars, immediately says "Whoo, press my luck!" and keeps refusing to bank, eventually choosing "free Advil for life" over $5 million because "it's too complicated" — "where, where?" — "CVS, you stupid bitch." Donald and Lucas fold early.

Commercial parody "Mean, Scary Girlfriend — Ready for Dinner" is an AI girlfriend (Sarah Sherman) who roasts the boyfriend's haircut ("you look like a pedophile on vacation"), recommends Turkiye for hair transplants, only wants sex on the worst day of his life, comes with a dog that hates him, insane ex-boyfriends ("hot tattoo artist, jacked blue-collar guy, and randomly Bobby Flay"), a cocaine-addicted best friend, and a gay friend who pays with a debit card. Whenever she occasionally says something nice ("That's a good shirt") he cries and gets called "pussy."

The pre-taped hospital break-room "Gossip Girl Krista" introduces nurse Becca (whose surgery patient didn't make it but "we seriously had so much fun"), Dr. Sylvie hooking up with Dr. Mandy (her subordinate), Leslie from neuro faking maternity leave to spend two months in Azerbaijan, Eric from legal (present in the room) whose wife is a lesbian who told him so ("he threw up in a bag"), whose therapist just called him "pathetic," new pharmacist Brian selling Adderall to his daughter's classmates and dating one of them, ortho's Chris promoted, Megan jailed for picking Eric up because he had been "begging to be arrested," Jermaine's pièce de résistance that Steve and Emily from the downstairs café are full siblings after a 23andMe — they were triplets, the third a woman now in al-Qaeda — Helen from research running an OnlyFans that has blocked Eric, and a segue about husbands wanting guns.

"Guns for Women" spoofs a femvertising spot: a vagina-shaped firearm, a trigger that's "really hard to find," you have to "warm it up," it "doesn't go bang" — it goes "heyy," and the slogan "the only thing that stops a bad guy with a gun is a good girl with a va-gun" / "glunt," with Sheryl (Sherman) shooting the boyfriend in the crotch and leaving, ending "Available at the opposite of Dick's Sporting Goods."

A "Game of Thrones"-style sketch (Johnson as queen, Hernández as Sir Krestley, Dismukes as Sir Aleric, with dragon Thoraxis and eunuch Jhogo in the room) has the queen propositioning Sir Aleric, who keeps pausing to ask if Thoraxis is watching, "talks," and thinks he's hurting her, before requesting to be had "brother-style." She finally orders him to slay Thoraxis, the dragon "murders the absolute hell out of Sir Aleric," and the queen spends another lonely night pining for someone to do her brother-style.

Turnstile performs three pieces ("These Birds," "Sour," and a duet "Deep in the Night I'm Waiting for the Call"), and Dakota Johnson returns between numbers to thank them for "having my birthday with me."

Weekend Update covers Pete Hegseth's "no more drag shows, no fatties, no beardoes, no weirdos, no wimps, no radical" — "basically he's banning the entire career of Mike Myers"; CNN pulled mid-flight from Trump's Texas press pool; Trump's "Bowinkle" posture; AI execs signing a deal "morally binding, like the devil"; the new White House ballroom's armed drones, moat, laser gun, giant water slide; Trump telling Hispanic Heritage Month attendees "only a Hispanic could survive that fall" (no reaction because, Jost notes, "people prefer racism to make sense"); Vance saying the midterms are an "uphill climb"; Paxton's 275 out-of-state trips ("Damn, he must have needed a lot of abortions"); Republicans attacking James Talrico by calling him "low T" as he blows dandelion seeds; sending Vance to South Texas ("if that doesn't work, they'll send in the Chuppacabra"); Kristi Noem divorcing Brian ("I guess we'll never know why" — "always happy to separate another family"); Tennessee failing twice to execute a woman on death row ("Okay now do a silly one"); and a sincere moment on the Cornell sexual-assault case, agreeing this is "a good time to talk about men holding other men accountable" and pointing at "that one friend that seems like a nice guy but you know they're definitely not" — "weird privileged Ivy League types."

Cory Booker introduced three bills to stop the carrier named after Trump and instead honor a Black war hero — Trump's compromise is "his favorite Black war hero, Robert Downey Jr." Che brings on "Jason from GTA6 and Link from Legend of Zelda" (Dismukes), with Link insisting his outfit is "my adventure tunic," not a "little green dress," that he and Zelda are "friends and that's it," the ceramic wind flute "heals all your hearts," and Jason stepping on Link's fairy. Each plugs their own game.

When he tries to tell a story about being "robbed during a threesome" at 14, asks to say "fellatio" on TV, and admits he has never voted, Che cuts him off with "just be yourself, okay? That's who we hired."

A second Update guest, NYC matchmaker Debbie Mush (Heidi Gardner), tries to fix up Che with J.K. Rowling ("I can do a little better"), a sweet girl from "a really good family" who has "passed" but whose grave site is open for visits, then Mrs. Potato Head ("she's a potato … she's married but they're open"). # S52E03 Summary

## Cold Open — USS Lincoln Homecoming
Setting: USS Abraham Lincoln returning to San Diego after 322 days at sea. "Secretary of War Pete Hegseth" (cast member) addresses sailors with crude humor — fireball-cinnamon whiskey ammunition belt, jokes about suicide attempts, an "eight of you" punchline, and a bizarre "let's execute a guy in a wheelchair on live TV / fight Jake Paul" announcement. He unveils a new Department of War logo that looks identical to a Nazi eagle ("American eagle with a Nazi vibe"). RFK Jr. appears to brag about food-safety reforms ("cold and flu and listeria and measles and trench brain season") and 200,000 lbs of recalled meat. Closes with a pun on Linkin Park's "In the End" ("you tried so hard and got so far…") misattributed to Lincoln Park. Standard "Live from New York" outro.

New recurring bit: Hegseth's Department of War logo (Nazi eagle lookalike) — also picked up later in Weekend Update.

## Monologue — Shane Gillis
Self-deprecating stand-up about being overweight, a Bud Light commercial he hates seeing every ten minutes, a failed solo hike where he encountered a woman (Adidas tracksuit, gas-station-egg complexion), going to a NYC public gym (gay guys, women's neon shorts, TikTok background appearances), and using Joe Rogan's private gym/sauna in Texas. Closes with his "Adolf Hitler haircut" — "you're either gay or you're Hitler" gag.

Useful proper names to keep straight: Shane Gillis (host), Joe Rogan, Theo Von, Stephen (unidentified buddy in sauna). Bud Light brand name referenced as a sore point.

## Sketch 1 — Cop Talk
Talk-show parody where police hosts run guest interviews as arrests. Recurring cast: Officer Doyle Connolly and Officer Dan Carducci. Beats: an author named Lawrence Kornfeld (license address read on-air at "83 Broadview Drive, Connecticut"); a fake AA-meeting sponsor spot urging Doyle to attend; an artist accused of being on drugs and tased for possessing Altoids; a comedian who refuses to come onstage; a final guest described as "some broad from some TV show" who is drunk/high and gets a gun pulled on her.
