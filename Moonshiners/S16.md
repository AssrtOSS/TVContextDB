## S16E02

Episode S16E02 follows three crews colliding with an unusually early start to shine season.

Plot. In Cocke County, Tennessee, Mark and Digger gamble $70,000 on 20,000 bushels of discounted corn to make all-grain, no-sugar moonshine. They mash in five totes, add amylase enzyme to convert starches, and stash everything behind Kelly's hay barn. The mash stalls under 50°F; they rig heat lamps under reflective emergency blankets, dubbed "the spaceship." First run yields about 40 gallons at roughly 8 percent ABV instead of the usual 10. In Maggie Valley, North Carolina, Mike Cockrell and Jerry Benson take a $4,000 strawberry-jalapeno brandy order from an anonymous buyer, two months ahead of schedule. They build a copper-coil mash heater called the "little poppy box," run 20 gallons, and drop the liquor at an old knuckle boom dead-drop on a logging trail. In Franklin County, Virginia, Tim Smith plus Tickle, Henry, and Howard rush a 100-gallon run for a Canadian buyer. A stranger walks onto the still site; Henry grabs his shotgun, the crew relocates to night work. They meet Canadian buyer John Luke on a private runway, load four barrels onto his jet, and on the return get pulled over by unidentified cops in black SUVs.

Important character actions. Tim Smith produces and delivers to John Luke, gets pulled over on the way back, and connects the traffic stop to black SUVs that spooked Henry's grain supplier. Tickle greets John Luke at the jet, rejoining Tim's active crew. Henry confronts the stranger with the shotgun and recalls old-school tree-tying tactics as a joke. Howard rides with Tim, gets pulled over, and hands over registration from the glove box. Mark and Digger run the Tennessee corn gamble; Mark invents the heat-lamp rescue and pulls the first ~40-gallon run. Amanda and Kelly help at the farm where the hay barn hides the mash. Mike Cockrell takes the anonymous NC order, blames Mark Rogers after sabotage, dissolves the partnership with Jerry, and announces a solo return to Tennessee. Jerry Benson finds raccoon carcasses inside the drop-off buckets, blows up at Mike, and storms off on a four-wheeler.

Newly established names and terms. John Luke, the Canadian buyer arriving by jet. Mark Rogers, rival shiner whose liquor Mike found on his turf last season. Jerry Benson, Mike's longtime partner, full surname revealed here. Michael Cockrell, Mike's full name. David, a former partner of Mike and Mark Rogers who used to keep the same buyer supplied. Brandon, an unvetted fellow Tim is reluctant to bring onto still work. Amanda and Kelly, helpers at the mash-site farm. The "poppy box," Mike's mash warming rig. The "spaceship," Mark's heat-lamp and reflective-blanket tent. "Dead drop" at an old knuckle boom on a logging trail.

Continuity-relevant facts. Henry's grain supplier is cut off; Tim's crew must source covert grain before John Luke returns. Mike and Jerry lost last season to the Mark Rogers feud; their partnership formally ends in this episode. Mike plans a solo Tennessee comeback, already thousands in the hole. Tickle, Henry, and Howard are now active in Tim's Virginia operation. John Luke wants fast runway turnaround, raising per-trip risk. Mark and Digger owe 70 runs in under 60 days to break even on the corn, and Mark admits the debt is costing sleep. Mike notes he used to work with both Mark Rogers and David to keep the same anonymous buyer supplied — useful backstory for that feud.

Jokes and references useful later. Mike's capsaicin splash on his "damn pecker," with soap (not water) as the cure — likely to be referenced again. Mike's proverb "Tough titties, said the kitty, but the milk went dry," applied to holding onto Mark Rogers's disputed still cap. Mike's tasting note "It's corn hell west and crooked" for full-corn shine. Mike's "Moonshine Rebel" moniker surfaces in the location chyron. Henry's tree-tying flashback is played for laughs and may recur. The "spaceship" rig becomes Mark and Digger's recurring nickname for the heat-lamp setup. Mark quips the heat lamps would fry baby chickens into nuggets. Mike's capsaicin burn and the raccoon-in-the-brandy gag both land as season-typical gross-out humor.

Unresolved threads. Who Brandon is and why Tim is leery of him. Whether Mike's anonymous NC buyer resurfaces or whether the raccoon sabotage ends that pipeline. How Tim's crew replaces Henry's grain source without further law enforcement tipping. Whether the two black SUVs at the grain store and at the traffic stop are connected — ATF, federal agents, or coincidence. Whether Mark and Digger can hit 70 runs on the heat-lamp system under the 60-day clock. Mike's solo Tennessee comeback and any potential reconciliation with Mark Rogers or David. Jerry Benson's next move after walking out. The leftover strawberry mash and jalapenos still at Mike's site, now unmade.

## S16E03

# Moonshiners S16E03 Summary

## Episode Plot

**Josh Owens (Polk County, NC)** — Broke and desperate, Josh gambles on a "mining and shining" double operation. His buddy Bill fronts him a rock crusher, 4-inch dredge, and clean-up table on an IOU, putting Josh thousands in the hole before the season starts. Josh hauls a load of corn from Mark and Digger at $4.25/bushel, but then gets an urgent call from buyer Rick: 80 gallons/week on short notice. Josh realizes he cannot run the still, make mash, *and* gold-mine alone — he needs a partner.

**Mark Ramsey & Digger Manes (Cocke County, TN)** — They bought 20,000 bushels of grain (corn + ~2,000 bushels of soybeans) and have 60 days to move it. The soybeans are a liability: 59% protein, 40% fat, only ~1% starch. Digger insists they're useless for liquor ("putrefaction"). Mark wants to experiment; Amanda runs test mashes using **koji (Aspergillus mold)** to break down soybean starches. Four test batches: 100% soybeans (fail), soybeans+corn (sour/off-flavors), soybeans+corn+koji (clear, clean, +1 pint per 5-gal run), and corn control. The koji-enhanced mash yields extra alcohol without flavor penalty. Digger admits he was wrong; they'll scale the koji method.

**Mike Cockrell (Haywood County, NC → Tennessee)** — After a blowup with 7-year partner **Jerry Benson**, Mike loads the still (which he claims he helped build) and crosses back into Tennessee. He tracks down his old partner **Daniel Maner** (they ran a profitable Sevier County operation 7 seasons ago until Mike blew up Mark Rogers & Digger's still). Mike apologizes; Daniel is bitter but agrees to consider rejoining — *if* Mike returns Mark Rogers' stolen still cap and buries the hatchet. Mike produces the cap from his mantel, delivers it, and warns: if Mark or anyone crosses him again, "it's gonna be war."

## S16E06

# Moonshiners S16E06 Summary

## Episode Plot

Three parallel storylines converge around the pressure of the shine season:

**Mark, Digger & Jordan (Cocke County, TN)** — Attempting a Scotch-style peated whiskey using 100 lbs of malted corn smoked over peat. Their first hand-built smoke box suffers a design flaw: an overnight storm fans the fire, incinerating the corn. They redesign with a separate firebox (repurposed grill) ducting smoke away from the grain. The second attempt works — clean smoke, no fire — and Jordan (a Scotch expert from Islay) approves the peated corn. They distill a trial run: "hillbilly Scotch" with a subtle peat note balancing the corn sweetness. Jordan calls it "smoky perfection."

**Jerry Benson & Josh Owens (Haywood County, NC)** — After Josh blows up and quits, Jerry is left alone to fulfill an 80-gal/week ($8K/week) order. He scavenges four stainless barrels from a junkyard and builds a **vacuum still** with a manifold linking all barrels to his old copper cap/condenser. The vacuum lowers the boiling point, promising faster, cleaner runs without scorching. Risk: barrels could implode under vacuum. Josh returns, apologizes, and secures two extra days from their buyer Rick. They salvage a vacuum switch from an old furnace to auto-regulate pressure (cut pump on/off at set points). The regulated vacuum still tests perfectly — "game-changer," "never been done before."

**Mike & Daniel (Sevier County, TN)** — Reunited after 7 years, they harvest honeysuckle at peak bloom to revive their signature honeysuckle honey shine. They run a 10-jar rig, infusing fresh blossoms into the vapor path. The product matches their memory from 8 years ago. Unable to sell locally (market shifted to cheap corn liquor), Mike drives to Maggie Valley — Jerry's territory — and sells 10 gals to Jeff (Jerry's old buyer) at $175/gal. Jeff also orders 15 gals of **sassafras shine** at the same price. Mike asserts independence: "Jerry don't own all our customers."

## Newly Established Names / Terms

- **Vacuum still** — manifolded multi-barrel boiler under reduced pressure; lowers ethanol boiling point (172°F at sea level → much lower)
- **Manifold design** — connects 4 barrels to single cap/condenser; pulls even vacuum across all vessels
- **Vacuum switch / regulator** — salvaged from gas furnace inducer fan; auto-cycles pump to maintain set vacuum, prevents implosion
- **Peated corn / hillbilly Scotch** — 100% malted corn smoked over peat; Scotch-style flavor profile in Appalachian context
- **Smoke box redesign** — external firebox + metal ducting; separates heat from grain
- **Sassafras shine** — new specialty recipe ordered by Jeff; root beer–like flavor from sassafras root
- **Maggie Valley** — Jerry's operating territory; Mike encroaches to sell honeysuckle/sassafras

## S16E07

Moonshiners — S16E07 Summary

## Episode Plot

Franklin County, Virginia: Tim, Tickle, Howard and Henry deal with an armed rival syndicate linked to Brandon, who has moved his still site and scared off their Canadian buyer. The crew tests a protection dog at a canine training center; Howard volunteers as the bite guinea pig and gets his hand grabbed. The dog works but costs $20,000–25,000, about half the run's profits, so they scrap it and decide to move the still site. Tim then takes a cryptic call from a self-described "patriot" offering money and a meeting pin at a historical site. He brings Howard and Henry as backup and meets the Fincastle Company, 18th-century living historians who want period-correct moonshine and Indian-corn whiskey for America's 250th birthday; they offer a musket and drilling lessons as payment. Tim accepts and begins prepping a wood-fired, 18th-century-style still using his roughly 100–150-year-old copper "turnip type" still, but Indian corn is no longer grown commercially.

Cocke County, Tennessee: Mark, Digger and stillhand Kelly face a two-week deadline to move four silos of corn worth about $35,000 at break-even, and want higher-margin specialty whiskey. Digger follows his great-grandfather Hiram Parks' ("Papaw") soured-corn recipe: let the corn sour two or three days before mashing to borrow a sour-mash flavor without making true sour mash. They build mash for 200-gallon pots, fire the still, and a flash burns someone's eyebrows. The run succeeds — the sour note carries through and the whiskey tastes "off-the-chain good" — just as rain begins, which the crew credits to Papaw.

Haywood County, North Carolina: Jerry Benson and Josh Owens run Jerry's backwoods vacuum still with a gravity-fed tote filtration system for the first time. At 100 degrees the still produces alcohol, described as "unheard of," with no scorching, faster and cleaner output, around 135–140 proof. The 80-gallon-a-week order is back on track.

Separately, Josh Owens has a same-day corn-delivery run for Digger — 1,200 pounds to North Carolina — plus a liquor drop for a buyer named Rick. He and a companion buy Jimmy Red and heirloom corn from a South Carolina farm (Indian corn unavailable until fall, $10 a bag), but en route their truck catches fire with barrels aboard, Digger's corn burns, and Josh fears he has blown the Rick run.

## Important Character Actions

- Tim Smith: takes the risky coded phone call, meets the reenactors at a public historical site, commits to the period-correct order, and plans the wood-fired still setup.
- Howard: tests the guard dog's bite and is bitten on the hand; later joins Tim's still-site crew.
- Henry: accompanies Tim to the meeting and helps with corn and site prep.
- Tickle: fetches Jimmy Red and heirloom corn with Henry.
- Mark Ramsey / Digger Manes / Kelly Williamson: execute the soured-corn recipe, explain sweet versus sour mash, deal with nosy neighbor Jerry (who demands a cut or threatens to report them), and feed soured mash to Jerry's cows, which gives them the scours.
- Josh Owens: drives recklessly to the corn delivery, then loses control of the situation when the truck catches fire.
- Jerry Benson: operates the vacuum still's first run.

## Newly Established Names / Terms

- Fincastle Company — 18th-century living-history / reenactment group commissioning 250th-birthday whiskey.
- Jimmy Red corn — heirloom breed brought back from extinction; possible Revolutionary-era substitute for Indian corn.
- Indian corn — period-correct grain nobody grows anymore; main sourcing problem for the reenactor order.
- Hiram Parks / "Papaw" — Digger's great-grandfather, source of the soured-corn recipe.
- Sweet mash vs. sour mash — first-run fresh mash versus reused soured mash; the crew wants a sour-mash flavor profile without a true sour mash.
- "Turnip type" copper still — Tim's roughly 100–150-year-old still; wood-fired, no doubler, pre-1900s design.
- Chimley — period term for chimney; the new site also uses a worm/condenser, buckets, and no pumps.
- Brandon — armed rival syndicate member who moved his still site and scared the Canadian buyer.
- Rick — liquor buyer Josh is due to meet the same day as the corn delivery.
- Paige — named as part of the still-site setup crew.
- "The scours" — severe cow diarrhea; Jerry's cows get it from eating the soured mash.

## Continuity-Relevant Facts

- Season arc pressure: shine-season deadlines, and the 250th anniversary of American independence drives the reenactor order and period-correct grain requirements.
