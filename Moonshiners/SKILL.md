## Overall Plot Premise

The series dramatizes the lives of people producing illegal moonshine in the Appalachian Mountains of the United States — specifically in Kentucky, North Carolina, South Carolina, Tennessee, and Virginia. It follows a diverse group carrying on a roughly 200-year-old tradition passed down from their forefathers, depicting their liquor production efforts, law-evading techniques, interpersonal partnerships, family ties, and run-ins with state alcohol law enforcement. The premise is that of a continuing cat-and-mouse game between Appalachian moonshiners and the authorities (state ABC agents, deputy sheriffs) across each season's "shine run."

## World Setting

- Geography: Appalachian Mountains — rural, forested, mountainous terrain. Specific states referenced are 北卡罗来纳 (North Carolina), 南卡罗来纳 (South Carolina), 田纳西 (Tennessee), 弗吉尼亚 (Virginia), 肯塔基 (Kentucky); later episodes also touch 密西西比 (Mississippi).
- Law enforcement bodies: state ABC (Alcoholic Beverage Control) bureaus, county sheriff's offices, ATF (联邦烟酒枪炮及爆炸物管理局) in real-life background cases.
- Cultural setting: deep Appalachian / Southern U.S. mountain culture — strong family lineage, "shine runner" tradition passed down generations, "outlaw" self-identity, distrust of government, off-grid living.
- Season cycle: a "shine season" runs roughly late fall through early spring; summer is the off-season for repair, scouting, and (sometimes) legal distillery work.

## Speaking Style / Register

Cast members speak in heavy rural Appalachian / Southern American English with:

- thick regional accent, dropped final consonants, "y'all" (你们这帮家伙), "fixin' to" (打算), "ain't" (没有/不)
- frequent use of moonshiner code slang: "the law", "the man", "shine", "run", "the still", "cash money"
- rustic, plain-spoken, often folksy and humorous; father/mentor-to-apprentice banter
- narrator (Jeremy Schwartz) speaks in a measured, dramatic documentary voice-over tone — distinguish from on-screen dialogue in translation.

On-screen text (location chyrons, case captions): translate subtitles only; do not translate on-screen graphics literally if they are part of the show's stylized title cards (e.g., "OUTLAW BROTHERHOOD" 可保留英文片名或在首次出现时附译文).

## Cultural Context Notes for Translation

- "Moonshine" carries outlaw / folk-hero connotations in Appalachia; preserve that flavor — avoid neutralizing to neutral 中性词.
- Family relationships matter: many pairs are 叔侄 / 兄弟 / 父子; preserve relational nuance.
- "Outlaw" is a self-identifier, almost a badge of honor here — don't translate it as 罪犯 unless the context is genuinely accusatory (e.g., a law-enforcement speaker).
- The show has been publicly questioned by Virginia ABC officials who state no real illegal production is occurring; this is a show-known controversy. If referenced, translate neutrally as 节目真实性争议.
- Some early-season Popcorn Sutton material is recycled from the 2002 documentary "This is the Last Dam Run of Likker I'll Ever Make" — only translate if directly referenced.
