## S05E01

# Son of a Critch — S05E01 Summary

## Episode Plot
It is the last summer before Mark Critch's final year of high school, and Regatta Day has arrived in St. John's. Mary yanks Mark out of bed; Pop and his "club" friends are already prepping a booth (and liberating Mary's flowers). Mike Sr. covers the official call on VOCM — the Regatta is a go. Mike Sr. and co-anchor Dick Dunphy then need someone to wear the "Bursey Meats Wild Bologna" mascot suit after the summer student calls in drunk, so Mike Sr. ropes Mark into a $50 day job on the grounds that "every part, no matter how small, is a chance to ply my craft." Mark is mocked by kids ("big wiener"), taunted by Pop's carnie-rigged games, hit with free bolognas, bitten by a dog, and attempts a brooding Method-actress monologue as the bologna breaks down his Italian-vs-German name origins. Fox turns up, claims he is only the 88th most embarrassing thing she has seen him do, and the two enjoy games together. Pop's balloon-toss booth is rigged (and he openly admits it), but Fox handily beats him. Meanwhile, Mary's Tourism Department "cod-kissing booth" under-performs because she has no prizes (only "Soiree" buttons). By the end Mark has survived the day; Mike Sr. pays him $50 in cash; Mark uses the money to buy Fox ice cream.

## Important Character Actions
- Mark: sleeps through the morning, resists working, accepts the Bologna mascot gig for $50, gamely endures ridicule, chases the dog that bit him, and ultimately spends his pay on Fox.
- Mike Sr.: anchors VOCM's official Regatta coverage live, then recruits (blackmails) Mark into wearing the mascot suit so he can return to the broadcast.
- Dick Dunphy: fellow VOCM reporter, abandons his end of the lakeside call to hit the beer tent; his slot is filled by a stand-in named Mike Campbell.
- Mike Campbell (Pop): dubbed in at the last minute; broadcasts the race with comically tautological commentary, then sneaks off to the beer tent with Dick.
- Pop: rigs his club's booth games, mocks Mark, and spars with Fox at the balloon toss.
- Mary: draws tourists to her under-resourced cod-kissing booth on behalf of the Come Home Year / "Soiree" tourism push.
- Fox: shows up unannounced, defends Mark from bullies, then hustles Pop's rigged games.

## Newly Established Names / Terms
- Wild Bologna / Bursey Meats Wild Bologna — Regatta sponsor whose oversized mascot suit Mark wears; its jingle ("Listen for the call of the Wild Bologna") is also a sponsor-credit vehicle for VOCM.
- "Soiree" — local tourism brand / button, tied to the Come Home Year promotional campaign Mary is staffing.
- Cod-kissing booth — Mary's (unlucky) Tourism Department attraction.
- Mike Campbell — Pop's real given name, used on-air when he fills in for Dick Dunphy.
- Mike Jr. and Fox's brother / "Silver Fox" Stephen — referenced by Fox in passing ("the second-worst person in this family"). Fox's mother Suzanne and her husband Paul Royle are also name-checked.
- Royal St. John's Regatta — set up here as the official start of summer and the biggest day of the Newfoundland calendar, providing backdrop for later S5 episodes.

## Continuity-Relevant Facts
- Mark is entering his final year of high school (echoing the SKILL's note that S5 sends him to ~15).
- Mike Sr. is pronounced the official Regatta caller on VOCM, consistent with his status as a news reporter there; he still collaborates regularly with Dick Dunphy.
- Mike Jr. is named but absent from this episode (Fox's line is the only reference).
- Pop is shown as active and spry — not yet exhibiting the dementia symptoms the SKILL notes became pronounced in the S4 finale.
- Mary's career pivot (the SKILL has her enrolling at a career college in S4) has continued into a Tourism Department gig.
- Fox and Mark are now openly a couple (he calls her his girlfriend; she treats him like a boyfriend). The "88th most embarrassing" line also nods to a running bit about Mark's long history of embarrassments.

## Jokes / References Useful for Later Episodes
- "Lazy as a cut dog" — Mary and Mike Sr.'s recurring put-down for Mark.
- Pop as a carnival-rigging hustler — established gag that can be replayed whenever Pop runs a booth or game.
- Dick Dunphy's beer-tent streak — Mike Sr. is stuck covering for him, a dynamic to revisit.

## Unresolved Threads

# S05E03 — Debating God, Lobster, and the Future

## Episode Plot

Mark joins the school debating club but treats it like a standup set, losing to Cara on the "jeans in school" topic after recycling a denim pun three times. Fox, also a member, scolds him for not taking it seriously. Sister Rose then drops a bombshell: the Premier is planning a referendum to abolish Newfoundland's 275-year-old denominational school system. She recruits Mark and other Holy Heart students to appear on the cable call-in show "Community Chat" (Channel 9, Wednesdays at 7) to defend Catholic education. She explicitly casts Mark as her puppet — "I will be Jim Henson, and you will be my Muppet" — and scripts his talking points.

Meanwhile, at VOCM, owner Bill Bartlett audits the books: advertisers are fleeing since the cod moratorium, half the province is moving to Alberta, and he wants cuts. He reveals he owns the company house the Critches live in (a decades-old arrangement offsetting Mike Sr.'s low salary) and threatens to demolish it for a moving-truck rental lot, since the city has grown and it's now prime highway real estate. He also discovers Dick Dunphy lives in the basement, prompting "Am I housing the entire station?"

Mary brings home free lobsters — the Tourism Department is pushing lobster now that cod is gone, with a "Great Lobster Boil" planned on the waterfront. Pop is disgusted: in his day lobster was poor-people food ("bottom feeders"). Bartlett stays for dinner, during which Pop loudly complains about the house's disrepair and Bartlett notices another empty bedroom — Mike Jr. has apparently moved out.

On "Community Chat," Mark initially recites Sister Rose's scripted pro-Catholic lines (the Bible is his favourite book — "war, romance, zombies"), but the debate spirals: Fox (a United Church kid who transferred to Holy Heart) clashes with a Protestant girl on-air, a suspiciously familiar-sounding "youth" calls in to support religion, and the host's sign-off is interrupted. In the final segment, when the host asks for a show of hands on the referendum question, Mark breaks from the script and delivers a genuine speech: the Protestant kids are no different from him, he lives closer to their school, sectarian divisions "should have stayed in the old country" (quoting Pop), and merging schools would mean "more friends." He credits his teachers for teaching him to think for himself — a line that ironically indicts Sister Rose.

Back at VOCM, Bartlett surveys the station declaring "out with the old, in with the new," hinting at reorganisation. Adult Mark narrates the episode's thesis: change creeps up on you, and the things you thought were immovable shift overnight.

## Important Character Actions

- **Mark**: loses the debate by turning it into comedy; is recruited by Sister Rose as a mouthpiece for the anti-referendum Catholic position; goes off-script on live TV and delivers a sincere pro-unification speech, effectively betraying Sister Rose's trust.
- **Sister Rose**: rallies students against the school-system merger; scripts Mark's lines and directs him like a Muppeteer; her control backfires when Mark thinks for himself.
- **Fox**: present at both the debating club and the TV show; defends herself physically when a Protestant girl calls her a "red-headed hussy" on-air; her United Church background is revealed (her mother fought to get her into Holy Heart).
- **Mike Sr.**: navigates Bartlett's cost-cutting pressure; tries to defend the company-house arrangement; watches Mark's TV appearance from home.
- **Mary**: brings lobster home from her Tourism Department job; hosts Bartlett for dinner, awkwardly.
- **Pop**: hates lobster, hates change; quotes the Terms of Union (Term 17) defending Catholic school rights; his "old country" remark is later echoed by Mark on TV.
- **Bartlett**: threatens the Critches' housing arrangement; eyes VOCM restructuring; reveals he also owns the lot where Dick Dunphy's basement apartment sits.
- **Dick Dunphy**: revealed to live in the Critch house basement (not previously established); absent from the episode itself.
- **Cara**: wins the debate against Mark; established as a competent, serious debater.

## Newly Established Names / Terms

- **Community Chat** — cable TV call-in show on Channel 9, Wednesdays at 7pm; up against "The Price Is Right"; described as a "terrible slot for a terrible show."
- **Term 17** — the clause in Newfoundland's Terms of Union with Canada guaranteeing the church's right to administer education; Pop cites it as constitutional grounds for keeping denominational schools.
- **Denominational school system / referendum** — the Premier's plan to merge Catholic, Anglican, United, Presbyterian, Salvation Army, and Seventh-day Adventist schools into one public system; the show positions this as the central political backdrop of S05.
- **Great Lobster Boil** — Tourism Department event on the waterfront, part of the pivot from cod to lobster tourism.
- **Dick Dunphy's basement apartment** — newly revealed: Dick lives in the Critch house's basement, extending the company-house arrangement.

## Continuity-Relevant Facts

- The cod moratorium (referenced since S4) continues to devastate NL's economy; advertisers are abandoning VOCM, and half the province is relocating to Alberta.
- VOCM's financial decline is accelerating under Bartlett's ownership; the company-house perk that has sustained the Critches for decades is now under threat.
- Mike Jr. has moved out of the family home (his basement bedroom is empty); his current whereabouts are unspecified.
- Pop is still lucid and opinionated here — no dementia symptoms visible — but his worldview (sectarian separation, old-country grudges) is gently being passed down and then transcended by Mark.
- Fox's religious background is clarified: she is United Church, not Catholic; her mother fought to transfer her to Holy Heart, a Catholic school, and she lives next door to the school.
- Mark and Fox are a functioning couple; she calls him out when he's foolish but stands by him.
- The school-system referendum is positioned as the season's defining political event, echoing the real 1995-97 Newfoundland referendum on denominational school reform.
- Mark's TV appearance and his break from Sister Rose's script represent a turning point: he is beginning to form his own political and moral opinions rather than performing others' lines.

# S05E04 — Halloween

# Son of a Critch — S05E02 Episode Summary

*(Note: The provided aligned subtitle file appears to contain the Season 5 premiere — first day of Grade 12, cod moratorium announcement — which is S05E01. The summary below reflects the content in the file.)*

## Episode Plot
Season 5 opens on the first day of Grade 12 (1991–92 school year). Fisheries Minister John Crosbie announces a total shutdown of the northern cod fishery — the largest layoff in Canadian history (30,000 jobs). The news upends St. John's: fishermen are locked out of the announcement venue, protests erupt, and the Critch family confronts what the collapse means for their futures. Mark, now 17, faces a course-selection deadline and wrestles with choosing a practical trade (Shop) versus his dream of comedy/acting. After a crisis of confidence, a talk with Pop, and Mr. Lewis's "parachute" metaphor, he stays in Advanced Writing and Theatre Arts.

## Important Character Actions
- **Mark**: Drives to school in a beater car (Middle Fox has learner's permit); attempts to drop Creative Writing for Shop; reads Frost's "The Road Not Taken" in class; confides fear of failure to Pop; ultimately recommits to arts stream.
- **Mike Sr.**: Reports live on the moratorium; tries to enter the locked-down announcement; gets shoved back by police; insists on journalistic duty despite Mary's warnings.
- **Mary**: Works the "Soiree" (St. John's 100th birthday / Come Home Year); argues with Mike Sr. about celebrating while the fishery dies; serves processed American fish sticks for supper.
- **Pop**: Joins the protest ("Shillelagh Law"); takes a black eye; tells Mark "nobody ever caught anything worth keeping by hugging the shoreline" — urging him to push into deep water.
- **Mike Jr.**: Downplays the crisis (notes retraining package); supports Mark's practical pivot.
- **Fox**: Reassures Mark he'll make it; worst case = VOCM like dad/brother.
- **Mr. Chafe**: Returns from retirement as a substitute (wife left him, took everything); teaches both Woodworking and Advanced Writing; cynically reduces Frost to "jobs."
- **Father Lewis (Mr. Kevin Lewis)**: Counsels Mark with the parachute metaphor — better to go broke doing what you love than what you hate.

## Continuity-Relevant Facts

## S05E05

# S05E05 — A Wake for the Fishery

## Episode Plot

Pop tells Mark that comedy is "tragedy plus time" and urges him to "stick it to the arseholes in power." Meanwhile, Mike Jr. prepares to fly to Ontario to see Amanda, who's been away at school for a year and a half. Mike Sr. receives an invitation to afternoon tea at the Confederation Building with the Premier and his wife Ellie. Mary is thrilled (she wants a new dress); Mike Sr. resists because he's writing a story about the Premier and needs distance.

Pop's dementia worsens — he can't work the TV remote or his alarm clock and gets confused when Mark offers to wake him ("A wake."). Pop plans to join a "peaceful sit-in" at the Legislature to mourn the dead fishery and tells Mark not to tell his parents. Mark insists on going with him and skips school.

At VOCM, Dick Dunphy tips Mike Sr. to a brewing story — a million-dollar untendered contract in the Premier's office, rumoured to have gone to the Premier's brother-in-law's sign company for "Soiree" buttons and hat logos. Mr. Bartlett tells Mike Sr. to take the day off for the tea but asks him to lobby the Premier for a land rezoning (Bartlett had donated heavily to the Premier's campaign).

Mike Sr. and Mary go to the tea. The Premier thanks Mike for past scoops (referencing Mike's Meech Lake coverage from S4E3). Mike keeps reaching for his notepad; Mary keeps telling him to put his press pass away. In Ottawa, Amanda tells Mike Jr. she's been offered a copywriting job there that could lead to on-air work; Mike Jr. suggests he could move to Ottawa to be near her, but she is noncommittal.

Pop and Mark arrive at the Confederation Building with a small coffin draped in black, the Premier's face airbrushed off the portrait mounted on it. Security won't let them in. The protest swells into a noisy Irish wake — drumming, chanting "We want the Premier!" — and inside Mark watches fishermen sing a protest song ("Empty Nets," with verses about empty nets, foreign trawlers, and bankers). Pop's words about comedy land differently now, and Mark wishes he could "write a joke the way he writes a song."

Inside, the Premier offers Mike Sr. the job of Communications Director ("Help fix things before they get all twisted"). Mike Sr. asks for time. The Premier also performs for Mary and Ellie — accordion folk songs, joking that he's "the singing Premier," the first elected Premier who can play accordion. The coffin is hoisted up the outside of the building; the Premier sees it from his window and shouts "Clear the room!" Officer Butt tries to evict the protesters and gets called a sellout — "Your uncle's a fisherman! Shame!"

The Premier finally comes out and delivers a rambling, self-mocking speech: he's "in the business of avoiding responsibility and I'm doing a damn fine job." He announces the federal government will extend fishery workers' financial supports by 10 weeks, and that he will not run for a third term. Mark takes the stage and does an impression of the Premier mid-speech; the Premier joins in with self-deprecating jokes ("I was born with my head up my arse," "two-faced," pulling out two faces from his collar). The wake becomes a standup set and the crowd cheers.

Mary and Mike Sr. leave the tea to join the yelling. Pop praises Mark: "Not only did you do an impression... you made one. Sometimes you can say the unsayable with a joke, because sometimes the funniest joke is the truth." Mark's voice-over: "No matter how scary something is, you take away its power when you laugh at it." Back at home, Mr. Bartlett has messaged Mike Jr. offering him the Head of the News Department at VOCM — a role Mark notes conflicts with Dad's. Mike Sr. says "No... congratulations," meaning Bartlett is congratulating Mike Jr. on something separate, while the Head of News offer remains open. Final voice-over: "Where there's laughter, there's still hope."

## Important Character Actions

- Mark: skips school to accompany Pop to the protest, watches the fishermen sing, and takes the stage for his first real public standup set — an impression of the Premier that defuses a politically tense crowd.
- Pop: shows fresh dementia symptoms (TV remote, alarm clock, "wake" confusion) but is still lucid enough to plan and lead a protest, refusing to leave when Mark tries to take him home; is moved to call Mark's impression a breakthrough.
- Mike Sr.: receives the untendered-contract tip from Dick, accepts the Premier's tea invitation under pressure from Mary, is offered Communications Director, and is asked by Bartlett to lobby for rezoning — his journalistic integrity is being squeezed from both sides.
- Mary: pushes hard for the tea (new dress), enjoys coffee with Ellie, is offered a full-time tourism job by Bartlett, and ultimately leaves the tea to join the protest.
- Mike Jr.: visits Amanda in Ottawa, considers moving there, and is offered Head of News at VOCM by Bartlett.
- Amanda: tells Mike Jr. about an Ottawa copywriting offer; is touched but does not commit to a long-distance plan.
- Mr. Bartlett: tells Mike Sr. to take the day, asks him to lobby for rezoning, offers Mary a full-time tourism job, and offers Mike Jr. Head of News at VOCM.
- Dick Dunphy: leaks the untendered-contract rumour to Mike Sr.
- The Premier: thanks Mike for Meech Lake coverage, offers Comms Director, plays accordion, announces 10-week federal support extension, announces he will not seek a third term, then leans into the standup set with self-deprecating material.
- Officer Butt: tries to clear protesters, gets heckled as a sellout with a fisherman uncle.

# S05E07 — Christmas at the Critches

## Episode Plot
The Critch household wrestles with a broken artificial tree on a snow-less "green Christmas." Mary, on the phone, admits this is her first Christmas without her own family.
