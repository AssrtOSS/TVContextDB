## Official synopsis (Netflix)

The story of Sam Bankman-Fried and Caroline Ellison, two hyper-smart, ambitious young idealists who tried to remake the global financial system in the blink of an eye — before they were accused of stealing $8 billion and became Gen Z's own Bonnie & Clyde.

## World setting

- **Primary world:** The cryptocurrency and effective-altruism ecosystem of the late 2010s through 2022, centered on crypto trading firm Alameda Research (founded 2017) and crypto exchange FTX (founded 2019), with FTX's headquarters in the Bahamas.
- **Secondary worlds:** Wall Street hedge funds (where Sam and Caroline meet in 2016); elite academia (both are children of successful academics, raised as prodigies); U.S. federal courtrooms (the 2022 collapse, bankruptcy, wire-fraud charges, and sentencing); the Bahamas penthouse-cum-office where FTX operated.
- **Timeframe:** Dual timeline — forward from the 2016 meeting, and the fraught 2022 period of FTX's collapse. The series covers the full arc from idealistic beginnings to criminal accusations.

## Overall plot premise

Sam Bankman-Fried and Caroline Ellison are two socially awkward math prodigies who meet on Wall Street in 2016. Bonding over shared outcast status and a belief in "effective altruism" (the philosophy of earning money to give it away), they build Alameda Research and then FTX into a global crypto empire. Their personal and professional lives become inseparable as they pursue the mission of remaking the global financial system while postponing philanthropy with the rationale that they can save more lives later. When FTX collapses in 2022 amid accusations that $8 billion in customer funds was stolen, their relationship — euphoric, hubristic, and eventually destructive — is put under the microscope. The series is a character study and love story shadowed by ego and sadness, not a simple villain narrative.

## Speaking styles

- **Sam Bankman-Fried:** Socially awkward, "almost dallying with reality's demands"; starts cute, becomes delusional. Math/programmer cadence; algorithms matter more than Armani; bewildered by social cues.
- **Caroline Ellison:** "A mass of cluttered, sped-up sentences and nervous micro-grins"; fast, anxious speech patterns; socially outcast; geeky sidekick energy.
- **Shared dynamic:** "Love on the hedge fund spectrum"; two prodigies raised by successful academics; Ivy League self-righteousness coating moral erosion; intimate, morally conflicted dialogue rather than courtroom exposition.
- **Supporting voices:** Gary Wang as comic relief; Nishad Singh as the shocked moral center; CZ as cheerfully malicious/cynical.

## Cultural context

- Based on the real 2022 collapse of FTX, one of the most notorious financial scandals in recent American history; U.S. District Judge Lewis A. Kaplan (2024) called it potentially the "greatest financial fraud ever perpetrated in this country."
- Rooted in the effective-altruism movement and its "earn to give" doctrine, popular among elite-academic and tech circles.
- Produced by Higher Ground Productions (Barack and Michelle Obama), with Graham Moore (The Imitation Game) as creator/showrunner and Jacqueline Hoyt as co-showrunner.
- Inspired by New York Magazine reporting; blends documented public events with imagined "off-camera" intimate moments.
- Release model: all 8 episodes dropped simultaneously on Netflix (November 19, 2026), fitting the binge-watch limited-series format.
- The series treats the subjects as complex idealists rather than simple villains — translation should preserve moral ambiguity, not flatten characters into heroes or criminals.
