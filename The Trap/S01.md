## S01E01

# S01E01 — "The Monster"

## Plot

A reclusive crime novelist living under a pseudonym has spent the past decade confined to her house following her sister's unsolved murder. She believes she has just spotted the killer on a televised press conference and decides to act: she calls the cold-case detective, begins writing a confessional crime novel modeled on her own trauma, and grants her first-ever personal interview to a journalist she has hand-picked.

The episode intercuts three timelines:

- **Present-day action**: the novelist's reclusive routine, her calls to police and journalist, a session with her therapist, and the meeting with the journalist.
- **A novel-within-the-show**: a manuscript titled *My Sister* whose protagonist "Sophie" is an obvious analogue of the author — same murder, same scream-in-the-night, same inability to describe the killer's face.
- **Ten-year-old flashbacks**: Anna's funeral, police questioning, and the revelation that Anna's body carried an unidentified DNA sample besides the sister's.

The episode ends on a confrontation in which the novelist is told that the second DNA on her sister's body is her own — implying she was closer to the crime than anyone (including her) has acknowledged.

## Important Character Actions

- **Lisa Wilking / "Lisa Konrad"** — calls K11 (Leon Weber is out, Akbari answers); researches him online; later requests Robert Lenzen by name for her interview; tells her agent Pia and publisher Steinmayr not to reveal the new project; confronts her therapist; tells the detective she can no longer describe the killer's face; reveals the unpublished novel is autobiographical.
- **Yuma** — caretaker/aide who delivers groceries, mail, the agent's flowers; feeds Bukowski; collects laundry; will return Wednesday.
- **Pia** — agent; protective, warns Lisa to take care of herself; is sworn to secrecy about the new book.
- **Mr. Steinmayr** — publisher/long-time friend; sets a 30 June deadline for the new book; is surprised Lisa will both write a crime novel and grant a press interview.
- **Dr. Anger** (mentioned only) — therapist for whom Yuma is to make coffee.
- **Lisa's therapist** — treats Lisa's writing as escapism; is unconvinced by her "monster on TV" claim; reframes her as "a traumatized woman with too much anxiety" and offers to adjust medication.
- **Detective Leon Weber (K11)** — out when Lisa calls; Rainer tells him Lisa rang, "last time almost two years ago"; no new charges against her.
- **Akbari** — K11 switchboard officer who logs Lisa's call.
- **Robert Lenzen** — journalist at *Reflekt Berlin*; arrives at Lisa's house for the first personal interview she has ever given.
- **Flashback detective** — questions Lisa at Anna's funeral; later, in another memory, dismisses her inability to describe the killer; suggests she draw him because she is "a painter."

## Newly Established Names / Terms

- **Anna Wilking** — Lisa's younger sister (3 years younger), murdered.
- **Lisa Konrad** — pseudonym adopted ~10 years ago; awarded the German Literature Prize; born 1987; lives near Munich with her dog.
- **Bukowski** — Lisa's dog (likely a Charles Bukowski reference).
- **K11 / Criminal Unit One** — Berlin homicide unit handling Anna's case.
- **The Monster** — the killer, as Lisa calls him in therapy; the episode's title.
- **"Sophie"** — protagonist of Lisa's in-progress novel *My Sister*, a thinly-veiled Lisa analogue.
- ***My Sister*** — Lisa's new crime novel; deadline30 June.
- **Capital Press Conference** — televised political event where Lisa believes she sees the killer.
- **Ares Pantos** — journalist at Capital Radio, seen/mentioned during the press conference clip.
- **Ellen Demir** — *Network Berlin* politics/society reporter; 687 followers, 128 contacts; appears in Lisa's LinkedIn searches alongside Lenzen.
- **Robert Lenzen** — senior editor, investigative desk, *Reflekt Berlin*; b. 1974, age 51; ex-*Guardian* (to 2010), ex-*Münchener* foreign desk (to 2015), freelance Middle East correspondent (to 2023); speaks four languages; chess player; marathon runner; divorced from Alexandra Faust (daughter of *Münchener* publisher) in 2016; father of a 12-year-old daughter.
- **Steinmayr** — Lisa's publisher; old friend; gifted/sold her the publisher's guest mansion for 50% of the royalties on her next five books.
- **Pia** — Lisa's literary agent.

## Continuity-Relevant Facts

- Anna was killed on **5 July 2015**; case file is **closed**.
- Cause of death: stabbed **eight times** in the torso.
- The murder weapon (knife) carried **only Lisa's prints** — implying it was wiped.
- Anna's apartment yielded **~60 fingerprints** from a birthday party the weekend before; none matched party guests.
- **Two non-victim DNA samples** were recovered from Anna's body: one matches Lisa; the other is unidentified and presumed to be the killer's.
- Lisa was supposed to be back with Anna by **9 PM** the night of the murder and was late.
- Lisa has **never left the house** she moved into ten years ago (the publisher's guest mansion).
- Only her therapist knows Anna was murdered; only her closest intimates know where she lives.
- Lisa calls the police repeatedly; Leon's colleague notes the previous call was almost two years ago.
- Germany has held a **nationwide DNA database since 1998**, allowing cold-case re-tests.
- Lisa is on medication for anxiety/psychiatric trauma and fears her dosage will be increased.
- Lisa has hidden the existence and content of the new book from her publisher until now.

## Jokes, References, Useful Motifs

- **Italy "vacation"**: Lisa's recurring imagined trips (Rome, Vespa, market peppers and olives, "buongiorno/grazie" greeters, "Madame"). She meets "Anna" there but also the monster.
- **Vespa line** — "Only her Vespa was red" — recurring color-of-blood motif.
- **The novel mirrors reality almost verbatim** ("Sophie"/Lisa, the scream, the boyfriend who leaves, the parents who withdraw, the detective who doesn't believe her). Useful as a diegetic frame for foreshadowing.
- **"The reason"** — leitmotif repeated by both Robert Lenzen and the novel manuscript; the question of motive is flagged as the next thing to be written.
- **Anna "hated cut flowers"** — establishes a tiny character beat used at her funeral.
- **"Monster"** — Lisa's name for the killer, used in therapy and as the episode title.
- **Bukowski (the dog)** — likely named for Charles Bukowski; may signal Lisa's literary tastes beyond the bestseller crowd.
- **Lisa is also a painter** — established by the flashback detective; "Have you even seen my paintings?" implies she has shown her art privately, perhaps to Anna.
- **Journalist choice** — Lenzen notes he never asked for the interview; Lisa's reason is deliberately withheld, suggesting a hidden motive for picking him specifically.

## Unresolved Threads

- Who is the killer? Lisa claims to have seen him on the **Capital Press Conference** broadcast; the clip names **Ares Pantos** but does not confirm whether he (or someone else on screen) is the suspect.
- Why did Lisa pick **Robert Lenzen** specifically? His prior Middle East and investigative work, his ex-wife's publishing family, and his existing daughter may all be relevant.
- Will Lenzen be told who his interviewee really is? (Lisa's real name and the murder are still secret.)
- Will Lisa succeed in obtaining the killer's DNA, as she states is necessary?
- Will the police reopen Anna's file, now that Lisa is openly writing about it?
- Whose is the second DNA sample, and what does it mean that Lisa herself was that close to the murder scene?
- Will the therapist adjust Lisa's medication, and could that suppress the recognition she claims?
- The publisher's backstory — why the guest mansion was sold at 50% royalties and what Steinmayr knows about Lisa's past.
- The "fantasy vacations" — extent of hallucination vs. deliberate escapism.
- Lisa's paintings — what she has depicted and whether any of them are of the killer.
- Whether the manuscript *My Sister* will become a public accusation before Lisa has proof.

## S01E02

# The Trap — S01E02 "The Novel" (小说)

## Plot

The episode weaves two timelines. In the present, publisher Norbert Rudow drives through the night to confront his star author, Lisa Konrad, furious that she's abandoned literary fiction for a crime novel. Their argument reveals long-buried shared history: Lisa's younger sister Anna was murdered ~10 years ago, the case was never solved, and Lisa has been living reclusively in Norbert's guest villa near Munich ever since a psychiatric breakdown. Norbert demands a preview of the manuscript before publication. In parallel, journalist Robert Lenzen is assigned by his magazine Reflekt to interview Lisa; suspicious of her anonymity, he hires an investigator (Bassem) to dig up her real name, address, medical records and relationships.

The past timeline follows Sophie (Lisa's real name) on the night of Anna's death: Anna's cheerful voicemail plays ("Leave some good news after the beep!"), Sophie arrives late, finds her sister dead and locks eyes with a male killer. Police process the scene, find blood letters "L" and "I" (possibly "E"), note Anna was left-handed, and take Sophie's statement in a van. Years later, Lisa takes self-defense lessons and has surveillance cameras installed around the villa.

## Important Character Actions

- Norbert drives overnight from a50th birthday event, confronts Lisa about her genre switch, and demands pre-publication access to the manuscript.
- Lisa reveals Anna's murder, explains why writing is her way of processing trauma, and insists on the interview as a deadline to force completion.
- Sophie/Lisa discovers Anna's body, screams "Anna, stay with me!", and freezes in shock as the killer flees.
- Robert accepts the Reflekt assignment, then secretly commissions Bassem to investigate Lisa Konrad off-server, seeking childhood, education, relationships, health records and the villa address.
- Pia, Norbert's proxy, warns Lisa that Norbert wants to see the novel before publication; Lisa insists Norbert call her directly.
- Lisa commissions a security system (UTA) and Wi-Fi cameras covering the driveway and ground floor, and trains in combat basics (uppercut, duck, fall-and-rise drills).
- Norbert calls to ask whether he leaked the villa photo himself; Lisa counters that the land registry would expose "Lisa Sophie Wilking."

## Newly Established Names/Terms

- Lisa Sophie Wilking — Lisa's real name (registered only to a P.O. box).
- Lisa Konrad — her pseudonym, used for all published work over ~10 years.
- Light and Sounds (《光与声》) — debut novel under the Konrad name, which saved Rudow Publishing from bankruptcy.
- Anna Wilking — Lisa's sister, murdered ~10 years earlier; left-handed; hated cut flowers.
- Reflekt — Berlin magazine commissioning the interview.
- Robert Lenzen — the assigned journalist; veteran of Syria-war reporting; ex-wife keeps Lisa Konrad's books; culture-desk colleague "Katrin" handles logistics; contact "Bassem" handles off-book investigations.
- Pia — Lisa's assistant/handler and Norbert's informal envoy.
- Inspector Akbari, Narges, Leon, Hannes — police on Anna's case.
- Marc — Norbert's late boyfriend; vanished from both Norbert's and Lisa's lives; his name surfaces around a party scene mixing pills and champagne where Norbert's wife later died by suicide.
- Mr. Faber — man who came to Anna's home to accept an advertising concept the night of the murder; left his DNA via the doorbell.
- The Galant — magazine that ran a 2012 stock photo of the "guest villa near Munich," spooking Lisa.
- UTA Security Systems — installing cameras and a provider connection outside the house.
- Al-Manar / Darabi Clan — Bassem's other Beirut/Lebanon investigation files Robert is reviewing.

## Continuity-Relevant Facts

- Lisa has been Rudow Publishing's top author for ~10 years; every Konrad novel topped the bestseller charts.
- Rudow Publishing was near bankruptcy when Lisa signed; her debut made it solvent.
- After her breakdown, Lisa refused to return to her apartment, her parents' home, or go outside; Norbert gave her the guest villa as a permanent residence.
- Anna was murdered in summer; the killer was never caught; Sophie/Lisa never saw the man's face clearly, only eye contact.
- Crime-scene blood writing: letters "L" and "I," possibly intended to become "E" or something else.
- Anna was left-handed — flagged for forensic comparison.
- Norbert's wife died by suicide; Marc, Norbert's boyfriend at the time, left both their lives afterward.
- Norbert waited years before broaching Anna's death because "medical staff told me not to ask you anything."
- The villa is registered to Rudow Publishing (2012), not to Lisa, but the land registry would reveal her real name.
- Sophie/Lisa was late to Anna's that night because of work; Anna had a surprise planned.
- Lisa's therapist was a "word of mouth" recommendation from Norbert; Norbert claims never to have met her.
- Robert's contact "Katrin" handles Reflekt logistics without him; he's described as a political-investigation journalist, not literary — which is exactly why Lisa wanted him.
- Lisa has training scheduled and self-defense/surveillance infrastructure being installed across the property, including a feed to her laptop.

## Jokes / References for Later Episodes

- Anna's voicemail greeting — "Hi, it's Anna! Leave some good news after the beep!" — anchors the death-night timeline.
- Anna's hatred of cut flowers: childhood flashback of little Anna promising never to pick flowers again because "they'll be dying so young"; Sophie repeats this motif while imagining the killer.
- The trainer's mantra: "Picking yourself up is as important as falling."
- "Norbert, I thought you'd be a little happier here" — Lisa's opener while opening the 2020 Château de Vaudieu she brought from Paris.
- Pia's barb about Lisa's "mid-life crisis" arriving early, or her meds being adjusted again.
- "Norbert, I wanted to show you…" / "No, stop, don't say anything" — a recurring dynamic between Lisa and Norbert.
- The literary-suspect joke: theories that Konrad is really Ferdinand von Schirach, Juli Zeh, Daniel Kehlmann, or Rudow himself — none confirmed.
- Sweetheart / honey address between Lisa and Norbert hints at a closer-than-professional bond.

## Unresolved Threads

- What was Anna's "surprise" for Sophie the night she died?
- The meaning of the blood letters "L"/"I"/"E" left near Anna's body.
- Why Anna being left-handed matters for the forensic comparison.
- Who leaked the 2012 villa photo to The Galant — and whether it's a coincidence.
- Whether Norbert will get pre-publication manuscript access; whether the interview proceeds.
- Whether Robert's investigation will unmask "Lisa Sophie Wilking" before or after publication.
- Lisa's psychiatric history and therapist connections — Norbert claims ignorance, but recommended the therapist.
- Marc's fate and disappearance from both Norbert's and Lisa's lives.
- Bassem's Al-Manar / Darabi Clan case — Robert's other investigative thread.
- The full surveillance/security perimeter Lisa is installing, and what (or whom) she expects to face.

## S01E03

# Episode Summary — S01E03 "The Training"

## Plot

The episode intercuts three threads that all orbit the same murder — the stabbing of Anna, Sophie Konrad's younger sister — and Lisa Konrad's plan to confront the killer through what looks like a normal author interview.

**The training montage.** Cold narration explains that Sophie only leaves her apartment at night, in a compulsion to jog while searching for the man she saw leaving Anna's flat. We see a fitness instructor drilling Sophie (the "self-defense / stamina / reactions" sequence), then intercut shots of Lisa Konrad going through parallel drills:
- A **childbirth-Push** scene that doubles as a recording on the cinema's concession audio (calls back to sound bleed later).
- A **gun range** lesson ("hold the gun still, only the trigger finger moves"), where Lisa remarks that a gun would help more than anything.
- A **CIA-style interrogation resistance course** run by Reserve Major Nils Christensen, who keeps her awake 30 hours and grills her 14 hours, teaching her to withstand pressure, suppress needs, and read external indicators. Lisa's real objective surfaces only late: *"I want to know why my sister was killed."* Christensen warns she is going through withdrawal and should postpone detox before the interview.

**The publishing plot.** Robert Lenzen, an award-winning journalist based in Berlin (with girlfriend Katja), is in Munich to handle his late parents' estate. He drops by Rudow Publishing and tells publisher Rudow he wants to do the Lisa Konrad interview himself, although editor Katrin has already complained it's not suitable and the chief editor has reserved the cover slot. Rudow is undecided and warns it might still be cancelled; the manuscript will only be delivered a week before the interview, four weeks out. Lenzen's stated reason for being in town longer: *"I'm selling my parents' house."*

**Lisa's therapy / parental cover-up.** Lisa is treating with Dr. Anger, who explicitly warns that long-term isolation produces sleep/eating disorders, hallucinations, and that her medication is "a necessity for survival." Lisa asks to suspend sessions for the final two weeks of her deadline. In a parallel session, Sophie and Anna's parents tell Dr. Anger there is no violence history, claim it's a "crime of passion," and reveal they didn't know Sophie had been charged with attacking a man on the street that night. Dr. Anger and Rudow privately agree: medication will keep Lisa calm, her book is in a "compensation" phase, and if Sophie ever remembers, they'll frame it as false memories. Rudow later mutters, *"If she remembers… you're screwed."* Over rosé, Rudow is told he is not the selfless type.

**Sophie, the witness.** Sophie is losing function — she can't eat, sleep, love, or paint anymore; Anna's dying face follows her everywhere. A detective (immigration-officer-style uniform not specified) visits her apartment: Marc, her ex, is moving out and still needs to sign a statement. The investigator tells her to stop hunting the killer herself and warns that she has been charged **again** for grabbing another stranger's arm "just to be sure." The investigation is being closed at the end of the month. Sophie is told to stop calling daily (each call is logged into the case file). Her final line into the mirror: *"He's still out there. And he knows that I saw him."*

**The final sequence.**
1. The detuned lullaby fragment and shaking of a stuffed Bukowski announce something wrong in Lisa's flat — it's "too quiet."
2. Lenzen arrives to find Lisa collapsed in a pool of blood, with Rudow already there. Rudow calls someone named **Shelley**, who arrives with a medical bag. Rudow treats Lisa while telling Lenzen she has been off her medication and "doesn't remember" anything from the manuscript she just wrote.
3. Rudow quietly injects Lisa with an unknown sedative ("You don't want to know. But she'll sleep now, for many hours. And when she wakes up, she'll feel like a brand-new person.") and orders Lenzen to close his eyes.

## Important character actions

- Sophie: continues nocturnal searching, physically assaults a third stranger on the street, and demands the detective find him.
- Robert Lenzen: insists on doing the Lisa Konrad interview personally, visits his own daughter for the third time in four weeks (plans a Pentecost trip to London), and reads Lisa's manuscript "MY SISTER" on the train home, then brings it to her flat.
- Lisa Konrad: stops her medication, undergoes deception-resistance/interrogation training, and finishes her manuscript.
- Rudow (publisher): edits and protects Lisa, supplies Dr. Anger with Lisa's case details, declares book restrictions (*"you can never, ever reveal who it was"*), treats Lisa's collapse, and injects her with a sedative whose contents he withholds.
- Dr. Anger: pressures Lisa to keep taking her medication, coordinates with Rudow to attribute recovered memories to her "condition," and gathers background from Anna and Sophie's parents.
- Nils Christensen: runs the interrogation-resistance course; warns Lisa about withdrawal before the interview.
- Marc, Sophie's boyfriend-turned-ex, is moving out but must still sign a statement.

## Newly established names / terms

- **Rudow Publishing** — the firm publishing Lisa Konrad.
- **Katrin** — editor at Rudow who is sidelined from the Lisa interview; reports it being difficult for her.
- **Robert Lenzen** — Berlin-based journalist, the assigned interviewer; girlfriend Katja still in Berlin.
- **Nils Christensen / Bassem** — Reserve Major, head coach of interrogation technique training.
- **Dr. Anger** — Lisa's therapist.
- **Marc** — Sophie's now-ex, packing up.
- **Shelley** — arrives with a medical bag when Lisa collapses; "Please help her. Do something!" implies she is part of Rudow's circle.
- **"The Training"** — episode title (the suicides/montage theme).
- **"MY SISTER"** — title of Lisa's finished manuscript; cover line: "an invitation."
- **"Bukowski"** — name Lenzen calls out as he enters; appears to be the pet.
- **"Mama/Papa"** — Anna and Sophie's parents (still grieving the dead child).
- **"Cigarette"** — leitmotif for Lisa's smoking / withdrawal; Dr. Anger notices the smell.

## Continuity-relevant facts

- Anna was **stabbed eight times**. Sophie saw a man entering her flat; that night Sophie was charged with assaulting a stranger.
- The parents had insisted Anna was in a relationship, but friends maintain she had **been single for almost a year**, undermining the "crime of passion" theory.
- Sophie is described as a former painter who can no longer paint.
- The detective names a partial identifier for the killer: a man whose name starts with **"LI" or "LE"**.
- Lisa is on heavy medication; she has chosen to stop ("how long have you been off?"). Her mental condition includes possible hallucinations and memory gaps.
- Lisa's stated goal for the interview: a long, possibly traumatic conversation — *and a DNA sample.*
- Manuscript "MY SISTER" is delivered to Lenzen attached to an email dated **June 23rd**, the day of the interview.
- Closing date of the official investigation: **end of this month**.
- Rudow sets firm boundaries on the book: *"you broke all the genre's rules… no resolution, no redemption."* Combined with the warning *"you must never reveal who it was,"* the manuscript functions as a trap.

## Jokes & references useful for later episodes

- **"Sour fries" candy** ("WE LOVE SOUR FRIES") — the unpopular concession item at the cinema; the seller jokes the snack is so unpopular she can't give them away. It is the running gag item Sophie buys three bags of. Also referenced in an earlier "carton" line of cigarettes later.
- **Sound bleed** — the cinema worker can hear every movie in her apartment above the box office; *"the rent is unbeatable."* It is a deliberate echo of the "too quiet" cue at episode's end.
- **Train whistle at the mouth** — the very first scene with Sophie chasing the moving train right under a passing train. Mirrors the final "too quiet" entry into Lisa's flat.
- **The "Gun might help me more"** reply is cut against Lisa's interview training, foreshadowing that the interview is meant to be her weapon.
- **"Never speak ill of the dead"** — Dr. Anger's aside about Anna being "lovable and funny," which clashes with the murder plot. The line is repeated as a thematic motif.
- **Rosé with two ice cubes** — Rudow and Dr. Anger's signature drink when colluding.
- The **CIA footage titles** ("Former CIA Officers Teach How to Detect Deception," "Systemic Questioning Techniques") foreshadow Lisa's manipulation of Lenzen.
- **"Bukowski"** — the name Lenzen calls out will read like an inside joke unless confirmed.
- The opening **"Morning!"** narrator (Sophie's perspective, "I thought I'd…" cut off) recurs throughout as the through-voice.
- The **"self-defense instructor"** opener is the silent callback for the episode title — the trainer asks if Sophie is on "strong medication." Set up vs. payoff for her actual withdrawal.

## Unresolved threads

- The identity of the man Sophie saw leaving Anna's flat — only the **"LI / LE"** initial pattern is established; Lisa herself has apparently known all along.
- Who Lisa's book is actually "inviting" — and what Lenzen will be subjected to on June 23rd.
- Why Rudow is so invested: "I'm doing this for her too" plus "selflessness doesn't suit you" imply a hidden motive.
- What Rudow **injected** Lisa with, and what Shelley knows about it.
- Whether the novel breaking every genre rule was Lisa's design or a warning to Lenzen.
- Whether Sophie will remember; Dr. Anger has pre-prepared a "false memories" cover story and privately admits *"if she remembers, you're screwed."*
- The state of the official investigation: "closed at end of month" — what happens to Sophie then?
- Who originally owned the Bukowski pet, and where it was when Lisa collapsed.
- The identity of the unnamed third character Sophie's father wanted them to ask about ("You have to ask them").
