## S02E04

# The Rainmaker — S02E04 Summary

## Episode Plot
Rudy Baylor and the J. Lyman Stone team pursue a class-action lawsuit against Medford Juvenile Detention (Harrowsgate) on behalf of juveniles illegally sentenced by Judge Halloran. After a disastrous initial meeting where Rudy fails to sign up families, he personally calls each parent and wins them back. In court, Amanda Vonn (representing the state/DJJ) moves to consolidate the habeas and §1983 actions and demands a plaintiff list. Rudy presents 44 names, but the judge discovers the list was procured by a 12-year-old detainee, Victor, at Rudy's initial request — an ethical violation (Rule 7.3). The judge refers Rudy to the state bar but shows leniency, giving the team one week to ethically find 40 new class members; if they succeed, the original list may be admitted.

Parallel thread: Tom (Boone's former manager) is revealed to have been covering for his wife Lynette's gambling debts to the Dixie Mafia ("Gator"). Lynette pressured Tom to pull Boone from a no-hitter game to fix the outcome. Boone discovers the truth, confronts Tom, and Lynette is exposed as the true debtor. Bruiser warns Lynette that divorcing Tom would waive spousal privilege, forcing her to stay married and protect him.

## Important Character Actions
- **Rudy Baylor**: Recovers the class by calling every family personally; takes the ethical hit in court to protect Victor; granted one week to rebuild the plaintiff list cleanly.
- **Victor (juvenile detainee)**: Initially lies to protect Rudy, then admits he continued compiling the list after Rudy told him to stop.
- **Amanda Vonn**: Files motion to consolidate; adds Sarah Plankmore as second chair on Harrowsgate; uses the numerosity trap to force disclosure of the plaintiff list.
- **Sarah Plankmore**: Warns Rudy not to use the tainted list; claims she's trying to help; Rudy rebuffs her.
- **Boone**: Learns Lynette (not Tom) owed Gator; punches Tom; realizes he was pulled from the game to throw it.
- **Lynette**: Confronted by Bruiser; threatened with burial if she doesn't keep Tom alive and out of prison.
- **Bruiser Stone**: Coaches Rudy on connecting with clients; brutally handles Lynette; calls Sarah "a snake."
- **The Judge**: Calls out the ethical violation but respects Rudy's sacrifice for his client; sets a hard deadline (one week, 40 names).

## Newly Established Names / Terms
- **Medford Juvenile Detention** — facility where juveniles were illegally sentenced (also called **Harrowsgate** in pleadings)
- **Judge Halloran** — the judge who sentenced the juveniles improperly
- **Victor** — 12-year-old detainee, lead plaintiff, compiled the class list
- **Rule 7.3** — ethical rule prohibiting solicitation of clients (cited by judge)
- **Dixie Mafia / "Gator"** — criminal organization behind the gambling debts
- **Spousal privilege / crime-fraud exception** — legal mechanism Quinn uses to pierce Lynette's communications
- **Blue Peregrine Club** — exclusive Charleston club; Bruiser is first female member (6 years ago)

## Continuity-Relevant Facts
- Amanda Vonn now represents South Carolina DJJ (state) *and* Harrowsgate — consolidated defense.
- Sarah Plankmore is officially second chair on Harrowsgate for the defense.
- Rudy is under referral to the state bar for disciplinary proceedings (held in abeyance pending the one-week cure).
- The class-action hinges on **numerosity** (40+ plaintiffs), **commonality**, and **adequacy** — judge will only proceed to the latter two if Rudy delivers a clean list.
- Tom's debt was Lynette's all along; she managed the texts to Gator ("Pull him out. You can't win this game").
- Boone is no longer under Tom's management; Bruiser is now his attorney.
- Bruiser's father (J. Lyman Stone) is imprisoned for the Rosalie Sutton witness case.

- The "bigger fish" the Dixie Mafia wants (Gator) — is there a larger conspiracy?
- Sarah's allegiance — genuinely turned, or playing a long game for Amanda?
- Victor's fate — released? Still in Medford?

## S02E05

## Episode Plot
- Chuck Lawson, Victor Suffredes’ stepfather and a charter boat captain, pressures Rudy for more money and implies he was paid to lie in court about Victor’s case. Soon after, Chuck disappears; his boat is found adrift about 60 kilometers offshore with no body, and he is presumed drowned.
- The J. Lyman Stone team scrambles to keep the Medford/Harrowsgate class action alive. They need enough plaintiffs for numerosity and initially have only a handful; a last-minute influx from former Medford kids (organized by Luke through a WhatsApp group) brings the count to 312 in court.
- Victor is beaten while in custody at Medford. Rudy seeks temporary release but is initially denied for lack of evidence. To protect Victor and force movement, Rudy submits a shredded plea agreement recovered from Louis V. Palermo’s dumpster, arguing it shows Victor was promised probation rather than commitment and suggesting unlawful intent/conspiracy.
- Rudy tells the judge the firm’s theory: a broad conspiracy involving multiple individuals and institutions to prosecute and convict youths to increase occupancy at Medford and possibly other Harrowsgate/Akram Holdings facilities. The judge refers the matter to the district attorney, issues a preservation order against the defense, finds commonality satisfied, and orders Victor’s temporary release to approved supervision.
- On adequacy of counsel, Sarah Plankmore attacks J. Lyman Stone as under-resourced and highlights the Great Benefit judgment being on appeal. Dot Black accepts the $10 million Great Benefit settlement and pledges it to fund the class case; Bruiser pledges 100% of the firm’s fees. The judge certifies the class.
- With Chuck missing and no suitable family placement, Rudy takes Victor into his own home rather than let him go into the system.
- In the Boone/Lynette/Tom thread, Tom apologizes to Boone for manipulating him (including pulling him from games), but tensions flare over Boone’s involvement with Jocelyn. Later, Tom is ambushed after Lynette texts him to meet; he escapes White Mike and Gizzard, breaks into Jocelyn’s house for help claiming Lynette set him up, and the episode ends on an abrupt new threat as Tom is confronted offscreen (“What happened?”).

## Important Character Actions
- **Rudy Baylor**: Pursues Chuck to secure Victor’s post-release placement, argues for Victor’s emergency release, deploys the shredded Palermo plea agreement in court, advances the broader occupancy/conspiracy theory, and ultimately brings Victor home to live with him.
- **Jocelyn "Bruiser" Stone**: Pushes strategy (including when to use evidence), mediates the Tom/Boone blowup, commits the firm’s fees to the Harrowsgate case, and supports Victor’s transition.
- **Deck Shifflet**: Drives outreach efforts (including the recruitment video) and helps manage the scramble for class members.
- **Dot Black**: Rejects letting Great Benefit off easily at first, then chooses to accept the $10 million settlement and dedicate it to funding the Medford class action.
- **Victor Suffredes**: Is assaulted in Medford, resists naming attackers, is released under supervision, and moves in with Rudy.
- **Luke**: A former Medford detainee who mobilizes contacts through a WhatsApp group, producing the surge of plaintiffs that saves numerosity.
- **Amanda Vonn**: Leads the defense response in court, questions the evidentiary basis for the conspiracy claim, and does not object to Victor’s temporary release when the shredded plea is introduced.
- **Sarah Plankmore**: Serves as defense counsel attacking class adequacy and the firm’s resources; sits at the defense table as the only Tinley Britt-related lawyer noted as unindicted from the Great Benefit fallout.
- **Megan Grenier**: Reports Chuck’s attempt to buy more money/testimony; is pressed by her handler for usable information and reminded she must provide continuing substantial assistance until an arrest to secure a non-prosecution agreement in the Great Benefit matter.
- **Tom**: Apologizes to Boone but clashes violently with him; is later targeted in an apparent setup involving Lynette and flees to Jocelyn.
- **Boone**: Confronts Tom over being manipulated/pulled from games and his relationship with Jocelyn; remains entangled in the fallout.
- **Lynette**: Implicated in luring Tom into an ambush by texting him to meet (per Tom’s account).

## Newly Established Names / Terms
- **Charleston Bump**: Offshore fishing spot Chuck describes at the episode’s start.
- **Chuck Lawson**: Victor’s stepfather and charter captain; key witness/payment claimant who goes missing.
- **Louis V. Palermo**: Victor’s former attorney; office dumpster is the source of the shredded plea agreement.
- **Shredded plea agreement (Exhibit A in effect)**: Alternate plea promising probation rather than commitment; used as evidence suggesting improper handling of Victor’s case.
- **Luke**: Ex-Medford contact who helps recruit a large number of claimants.
- **Frank Frantangelo / Frankie**: Parent and son connected to Medford; Frank pressures for Frankie’s release and is manipulated around cooperation.
- **Megan Grenier**: Inside Becker, Sinclair, and Vonn while cooperating under pressure for a non-prosecution agreement.
- **Ronan**: Contact who relays information about Chuck’s disappearance and suspects Amanda’s involvement.
- **White Mike / Gizzard**: Men Tom says were waiting to kill him in Lynette’s setup.
- **Akram Holdings**: Named in court as Harrowsgate Systems’ parent company in the alleged occupancy conspiracy.
- **Numerosity / commonality / adequacy**: The three class-certification pillars explicitly adjudicated in this episode.

## Continuity-Relevant Facts
- The class count jumps from 11 to 312 claimants during the numerosity fight, clearing that threshold.
- The judge finds commonality satisfied based on the alleged pattern and orders Victor Suffredes temporarily released to approved supervision.
- The court refers the plea-agreement allegations to the district attorney for investigation and issues a preservation order barring destruction, alteration, or movement of evidence.
- Dot accepts the $10 million Great Benefit settlement (instead of holding out for the $82 million award on appeal) to finance the Medford litigation; Bruiser commits all firm fees from the case as well.
- The class action against Medford/Harrowsgate is certified.
- Chuck Lawson is missing and presumed drowned after his boat is found offshore; Victor no longer has a home to return to.
- Rudy becomes Victor’s caretaker, taking him into his home.
- Megan remains under obligation to provide continuing substantial assistance until an arrest to obtain her Great Benefit non-prosecution agreement.
- Tom reports Lynette set him up to be killed by White Mike and Gizzard, escalating the Dixie Mafia/Lynette danger thread.
