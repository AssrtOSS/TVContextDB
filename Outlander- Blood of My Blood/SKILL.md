# Outlander: Blood of My Blood — Subtitle Translation Background

## Series Overview
- **English Title**: Outlander: Blood of My Blood
- **Chinese Title**: 古战场传奇：血脉相连 / 外乡人：我的血脉（暂定，以官方发布为准）
- **Type**: Television series (historical romance drama / prequel)
- **First Air Date**: August 8, 2025
- **Broadcaster**: Starz (US/UK)
- **Seasons**: 2 (Season 1: 10 episodes, Aug 8 – Oct 10, 2025; Season 2: 10 episodes, Sep 18 – Nov 20, 2026)
- **Developed by**: Matthew B. Roberts
- **Based on**: Characters by Diana Gabaldon (Outlander novel series)
- **Production**: Left Bank Pictures, Sony Pictures Television; filmed in Scotland

## Premise
A prequel to *Outlander* (2014–2026) following two love stories across time:
1. **18th-century Scottish Highlands** — Ellen MacKenzie and Brian Fraser (parents of Jamie Fraser), caught between rival clans (MacKenzie vs. Fraser/Grant) and an arranged marriage.
2. **World War I England** — Julia Moriston and Henry Beauchamp (parents of Claire Beauchamp), whose romance unfolds through letters and a mysterious time-travel element linking them to the 18th-century timeline.

The two storylines intersect via the series’ signature standing-stone time travel.

## Relationships Quick Reference
- **Ellen ↔ Brian**: Star-crossed lovers; clans historically hostile.
- **Ellen ↔ Colum/Dougal**: Sister–brothers; Colum becomes laird, Dougal war chief.
- **Brian ↔ Lord Lovat**: Father–son; Lovat manipulates prophecy for clan gain.
- **Julia ↔ Henry**: Wartime correspondents; separated by injury/class/confinement.
- **Claire (original series) ↔ Jamie**: Their parents’ stories are the emotional/genetic foundation.

## Speaking Styles & Register
- **Highland Scots (18th c.)**: Formal, poetic, peppered with Gaelic endearments (*mo nighean donn*, *mo chridhe*); honor-bound oaths; clan-political subtext.
- **WWI England (1910s)**: Stiff-upper-lip epistolary style (Julia/Henry’s letters); military bluntness (Henry); repressed upper-class restraint (Julia’s family).
- **Modern framing (if any)**: Claire’s voice-over narration in original series — reflective, medical precision, emotional intimacy.
- **Nobility vs. commoners**: Laird/Lovat speak with authority; servants/soldiers use plainer Scots/English.
- **Gaelic insertions**: *Sassenach*, *mo duinne*, *a ghaoil*, *slàinte mhath* — keep in italics or transliterate with gloss.

---

## Cultural & Historical Context
- **1740s Highlands**: Post-1715 rising, pre-1745; clan system intact but pressured by British law; tacksmen class; oral tradition; prophecy/seers respected.
- **WWI Britain (1914–1918)**: Class rigidity, censorship (DORA), shell shock, women in clerical roles; letters as lifeline; emerging modern medicine.
- **Outlander universe rules**: Time travel via genetic sensitivity + stone circles on solstices/equinoxes; travelers retain memories; changing history has consequences (butterfly effect).
- **Diana Gabaldon’s influence**: Episode 9 (“Braemar”) and 10 (“Something Borrowed”) written by Gabaldon; her book material on Jamie’s parents (“The Exile” graphic novel, unpublished manuscripts) informs backstory.

## Translation Guidelines for Subtitlers
1. **Consistency**: Lock character/place/term renderings across all episodes/seasons; maintain a shared glossary.
2. **Gaelic**: Retain key terms (*Sassenach*, *mo chridhe*) with first-use parenthetical gloss; do not over-translate endearments.
3. **Register shift**: Distinguish 18th-c. Highland formal Scots from 1910s British military/upper-class English.
4. **Epistolary scenes**: Julia/Henry’s letters — preserve literary tone; use slightly archaic written Chinese (文言色彩) where appropriate.
5. **Prophecy/motto**: “Luceo Non Uro” → “我发光而不燃烧” (established); other Latin/Gaelic mottoes similarly.
6. **Clan politics**: Translate titles (Laird, Tacksman, Chieftain) consistently; avoid “chief” for *laird* unless context demands.
7. **Time-travel mechanics**: Use established *Outlander* terminology (穿越, 石圈, 召唤, 基因敏感性).
