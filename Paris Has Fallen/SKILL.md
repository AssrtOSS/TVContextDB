## Series Overview

**Title:** Paris Has Fallen (2024)
**Type:** Television series (not a film)
**Genre:** Action / Thriller
**Creator/Writer:** Howard Overman
**Directors:** Oded Ruskin, Hans Herbots
**Countries:** France, United Kingdom, United States
**Languages:** French, English (bilingual)
**Network:** Canal+ (France, premiered 23 September 2024)
**UK release:** Amazon Prime Video, 1 November 2024
**US release:** Hulu, 6 December 2024
**Seasons:** 1 (8 episodes), with a sequel series *Apollo Has Fallen* in production as of 2025
**Franchise:** Part of the *Has Fallen* universe, which includes the films *Olympus Has Fallen* (2013), *London Has Fallen* (2016), and *Angel Has Fallen* (2019). The films star Gerard Butler as US Secret Service agent Mike Banning. The TV series introduces an entirely new cast set in the same cinematic universe, with Gerard Butler serving as executive producer.

## Key Relationships

- **Taleb and Levesque** — Past romantic relationship when Taleb was on her protection detail. They rekindle briefly; she later orders a secret cover-up that puts them at odds.
- **Taleb and Taylor** — Professional partnership across agencies (French protection vs. MI6). Mutual respect forged through combat.
- **Pearce and Amina/Esin** — After escaping captivity in Pakistan, Pearce lived in Lahore with Amina and her children. A car bomb killed Amina; Esin survived and later comes to Paris. Pearce's paternal protectiveness toward Esin is a key emotional thread.
- **Taylor and Thea** — Romantic partners. Thea's drug addiction creates personal strain for Taylor during the crisis.
- **Pearce and the French government** — Pearce's Foreign Legion unit was sent on a mission in Afghanistan, betrayed, captured by the Taliban, and tortured for six years. The betrayal was ordered by KMI CEO Moulin and Defence Minister Bardin for business reasons, then covered up by President Levesque.

## World Setting

The series is set in contemporary Paris within the *Has Fallen* universe. Unlike the films, which center on a US Secret Service agent protecting the American president, the TV series shifts the action to France, focusing on French and British intelligence cooperation. The narrative involves real French security agencies, real Parisian landmarks, and the politics of the Fifth Republic.

Key locations: British Embassy in Paris, Élysée Palace, Hôtel de Brienne (Defence Ministry), Orly Airport, 7th arrondissement, Jupiter Command Post (presidential emergency bunker).

## Overall Plot Premise (Full Season 1)

Jacob Pearce, a former French Foreign Legion soldier, was sent on a mission in Afghanistan with his squad. Their operation was sabotaged by French defence interests: Pascal Moulin (KMI Systems CEO) pressured Defence Minister Bardin to betray the mission because Pearce's actions threatened KMI's business interests. Pearce's men were killed by the Taliban; he was tortured for six years before being rescued by chance during a US Special Forces raid.

After escaping and relocating to Lahore, Pakistan, Pearce was targeted again — a car bomb killed his girlfriend Amina and her children, ordered by DGSE agent Cedric Duval on the instructions of handler Beatrice Paquin. The ultimate order came from then-incoming President Juliette Levesque, to prevent Pearce from exposing the truth.

Pearce launches a methodical revenge campaign against every person involved. His attacks escalate from a terrorist assault on the British Embassy, to kidnapping and extortion of defence executives, to hijacking a cargo plane carrying uranium, and finally to constructing a dirty bomb with which he threatens to destroy Paris unless Levesque surrenders herself. A mole within the DGSI taskforce (agent Simone) feeds Pearce intelligence throughout. The season culminates with Taleb and Taylor forced to devise an audacious plan to use Levesque herself as bait to draw out Pearce.

## Speaking Styles

- **Taleb** — Direct, military-inclined speech. Code-switches between French and English. Protective and morally conflicted. When speaking to Levesque, his tone shifts to more intimate, personal register.
- **Taylor** — Professional MI6 language, sharp and analytical. Occasionally confrontational. Her personal vulnerability emerges with Thea.
- **Pearce** — Controlled, articulate menace. His speech is that of a man who believes his cause is righteous. He delivers manifestos with rhetorical precision; when angry, his calm breaks into raw fury about betrayal and injustice.
- **Levesque** — Political register. Authoritative and calculating. When alone with Taleb, her speech softens. Defends her decisions as pragmatic necessity.
- **Garnier** — Institutional, bureaucratic language. Committed to procedure.
- **Freja** — Terse, pragmatic. Swedish mercenary operating in French/English spaces.

## Cultural and Contextual Notes

1. **Franco-British intelligence dynamic** — The series reflects real-world close cooperation between French and British intelligence, but also the tensions and jurisdictional friction. Taylor being MI6 in Paris is a significant plot device.

2. **The French Foreign Legion** — A legendary military unit composed largely of foreign nationals serving France. The betrayal of Pearce's Legion squad resonates with real debates about how France deploys and discards Legion soldiers.

3. **French political structure** — The President of France holds significant executive power, especially in defense and intelligence matters. The Élysée Palace and Jupiter Command Post are real sites of presidential authority. The show dramatizes the real tension between presidential secrecy and democratic accountability.

4. **"Has Fallen" franchise convention** — The films follow a pattern of a single competent defender protecting a head of state from a catastrophic attack on a major city or institution. The TV series adapts this formula for a French setting with a morally ambiguous antagonist whose grievances are legitimate, adding complexity to the action-thriller formula.

5. **Multilingual dialogue** — The series is bilingual French/English. Subtitle translators should note code-switching and preserve the distinction between characters' languages when relevant to plot (e.g., Taylor operating in English, Pearce and Taleb's French military background).
