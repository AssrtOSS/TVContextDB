## S03E01 — "Randy" (New York City)

**Plot**

The Creep, using the alias **Randy**, wanders New York City approaching strangers on the street, trying to pay someone to let him film them for the day. After being rejected or ignored by numerous passersby, he zeroes in on **Mo**, a young Black man sitting on a park bench. Randy escalates his offer from $1,000 to $1,500 to $2,000. Mo reluctantly agrees but quickly becomes uncomfortable and flees. The episode follows Randy's relentless pursuit of Mo across the city — through the subway, street corners, and eventually a wooded area — as Mo repeatedly tries to escape, seek help, and physically resist. The episode ends with Mo hiding behind trees, threatening to kill Randy, and delivering the closing line: "I don't want to need you. Because I can't... have you."

**Character Actions**

- **Randy (The Creep):** Approaches dozens of strangers with a camera and cash; quotes *The Bridges of Madison County* repeatedly; reveals his inner "wolf" called Peachfuzz to Mo; chases Mo on foot across multiple NYC locations; rubs Mo's back without consent; claims to walk 15,000 steps a day; weeps when Mo tries to leave him.
- **Mo:** Accepts the money under pressure; calls a security guard for help (mistaken for police); pulls a knife on Randy ("Somebody just filming you all day... How you like that?"); hides behind trees using urine as a deterrent; ultimately threatens to kill Randy.

**Newly Established Names / Terms**

- **Randy** — The Creep's alias for this episode.
- **Mo** — S03E01 victim. Lives with his mother, no job.
- **Mo' Better Blues / Modigliani / Mo' Money** — Nicknames Randy riffing on Mo's name.
- **B.O.M.C.** — Randy's shorthand for *Bridges of Madison County*.
- **"Love language"** — Randy describes filming people as his love language (line 490).

**Continuity-Relevant Facts**

- Peachfuzz is explicitly named and described again: a wolf living inside Randy, whom he used to fear but now embraces (lines 422-430). This restates the S1E5/S1E6 duality theme.
- The Creep still operates in the daytime, in public, targeting strangers — consistent with earlier seasons but now set in a dense urban environment (previous episodes were rural/suburban).
- The episode preserves the "victim films the Creep / Creep films the victim" found-footage structure: Mo ends up holding the camera and filming Randy at one point.
- Mo explicitly calls Randy "you fucking creep" (line 400/722), reinforcing the title-series metatext.

**Jokes / References Useful for Later Episodes**

- The *Bridges of Madison County* motif is a major running bit — Randy compares himself to Clint Eastwood and Mo to Meryl Streep, and complains that "Meryl always dumps Clint." This appears four separate times and could recur as a signature delusion.
- "Mo emotion, Mo anger, Mo everything!" — Randy's wordplay on Mo's name (line 298).
- "Why buy the cow when you can get the milk for free?" — Randy's self-pitying proverb when Mo declines to meet the next day (line 654).
- Dave Matthews Band "Ants Marching" reference (line 72).
- Mo calling Randy a "rent-a-cop" when he mistakes a security guard for a real officer (line 313).

**Unresolved Threads**

- **Mo survives** — unlike most victims, Mo physically fights back, threatens lethal force, and the episode ends without a confirmed kill. His fate is ambiguous.
- **No Peachfuzz mask appears** — Randy talks about the wolf persona but never dons the mask in this episode. Whether the mask appears later in S03 is unknown.
- **Mo's phone number / follow-up** — Mo gives Randy his number as a brush-off ("hit me up sometime"). Whether Randy uses it could be a thread.
- **The camera's final footage** — Mo has the camera near the end. If the tape is recovered, it's unclear by whom.

## Episode Plot

The episode is set entirely on a private plane. **Anisha**, an ambitious journalist, boards expecting to film a three-hour confession from a wealthy, politically powerful man named **David Beasley** who claims he's about to expose corrupt politicians and then kill himself. **Connor**, the nervous flight attendant, is already on board and immediately suspicious of the setup. Beasley emerges from the bathroom — he'd boarded early — and is instantly domineering: making absurd beverage demands (water no ice, coffee with four raw sugars and one Stevia, water with ice, a side of ice, AND a Red Bull), repeatedly sneaking up on Connor to scare him, and invading personal space. He monologues about his "political devastation wrought with the flick of a ballpoint pen" and describes Connor as a latent "killer" who should work for him for $1,000 a day.

When Connor finally snaps and curses at him over the beverage dispute, Beasley pulls out a pen ("mightier than the sword") and forces Connor to "choose your weapon." He kills Connor in the bathroom (flesh tearing), then tells Anisha Connor has "a poo-poo diarrhea situation." The cockpit intercom rings; Beasley covers, claiming Connor is unavailable. He then goes to the cockpit and snaps the pilot's neck. Anisha witnesses the aftermath — Beasley is wearing the Peachfuzz wolf mask and covered in blood.

## The Farewell Ritual

Beasley performs his signature farewell over Connor's body — unzipping something, shaking, choking, sobbing "Don't go, David! Don't leave me!" He tells Anisha "it's like a death every single time — you never get used to it," and refers to all his victims collectively as "the rest of the family."

## The Personality Switch

Beasley warns Anisha he'll "be a little different" when he returns from the cockpit. He comes back as a completely different persona — polite, confused, meeting Anisha as if for the first time. He acts shocked by the carnage ("somebody went crazy in here"), feigns ignorance about the bodies, and says he "must be on his way."

## The Exit

Anisha confronts him: "So this is all a lie? There's no David Beasley?" He responds with apparent genuine confusion: "Who the heck is David Beasley?!" He descends the plane to 2,000 feet, opens the cabin door, and Anisha is sucked/falls out. He watches her fall — "Oh, there she goes. Bye." — then gazes at the sky and repeats his mantra: "Pivot." The episode ends with him alone on the autopilot plane, searching for a bag, muttering "Bag... bag..."

## Newly Established Names / Terms

- **David Beasley** — The Creep's alias this episode, posing as a wealthy political whistle-blower.
- **Connor** — Private-plane flight attendant, first victim.
- **Anisha** — Journalist hired to film the "confession," second victim (ejected from the plane).
- **"Pivot"** — Beasley's core personal philosophy, repeated as a mantra: "If you don't pivot, you die. You stagnate, you die." Recited after each kill and at the episode's close.
- **"Mightier than the sword"** — Recurring callback phrase tied to the pen-as-weapon motif.

## Continuity-Relevant Facts

- The Creep's farewell ritual (crying, physical contact with the body, "I love you," "you'll always be in my heart") continues to be a consistent post-kill behavior, now explicitly acknowledged as something he does with every victim.
- He refers to all past victims collectively as "family" — deepening the mythology around his attachment to kills.
- The Peachfuzz wolf mask makes an appearance mid-episode, worn during the pilot's murder.
- The personality switch is the most explicit depiction yet in the series: after going to the cockpit, he returns as an entirely different person who doesn't recognize Anisha, denies being Beasley, and acts innocent — suggesting the "David Beasley" identity was another disposable persona, consistent with the show's pattern of one-episode aliases.
- The Creep kills two people directly (Connor, the pilot) and causes Anisha's death (ejected from the plane at altitude) — three victims in one episode, an escalation.

## Jokes / References Useful for Later Episodes

- "My father always told me, if you're not early, you're late" — Beasley's opener, same folksy-wisdom delivery the Creep uses across identities.
- The absurdly specific beverage order (four raw sugars, one Stevia, side of ice, Red Bull) is peak Creep entitlement comedy.
- "He's in the service industry. He wants to be nice, but he's actually a killer, and he doesn't even know it" — Beasley projecting onto Connor; potentially a self-reflective line worth revisiting.
- "You're a little teddy bear" — another of the Creep's uncomfortable pet-name moments.
- The public-defender metaphor ("You have a particularly unsavory client, you still got to work for him") is so bad even Connor calls it out.

## Unresolved Threads

- **The bag/backpack**: Beasley spends the final moments searching for a "bag" on the now-empty plane. He fights Anisha over a backpack mid-ejection ("Give it to me! It's mine! My backpack!"). Contents unknown — possibly evidence, possibly something else entirely.
- **The autopilot plane**: The door is open, the pilot is dead, and the plane is flying itself. Beasley's survival/landing method is unexplained.
- **Was there a real confession?** The political-expose angle may have been entirely fabricated as a lure, or there may be real information Beasley intended to release — left ambiguous.
- **"The rest of the family"**: The Creep's growing catalogue of victims-as-family is becoming a larger thread across seasons.
- **The cockpit intercom call**: Someone from ground operations called to speak with Connor; they'll presumably notice the plane going off-grid.

## S03E06

## The Creep Tapes S03E06 Summary

### Episode Plot
Three teenagers — **Archie**, **Jody**, and **Brie** — hang out at Brie's house while her parents are away. Archie arrives with a new camcorder bought from a pawn shop, funded by a Columbia House record club scheme he ran as a minor. He proposes they make a movie together instead of watching one. They wander into the woods, discover an animal den, and Archie scares them with a wolf mask. Tensions rise: Jody grows suspicious of Archie's constant filming and odd behavior, calling him potentially dangerous. Archie manipulates the situation, framing Jody's discomfort as jealousy over Brie. When Jody finally snaps and attacks Archie, Archie kills him with a concealed blade. He then calmly instructs Brie to report the murder to police, says he'll leave town, gives her a keepsake, and insists they'll "always be friends."

### Important Character Actions
- **Archie (the Creep)**: Orchestrates the entire encounter as a "movie"; uses the camera as both prop and surveillance tool; wears a wolf mask (Peachfuzz); murders Jody on camera; stage-manages the aftermath with chilling politeness.
- **Jody**: Senses danger early, confronts Archie, gets drunk/aggressive, ultimately attacks Archie and is killed.
- **Brie**: Caught between the two; Archie praises her "subtle" performance; spared and directed to become the witness.

### Newly Established Names / Terms
- **Archie** — new alias for the killer (not previously listed in S1–S2 aliases)
- **Jody, Brie** — victims (Jody killed, Brie spared as witness)
- **"Peachfuzz" mask** — wolf mask worn by Archie, confirming the Peachfuzz persona's physical prop
- **Columbia House scheme** — killer's backstory detail (funding equipment through minor's contract fraud)

### Continuity-Relevant Facts
- Killer continues the pattern: lures victims via casual social contact, films everything, uses a persona/mask, kills on camera, leaves a witness or recording.
- The "found footage" meta-layer: Archie explicitly plans to anonymously deliver the tapes to police as a "found movie" — mirroring the series' own conceit.
- Peachfuzz mask appears as a tangible object, not just a reference.
- Killer's MO includes grooming a survivor to deliver the evidence (Brie), ensuring the footage enters circulation.

### Jokes / References for Later Episodes
- "It's like jazz" / "Jinx!" — improvised filmmaking as metaphor for the killer's improvisational murder method.
- Screenwriting book line: "end the scene early, right at that moment where you don't know what's gonna happen next" — meta-commentary on the episode's cut-to-black kill.
- Archie's "capital 'G' gay" self-questioning — disarming vulnerability act.
- "We'll always be friends. We just can't hang out anymore" — recurring killer sign-off.

### Unresolved Threads
- **Brie's fate**: She holds the footage and the killer's instructions; will she comply or resist?
- **Archie's disappearance**: He claims he'll leave town — does he resurface under another alias in S03E07+?
- **The pawn-shop camera**: Provenance unknown; could link to prior victims' gear.
- **Police involvement**: First time the killer explicitly plans for law enforcement to receive the tapes — may trigger investigation arc.
- **Jody's body**: Left in the woods; discovery timeline unresolved.

## S03E05

## S03E05 — The Creep Tapes

### Episode Plot
The Creep infiltrates **Greg**'s apartment and lives there secretly for six days while Greg remains unaware. Greg cancels lunch with his parents, citing work overload. The Creep reveals himself in Greg's bathroom, initiating a forced "connection" — sharing a fable about a queen who chooses to drink from a poisoned well so she and her king can be "the same" as the mad villagers. When the Creep tries to physically bond ("touching extremities"), Greg fights back: he stabs the Creep and breaks his nose. The Creep laughs it off ("Blood brothers"), refuses to leave, and manipulates Greg's 911 call — Greg tells the operator he doesn't know the intruder. The episode ends with the Creep listening to Greg's voicemail to his father ("I'm just so busy") and Greg singing in the tub.

### Important Character Actions
- **The Creep**: Stalks Greg for six days; hides in apartment; adopts fake environmental canvasser persona at door; forces confrontation; gets stabbed and nose broken; refuses to leave; hijacks 911 call; frames Greg's reluctance to let him go as proof of loneliness.
- **Greg**: Cancels family lunch; unaware of stalker; sings/vocalizes in bathtub repeatedly; stabs Creep in self-defense; breaks Creep's nose; calls 911 but denies knowing attacker; retreats to phone/tub instead of engaging.

### Newly Established Names / Terms
- **Greg** — victim (S03E05)
- **663 Mainfield** — Greg's address
- **"Touching extremities"** — Creep's phrase for desired physical contact
- **Poisoned well fable** — parable about choosing conformity over sanity

### Continuity-Relevant Facts
- Creep's MO: long-term covert surveillance in victim's home before reveal
- "Always with the doors" — meta callback to doors as barriers in prior episodes
- Creep's fixation on "gentle proximity," hearing breathing, smelling breath (echoes S01E5 codependency theme)
- Victim's phone/technology as shield against human contact — recurring motif
- Creep's willingness to be injured as part of "connection" ritual

### Jokes / References for Later Episodes
- **"Tub brothers"** — Creep bonding over bathtub singing
- **"Blood brothers"** — said after being stabbed
- **Nordic nature documentary (3.5 hrs) vs. 3.5-second internet video** — attention span contrast
- **Fake Greenpeace canvasser** — disposable persona
- **Creep apologizing for breaking his own nose** — "Oh, Greg, I'm so sorry!"

### Unresolved Threads
- Greg's fate — police dispatched but not shown arriving
- Creep remains in apartment; no arrest or departure
- Greg's parents — cancelled lunch, father's voicemail unanswered
- Whether Greg's "I don't know him" to 911 enables Creep's escape
- Creep's injury (stab wound, broken nose) — no medical attention shown
