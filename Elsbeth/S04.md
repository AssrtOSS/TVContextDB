## S04E01

### Plot
Cold open: relationship therapist Serena Collyer presents her annual couples workshop alongside her husband Graham. Cut to the victim, Casey Arthurs, a 48-year-old struggling musician from LA house-sitting a Lower East Side apartment for an owner walking the Camino de Santiago. Casey argues with his lover (offscreen) about her staying in NY through November for a Queens music residency; she gifts him back a sentimental Hacky Sack. Casey then receives an angry text from Luke Prescott about "Lauren," plays dumb, and a confrontation escalates. The body is discovered the next morning: single gunshot to the chest using Casey's own registered handgun, defensive wound over the left eye from a fistfight, no forced entry, blood on the doorknob, gun wiped clean, no gunshot residue on Luke's hands or shirt.

Investigation: Luke Prescott is held overnight as the jealous-husband suspect; he admits to fighting Casey but denies shooting. His wife Lauren admits an affair with "Steve" (a finance guy with a third Hamptons house and Luke's business partner), not Casey. Phone records show no connection between Casey and Lauren. Elsbeth notices Casey's phone stores a "Careena" — Serena's name misspelled — and that the freezer holds an expensive Riesling incongruous with Casey's cheap beer. Serena is identified as Casey's grad-school ex turned couples guru; she wrote "Keeping It Together," which features the Prescotts as "Jack and Jill." Casey's old drummer "Viper" (real name Jeff, Brooklyn) confirms the relationship was on-and-off and toxic long after grad school. Viper also reveals Casey only owned a cell phone since 2014, contradicting Serena's "we lost touch" story. Surveillance from a neighborhood wine shop captures Serena buying the exact Riesling two days before the murder. The book chapter titled "Sam and Diane" is decoded as a thinly-veiled portrait of Serena and Casey. The Costa Rica bracelet Serena never takes off — retrieved from Graham — carries Casey's blood and gunshot residue. Serena is arrested mid-book-event.

## S04E02

EPISODE PLOT
Richard Perrick, a celebrated playwright who recently died, established the Richard Perrick Estate (widow Sheila, son Jeremy, longtime producer Lenny Swift) to protect and revive his plays. Before passing, he had already granted the rights to his controversial first hit "Gilbert's Boy" to the inaugural winner of the new Richard Perrick Playwriting Prize — Nelson Thibodeau, a rising Off-Broadway "theater maker" whose current show Scant is moving from a small theater to the Lortel.

Nelson dies alone during load-out. Initially ruled a freak accident (car left in neutral rolled into him on the concrete stairs), Elsbeth flags two red flags — the body faced the car (consistent with being struck from behind), and a car in neutral doesn't sit still for ten minutes before rolling. The car's event data recorder later confirms it: Park → Reverse → Neutral. Someone reversed into him, then staged it.

The suspects appear at various angles: rival playwright Klea Zane, publicly accusing Nelson of stealing her play "The Discrepancy Play"; Jeremy, who openly envied Nelson's Gilbert's Boy rights and lied about his alibi; Sheila, whose Broadway-marquee-dimming alibi is corroborated by Lenny but contradicted by photo records. Elsbeth traces DNA in the car to Richard Perrick's lineage via a souvenir Edgar put in the car — and Sheila drops the bombshell that Richard was never Jeremy's father. Jeremy is actually Lenny's son. Then Elsbeth reveals Nelson was Richard's illegitimate son by Connie Thibodeaux, a former Broadway usher, who had put a photo of Richard's writing cabin on Nelson's prize submission as a tell. The "Flog or fade" deal: Richard awarded Nelson the prize and Gilbert's Boy to keep the affair quiet.

Sheila, who recognized Connie's voice from eavesdropping on Richard's calls over the years, killed Nelson during load-out to redirect the Perrick legacy to her (real) husband Lenny's son. She used her actress-era subway knowledge (7 → G → L-M-N-O-P) to dash from the Lortel to Greenpoint to Broadway and arrive "last minute" at the dimming ceremony, with Lenny unwittingly serving as her alibi. After Lenny realizes Sheila used him, not the other way around, she confesses and is arrested.

# Elsbeth S04E03 Summary

## Episode Plot
The "Seasons" sister duo — **Summer Redgrave** (famous musician, *USA Sings!* winner) and **Winter Redgrave** (founder of luxury wellness brand **Kreem**) — are locked in a bitter battle over selling Kreem to mass retailer **ShopBuy**. Summer, the initial investor, invokes a **drag-along clause** to force the sale; Winter opposes it. Summer dies inside a **ShopBuy prototype "Smoothing Envelope"** (a compression beauty device) at her loft. The death is staged to look like an accident caused by the cheap knockoff's faulty zipper and missing safety shutoff.

Elsbeth and the 11th Precinct investigate. Prime suspect **Paul Griffin** (Kreem's fired product development manager) has a gap in his alibi — he left the mandatory **collective unloading detox** retreat at 3:40 p.m. and returned at 5:00 p.m. Winter claims she was in a **sensory deprivation tank** the whole time (3:20–5:00 p.m.), but the wellness attendant was also at the retreat, so no one can verify she never left.

Elsbeth realizes Winter *did* leave the tank, snuck to Summer's loft, and zipped her into the unsafe prototype, intending the defective zipper to pass as an accident. The clincher: **red moon ladybugs** (endangered, unethical, never used in Kreem products) are found in a nail-polish flake on the zipper. Paul confesses he'd been putting them in Winter's *personal* nail polish for years at her request. Winter is arrested.

## Important Character Actions
- **Winter Redgrave**: orchestrates the detox retreat as cover; leaves the deprivation tank; murders Summer by locking her in the ShopBuy prototype; frames the accident on the faulty product; cancels the ShopBuy deal post-murder to feign innocence.
- **Paul Griffin**: fired after a psychotic break (shrooms on company trip to Sanya); becomes prime suspect; ultimately provides the forensic link (red moon ladybugs in Winter's polish).
- **Elsbeth**: spots the alibi hole (attendant at retreat); tests the deprivation tank herself to prove sounds carry; deduces Winter overheard Paul's pants comment in the hallway while sneaking out; nails the nail-polish evidence.
- **Lydia G. (Elsbeth's mother)**: visits unannounced; reorganizes the precinct supply closet; bonds with Captain Wagner; tension with Elsbeth over boundaries; tentative reconciliation at the end.

# Elsbeth S04E04 Summary

## Episode Plot
Elsbeth visits New Jersey for the weekend to meet Roy's parents (Glenn & Yvette Stratton) at their exclusive **Nickerson Club**. During a charity golf tournament, member **Perry MacIntyre** (a dentist) hits an apparent hole-in-one on the 11th hole, winning a $250k Aspen trip. Club president **Trip Langenhagen** is later found dead near that hole, supposedly crushed by a falling **Osage orange tree** branch.

Elsbeth teams up with **Rory Torkelson**, a hole-in-one insurance investigator suspicious of Perry's claim. They discover blood on a rock (not the branch) and a trail of ball markers leading from the rock to the body. Investigation reveals Perry *faked* the hole-in-one—he actually hit his Molotov 6 ball into neighbor Hector's koi pond. Trip found the ball, recognized it as Perry's (they'd played together that day), and Perry bludgeoned him with a rock, staged the tree branch, and retrieved the incriminating ball. Tree sap from the Osage orange on the cleaned ball and Elsbeth's shoe clinches the case. Perry is arrested; his insurance claim is denied.

**B-plot:** At the 11th Precinct's community outreach day, Commissioner **Tully** undermines Captain **Wagner**'s "Officer Says" game with kids, then formally reprimands Wagner—his first in 30 years—declaring a new era: "Commissioner Says."
