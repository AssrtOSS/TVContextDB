- Format: standalone "case-of-the-week" with an inverted / "howcatchem" mystery structure (audience sees the killer commit the crime at the top of the hour, then watches Elsbeth close in). A serialized thread about Captain Wagner's corruption is peppered across S1.

## Premise
After a successful legal career in Chicago, eccentric attorney Elsbeth Tascioni relocates to New York City. Ostensibly, the U.S. Department of Justice assigns her to oversee an NYPD monitorship / consent decree following controversial arrests; in reality (revealed across Season 1) she has been sent to investigate Captain C. W. Wagner for corruption. She is given wide latitude and effectively functions as an ad-hoc murder investigator alongside the 11th Precinct, using off-kilter observations and a disarmingly chatty manner to corner brilliant killers each week — a structure openly modeled on Columbo and Poker Face.

- case-of-the-week / killer-of-the-week — 本周案件 / 本周凶手## Speaking Style & Tone
- Elsbeth's voice: warm, chatty, scattered, sometimes literal-minded; she pivots mid-sentence into tangents (knitting, fashion, pop-culture asides) but lands every question with surgical precision. Translators should keep her lines short-clause, conversational, occasionally childlike in phrasing, and preserve her folksy Midwestern warmth even when she's trapping someone.
- Wagner: curt, formal, gravitas-laden; occasional dry sarcasm. Use measured, clipped sentences.
- Kaya Blanke: practical, working-class cop diction; increasingly comfortable bantering with Elsbeth from S2 onward.
- Guest killers: archetype-driven (theater director, matchmaker, billionaire, biohacker, tradwife, etc.) — each gets a register (theatrical, polished, academic, etc.).
- Episode titles are often playful New York–themed puns (e.g., "A Classic New York Character", "Ball Girl", "Doll Day Afternoon", "Deadutante", "Glamazons", "Murder Six Across"); translate titles to preserve the pun/wit, falling back to literal translation when the wordplay resists.

## Cultural & World Context
- New York City as character. Real locations (the11th Precinct in Midtown Manhattan, Hamptons, hotels, theaters, hospitals) are foregrounded; Manhattan street-level geography is part of the texture.
- Consents decrees / monitorships — actual U.S. DOJ legal instruments; preserve as legal terms of art.
- Broadway, U.S. tennis circuit, late-night TV, nonprofit arts, magazine media, ballet, crossword tournaments, debutante balls — these worlds rotate weekly as episode settings.
- Comedy tone over hard procedural grit: this is a lighter, character-driven Columbo/Poker Face-style show, not a gritty Law & Order; subtitles should not over-translate into hardboiled detective noir.
