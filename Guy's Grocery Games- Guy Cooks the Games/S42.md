## S42E08

### 剧情概述

- 《Triple G 全球赛》第四部分进入半决赛，四位代表各国的主厨争夺决赛资格、冠军杯及五万美元奖金。
- A组由韩国凯文·李对阵意大利盖布·贝尔塔奇尼，主题是海鲜晚餐；掷硬币后只能使用偶数货架，获得新鲜海鲜，却无法使用新鲜农产品。
- 凯文凭记忆制作母亲常做的酱油红烧黑鳕鱼，搭配海鲜高汤、炸红薯粉丝、炸葱蒜和泡菜。盖布制作托斯卡纳风格卡乔科海鲜汤，搭配奶油波兰塔和炸鱿鱼。
- 凯文的鱼肉熟度、甜酸平衡和粉丝口感得到高度评价；盖布的汤底出色，但扇贝略显纤维化、炸鱿鱼湿软。最终韩国胜出，凯文晋级决赛。
- B组由墨西哥克劳黛特·塞佩达对阵越南越·范，挑战为“关门倒计时”：只有三分钟采购食材，并制作冷热双拼。
- 越·范制作咖喱比目鱼配冷米粉和酥脆虾片，却忘记关键的椰奶；他用虾、黄油、香料和高汤调整风味。克劳黛特制作冷味噌牛肉 aguachili negra，以及热的墨西哥奶酪豆配牛排。
- 越·范的咖喱和虾片极具创意，但冷面单独成菜的完成度不足；克劳黛特严格完成冷热双拼，炭烤玉米片带来烟熏味，味噌豆也获得好评。牛排熟度和静置时间略有不足，但她最终获胜，代表墨西哥进入决赛。

### 重要人物行动

- 盖伊负责公布规则、推动倒计时，并以“痛苦之板”等夸张道具制造戏剧效果。
- 亨特协助控制回放节奏，并与盖伊围绕奇偶货架和硬币挑战互相调侃。
- 蒂法尼·费森、乔纳森·索耶和曼尼特·乔汉担任半决赛评委。
- 凯文强调韩国料理、母亲的记忆和代表韩国的责任感。
- 盖布继续谈论赢得奖金后在蒙大拿建牧场、餐厅并酿酒的计划。
- 越·范分享父母逃离越南、Bo族背景，以及家人通过给孩子取名“Vietnam”来铭记故国的故事。
- 克劳黛特提到两个正在读大学的孩子，希望奖金帮助女儿攻读硕士，并带儿子进行毕业旅行。

### 新确立的名称与术语

- Triple G Global Games：Triple G 全球赛。
- Odd-or-even aisles：奇偶货架挑战；奇数货架偏向农产品，偶数货架提供新鲜海鲜和国际食品。
- Closing Time：关门倒计时，三分钟内完成采购。
- Hot and cold duo：冷热双拼。
- Ganjang saengseon jorim：韩国酱油红烧鱼，通常使用鳕鱼。
- Cacciucco：意大利利沃诺沿岸的多种海鲜汤。
- Aguachili / aguachili negra：以辣椒、酸味和炭烤元素为核心的冷菜。
- Bún kèn：越南冷米粉配椰香咖喱汤。
- Bo people：越·范父母所属的族群。
- “MacGyver-ing it”：形容在限制条件下临时巧妙应变。

### 连续性相关事实

- 决赛阵容已经确定：韩国凯文·李对阵墨西哥克劳黛特·塞佩达。
- 意大利盖布和越南越·范分别在A组、B组半决赛出局。
- 凯文距离五万美元、冠军头衔和奖杯只剩最后一轮。
- 克劳黛特凭借完整执行冷热双拼规则晋级；越·范虽然料理风味极佳，却因双拼结构不够独立而落败。
- 评委继续关注料理的熟度、静置时间、酸度、质地，以及菜品是否真正符合题目要求。
- 凯文的母亲料理、韩国成长记忆和为国家及家族争光的动机，将继续影响决赛叙事。

### 笑点与后续可用参考

- 盖伊和亨特把半决赛称作“痛苦之板”，反复调侃比赛规则的残酷。
- 盖伊用意大利和韩国都位于长半岛、拥有海岸线和渔业为由，设计海鲜主题。
- 盖布抱怨无法使用新鲜农产品，随后依靠罐装番茄、管装香蒜酱和无酒精葡萄酒完成料理。
- 盖布落败后说下次要自带自己的硬币，成为奇偶货架挑战的延续性笑点。
- 评委称凯文的料理像“韩国情报局”，形容其复杂风味藏在表面之下。
- 越·范忘记椰奶后，以“越南神明相助”自嘲式地解释料理仍然成功。
- 克劳黛特解释墨西哥北部受日本、中国移民影响，借味噌鸡汤说明墨西哥饮食的融合性。

### 未解决线索

- 凯文与克劳黛特将在决赛中争夺五万美元、冠军杯和代表国家的最终荣誉。
- 凯文能否延续母亲料理带来的情感力量，并克服决赛更高压力，尚未揭晓。
- 克劳黛特能否修正牛排熟度与静置问题，同时保持冷热双拼的完整性，仍待决赛验证。
- 盖布的蒙大拿牧场与餐厅梦想尚未实现。
- 越·范未能代表越南进入决赛，但希望通过节目故事鼓励观众了解并喜爱越南。

## S42E09

# S42E09 — Triple G Global Games Finale: Kevin Lee vs. Claudette Zepeda

## Episode Plot
The first-ever Triple G Global Games (eight chefs, eight countries) reaches its finale between two "global gladiators": James Beard-nominated Kevin Lee (South Korea) and chef/culinary anthropologist Claudette Zepeda (Mexico). Guy announces a two-round final judged on the old-school Flavortown scoreboard across three categories — gameplay (20), taste (20), plating (10) — for 50 points per round, with scores accumulated across both rounds. Winner takes the winner's cup and a $50,000 check.

Round 1 (showstopping appetizer, 20 min): Kevin plates yukhoe (Korean beef tartare) with Korean pear, crispy sweet potato chips in a ramekin, and raw horseradish — he pivoted off the quail-egg yolk after failing to separate yolks, and forgot his chives. Claudette plates a tuna tostada: ancho-salsa-seared ahi tuna sliced sashimi-style over black-bean purée and avocado, served on a metal plate with paper. Scores: Kevin 41 (16/17/8), Claudette 40 (15/17/8) — Kevin leads by one.

Round 2 (Budget Marathon): 40 minutes for a country-defining entrée plus dessert on $30 each — Guy then reveals it was "supposed to be $40" and hands each chef $10 extra mid-round. Kevin makes bibimbap with bulgogi (soy, sugar, garlic, sesame oil), stone-bowl rice cooked on the grill for socarrat crust, fried egg and gochujang on the side, plus patbingsu (shaved ice, sweetened red bean, condensed milk, kiwi and peaches) — the free-ice base being a "baller" budget move. Claudette makes pescado zarandeado (branzino, $12.99 — half her budget) with achiote marinade and red-wine vinegar subbing for citrus, garlic-butter rice al ajillo, tortilla and salad, plus churros with cajeta (goat-milk caramel spiked with free red-wine vinegar, cinnamon-sugar with orange zest). Round 2: Kevin 40 (16/16/8) for 81 total; Claudette needed 42 to win and delivers 42 (17/17/8) for 82. Claudette wins the cup and $50,000.

## Important Character Actions
- Guy: hosts, scores on the old-school board, springs the $10 budget correction mid-round.
- Hunter Fieri: built the bracket board ("All night, I'm working on this thing") and introduces it, calling Guy "Mr. Fieri."
- Kevin: cooks yukhoe/bibimbap/patbingsu; carries a towel hand-sewn by his daughter Liliana to wipe sweat; makes in-the-moment pivots (horseradish over egg yolk, dropping the chives, cutting one item at checkout).
- Claudette: cooks tuna tostada, zarandeado, churros; famously edits her cart at the register with cashier Wendy; reveals she was formerly a pastry chef.
- Judges (Tiffani Faison, Jonathon Sawyer, Maneet Chauhan): Tiffani calls the bibimbap "the single best I've ever had — like falling in love," but the patbingsu "a little like breaking up"; churros deemed "ethereal," the vinegar-in-caramel "next dimension"; both dishes docked for wanting more salt/seasoning on the fish.

## Newly Established Names/Terms
- Triple G Global Games — the tournament arc (first ever), finalists called "global gladiators."
- Old-school Flavortown scoreboard; scoring categories gameplay/taste/plating (20/20/10).
- Budget Marathon — the $30 (later $40) entrée-and-dessert budget round.
- Dishes: yukhoe, patbingsu, pescado zarandeado, cajeta, socarrat, al ajillo.
- Claudette's family: father born in Nayarit, orphan grandfather, children James and Hailey, parents Hugo and Nancy; road trips in dad's 20-foot Lincoln Continental from Tijuana to Guadalajara.
- Kevin's family: wife Heejin, daughter Liliana (made his towel), son KJ; married 11 years; from Oklahoma City.

## Continuity-Relevant Facts
- Kevin entered as the one-point leader; Claudette's round-2 score (42) is what won it — cumulative scoring decided the title.
- Claudette's motivation themes: Nayarit/Zepeda family roots, single-mom history (kids at 18 and 20), hope for next-gen Mexican and immigrant cooks; her mantra: "What is for you will never leave you."
- Kevin's stakes: first family trip to Korea in 12 years, kids have never been — the $50,000 was earmarked for that.
- Judge credentials repeated: Tiffani (TOC III champ), Jonathon (James Beard, wood-fired), Maneet (only two-time TOC champ).
- Guy's parting line to Kevin: "I do not know where the limit is" — sets up a possible return arc.

## Jokes / References

- Kevin: "$1.12 left. Rich, rich."

## Unresolved Threads

- Whether Kevin still makes the Korea trip with his family is not addressed.

## S42E10

# S42E10 — Firefighters and All-Star Friends

## Episode Plot

Guy hosts a special themed competition uniting three firefighters with three all-star chefs. Each team must complete a 60-minute non-stop showdown: a firehouse breakfast due at the 20-minute mark and a firehouse dinner due at the 60-minute mark. The winning team earns a Flavortown Market shopping spree worth up to $20,000.

Teams: Dushone Roman (LA County Fire Station 68, Calabasas) with Chef Jet Tila; Tyler Powell (Fire Captain, Rapid City FD, South Dakota) with Chef Justin Warner; Julie Dejarlais (San Francisco FD, Station 13, training division) with Chef Tiffani Faison. Judges: Jonathon Sawyer, Damaris Phillips, Maneet Chauhan.

Game twist (first time ever on the show): "Divide and conquer" — aisle 5 is split by a big yellow line. Blue baskets shop the frozen side (aisle 5 to A), red baskets shop B side (aisle 5 to aisle 1). Teams meet at the back of the store to coordinate menus, then shop separately with no returning to the station once cooking starts.

Round 1: Dushone and Jet win $2,500 for French toast with persimmon-mandarinkwat syrup, chorizo-potato hash, eggs and bacon. Round 2: Tyler and Justin's chicken-fried steak with chipotle mashed potatoes and morel garlic bread takes the win. They skip the five-clue shopping spree ($4,000 per clue, two minutes) and gamble on "what's behind the door" — a full $20,000 check, making them "Culinary Champs" of Rapid City's Station One.

## Important Character Actions

- Guy: opens by honoring firefighters as "unsung heroes," introduces the yellow-line basket twist, and jokes that the firehouse is asking who covers their shifts.
- Jet Tila: uncharacteristically intense — "my singular focus is to take it home for LA County"; both he and Dushone served ~five days in the Palisades fires, the worst of their careers; he dedicates the run to the department and to everyone who lost homes.
- Dushone Roman: calm under pressure ("Why not?"), a fanboy who met Jet at a coffee shop; his house recently flooded, so his $2,500 winnings are earmarked for house repairs ("We'll ask the wife").
- Tiffani Faison: forgets to grab white wine and spinach for Julie's Joe's Special, improvises micro pea greens and renames the dish "Julie's Special."
- Julie Dejarlais: 23-year veteran, nickname "Surround Sound"; describes Station 13 as a melting pot (Vietnamese, Salvadoran, Irish) that is "home"; Caesar salad is an SF firehouse tradition.
- Tyler Powell: second-generation firefighter and wildland-fire veteran; stressed over omelet portions but nails the chicken-fried steak; wants to buy something nice for his wife and two "ornery" little girls.
- Justin Warner: plays calm strategist — grates hash browns from scratch when Tyler forgets frozen potatoes, takes over the runaway omelet pan, and calls Tyler "my ride-along" at the end.

## S42E11

## S42E11 — Triple G Call-Outs (Redemption Night)

### Episode Plot
The episode features two head-to-head "Call-Out" battles where chefs settle past Triple G grudges in winner-takes-all $10,000 throwdowns.

**Battle 1 — Laura Fonner vs. Aarti Sequeira**  
Laura (a veteran Triple G competitor) calls out judge Aarti Sequeira over a tournament finale loss decided by one point — Aarti docked Laura for using a red plate on a high-end dinner challenge, saying it wasn't "elegant enough." Laura returned the slight by opening a restaurant with a "red plate special" that became a top seller. The challenge: recreate the dish that started the feud — a high-end entrée incorporating a grocery list of **red vegetable, red fruit, and something red in a jar**. Laura made sesame-crusted ahi tuna with chili-crunch sushi rice balls and a raspberry–red bell pepper–Fresno sweet-and-sour sauce. Aarti made whole fried rainbow trout with smoked paprika dredge, quick-pickled rhubarb salad, and smashed cherries. **Laura won** — judges praised her stunning plating, layered red components, and the chili-crunch rice ball, though they wanted more visible red pepper texture. Aarti's fish was perfectly cooked but lacked a unifying sauce and enough smoked paprika punch.

**Battle 2 — Crista Luedtke vs. Stephanie Izard**  
Eight-time Triple G winner Crista calls out Iron Chef Stephanie Izard over the *Greatest of All Time* tournament where they were semifinal teammates in a Frozen Food Feud (lost), then faced off in the elimination round (Crista lost). The challenge: revisit the scene of the crime — a **comfort classic using only frozen-aisle ingredients** (Frozen Food Feud redux). Crista did Thai-style fish & chips: fried salmon with corn purée (enriched with Earl Grey ice cream), cassava tortilla chips, salmon-skin chips, and Thai tartar sauce. Stephanie made a crab rangoon seafood chowder — puréed Flavortown crab rangoons as thickener, mixed frozen seafood (scallops, crawfish, seafood mix), tricolored carrots (which turned the broth purple), and pickled aji amarillo chilies. **Crista won** — judges loved the corn purée's clever Earl Grey twist, perfect fried salmon, and addictive skin chips, though the purée was slightly thick. Stephanie's chowder had incredible texture (wet crunch on the crab rangoon chips, perfect scallops) but suffered from overcrowding (too many crawfish, not enough vegetable balance) and a slight acrid note from charred scallops.

### Important Character Actions
- **Laura Fonner**: Revealed major life upheaval since last appearance — ended 15-year marriage, became executive chef at a new restaurant, single mother; framed the $10K as crucial for financial restart.
- **Aarti Sequeira**: Good-naturedly accepted the call-out, wore red lipstick as a nod to the red-plate controversy, joked about Laura coming for the other judges next.

## S42E12

# Guy's Grocery Games — S42E12: Guy Cooks the Games (Throwback Throwdown)

## Premise

A special "Throwback Throwdown" reunion featuring three Season 1 winners of Triple G, returning to compete for up to $20,000 in a shopping spree. Guy reveals that Triple G began in August 2013 in a small Southern California market (jokingly called "Flavorville" or "Flavor Bodega"), and has since produced over 500 episodes and more than $7 million in prize money.

## New / Returning Names

- Tom Ramsey — investment banker turned soul-food chef; won $18,000 in the Triple G premiere; lost 50 pounds, opened restaurant Bon; survived COVID (47 days hospitalized, 2 weeks in ICU).
- Chef Plum (Kris, later nicknamed "Dr. Plum" by Guy) — private chef and father of three (kids now 17, heading to college); won $14,000 in Episode 2; now an Emmy-nominated culinary host.
- Cristina Topham — Lebanese-American chef; won $20,000 in Episode 3, the first $20,000 winner in show history; runs a catering company and her own restaurant.
- Ryder Fieri — Guy's younger son, was 7 years old when Triple G began, now home from college; serves as the special guest and rule-caller for the evening.
- Judge panel (the "OG" judging trio, all from Season 1):
  - Jet Tila — James Beard-nominated chef, husband of Ali.
  - Catherine McCord — cookbook author, founder of Weelicious.
  - Troy Johnson — award-winning food writer, publisher of San Diego Magazine.
- Each judge originally judged a matching OG chef: Troy with Cristina, Jet with Chef Plum, and Catherine with Tom.

## Game 1: Burger and a Side (Ryder's Rule)

Guy sets the base challenge — classic burger and a side — then hands control to Ryder, who picks the game on a "need-to-know" basis. Midway through shopping, Ryder announces "Out of Stock": all burger buns are removed from the market. The chefs must improvise.

- Tom: Smash-style burger using Wagyu and a bacon-blended ground beef, American cheese, and a focaccia circle cut to mimic a bun. Side: smashed baby potatoes ("flying saucers"), double-fried, branded with an "R" branding iron. Dramaturgically runs out of time for sauce and only squeezes ketchup on the plate at the last second.
- Chef Plum: A "burger-rito" — smash burger on a flour tortilla with grilled onions and cheese, double-fried potato wedges on the side. Judges note only one side seared properly, internal temp issues, and the dish reads more like a quesadilla ("burger-dilla") than a burger.
- Cristina: Mediterranean lamb burger seasoned with cinnamon and cumin, topped with whipped feta and labneh on griddled focaccia. Side: bright couscous salad. Judges call the patty perfectly cooked but slightly under-seasoned in the burger itself.

## Game 2: Upscale Dinner (Frozen Food Feud)

The final two must produce a high-end dinner using only frozen ingredients. Tom previously won on this exact game; Cristina is out of her element (her California-Lebanese style relies on fresh produce).

- Tom: Repeats his original winning dish — maque choux, this time using ice cream to supply the creaminess, scallops and shrimp seasoned with Creole spice, a lemon "cream" sauce built from frozen lemonade and ice cream. Judges praise the plating, portioning, and country-Louisiana character, but find the scallop exterior slightly gummy and the maque choux under-seasoned and short on acid.
- Cristina: Paprika-seared shrimp over a succotash of edamame, pearl onions and corn, with a corn-and-butternut-squash purée (ginger and garlic) on a bed of microwave rice. Judges call the shrimp "perfect perfect" and love the bright presentation, though Troy finds the purée more like a thick porridge or smoothie than fine dining.

Decision: Cristina wins Game 2, becoming the Throwback Throwdown champion.
