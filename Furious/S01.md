## S01E01

### 剧情概述

- FBI值班探员爱丽丝·布莱克接手凯莱布·伊斯顿死亡案。凯莱布是亿万富翁杰伊·伊斯顿的儿子，表面死于药物过量，现场却存在割伤、过于整齐的针眼、尸斑和漂白痕迹，显示现场经过伪造。
- 爱丽丝与前同事、NYPD凶案警探丹尼·凯利合作，发现案件与一年前阿德里安·穆拉多的类似死亡有关：两名死者都有一个无人见过、随后失踪的女友。
- 调查指向一名擅长隐藏面孔、清理现场并伪装死亡原因的女性。邻居家的孩子称她曾露面，身上有一朵小红花纹身。
- 爱丽丝试图进入诺拉·华盛顿负责的FBI性犯罪组，并从富有的律师奥利弗·查尔斯处获取穆拉多旧案资料。她随后得知，穆拉多曾涉嫌人口贩运，但案件因受害者难以提供符合程序要求的证词而未能推进。
- 爱丽丝帮助家暴受害者金妮进入匿名庇护所，也协助受害女孩蒂娜准备出庭。她最终发现联邦领导层决定停止追查凯莱布案。
- 剧集结尾，爱丽丝与奥利弗约会并试图套取资料；奥利弗谈到女性连环杀手、暴力受害者与美杜莎的故事，令两人的对话逐渐转向创伤、愤怒和复仇。

### 重要人物行动

- 爱丽丝：识破凯莱布案的伪造现场；追查穆拉多旧案；通过邻居证词寻找女性嫌疑人；帮助金妮和蒂娜；隐瞒部分真实动机接近奥利弗。
- 丹尼：协助爱丽丝调查凯莱布案，提供穆拉多资料和现场线索；对上级淡化女性嫌疑人的判断感到无奈。
- 诺拉：拒绝爱丽丝直接加入性犯罪组，却认可她在相关案件中的经验，并指出人口贩运案在司法程序中极难成立。
- 金妮：逃离施暴者后进入匿名庇护所；身份、经历以及她与后续案件的关系仍不明。
- 奥利弗：富有的离婚律师，有性瘾史，参与女童国际教育公益项目；持有穆拉多相关资料，并表现出强烈的自我辩护与控制倾向。
- 马歇尔：爱丽丝的前任及警察。爱丽丝曾因他遭受伤害并住院，之后离开NYPD；这段关系仍是她的重要创伤来源。

### 连续性关键信息

- 凯莱布案与穆拉多案可能属于同一女性连环杀手模式。
- 嫌疑人可能以“女友”身份接近目标，遮挡面孔，留下红色花朵纹身，并在事后清理现场。
- 两名死者社会地位差异极大：穆拉多是前科犯，凯莱布来自富裕权势家庭，二人潜在关联尚未揭示。
- 穆拉多案涉及16年前的人口贩运调查，受害者可能与当前连环杀手的动机有关。
- 爱丽丝曾与马歇尔有亲密关系，并因这段关系遭受身体伤害；她离开NYPD后转入FBI。
- FBI已决定不继续调查凯莱布案，暗示伊斯顿家族可能施加了机构层面的影响。
- 女性受害者缺乏住处、身份证件和经济来源，容易重新落入人口贩运网络；庇护所和社会服务系统的重要性由此确立。

## S01E03

### Episode Plot

Alice Black joins a joint FBI–NYPD investigation into the suspicious deaths of Caleb Easton, Adrian Murado, and Oliver Charles. All three appear to have died from staged overdoses, and security footage links them to the same masked woman. The investigation suggests the killings are revenge for sexual exploitation and trafficking rather than financially motivated crimes.

The team traces the woman through a catering job and domestic-violence shelters. A false lead sends them to the wrong Jinny Allen, but a shelter resident, Becky Lydgate, provides a connection. The suspect returns to the shelter to rescue another woman from an abusive trafficker, then escapes after leaving her backpack and supplies behind.

Oliver Charles’s daughter, Mira, becomes the key witness. She reveals that her father paid the suspect for sex when she was a teenager, and that the suspect told Mira he was “sick.” The suspect deliberately spared Mira and asked her to give the police a clue.

### Important Character Actions

- Alice insists she can work the case despite Marshall Connelly being involved and refuses to let Frank Choi replace her.
- Nora Washington assigns Alice and Choi to lead the FBI side of the investigation, while Danny Kelly leads the Caleb Easton case and Marshall leads Oliver Charles’s homicide.
- Danny and Alice resume working together, with Danny helping Alice interview traumatized witnesses.
- Alice confronts Marshall about previously hitting her. He apologizes but later pressures her to recant the allegation so his career will not suffer.
- Alice pulls a gun on Marshall after he continues manipulating her; Nora warns that Alice’s conduct could cost her job.
- Alice comforts Mira and uses a trauma-informed approach to help her remember. Mira identifies the suspect’s neurological damage and confirms the abuse she suffered.
- The investigation finds a photograph linking Jay Easton to two underage girls.
- Alice and Nora discover that Nora’s name appears in a report that may reveal concealed information about the investigation.

### Newly Established Names and Terms

- Frank Choi: FBI agent assigned to work with Alice.
- Detective Connelly / Marshall Connelly: NYPD detective and Alice’s former partner.
- Danny Kelly: NYPD homicide detective and Alice’s former colleague.
- Caleb Easton: homicide victim and central link between the cases.
- Adrian Murado: suspected trafficker of underage girls and homicide victim.
- Oliver Charles: wealthy lawyer and homicide victim.
- Jay Easton: Caleb’s associate, shown partying with underage girls.
- Mira: Oliver Charles’s daughter and surviving witness.
- Jinny Allen: alias used by the suspect at shelters.
- Becky Lydgate: catering employee who may have been romantically involved with Jinny.
- Isabel Luna: possible identity of the suspect; records show she entered foster care after abandonment and disappeared from the system at age fifteen in 2012.
- “Memory distortion”: trauma-related fragmented or unreliable recall.
- “Avenging angel”: the media-style description of the female killer.

### Continuity-Relevant Facts

- The killer is a white woman in her late twenties who hides her face and has a poppy tattoo near her pelvis.
- She carefully destroys surveillance systems and appears highly trained at blending into public spaces.
- Investigators suspect she may have been trafficked or exploited from childhood.
- The victims are connected through law school, trafficking allegations, and a 2012 bodega incident.
- The killer’s apparent identity as Isabel Luna is not confirmed; the photograph may be deliberate misdirection.
- Isabel’s records end after age fifteen, shortly after the 2012 photograph.
- She may be targeting men involved in an exploitation network, not simply wealthy men for money.
- She refuses to kill children but expects traumatized witnesses to be disbelieved.
- Alice’s history with Marshall remains an active professional vulnerability.
- Nora is apparently withholding information connected to the case.

### Jokes and References Useful Later

- Choi’s flashy “cool jacket,” fizzy-water break, and Danny’s joke about sciatica undercut the grim joint-operation briefing.
- Danny and Alice recall their old blue coffee-mug incident, highlighting their long familiarity and teasing rapport.
- The team repeatedly jokes about Alice’s ability to make frightened women trust her.
- The episode uses Rihanna’s “We Found Love” alongside self-help affirmations, creating an ironic contrast between recovery language and the violence surrounding the characters.
- Mira’s oversized cookie and the affirmation “My heart is my strongest weapon” echo the episode’s blend of earnest healing language and dark humor.

### Unresolved Threads

- Who is the killer really: Isabel Luna, Jinny Allen, or someone using both identities?
- What happened to Isabel after she disappeared from foster-care records?
- Who are the two underage girls in the photograph with Jay Easton?
- What role did Jay Easton play in the trafficking network?
- Why did the killer leave Mira alive and give her a clue?
- What is Nora hiding, and why is her name on the report?
- Will Marshall retaliate against Alice or successfully damage her credibility?
- Can Alice remain on the investigation after threatening Marshall with a gun?

## Episode Plot

The joint FBI–NYPD task force intensifies its hunt for the female killer. Alice Black and Danny Kelly travel upstate to a group home outside Albany where Isabel Luna was placed as a teen. They learn Isabel had a boyfriend named Adrian, was pregnant at fourteen, and protected a strange, friendless girl named Catherine Grace. The team confirms Catherine Grace—born 1997 in Hoosick, New York—as the suspect; her eyes match the bodega photograph. A 2012 cold case surfaces: a pregnant minor, overdose, facial destruction, found in the Byram River.

Meanwhile, Catherine (posing as "Marta," a former sex worker and advocate) dates a man named Blanchard. When he uses degrading language during sex she freezes, then reveals her history. Later she enters his home, uses WiFi, and blackmails the family when his mother discovers her past and threatens to call police. She takes Blanchard away and paralyzes him, telling him she wants him to be her boyfriend as he loses feeling in his fingers.

Marshall Connelly reports to Nora Washington that Alice showed up at his house and pulled a gun on him. Nora removes Alice from the case for her mental health. Alice insists Marshall is her abuser, not the other way around. Nora invokes rank and orders Alice back to the bureau.

## Important Character Actions

- **Alice**: Identifies Isabel Luna through CPS records; travels upstate to interview Jolie Dixon; discovers the Isabel–Catherine connection; is pulled from the case by Nora after Marshall's complaint.
- **Danny**: Partners with Alice on the upstate trip; confirms Catherine's identity through the foster-home connection; backs Alice when Nora benches her.
- **Nora**: Attends a bureau wellness seminar on PTSD and anger; removes Alice from the case; hints she once faced a similar situation ("a chance I never got when I was in your shoes").
- **Catherine**: Goes on a date with Blanchard, testing intimacy and control; triggers herself when called "slut"; manipulates Blanchard's family by threatening exposure; kills Blanchard after declaring romantic intent.
- **Marshall**: Complains to Nora about Alice's late-night visit and gun use, framing himself as the victim.

## Newly Established Names and Terms

- **Jolie Dixon**: House mother at the Albany-area group home; former pediatric nurse; has run the home for 25 years.
- **Catherine Grace**: Isabel Luna's best friend from foster care; born 1997 in Hoosick, NY; placed with a foster family but acted out to be returned to Isabel.
- **Adrian**: Isabel's boyfriend (last name unknown); possibly the father of her child.
- **Blanchard**: Catherine's date and eventual victim; lives with his mother Raquel.
- **Raquel**: Blanchard's mother; threatens to call police on Catherine when learning she is a sex worker.
- **Ed George**: Assistant Special Agent in Charge running the wellness/resiliency seminar.
- **Byram River Bridge / 95**: Location where the 2012 Jane Doe was found.
- **Menis / Thumos / Orge**: Greek categories of anger discussed in the seminar—society's rage, heroic battle-rage, and slow-burning quiet fury.

## Continuity-Relevant Facts

- Isabel Luna's records end at age fifteen; she may have been pregnant when she ran away.
- Catherine Grace and Isabel Luna were inseparable in foster care; Catherine was bullied for her strange stare.
- The 2012 Jane Doe was a minor who had given birth and showed signs of sexual assault with a foreign object—consistent with trafficking violence.
- Catherine deliberately hides her face, destroys surveillance, and uses multiple aliases (Isabel Luna, Jinny Allen, Marta).
- Alice's son Liam is in a group home; Alice is behind on payments and financially strained.
- Marshall is actively working to discredit Alice within the bureau.
- Nora appears to have personal history connected to the case or to Alice's situation.

## Jokes and References Useful Later

- Catherine's earnest conversation about free buses creating a "virtuous cycle" contrasts with her predatory intentions.
- Blanchard's mother's line about him sticking his dick in a watermelon if left on the table.
- Nora's dry wit at the wellness seminar: "Is this a good time for a break?"
- The Chi-Lites' "Have You Seen Her" plays over the closing montage, underscoring the search for Catherine.
- Nora's Greek anger lecture (menis, thumos, orge) frames the series' central question about rage and justice.

## Unresolved Threads

- Is the 2012 Jane Doe connected to Isabel Luna, Catherine Grace, or another trafficking victim?
- What happened to Isabel Luna after she ran away pregnant?
- Will Alice be permanently removed from the case, or can she fight Nora's decision?
- What is Nora's personal connection to the case ("it was your case?")?
- Will Marshall face consequences for his violence toward Alice, or will his complaint stick?
- How many men are in Catherine's target network, and what ties them together beyond the 2012 bodega photo?
- What is the significance of Catherine's neurological damage (hinted at in Mira's earlier testimony)?

## S01E05

### Episode Plot
Alice Black is temporarily benched by Nora Washington after Marshall Connelly reports she pulled a gun on him. Alice pushes back, suspecting Nora is withholding information about the 2012 Jane Doe case. Meanwhile, Danny Kelly warns Alice that Marshall is dangerous and career-driven, and reveals Marshall pressured her to recant.

Catherine (posing as a woman recovering in a hospital/ventilator setting) deepens her intimate relationship with a man she's engaged to, sharing secrets about her deteriorating heart condition (25% function) and her addict uncle Hal. She reveals her true identity to him and they bond over trauma.

Catherine simultaneously interrogates a lawyer connected to Jay Easton—who admits he was Jay's fixer, cleaned up Isabel's death, paid off her pimp, and has been hoarding photos and files from Jay's computer for years as leverage. Catherine tortures him (hot oil/burning) to extract who was in the room when Isabel died. He claims Jay wasn't present; someone else was. He offers Catherine the evidence to destroy Jay legally.

The FBI/NYPD joint task force, now led by Ed George (ASAC), identifies Catherine Grace as the suspect. Ed orders a targeted search focused on her possible nursing background, explicitly sidelining the 2012 Jane Doe investigation. Danny and Alice (unofficially) trace a lead to Alden Drummings, a man registered as needing a full-time nurse. They raid his home and find him alive but incapacitated.

Alice visits a hospital safe-surrender box contact (Yara) and learns about coded bracelets given to mothers and newborns—a potential link to the 2012 Jane Doe's hospital-style bracelet.

### Important Character Actions
- **Alice**: Confronts Nora about the Jane Doe cover-up; refuses to recant her allegation against Marshall; continues investigating off-book with Danny; visits Yara to trace the bracelet lead.
- **Danny**: Warns Alice about Marshall's true motives; confronts Marshall at a bar, calling out his manipulation and drinking; participates in the raid on Alden Drummings' house.
- **Nora**: Benches Alice citing mental health; shuts down Alice's Jane Doe inquiry; attends Ed George's wellness seminar where Greek concepts of rage (menis, thumos, orge) are discussed; appears to be protecting institutional interests.
- **Catherine**: Manipulates her fiancé with vulnerability (heart condition, engagement); tortures Jay Easton's former lawyer for the truth about Isabel's death; collects evidence to take down Jay.
- **Marshall**: Reports Alice to Nora to protect his promotion; drinks heavily; pressures Alice to lie for him.
- **Ed George (ASAC)**: Takes command of the joint operation; orders "targeted searches" only; suppresses the Jane Doe angle to maintain control.
