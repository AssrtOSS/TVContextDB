## Synopsis
Reclusive puzzle-setter **John Taylor** (pen name "Ludwig") is enlisted by his sister-in-law **Lucy** to find his missing identical twin brother, **DCI James Taylor** of Cambridge Police. John assumes James's identity at the station, using his puzzle-solving mind to crack cases while secretly hunting for clues to James's disappearance. The ruse draws in DI Russell Carter, DCS Carol Shaw, and a widening conspiracy (the "Sinclair case") that implicates senior officers and a local MP.

## Speaking Styles
- **John / Ludwig**: Precise, pedantic, internally monologuing; treats dialogue as clues; avoids eye contact; deadpan delivery; vocabulary reflects puzzle-setter (e.g., "clue," "grid," "anagram," "solution").
- **James (flashbacks/phone)**: Authoritative, clipped police register; warmer with Lucy/Henry.
- **Lucy**: Sharp, emotionally grounded; mixes domestic concern with investigative instinct; dry wit.
- **Carter**: Procedural, loyal, gradually baffled by "James's" methods; "right, then" energy.
- **Shaw**: Bureaucratic, protective of institution; terse.
- **Ziegler**: Political, evasive, gravitas.
- **Holly (S1)**: Tech-savvy, informal, leaks info.
- **Kemper (S2)**: Polished, rhetorical, deflection.

## Cultural Context
- **British cosy crime tradition**: Echoes *Midsomer Murders*, *Death in Paradise* but subverted by protagonist's incompetence-at-policing / brilliance-at-puzzles.
- **David Mitchell persona**: Peep Show's Mark Corrigan energy — socially awkward, internal panic, verbal precision.
- **Cambridge iconography**: Colleges, punts, tourists, bicycles, "town vs gown" tension.
- **Police procedural accuracy**: Ranks, PACE cautions, custody sergeant, CPS charging standards — lightly depicted.
- **Crossword culture**: Guardian cryptic crosswords are a national institution; Alan Connor (Guardian crossword editor) consulted on titles.
- **Class markers**: John's reclusive middle-class intellect vs. police working-culture banter; Kemper's MP privilege.

## Translation Notes
1. **Ranks**: Retain UK abbreviations (DCI, DI, DC, DS, DCS, CC) with first-use gloss if audience unfamiliar; or map to Chinese equivalents (警司, 探长, 警员, 警探, 高级警司, 局长) consistently.
2. **Puzzle terminology**: Preserve cryptic-crossword jargon (anagram, hidden, charade, definition, enumeration) — Chinese crossword community uses loan terms or calques (藏头, 变位, 字面义).
3. **Twin distinction**: Subtitles must visually/aurally signal which twin is on screen — use "约翰" vs "詹姆斯" in speaker IDs; when John impersonates James, label "约翰(冒充詹姆斯)" or similar.
4. **Humour**: Dry understatement, self-deprecation — avoid over-explaining; trust visual timing.
5. **Series 2 additions**: New regulars (Fisher, Kemper, Cole, Sullivan) need consistent naming from Ep 7 onward.
