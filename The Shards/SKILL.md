## Key Relationships

- **Bret ↔ Robert**: Obsession, suspicion, homoerotic tension; Bret believes Robert is the Trawler
- **Bret ↔ Debbie**: Girlfriend; relationship strains as Bret spirals into paranoia and engages with Matt/Ryan
- **Bret ↔ Thom/Susan**: Close friends; Bret's accusations about Robert alienate him from the group
- **Susan ↔ Robert**: Secret affair; catalyst for group fracture
- **Thom ↔ Susan**: Homecoming king/queen couple; attacked by masked assailant
- **Debbie ↔ Terry**: Father-daughter; Terry sexually assaults Bret under pretense of helping with script
- **Bret ↔ Matt/Ryan**: Casual sexual encounters; Matt becomes a Trawler victim
- **Group dynamic**: Affluent Hollywood Hills teens; children of industry elites; heavy drug/alcohol use; casual sex

## World Setting

- **Time**: 1981 (senior year of high school)
- **Place**: Los Angeles, specifically Hollywood Hills, Benedict Canyon, Buckley School area
- **Social milieu**: Ultra-wealthy children of film directors, producers, Hollywood power players
- **Institutions**: Buckley School (elite prep school, fictionalized version of real Buckley School in Sherman Oaks)
- **Atmosphere**: 1980s excess — luxury cars, lavish parties, easy cocaine access, mansions with pools, mansions in canyons
- **Cultural texture**: Pre-Internet, pre-cellphone; landlines, answering machines, driving physical distances; VHS, vinyl, mixtapes; 1981 pop culture references (music, fashion, films)

## Overall Plot Premise

17-year-old Bret Ellis navigates his final year at Buckley prep school in 1981 LA. The arrival of charismatic new student Robert Mallory coincides with the rampage of a serial killer dubbed **The Trawler**, who targets teenage girls — rearranging furniture in their homes, killing pets, making hang-up calls, then abducting, mutilating, and arranging corpses into grotesque "assemblages." Bret becomes convinced Robert is the Trawler, but his friends dismiss his paranoia. Meanwhile, Bret grapples with closeted homosexuality, sexual encounters with male classmates, a predatory encounter with his girlfriend's father, and escalating psychological instability. The novel/series blurs autofiction and metafiction: Bret is an unreliable narrator constructing narrative from his life; the line between his obsession, his imagination, and reality dissolves. The ending is deliberately ambiguous — the Trawler's identity, Robert's death, and the attack on Thom/Susan remain unresolved.

## Cultural Context for Translation

1. **1981 Americana**: No cellphones, no Internet, no social media. Communication = landlines, notes passed in class, driving to see someone. Research = libraries, magazines, word of mouth. Translate "call me" as 给我打电话, not 发微信.

2. **Hollywood Insider Culture**: Characters casually reference agents, deals, scripts, premieres, "the business." Parents are producers/directors. Translate industry terms faithfully (agent = 经纪人, script = 剧本, greenlight = 立项/批准).

3. **Class Signaling**: Brand names function as class markers (Porsche, Mercedes, Gucci, Halston, specific restaurants/clubs). Keep brands; don't localize to Chinese equivalents.

4. **Drug Culture**: Cocaine is ubiquitous, casual, unremarkable. Quaaludes, weed, alcohol. No moralizing in dialogue. Translate street terms: "blow" = 可卡因/古柯碱, "ludes" = 奎宁/安眠酮.

5. **Sexual Politics**: 1981 — AIDS crisis not yet public; gay life deeply closeted for teens; predatory power dynamics normalized in Hollywood. Bret's homosexuality is open secret to reader, closeted to characters. Translate with period-appropriate discretion: "faggot" = 同性恋/基佬 (slur context dependent), "queer" = 怪人/同性恋.

6. **Music as Soundtrack**: Constant song references (The Human League, Devo, Blondie, Bowie, etc.). Keep English titles; add Chinese in parentheses if iconic (e.g., "Don't You Want Me" 《别说你不想要我》).

7. **Geography as Character**: Street names (Sunset, Doheny, Mulholland, Coldwater Canyon, Benedict Canyon) orient scenes. Keep English; they're proper nouns.

8. **Unreliable Narration**: Subtitles must reflect Bret's subjectivity. When he hallucinates or conjectures, Chinese should mirror uncertainty ("好像," "似乎," "我以为").

9. **Metafictional Layer**: Bret references writing the very story we're watching/reading. Preserve self-referential lines: "I'm writing this down," "This will go in the book."

10. **Ryan Murphy Aesthetic (Series)**: Heightened visual style, camp-adjacent horror, stylized violence, period-perfect production design. Subtitles should match: slightly elevated, cinematic, not purely naturalistic.
