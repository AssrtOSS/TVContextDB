## S11E04

## S11E04 — "Can with a Plan" (有罐有谋)

### Contestants
- **Colt Allen**, 22, bladesmithing for 7 years. Started at age 7 after seeing a blacksmithing demo; now teaches at the same place he learned.
- **Daniel Szugyi** (Danny), from Michigan, grew up in Puerto Rico, smithing for 4 years. Wife encouraged him to make his first knife and is his biggest supporter.
- **Eli Timm**, Colorado Springs, bladesmithing for ~6 years. Admits he's never sold any of his work because he's too emotionally attached to it.
- **Adam Gaulin**, 32, Windham CT, bladesmithing 3 years. Full-time industrial field service technician (hydraulic/mechanical/electrical equipment).

### Round 1 — Canister Damascus ("Can with a Plan")
3 hours. Each smith must use **at least 3 different filler materials** in their canister Damascus billet (options included ball bearings, fishhooks, bandsaw blades, chainsaw blades, razor blades, bike chain, etc.). Blade length: 12–14 inches (edge length).
- Colt: ball bearings, fishhooks, bandsaw blades. Used **Big Blu** power hammer (first time using one — said it moved metal incredibly well). Made a camp knife chopper.
- Daniel: ball bearings, fishhooks, bandsaw blades. Used **White Out** as a barrier between can and billet to make peeling easier. Made a Viking seax.
- Eli: razor blades, chainsaw blade, ball bearings. Made a sheep's foot chopper.
- Adam: band saw blades, bike chain. Made a drop point chopper.

**Critical failure:** Adam mismeasured — measured 13" overall length instead of edge length. Cut off ~10 inches in a panic; his blade ended up 4–5 inches too short. **Eliminated: Adam Gaulin.**

### Round 2 — Handles + Testing
2 hours. Each side of the handle must be a different scale material. Tests: coconut chop (strength/durability) + water tube slice (edge retention).
- **Colt:** Coconut chop passed, blade held up (obtuse edge helped). Water tube slice: cut cleanly through 2 bottles, dragged on the 3rd due to thick spine. Added fullers (weight relief). Handle battle: first attempt on handle scales failed (one too short, tang exposed), had to start over. Used drill press technique recommended by judges to align pins.
- **Daniel:** Coconut chop passed. Water tube slice: thinner spine allowed clean cuts. Judges noted handle was "blocky / square and wide."
- **Eli:** Coconut chop passed but had a small edge roll. Water tube slice: obtuse edge folded on the bottle, only cut one of three. Thickest blade of all. Handle not started at the end of R1 — had to catch up.
- **Eliminated: Eli Timm.** Reason: blade underperformed vs competitors; edge geometry was wrong. Ben Abbott (guest judge) delivered the explanation. Eli's exit quote: *"My journey has only begun."*

### Final Round — Condottiere Sword
4 days at home forges. Weapon: **Condottiere sword** — the sidearm of Italian mercenary captains from the late Middle Ages through the end of the Habsburg-Valois Wars (late 16th C). Single-handed, broadsword blade, double-edged, fullers on both sides, single finger ring on the guard, cross guard at least 8 inches, disk pommel. Blade: 30–32 inches (tip to ricasso).

**Colt's sword:** 5160 steel. Guard and pommel: mild steel (1016). Handle: leather wrap + cord underneath, secured with titanium pins. Result: very light and flexible, but had a **~10° blade misalignment** (warp from quench straightened but imperfect). KEAL test: cut through goat hide and into spine but not all the way through (light sword); had a bend that didn't worsen. Pot and Post Chop: flexed and rolled; indexing 10 degrees off. Jackfruit slice: sharp but lacked girth/weight. **Weakness:** fit and finish — misalignment and extreme warp.

**Daniel's sword — "Emma"** (named after his niece, his biggest knife-making fan). **Go Mai** (5-layer laminate): L6 core with O1 steel for contrast. Handle: maple harvested from his backyard + suede leather wrap. Guard: incorporated steel grate from the **Mackinac Bridge** (auctioned scrap — Michigan pride). KEAL test: cut through goat skin into spine; comfortable handle with "void" telling edge position, excellent balance. Pot and Post Chop: good feel, nice balance, no rolls. Jackfruit slice: clean cut all the way through. **Winner on fit and finish.**

### Winner
**Daniel Szugyi** — Forged in Fire Champion, $10,000. Named his winning sword "Emma" after his niece.

### New Names / Terms
- **"Can with a Plan" (有罐有谋)** — competition name for this episode's canister Damascus challenge.
- **Condottiere sword (雇佣兵队长剑)** — Italian mercenary captain's single-handed broadsword with single finger ring.
- **Go Mai (五枚钢)** — 5-layer laminate: L6 core + O1 contrast layers.
- **Big Blu** — a specific brand/model of power hammer used in The Forge.
- **Pot and Post Chop (锅桩劈砍)** — final-round strength test name.
- **White Out (修正液)** — used as a barrier between can and billet for easier peeling (risky — can cause delams if not fully set).
- **Mackinac Bridge steel grate** — historical/scrap steel incorporated into Daniel's guard (Michigan connection).
- **Emma** — Daniel's niece; the sword's name.

### Continuity-Relevant Facts
- **Ben Abbott** serves as a judge in this episode (his season/role continuity: former 2-time champion turned fill-in judge).
- A **10° blade misalignment** from quench warping was enough to lose on fit and finish.
- Canister Damascus with White Out is high-risk: if you forge past the point where you should peel the can, the mild steel can fuses to the billet and causes delamination.
- **5160 steel** for Colt's long sword vs **Go Mai (L6+O1)** for Daniel's — both passed the same tests; decision came down to fit and finish.

## S11E05

## Episode Plot

S11E05, “Top Ten Military Blades,” revisits military-themed competitions and highlights several iconic service weapons:

- Airmen forge Filipino bolos inspired by Operation Bolo. Joe’s blade develops delamination and suffers severe edge chipping during the flight-helmet test, eliminating him. Garrett advances over Robert after Robert’s heavier blade creates an uncomfortable hot spot.
- Fermin and Tyler compete to recreate military swords. Tyler wins the Mameluke round after Gene’s blade develops a warp and microcrack. Tyler later defeats Gene with a stronger lion-headed cuttoe and becomes the Battle of the Branches champion.
- Soldiers make V-42 stilettos. Jack’s blade performs well, but a broken drill bit and a loose pommel leave the weapon incomplete, forcing his elimination.
- Veterans forge M3 trench knives. Gene abandons a failed billet and restarts, while Mike struggles with bad welds and a loose pommel. Jon and Mike advance to recreate the M1917 naval cutlass; Jon wins the championship.
- Garrett and Fermin later build Ames cavalry sabers. Fermin wins despite Garrett’s saber being functional but having a loose guard.
- A Marine Corps challenge centers on the Ka-Bar. Garrett’s blade loses a substantial section during testing and he is eliminated.

## Important Character Actions

- Tyler Hackbarth repeatedly emphasizes military discipline, adaptability, and completing the mission; he wins the Battle of the Branches title.
- Gene accepts major setbacks, restarting his M3 billet rather than submitting a flawed weld.
- Jack works aggressively and breaks equipment, matching his joke that his former unit marked objects with “circle-X-Jack” because he often damaged them.
- Jon uses careful measurements, successful heat treatment, a nautical-star pommel nut, and a historically themed M1917 cutlass design to win.
- Fermin uses military-inspired black-and-gold styling representing the Army parachute team, the Golden Knights.
- The contestants frequently support one another as “brothers in arms,” with joking camaraderie about cuddling, beard rubbing, bourbon, and military teamwork.

## Newly Established Names and Terms

- Operation Bolo: an Air Force campaign used as the theme for the Filipino bolo challenge.
- Mameluke: the U.S. Marine Corps officer’s sword, adopted in 1825 and derived from a Persian/Ottoman cavalry sword.
- Lion-headed cuttoe: George Washington’s custom-forged American sidearm.
- M1942 machete: a lightweight World War II jungle weapon.
- V-42 stiletto: a double-edged, quad-hollow-ground dagger designed for the First Special Service Force.
- M3 trench knife: a World War II military knife requiring a false edge, through tang, and acid etch.
- M1917 naval cutlass: a Dutch-influenced U.S. naval sword with a large bow-shaped guard.
- Ames 1860 cavalry saber: a Civil War-era saber used by both Union and Confederate forces.
- Ka-Bar: the iconic Marine Corps combat knife, valued for fighting, wood chopping, survival use, and opening cans.
- E4 Mafia: a military nickname used for the enlisted-rank challenge.
- Technical terms emphasized include delamination, quad-hollow grind, false edge, through tang, langets, hot spot, and pommel nut.

## Continuity-Relevant Facts

- Tyler Hackbarth is identified as a sergeant and the new Battle of the Branches champion.
- Jon becomes a Forged in Fire champion after winning the M1917 naval cutlass competition.
- Fermin later wins the Ames cavalry saber final.
- Military experience strongly shapes the contestants’ working methods, especially teamwork, improvisation, preparation, and mission-focused decision-making.
- Historical accuracy includes construction details and visual symbolism: Gene uses an antler handle, Tyler makes a 123-layer Damascus blade with a bronze lion head, Jon marks his cutlass with D-Day’s date, and Mike engraves military service dates into his guard.
- A weapon may pass sharpness testing yet still fail because of balance, comfort, structural looseness, or missing components.

## Jokes and References

- The skull and head tests prompt “Alas, poor Yorick” and jokes about missing teeth.
- “It will KEAL” remains Doug Marcaida’s recurring catchphrase.
- Contestants joke about bourbon, though one repeatedly used cup is revealed to contain acetone.
- Jack’s destructive reputation produces the “circle-X-Jack” gag.
- Military camaraderie is repeatedly treated humorously as a kind of romance, including “some people do find love in Forged in Fire.”
- The “log whap” method becomes a recurring improvised technique for putting an arc into long blades.

## Unresolved Threads

- The episode leaves open whether contestants’ improvised casting and repair methods can reliably withstand later testing.
- Jack’s loose-pommel failure reinforces the continuing danger of incomplete handle construction, even when the blade itself performs well.
- The recurring tension between historical appearance, ergonomics, and battlefield functionality remains relevant for future military-weapon challenges.
