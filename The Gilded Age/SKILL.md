# The Gilded Age — Subtitle Translation Background Reference

## Series Verification

**Title**: The Gilded Age (TV series)  
**First air**: January 24, 2022  
**Broadcaster**: HBO (US)  
**Creator**: Julian Fellowes  
**Genre**: Historical drama  
**Seasons/Episodes**: S1 (9 eps, 2022), S2 (8 eps, 2023), S3 (8 eps, 2025), S4 (8 eps, premiered Nov 1, 2026) — 33 episodes total as of late 2026  
**Production**: Universal Television / HBO Entertainment / Neamo Film and Television  
**Running time**: 46–80 minutes  

**Synopsis**: In 1882, penniless Marian Brook arrives in Manhattan to live with her wealthy aunts, Agnes van Rhijn and Ada Brook, scions of old New York society. Across the street, railroad tycoon George Russell and his ambitious wife Bertha build a palace, igniting a social war between "old money" families guarding their exclusive world and "new money" industrialists buying their way in. Marian's friend Peggy Scott, an educated African American woman, pursues a writing career within the city's Black bourgeoisie. The series dramatizes the collision of class, race, gender, and modernity during America's Gilded Age.

## Key Relationships

**Van Rhijn/Brook Household (Old Money)**
- Agnes ↔ Ada: Sisters; Agnes controls the household and social standing, Ada defers but quietly resists
- Agnes → Marian: Aunt/guardian; grooms Marian for society, Marian gradually asserts independence
- Agnes → Oscar: Mother/son; pressures him to marry money, unaware of his homosexuality
- Oscar ↔ Marian: Cousins; confidants, share outsider perspective
- Marian ↔ Ada: Niece/aunt; warmer bond, Ada supports Marian's choices

**Russell Household (New Money)**
- Bertha ↔ George: True partnership; she manages social ascent, he builds empire
- Bertha → Gladys: Mother/daughter; pushes advantageous marriage, daughter rebels then complies
- George → Larry: Father/son; wants him in business, Larry chooses law/architecture
- Gladys ↔ Larry: Siblings; protective bond, shared outsider status in society

**Cross-Household (The Central Conflict)**
- Agnes ↔ Bertha: Neighbors/rivals; old money gatekeeper vs. new money invader
- George ↔ Old Money: Railroad wars, stock manipulation, exclusion from clubs
- Marian ↔ Peggy: Childhood friends; cross-race, cross-class bond tested by society
- Oscar ↔ John Adams: Secret romance; class and secrecy barriers

**Scott Family (Black Elite, Brooklyn)**
- Peggy ↔ Dorothy: Mother/daughter; tension over independence vs. protection
- Peggy ↔ Arthur: Father/daughter; stern but proud, pharmacy legacy
- Peggy ↔ Marian: Lifelong friends; Peggy enters van Rhijn world as companion

**Servant Hierarchy (Downstairs Mirrors Upstairs)**
- Bannister (butler) > Church/footmen > Mrs. Bruce (housekeeper) > maids
- Church/Watson (butler) > Mrs. Bauer (housekeeper) > footmen > maids
- Servants gossip network parallels society pages; loyalty to position

## World Setting

**Period**: 1882 onward (Gilded Age, ~1870–1900)  
**Primary Location**: New York City — Upper East Side (61st St. & Fifth Ave.), Brooklyn (Fort Greene/Clinton Hill)  
**Seasonal**: Newport, RI summer "cottages" (S1 ep7–9, S2, S3)  

**Social Strata**
- **Old Money (Knickerbockers)**: Hereditary wealth, Dutch/English ancestry, Mrs. Astor's "Four Hundred" gatekeepers, inherited mansions, opera boxes, charity boards
- **New Money (Robber Barons)**: Railroads, oil, steel, finance; buying entry via mansions, art collections, philanthropy, marriage alliances
- **Professional Class**: Lawyers, architects, doctors, journalists; aspirational mobility, serve both worlds
- **Black Elite**: Educated, propertied, church-centered (St. Philip's Episcopal), distinct institutions (T. Thomas Fortune's *New York Globe/Age*), parallel social calendar
- **Servants**: Strict hierarchy; butler manages household, valet/maid attend principals; loyalty to position

**Historical Anchors**
- Mrs. Astor's "Four Hundred" (ballroom capacity)
- Ward McAllister as social arbiter
- Academy of Music vs. Metropolitan Opera war
- T. Thomas Fortune & *The New York Globe/Age*
- Brooklyn Bridge completion (1883)
- Electric light, telephone, elevator adoption
- Chinese Exclusion Act context (background)
- Temperance movement, women's suffrage

## Speaking Styles & Translation Notes

**Agnes van Rhijn**: Archaic, precise, imperious. Uses full sentences, Latinate vocabulary, subjunctive mood. Never contractions in formal settings. Addresses inferiors with cold politeness.  
→ Chinese: 文言色彩重、敬语/疏离感、四字成语、被动句式少用、称谓严格（"表妹""侄女""女仆"）

**Ada Brook**: Softer, hedging, apologetic. Qualifiers ("I suppose," "perhaps," "if you don't mind"). Emotional leakage through politeness.  
→ Chinese: 语气词多（"呀""呢""吧"）、委婉句式、自我贬低、称谓温和

**Bertha Russell**: Direct, strategic, modern. Short declarative sentences. Business metaphors in social context. Code-switches: charming with equals, commanding with staff.  
→ Chinese: 短句、主动语态、商业隐喻（"投资""回报""筹码"）、切换语气

**George Russell**: Terse, technical, 威权. Railroad/finance jargon. Dismisses pleasantries. "I'll have my people handle it."  
→ Chinese: 专业术语、祈使句、省略主语、对下命令式

**Marian Brook**: Earnest, questioning, evolving. Early: naive, polite. Later: direct, morally grounded. Bridges old/new vocabularies.  
→ Chinese: 早期敬语多、后期直陈、内心独白感

**Peggy Scott**: Educated, literary, restrained anger. 19th-century Black bourgeois diction — formal, correct, coded. Double consciousness in white spaces.  
→ Chinese: 书面语色彩、克制的锋芒、双重语码（白人面前/黑人社区内）

**Oscar van Rhijn**: Witty, defensive, coded. Oscar Wilde-style epigrams. Camp as armor. Subtext-heavy.  
→ Chinese: 双关、反讽、文言虚词掩饰、留白

**Mrs. Astor**: Oracle-like, minimal. Pronouncements, not conversations. "It is done." "She will not come."  
→ Chinese: 短促判断句、无修饰、权威感

**Servants (Bannister, Church, Watson)**: Formal deference upstairs; dry, observant downstairs. Class solidarity masked by hierarchy.  
→ Chinese: 上楼敬语满格、下楼短促、黑话/暗语

**T. Thomas Fortune**: Editorial, rhetorical, race-conscious. Public voice vs. private weariness.  
→ Chinese: 辞藻华丽、论点分明、夹叙夹议

## Cultural Context for Translators

**Race & Representation**
- Black elite characters are not "enslaved narratives" — they are doctors, pharmacists, journalists, pianists, suffragists
- Colorism within Black community referenced (light-skinned privilege)
- Peggy's arc: professional ambition vs. family protection vs. white paternalism
- T. Thomas Fortune: real historical figure, *New York Globe* → *New York Age* editor, militant integrationist
- Avoid "white savior" framing; Peggy has agency, community, independent plot

**Gender & Class**
- Women's power exercised through social ritual: calling cards, "at home" days, charity boards, marriage brokerage
- Inheritance law (entails, primogeniture) drives plot — women cannot inherit titles/entailed property
- Widows have more autonomy (Agnes, Mrs. Astor) than unmarried daughters
- Bertha's "new money" status = she *builds* the social structure; Agnes *guards* it

**Historical Accuracy vs. Dramatic License**
- Real figures: Mrs. Astor, Ward McAllister, T. Thomas Fortune, Booker T. Washington, Stanford White, Emily Warren Roebling, Clara Barton, J.P. Morgan, Oscar Wilde
- Fictional composites: Russell family (Vanderbilt/ Gould inspirations), van Rhijns (Schermerhorn/Beekman inspirations)
- Timeline compressed: Opera war (1883), Brooklyn Bridge (1883), electric light adoption, Morgan's rise

**Translation Pitfalls**
- "Old money"/"new money" — never translate as "旧钱/新钱" literally; use 世家/新贵 or 老钱/新钱 per register
- "The Four Hundred" — keep as "四百人" with note; not "四百名单"
- "Coming out" — not "出柜"; use "出嫁" "踏入社交界" "初次登场"
- "Calling" — not "打电话"; use "拜访" "留卡" "回拜"
- "Settlement" — not "定居"; use "社区救济所" "安顿所"
- "Trust" — not "信任"; use "托拉斯" "企业联合"
- "Entail" — not "蕴含"; use "限嗣继承" "定嗣产业"
- "Compromise" (of a woman) — not "妥协"; use "名誉受损" "失贞" "被诱奸"
