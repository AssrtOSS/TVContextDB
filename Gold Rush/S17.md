## S17E02

Gold Rush — S17E02 Summary

Episode plot

A week into the new season, four operations advance in parallel:

Tony Beets' Midas Cut at Indian River (60 acres) — Tony's largest and most ambitious cut yet. The Beets crew has already stripped 20 acres down to pay despite battling water, but Tony delays hauling pay to wash plant Sluice-A-Lot so his brother Nick can keep stripping another 20 acres of black muck by week's end. Eldest son Kevin runs his own mine; youngest son Mike is opening up Paradise Hill; 18-year-old grandson Eagan Beets has joined the crew. Tension flares between Mike (dozer) and Eagan (excavator) after Eagan slings mud into Mike's cab and Mike blows a track. The wet dump turns into soup; two 30-ton rock trucks and the D7 dozer get stuck. Tony arrives silently in his big dozer and pulls everything out. D7 operator Meghan Roberts keeps the dump site safe through the chaos. The 20-acre stripping mission is back on track.

Parker Schnabel's Dominion Creek — Parker is four years into a seven-year plan to mine all of Dominion Creek's gold, spending close to a million dollars a week on four cuts. With gold prices up, he pushes a four-plants-in-four-weeks expansion under foreman Tyson Lee. Big Red, Parker's 14-year-old oldest wash plant (over a million yards processed), fires up to sluice leftover gravels from the Golden Mile after the coldest winter in 50 years, chewing 160 yards of top gravel per hour. A broken spray bar halts production; Tyson and Alec discover the ball valve has stripped teeth — closed when it should have been open — and replace it. By week's end Golden Goose delivers 163.6 oz (from the Airstrip cut) and Big Red delivers 47.8 oz, a season total of 356.9 oz.

Greg Gibson's Trail Hill (Bonanza Creek, 60 acres, 55 miles north of Klondike) — New player Greg Gibson, made in hard rock mining, launches his first placer operation with foreman Buzz Legault. Greg has invested $3 million total, including half a million on a brand-new wash plant called Hot Box. Greg's right-hand men Neil and Tyler are on site while Buzz assembles Hot Box (hopper, 30-foot tailings conveyor, sluice runs) before Greg returns. The sluice runs go on backwards at first but are corrected, and Hot Box stands ready to sluice.

Mitch Blaschke's Quagmire Cut at Ken and Stuart's (30 acres, drilling estimate 4,500 oz) — Boggy, undulating benches with pay layers 20–50 feet deep make this Parker's most technical cut ever. Mitch's pump can't keep up, and he traces the problem to an ice lens under the ditch wall: water from the pond flows back under the ice into the cut. The crew re-routes a 450-foot pipeline through a culvert to a new holding pond and drains the cut successfully.

## S17E03

## Episode plot

Tony Beets — Midas Cut (Indian River, 60 acres, his largest ever): two weeks into the season with no wash plant fired and zero gold in the bank, Tony throws every machine and every body at stockpiling pay for a 24/7 run. He and 18-year-old grandson Eagan load trucks side-by-side. A new hire (Eagan's school friend, who paid $1,100 to get there) is sent home after first-day radio confusion; a second worker is benched the same day. Daughter Monica is called in to run an excavator ("like riding a bike"). Benched driver Tim Meisner feels Tony "made an example" of him; 19-year-old Xander Lewis then drives his rock truck into a ditch, blocking the only road to the stockpile — Nick winches it out with a dozer, and Xander is fired, the 16th or 17th firing of the season. By week's end the whole family gathers, water flows, and Tony fires up his first plant, his first gold in "a month and 10 days."

Rick Ness — Duncan Creek: still under a stop-work order; no dirt can move until an engineer is employed on site to plan safe mining of the high-walled pits. In Whitehorse, Rick gets his sleep-study results: severe sleep apnea — he stopped breathing 89 times per hour, oxygen saturation dipped to 54%, REM sleep 5%; "I could have died like any day." He gets a CPAP machine and calls friend Zee (who got him north this year) for support. After a week of real sleep he arrives early and energized, but the mine still waits on the engineer's go-ahead.

Parker Schnabel — Dominion Creek: roughly 400 oz in toward a 13,000 oz goal, Parker pushes foreman Tyson Lee to fire a third wash plant, moving Bob two miles to the Bridge Cut by week's end. Big Red's stockpiled pay at the Golden Mile is frozen solid after the coldest winter in 50 years; Tyson works it with a D-11, "eating the outside of the doughnut" until the center thaws — back running after three hours. Bob's move is harrowing: the truck deck is falling apart and the 90,000-lb plant wobbles on steel-on-steel. The yard crew cranes on a new pre-wash (55-ft crane, 15,000 lbs), bolts a 53-ft tailings conveyor to the shaker deck, and walks Bob to the edge of a 60-ft pad with a 480. Three plants now run at Dominion. Cleanups: Big Red 43.30 oz (down from 48), Golden Goose 144 oz, Bob 45.5 oz — week total 232.8 oz (~$1.1M), season 589.7 oz. They need ~700 oz/week, so they're ~400 oz behind, with fourth plant Roxanne next and no bedrock cleaned yet.

- Justin and the wash-plant yard mechanics: build Bob's pre-wash under deadline ("you definitely owe us for this one").
- Rick: accepts diagnosis, starts CPAP, returns rested and motivated.
- Zee: phone support; credited with getting Rick up north.

## S17E06

# Gold Rush S17E06

## Episode plot

Nearly six weeks into the season, three operations run in parallel.

**Rick Ness (Duncan Creek).** A stop-work order still blocks his high-wall pits, but he is cleared to mine shallow ground. His crew has already sluiced last year's stockpile and pulled only a little over 30 ounces against a hoped-for 100, so he needs new dirt fast. Landman Troy Taylor comes to close out six years of purchasing — 1,700 acres of Duncan Creek ground for $1.4 million plus 1,600 more acres at Lightning Creek — and hands over 250 ounces of gold as the final payment, on a 10%-for-a-100-ounce-minimum deal. Troy then walks Rick to a 32-acre parcel two miles south of the main operation: virgin ground, 20–30 years of vegetation, an old sample pile, and old test holes only 3–6 feet deep where Troy and his father once panned black sand and gold. Rick carries the sample pile down to the river and pans it — five or six good-sized pieces plus fine gold along the edge. He calls it a possible game-changer. Rick assembles a small crew: mechanic Curtis Demeyer, longtime mine manager Jason Folster and his wife Heather, and excavator operator Bailey Carten, who returned from a highway patrol job. His CAT 700 then dies with a track link/pin about to fail, and the 750 is already down with a cracked depth pump, leaving him with no big machine to open the cut.

**Tony Beets (Midas Cut).** With Tony away for a week, his brother Nick runs two wash plants. Sluice-A-Lot has turned out 800 ounces toward Tony's 13,000-ounce target, and the new second plant Find-A-Lot has just been fired up. Nick's crew fights the plant: loader operator Jasmine Gatensby and Nick's second-in-command, Irishman Andrew Moran, clash over the radio ("put dirt in the box") while pay jams on the grizzly. Nick decides to widen the grizzly bar gaps so mid-size rocks pass; Andrew objects that large flat rocks could shake the shaker deck apart, then apologizes to Jasmine for shouting. The widened bars work and Find-A-Lot runs — until Jasmine shuts it down for buildup on the right side of the pre-wash, hits the bypass, and both bypass valves end up closed, blowing the main water intake hose off during startup. Jasmine admits she closed the wrong valve and opened the wrong one. The hose is replaced with one of Tony's winter-stock spares and the plant runs again. Cleanup follows: Sluice-A-Lot 449.96 ounces, Find-A-Lot 350.50 ounces in five days — an 800-ounce, $3.5 million weigh, the biggest of the season.

**Greg Gibson (Trail Hill).** Greg leaves for a week and orders a stockpile and a running plant for his return. A 15-ton feeder bought for the site is stranded at Sulphur; Buzz Legault must drag it 40 miles over two mountain passes (the highest above 3,800 feet), down the narrow Hunker Road hairpins, to Trail Hill, with truck driver Steve Lafreniere winched out after being stuck overnight. Buzz, Neil Gauthier and excavator operator Pat Stewart wrestle the 15-ton hopper conveyor into place, then fire up wash plant Hot Box for its first bucket of the season.
