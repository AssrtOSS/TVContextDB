# Adventure Time: Side Quests — Translation Background

## Series verification

- Title: Adventure Time: Side Quests (Chinese working title: 探险时光：支线任务, established by fandom for the franchise subtitle "Side Quests")
- Type: American animated television series (soft reboot / spin-off of Adventure Time)
- Developed by: Nate Cash (showrunner and executive producer)
- Studio: Cartoon Network Studios (co-produced with FredFilms)
- Based on: Adventure Time, created by Pendleton Ward
- First air date: June 29, 2026
- Country / language: United States / English
- Broadcasters: Disney+ and Hulu (United States); Cartoon Network and HBO Max (international, from October 5, 2026)
- Seasons / episodes at launch: 1 season, 20 episodes (~11 minutes each, half-hour paired slots)
- Second season announced (July 2026) for fall release

## Core relationships

- Finn and Jake are best friends and adoptive brothers; Finn is the only known human, Jake is a magical shapeshifting dog. Their bond is the emotional spine of the series.
- Princess Bubblegum is Finn and Jake's close friend and the scientist-ruler of the Candy Kingdom; she frequently asks them for help.
- The Ice King is the recurring comic antagonist in the early timeline; later franchise lore retroactively reframes him as the tragic figure Simon Petrikov, but within Side Quests he is played as a straightforward, magical villain.
- Marceline is a young vampire friend with a shared history with the Ice King (foreshadowed here, not yet explained).
- BMO lives with Finn and Jake in their tree house and acts as a third sibling-like companion.

## World setting

Set in the Land of Ooo, a post-apocalyptic fantasy landscape roughly a thousand years after the "Mushroom War" (a nuclear cataclysm that ended modern civilization). Despite the ruined backdrop, the world is a bright, candy-colored kingdom of small kingdoms, candy people, cloud people, wizard enclaves, and elemental kingdoms. Side Quests is positioned in the early chronology of the franchise, before the heavier serialization of Adventure Time: Fionna and Cake and before the Distant Lands specials, and is built around lighter standalone adventures similar to early seasons of the original show.

## Overall plot premise

Each episode follows Finn and Jake completing odd jobs, fighting minor villains (especially the Ice King), exploring strange regions of Ooo, and occasionally helping Princess Bubblegum, Marceline, or BMO. The narrative emphasis is on the early development of Finn and Jake's brotherly bond, played through self-contained episodic stories.

## Recurring terminology and Chinese renderings

- Land of Ooo — 噢噢之地 / Ooo之地 (the continent and kingdom)
- Mushroom War — 蘑菇战争 (the apocalyptic background event)
- Candy Kingdom — 糖果王国 (Princess Bubblegum's domain; its citizens are the Candy People, 糖果人)
- Candy People — 糖果人 (sentient candy beings)
- Cloud People — 云云人 / 云朵人 (winged cloud-based inhabitants)
- Tree House — 树屋 (Finn and Jake's home)
- The Ice King's crown — 冰冠 (the magical source of the Ice King's power)
- Side Quest (franchise term) — 支线任务 (also used casually in fantasy/TTRPG contexts)
- The Enchiridion — 英雄之书 / 大百科 (referenced in lore as a legendary hero's handbook)
- BMO — 宝宝 (literally "Baby", the established Mandarin dub name for the character)
- Wizard — 巫师; elemental kingdoms include Fire Kingdom (火焰王国), Ice Kingdom (冰之王国), etc.

## Speaking styles and tone

- The series is short-form (~11 minutes), surreal, and fast-paced, with rapid gag density and absurdist non-sequiturs typical of early Adventure Time.
- Finn: earnest, heroic, exclamatory, often uses "mathematical!"-style enthusiastic slang; in this younger incarnation he is more wide-eyed and impulsive than later seasons.
- Jake: laconic, slang-heavy, prone to puns and stretches of cool older-brother advice; his dialogue is the most casual and slang-driven.
- Princess Bubblegum: precise, scientific, mildly hectoring; uses long-winded explanations.
- Ice King: pompous, megalomaniacal, prone to rhyming boasts and kidnappings; English audio occasionally slips in a shaky Nordic accent.
- Marceline: sardonic, cool, music-metaphor heavy, deadpan.
- BMO: childlike, third-person self-reference, cheerful non-sequiturs, faux-video-game vocabulary.

## Cultural context

- The franchise has an established Cartoon Network Mandarin dub used across Greater China (mainland simplified, Taiwan traditional, with localized name choices that fans typically retain in subtitles).
- The original Adventure Time ran 2010–2018 and has a mature fan translation tradition; renderings like 老皮 (Jake), 泡泡糖公主 (Princess Bubblegum), 玛瑟琳 (Marceline), and 噢噢之地 (Land of Ooo) are widely accepted conventions.
- Side Quests openly mines tabletop RPG and video-game vocabulary (quests, dungeons, boss fights, NPCs, respawn), so subtitle translation should preserve RPG jargon feel where possible (任务、副本、Boss、刷怪) without over-localizing puns.
- Humor mixes wordplay, pop-culture parody, and dark undertones; the show's early-tone direction here is deliberately lighter and goofier than the later serialized entries.
