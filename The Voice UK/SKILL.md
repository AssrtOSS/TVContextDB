# The Voice UK — Subtitle Translation Background

## Basic Information
- **Title**: The Voice UK
- **Format**: British singing reality competition television series
- **First air date**: 24 March 2012
- **Broadcasters**: BBC One (Series 1–5, 2012–2016) → ITV1 (Series 6–present, 2017–)
- **Series/Episodes**: 14 series, 175 episodes (as of 2026)
- **Franchise**: Part of *The Voice* global franchise (originated from *The Voice of Holland*)
- **Production**: Talpa / Wall to Wall / ITV Studios / Lifted Entertainment
- **Winner prize**: Recording contract with Universal Music Group; from Series 12 (2023) also £50,000 cash + luxury holiday to Mauritius

## Core Premise
Four celebrity coaches search for unsigned singing talent through **blind auditions** — coaches sit with chairs turned away; if they like a voice, they press a button to turn around. Contestants who get multiple turns choose their coach. Coaches then mentor their teams through **battles** (duets), **knockouts** (solo performances), and **live shows** where the public votes. The last standing artist wins "The Voice UK" title and a record deal.

## Speaking Styles & Register
- **Coaches**: Supportive, mentoring tone; mix of encouragement ("You have a unique tone"), constructive critique ("Work on your breath control"), and playful banter with fellow coaches. British humor, occasional self-deprecation.
- **Presenters (Emma Willis)**: Warm, professional, empathetic backstage interviews; reassures nervous contestants; clear signposting ("Coming up next…", "Let's see what the coaches thought").
- **Contestants**: Range from nervous amateur to polished semi-pro; often emotional backstories delivered in interview VTs (video packages).
- **Voice-over narration** (pre-recorded segments): Dramatic, present-tense, promotional ("Four chairs. One voice. Who will turn?").
- **Regional accents**: Contestants from across UK (Scouse, Geordie, Welsh, Scottish, West Country, Multicultural London English) — preserve flavor in translation where character-relevant.

## Cultural Context
- **British TV landscape**: Direct rival to *The X Factor* (ITV) and *Britain's Got Talent*; positioned as "voice-only" alternative to looks-based formats.
- **BBC → ITV move (2017)**: Major industry story; framed as BBC losing flagship entertainment format to commercial rival.
- **Coach turnover**: Frequent lineup changes are public talking points; "chair order" (seating arrangement) signals hierarchy.
- **Public vote**: Viewer agency emphasized; voting via app/phone/text — "your voice decides."
- **Spin-offs**: *The V Room* (digital companion), *Louder on Two* (BBC Two aftershow).
- **Terminology alignment**: Chinese *Voice* franchise (e.g., *The Voice of China* / 中国好声音) uses "导师" (mentor), "战队" (team), "盲选" (blind audition), "战斗/对决" (battle), "直播" (live show) — adopt these for consistency.

## Relationships & Dynamics
- **Coach vs. Coach**: Competitive camaraderie; banter about "stealing" talent, blocking, chair-turn timing.
- **Coach → Artist**: Mentor-protege; song choice guidance, staging advice, emotional support.
- **Artist vs. Artist**: Friendly rivalry within team (battles) and across teams (live shows).
- **Presenter ↔ Contestant**: Empathetic bridge; backstage nerves, post-performance reaction.
- **Presenter ↔ Coach**: Light teasing; Emma often prompts coaches to explain decisions.

## World Setting
- **Studio**: Elstree Studios (primary, 2014–2017, 2021–present); Dock10 (Salford, blind auditions); formerly BBC TV Centre, Fountain Studios, LH2 Studios.
- **Set pieces**: Iconic red rotating chairs; LED floor/screens; coach podiums with team colors; live band upstairs.
- **Timeline**: Annual cycle — auditions (summer/autumn) → broadcast (Jan–Apr, later autumn for recent series).
