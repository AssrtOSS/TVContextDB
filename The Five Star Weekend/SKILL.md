# The Five-Star Weekend — Subtitle Translation Reference

## Overview

**The Five-Star Weekend** (2026) is an eight-episode American drama miniseries on **Peacock**, adapted from Elin Hilderbrand's 2023 novel of the same name. Developed by **Bekah Brunstetter**, the series stars **Jennifer Garner** as Hollis Shaw, a famed food influencer and cookbook author who, after the sudden death of her husband, hosts a meticulously planned five-star weekend at her Nantucket home, inviting four friends from four different chapters of her life. Over the course of the weekend, long-buried secrets surface, relationships fracture and reform, and each woman confronts the gap between her curated public image and private reality.

## World Setting

**Primary Location:** Nantucket, Massachusetts — an island off the coast of Cape Cod, known as a wealthy summer enclave. The series takes place almost entirely in and around Hollis's upscale Nantucket home over a long weekend (Friday through Monday).

**Social World:**
- **Food & influencer culture:** Hollis is a best-selling cookbook author and social media food personality. Her brand is built on warmth, perfection, and aspirational domestic luxury.
- **Sports agency:** Dru-Ann works as a sports agent; storylines involve professional athletes, endorsement deals, and public-relations crises.
- **Nantucket elite:** The weekend features spa outings, fine dining, shopping in town, and the social rituals of affluent coastal New England life.
- **Online vs. offline identity:** Hollis met Gigi through her blog; the series explores the gap between digital personas and real selves.

## Plot Premise (Series-Wide)

Hollis Shaw appears to have a perfect life — bestselling cookbooks, a beautiful home, a surgeon husband, a devoted daughter. When Matthew dies unexpectedly, Hollis suffers a very public breakdown. Unable to grieve privately and desperate to reclaim control, she orchestrates an elaborate five-star weekend at her Nantucket house, inviting:

1. **Tatum** — her childhood best friend (representing her past)
2. **Dru-Ann** — her college roommate (representing her twenties)
3. **Brooke** — her "mom friend" from the child-rearing years (representing her thirties)
4. **Gigi** — a woman she knows only from online comments on her blog (representing her present/digital life)

**Caroline**, Hollis's daughter, documents the weekend for a film project.

As the weekend unfolds across eight episodes, each woman's hidden struggles come to light:
- **Tatum** faces a possible cancer diagnosis (the disease that killed her mother)
- **Dru-Ann** deals with fallout from publicly accusing a golfer of faking a mental health crisis
- **Brooke** confronts her failing marriage and suppressed sexuality
- **Gigi** carries a secret that connects her to Hollis in an unexpected way
- **Hollis** must reckon with the truth about her marriage and her husband's death

The series builds toward revelations about Matthew's death and the real reason Hollis invited each of these women to Nantucket.

## Cultural Context

- **Influencer culture:** The series satirizes and empathizes with the pressures of maintaining a curated online persona, particularly for women in the food/lifestyle space.
- **Nantucket as class signifier:** The island signals old-money New England summer culture — sailing, hydrangeas, historic whaling wealth, and exclusivity.
- **Grief and public breakdown:** Hollis's very public collapse touches on how women's emotional expression is scrutinized in media.
- **Female friendship across life stages:** The "one friend from each era" structure explores how women's identities shift from childhood through college, career, and motherhood.
- **LGBTQ+ themes:** Brooke's storyline addresses late-in-life sexual identity realization and the complexity of leaving a heterosexual marriage.
- **Sports and cancel culture:** Dru-Ann's plotline engages with mental health stigma in professional athletics and the consequences of public accusation.
