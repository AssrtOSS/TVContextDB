Overall premise
The series is set in 1970s Korea during the Park Chung-hee era, a period of state-led industrialization and authoritarian rule. Baek Ki-tae, a high-ranking agent of the Korean Central Intelligence Agency (KCIA, 중앙정보部 / 中央情报部), secretly runs smuggling networks to accumulate personal wealth and power. Prosecutor Jang Geon-yeong launches an investigation into Ki-tae's operations and the high officials shielding him. The two men become locked in a dangerous rivalry that pulls in the Presidential Security Service (总统警卫处 / 警护室), the Busan underworld (the Manjae gang), and Japanese yakuza, exposing the corrupt ties between politics, intelligence, and organized crime. Season 1 closes on the fallout of this clash; Season 2 continues the escalation.

Relationships
- Baek Ki-tae ↔ Jang Geon-yeong: principal antagonist pair; prosecutor vs. intelligence officer, ideological mirror images (ambitious pragmatist vs. uncompromising idealist).
- Baek Ki-tae ↔ Baek Ki-hyeon: brothers; Ki-hyeon is an elite military officer pulled into Ki-tae's orbit.
- Baek Ki-tae ↔ Yuji Ikeda: close covert partners linking KCIA money to Japanese yakuza capital.
- Yuji Ikeda ↔ Osamu Ikeda: yakuza father-figure and protégé.
- Ki-tae ↔ Hwang Guk-pyeong: superior–subordinate inside the KCIA Busan branch; Hwang provides cover.
- Ki-tae ↔ Cheon Seok-joong: alliance spanning KCIA and the Presidential Security Service that protects the smuggling empire.
- Jang Geon-yeong ↔ Oh Ye-jin: prosecutor–detective duo, investigative partners.
- Ki-tae ↔ Bae Geum-ji: customer–hostess tie at the kisaeng house, also an information broker.
- Kang Dae-il: Busan gangster antagonist, control over the port economy.
- Baek So-young and Jang Hye-eun: civilian family anchors on each side of the conflict.

World setting
- 1970s Republic of Korea under Park Chung-hee: authoritarian developmental state, heavy industrialization, suppression of dissent.
- KCIA (중앙정보부 / 中央情报部): the de facto power center, controlling intelligence, political repression, and illicit business.
- Presidential Security Service (경호실 / 警护室): elite bodyguard unit with outsized political influence.
- Supreme Prosecutors' Office (대검찰청 / 大检察厅): nominally independent but pressured by the regime.
- Busan (부산 / 釜山) port economy, the Manjae gang, and the Korea Military Academy (육군사관학교 / 陆军士官学校) as institutional backdrops.
- Cross-border axis with Japanese yakuza and Busan–Kobe port traffic (Kobe was used as a stand-in for 1970s Busan during filming).

Speaking styles and cultural context
- Honorifics: characters use standard 1970s Korean address — 존댓말 between unequals (Ki-tae ↔ Hwang, Geon-yeong ↔ superiors) and 반말 between intimates or to subordinates. Translation may render these as 先生 / 部长 vs. 直呼其名.
- KCIA and prosecution dialogue tends toward clipped, formal Korean with Sino-Korean vocabulary (조사 / 调查, 체포 / 逮捕, 공작 / 谋略), conveying institutional weight.
- Yakuza dialogue mixes Japanese with subordinate Korean, often terse and threatening; Yuji toggles between Korean (as Choi Yu-ji) and Japanese (as Ikeda Yūji).
- Busan underworld speech uses tough-guy slang and short imperatives.
- 1970s Korea references to keep consistent: 유신체제 (维新体制), 박정희 시대 (朴正熙时代), 한일관계 (韩日关系), 개발독재 (发展型独裁), 경부선 (京釜线), 영도 (影岛), 항만 (港口).
