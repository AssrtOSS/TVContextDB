## World Setting
- **Location:** Fictional "Liceo Militare Minerva" in **Portici** (metropolitan Naples), Campania. Primary filming at the **Reggia di Portici** (18th-century Bourbon royal palace) and **Villa Campolieto** (Ercolano).
- **Inspiration:** Real Italian military high schools — **Scuola Militare "Nunziatella" (Naples)** and **Scuola Militare "Teulié" (Milan)** — run by the Ministry of Defence. Three-year programme starting at 3rd year of upper secondary (~age 16), highly selective, free tuition.
- **Regime:** Total institution. Dawn wake-up for **alzabandiera** (flag-raising); **absolute ban on smartphones & civilian clothes**; uniform mandatory; curriculum adds **military tactics, fencing, strategy** to standard subjects; **romantic relationships strictly forbidden**; infractions carry severe penalties.
- **Hierarchy:** Commander (Achille Giordano) → Vice-Commander (Teresa Bonaiuto) → Officers/Instructors → Civilian teachers → **Anziani** (3rd-year cadets) → **Matricole** (1st-year cadets). "Nonnismo" (hazing by seniors) exists informally.
- **Atmosphere:** "Out of time" limbo — historic palace architecture, rigid ritual, physical endurance training, psychological pressure.

## Overall Plot Premise
Three first-year cadets arrive at Minerva with clashing motives:
- **Salvo** — foster-care background; secretly investigating the disappearance of his best friend.
- **Vincenzo** — driven by family tradition; discovers military life is harder than imagined.
- **Camilla** — privileged Milanese rebel; dispatched by her father to be "reformed."

Parallel adult storyline: **Commander Achille Giordano** rules as an "absolute monarch" but wrestles with a son at the school and buried cracks in his certainty. **Vice-Commander Teresa Bonaiuto** balances loyalty with a more humane, modern gaze. **Professor Laura Canali** (civilian) enters as a critical outsider, questioning whether the school's archaic discipline still serves formative value today.

Central mystery: **What happened to Salvo's missing friend?** The investigation threads through cadet rivalries, forbidden romances, officer secrets, and the institution's own hidden history.

**Core thematic tension:** Individual freedom vs. institutional conformity; tradition vs. critical thought; punishment vs. choice; the price of "becoming an adult" in a system designed to forge obedience.

## Cultural Context for Translators
1. **Italian Military Schools** — Nunziatella (founded 1787) and Teulié (1802) are elite, co-ed since early 2000s, free, highly competitive. Graduates often enter armed forces, diplomacy, or leadership roles. The series fictionalizes but respects this reality.
2. **Mare Fuori Legacy** — Same director (Ivan Silvestrini) and producer (Picomedia). Expect tonal continuity: neorealist grit + lyrical cinematography + ensemble youth focus + Neapolitan setting. Subtitle style should echo established *Mare Fuori* conventions where terms overlap.
3. **Neapolitan Identity** — Setting is Portici/Naples; many young actors are local. Dialogue carries Neapolitan rhythm, idioms, and phonology (e.g., vowel dropping, "chisto" for "questo"). Subtitles should signal register without caricature.
4. **Gender Integration** — Series highlights recent female admission to military schools. Teresa (vice-commander) and female cadets navigate a historically male culture; language reflects this friction.
5. **Class & Mobility** — Three entry archetypes: **tradition** (Vincenzo), **punishment/reform** (Camilla), **social ladder/survival** (Salvo). Dialogue often encodes class markers (Northern vs. Southern Italian, educated vs. street).
6. **Historical Architecture as Character** — Reggia di Portici's halls, library, esedra courtyard are visually symbolic. Location names (Biblioteca, Sala Pompeiana, Esedra) may appear in dialogue; translate literally + note if recurring.
7. **Teen Drama Tropes, Italian-Style** — Forbidden romance, friendship triangles, generational clash, mystery backbone. Pacing: 8-episode arc, binge-structured. Subtitles must sustain binge readability (concise, rhythmic).
