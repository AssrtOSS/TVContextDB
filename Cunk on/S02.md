## S02E04

# Cunk on Earth S02E04 — "The Rise of the Machines"

## Episode Plot
Philomena Cunk traces humanity's leap from steam power to nuclear annihilation. The episode covers: the Industrial Revolution and steam's transformation of America (Manifest Destiny, railroads, Colt revolver, Civil War, Lincoln's assassination); electricity and Edison's light bulb/phonograph; the Wright brothers and aviation; Henry Ford's Model T and assembly-line mass production; the *Titanic*-parody "Titan 1C" single-use submarine; Marie Curie and radioactivity; Einstein's relativity (E=mc²); WWI as the first high-tech war; women's suffrage and flappers; the invention of cinema; and WWII's atomic bomb (Manhattan Project, Little Boy). The episode ends on the lingering nuclear threat and a teaser for S02E05 (Cold War → rock & roll → hippies → Steve Jobs → smartphones → social media → undoing all progress).

## Important Character Actions
- **Philomena Cunk**: Interviews experts (Brian Klaas on Manifest Destiny; Jonathan Ferguson on Colt 45; Patricia Fara on light bulbs; Ruth Adams on silent-film frame rates), asks absurd follow-up questions (can you outrun a bullet? do bears have arms? can phonographs record future sounds? do shadows go faster than light?).
- **Experts**: Patiently debunk Cunk's misunderstandings while she pivots to non-sequiturs.
- **Historical figures** (archive/mention): Edison, Wright brothers, Henry Ford, Marie Curie, Einstein, Lincoln, John Wilkes Booth, Oppenheimer/Manhattan Project scientists.

## Newly Established Names / Terms
- **Manifest Destiny** — 1840s–50s US ideology of God-given continental expansion.
- **Colt Single Action Army (.45)** — "the gun that won the West."
- **Edison's "pornograph" (phonograph)** — "sound writing," not a telephone.
- **Titan 1C** — satirical "world's first single-use submarine" (sank on maiden voyage).
- **Marie Curie** — "lady father of radiation," coined "radioactivity," died from exposure.
- **Einstein's E=mc²** — flagged as "important later" (payoff in atomic bomb segment).
- **Little Boy** — Hiroshima atomic bomb, "most powerful and inaccurately named weapon ever used."
- **Manhattan Project** — US nuclear weapons program, "run by men with hats on."

## Continuity-Relevant Facts
- Season 2 = "Cunk on Earth" (5 episodes); this is E04.
- Previous episode covered Renaissance → revolutions; this episode picks up with steam/electricity.
- Lincoln's assassination (Ford's Theater, 1865) → slavery abolished → "replaced with simple racial prejudice."
- WWI: "weaponry 20 million, mankind nil"; first high-tech war (planes, machine guns, tanks).
- Women's suffrage framed as "finally women could choose which man would tell them what to do."
- Nuclear weapons still exist; UK recently increased warhead count (Cunk: "they're blanks, aren't they?" → expert: "No, fully capable").
- Episode ends with explicit promise: "world agreed for the second time in this episode to never have a war again, a promise it wouldn't break for a full 52 seconds into episode five."

## Jokes / References Useful Later
- **"Henry Model-T Ford"** — Cunk treats "Model-T" as his middle name; car also called "Henry Model-T Ford."
- **Phonograph ≠ telephone** — "they can hear us but they can't reply" → "they can't hear us either."
- **Shadows faster than light** — Cunk's anti-science theory: planes fly on belief; Wi-Fi isn't real.
- **Titan 1C** — deliberate *Titanic* parody (Southampton → bottom of sea; "non-breathable atmosphere on ocean floor").
- **Marie Curie vs. Bruce Banner** — male scientist gets gamma radiation → Hulk/franchise; female scientist gets radiation → death. "Is it the fault of the patriarchy?"
- **Einstein as "professional tongue model"** — photo reference.
- **Silent films sped up** — "actors panicking they'll miss end credits"; "could we communicate with them and tell them to calm down?" → "they're dead."
- **ABBA "Dancing Queen"** — Cunk pivots to cheerful topic after nuclear dread.
- **Polar bears / polarizing** — "social media polarizing people, like what happened to the bears" → "polar bears."

## Unresolved Threads
- **Nuclear threat** — explicitly ongoing; Cunk's comfort ("we don't have nuclear weapons these days") immediately debunked.
- **Episode 5 teaser** — Cold War → rock & roll → hippies → Steve Jobs → computers/smartphones → social media → "undoing all the progress humankind had made so far." This causal chain is set up as the season's final arc.
- **Patriarchy question** — raised but not answered ("Also, what is a patriarchy?").
- **Chakras / Aunt Carol** — Cunk insists they're real because Aunt Carol charges £80/hr to realign them.

## S02E02

Episode plot

Philomena Cunk presents Cunk on Earth S02E02, subtitled around the theme of religion. After recapping season one (cavemen, farming, thinking, sand triangles, ancient Rome), she asks how humankind found religion and attempts to settle the centuries-old argument of whether the Bible or the "Korran" is better. The episode moves from the Roman Empire and the life of Jesus (birth in a stable, carpentry, crucifixion, resurrection, the spread of the "Bibbel"), through Roman persecution of Christians and Constantine's conversion, to the Vandal sack of Rome and the "Darkages." Cunk then contrasts Europe with the Mayan city of Chichen Itza (Temple of Kukulkan, sacrifices to Chaac, god of rain) while staying at the Casa de Lujo Hotel and Spa in Tulum. Islam arrives via Muhammad in Arabia; Cunk is warned the topic is sensitive and told to follow the script, then "busk[s] it" anyway. Christianity and Islam expand through universities, maths ("al-gebra"), missionaries, and the Crusades (Pope Urban II's "cruce-aid," capture and loss of Jerusalem, Saladin). Genghis Khan and the Mongols slaughter millions, boiled into soup with pelvis-bone ladles. The episode ends in a medieval castle feast that descends into chaos (arrow through the Bayeux Tapestry, Cunk as Thomas à Becket, Robin Hood's message about Gandalf, a space invader shitting a glass harpsichord), followed by everyone dropping dead of plague. A preview teases the Renaissance, when artists and brainiacs "challenged God to a fight."

Important character actions

- Cunk interviews historian Kate Cooper about whether Jesus was the first celebrity victim of cancel culture, feeding her lines for a "punchy soundbite" credit sequence.
- Cunk asks an unnamed expert to pretend that loads of Christians were thrown to the lions, then debates lion mental health.
- Religious philosophy professor Douglas Hedley explains purpose and value; Cunk counters that feeling small makes you feel slimmer.
- Cunk recounts her mate Paul's road-to-Damascus moment driving to Winchester: a jack-knifed lorry, a promise to believe in Jesus, survival with a fractured skull and broken legs, and Paul never forgiving Jesus ("If I ever see Christ again, he's a dead man").
- Cunk films a sponsored-walk anecdote about Paul walking 30 miles to Harrogate dressed as Spider-Man for Help for Heroes while suffering diarrhea, raising £368 before organizers stopped him.
- Cunk roleplays Thomas à Becket pulling an arrow out of the Bayeux Tapestry and reading Robin Hood's revenge threat against the Round Table for what they did to Gandalf.

Newly established names/terms

- Romanite Empire; "enlighteningment"; the "Bibbel" (Bible pronunciation gag); "Darkages" (Cunk's term, corrected to Dark Ages); Chaac (Mayan rain god); Temple of Kukulkan; Chichen Itza; Casa de Lujo Hotel and Spa, Tulum; "cruce-aid"; al-gebra; Muhammad; Islam; the Koran/Quran; Saladin; Pope Urban II; Genghis Khan ("khan" means king; "Genghis" means something else); Thomas à Becket (Cunk's self-cast role).

Continuity-relevant facts

- S02 is the "Earth" season; per series background it has 5 episodes.
- Recurring joke figures: Cunk's mate Paul and Uncle Steve (whose uninspiring Instagram account has more followers than Jesus's 12 disciples).
- Cunk does not know which episode number she is in ("episode three or episode four?"); the expert is also unsure.
- Jesus-as-first-celebrity-victim-of-cancel-culture and the "Bibbel" gag are established here and may echo later.
- Episode ends with an explicit next-episode preview: the Renaissance, new artists and brainiacs challenging God.

Jokes or references useful for later episodes

- Cancel culture framing applied to Jesus.
- Dark Ages put back the rollout of 5G by hundreds of years; constant annoying "hey nonny no" medieval music.
- "Cruce-aid" and the sponsored-walk motif (Paul in Spider-Man for Help for Heroes).
- Anachronism blend: Robin Hood, the Round Table, and Gandalf in one threat.
- Bayeux Tapestry as a wall an arrow can thud into.
- "Bear in a maternity ward" as a unit of religious disunity.

Unresolved threads

- No one knows why the Romans stopped throwing Christians to the lions.
- Whether the Enlightenment will be covered later (Cunk asks; the answer is implied to be later episodes).
- The Renaissance preview is set up but not yet delivered.

## S02E03

Cunk on Earth — S02E03 summary (╯°□°)╯

## Episode plot
Cunk presents a whirlwind history of Europe and the Americas from 1440 to the early 1800s: Gutenberg's printing press, the Renaissance in Florence, Columbus reaching the "New World," the Pilgrims and Plymouth, Galileo versus the Church, Descartes and the Enlightenment, Rousseau, the American Revolution, the French Revolution and the guillotine, Napoleon, and Beethoven — closing with a teaser for the Industrial Revolution.

## Key figures & actions
- **Philomena Cunk**: interviews assorted experts; dismisses Michelangelo's David for lacking an anus; asks if the Mona Lisa is holding a balloon between her knees; mistakes Descartes for "Jim Des Cartes"; wonders if Beethoven was dead rather than deaf; installs Beethoven's mind into a smart speaker.
- **Martin Andrews** (printing historian): defends Gutenberg's world-changing impact.
- **Dan Brown** (clip): cited as a beneficiary of the printing press.
- **Dr. Sarataliyalagamba** (da Vinci expert): explains the Vitruvian Man.
- **Christopher Colombo**: "Italian sailor and detective" who sails off the map like Pac-Man and bumps into America.
- **Galileo Galilei**: invents a telescope powerful enough to watch people changing on the Sun; forced by the Church to recant; sentenced to house arrest.
- **René Descartes**: founder of modern philosophy; "I think therefore I am," which Cunk grammatically dissects as "they am."
- **Jean-Jacques Rousseau**: preaches throwing off chains while excusing slavery through a "clever loophole."
- **George Washington**: crosses the Delaware standing up because he reserved no seat; becomes America's first president, "the single most revered role in the world, until 2016."
- **King Louis XVI** and wives Marie and Toiette: overthrown in the 1789 "topless revolution"; executed by guillotine.
- **Napoleon**: Emperor, unrelated to the Emperor Penguin.
- **Beethoven**: "profoundly deaf" (producer's notes typo: "dead"); his mind is installed into the **B8-Hoven Smart Home+** speaker, which screams "Mein Gott im Himmel, kill me!"
