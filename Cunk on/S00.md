## S00E01

Cunk on... — S00E01: Shakespeare

Episode plot
Philomena Cunk sets out to discover why we still talk about William Shakespeare 400 years after he "stopped happening," promising to probe his life, study his Complete Works, and speak to experts and actors. She visits Stratford-upon-Avon, examining the baptism record of "William, son of John Shakespeare" in a 1600 archive book and the house where he was born. She covers his marriage to Anne Hathaway at 18, the "lost years," his move to London "just like his most famous character, Dick Whittington," and his rise in theatre. Working through the seven genres she claims Shakespeare invented — tragedy, fantasy, romance, comedy, horror, historical, and Shakespearean — she discusses Titus Andronicus ("Tightarse And Ronicus"), Romeo and Juliet (interviewing Stanley Wells, genuinely shocked to learn the lovers die and noting Juliet is only 13), history plays centered on "Richard Three" ("King Richard of Third," the Wars of the Roses, dying in a car park), Macbeth (witches, "king of Scotchland," Banquo's Ghost played by the letter H), The First Folio (debating white gloves with an expert; folio means paper folded once), The Tempest, iambic pentameter with a fictional teacher from Educating Yorkshire, and a word-invention quiz where only "hobnob" earns a yes. She ties his late-life decline to the deaths of son Hamnet (1596) and father John, frames Hamlet as an indecisive version of Taken, and concludes that Shakespeare's "most greatest work" is Game of Thrones ("Queen Joffrey," played by a boy in a dress). She notes he died on his birthday and signs off with "Goodnight, sweet prince" and "I'm loving angels instead" over Handel's Zadok the Priest.

Important character actions
- Philomena Cunk (host): confuses waxy vellum with "waxy A4 paper," asks if audiences wore tunics with mud on their faces, insists she once stood through "a whole Shakespeare," invents the middle name Bartholomew for Shakespeare, calls iambic pentameter "pente-meter," and only accepts "hobnob" as a Shakespeare-coined word.
- Iqbal Khan (theatre director): describes Elizabethan audiences ranging from groundlings to aristocrats, patiently endures Cunk's questions about standing versus chairs, and suggests a giant bifocal lens over the stage.
- Stanley Wells (Shakespearean expert): confirms Romeo and Juliet die at the end, notes Juliet is not yet 14, and Cunk responds she would have called the police.
- Archive expert: shows Cunk the baptism and burial records; another expert corrects her that white gloves are not needed for handling old books.

## S00E02

Episode plot
Philomena Cunk investigates what Christmas is and whether it still matters in 2016. She traces the holiday from pagan winter-solstice celebrations (holly, ivy, Yule logs) to the nativity—questioning whether Jesus was impregnated by a "real ghost" or "a man in a sheet, like in Scooby-Doo"—through the three wise men (possibly three, possibly fifteen), Tudor feasting (peacock, swan, goose), Oliver Cromwell's 1647 ban on Christmas as a Puritan war on fun, the Restoration under a new king "who even had a head," Victorian innovations via Prince Albert's tree and Charles Dickens's A Christmas Carol, the World Wars (including the 1914 Christmas truce in No Man's Land), the rise of Santa Claus over Jesus, modern commercialization, and Christmas television and film. She ends by declaring the true spirit of Christmas to be Baileys.

Important character actions
- Cunk interviews a series of experts with deadpan questions: whether pagans existed before humans, whether pagans got around on all fours, how many wise men there were, and what Tudor people ate ("what comes out of their back holes?").
- She interviews food expert Jay Rayner about Tudor Christmas food and gravy, then another expert about the perfect Christmas dinner, rejecting prawn cocktails and charcuterie while puzzling over bread sauce ("it looks like... jizz").
- She speaks with a small child, Archie, outside Westfield, who reports meeting Father Christmas next to Westfield and theorizes that Santa shoplifts presents, that Batman is "just a man called Bruce," and that Superman and Father Christmas have met.
- Cunk visits a church to see if Jesus is in, notes the Wi-Fi is worse than Starbucks', and watches boys sing "Ding Dong Merrily on High" before asking how music gets attached to words in the throat.

Newly established names/terms
- Philomena Cunk (host), pagans, winter solstice / "winter soltits-es," wassailing, Oliver Cromwell and the Puritans, Father Christmas / Santa Claus / street name Santa Claus, Saint Nicholas, Prince Albert, "Charles Dickings" (Dickens), A Christmas Carol, Ebenezer Scrooge, Die Hard as "the most Christmassy film of all time," Baileys.
