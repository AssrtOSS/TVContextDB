## S01E04

## Cunk on Britain — S01E04

### Episode plot
Philomena Cunk continues her "journey" through British history, from the Edwardian era to the 1950s. Recap: the Victorians "invaded" Britain, won the Industrial Revolution, and Queen Victoria "stopped happening" as the 20th century began. Cunk moves on through King Edward's coronation and death (1910), WWI, the Roaring Twenties and the birth of the BBC, WWII and Churchill, the post-war welfare state and NHS, Windrush and a new queen, ending on the 1950s, Suez and the emergence of teenagers.

### Important character actions
- Cunk "shouts at helicopters" and walks through impressive locations with her mouth shut while her voiceover keeps speaking, as if talking aloud inside her own head.
- She ambushes off-screen experts with literal, naive questions: how instruments know what to play, whether a song would still feel British in Portuguese, where exactly the welfare state is, whether free crisps were ever planned, what was "up with the sewers."
- She sings along to "Land of Hope and Glory" and the *Brush Strokes* theme "Because of You."
- She runs a quiz on whether BBC shows inform, educate or entertain, planting a fake entry ("Inspector Phillips") as a trick question that fails.

### Newly established names/terms
- Royals: King Edward ("not a potato, but a man"), his abdication for his mistress, brother George, Elizabeth as "female king" at 25, the 1953 coronation.
- WWI: Franz Ferdinand (and the band named after him), Tommies, Fritz, No Man's Land (heard as "Norman's Land"), shells as brass-cased ordnance, Wilfred Owen, trenches, "elevenses."
- Culture/tech: "Land of Hope and Glory" (Elgar), Three Lions, Jeeves and Wooster, Lego haircuts, cinema as a "cross between YouTube and theatre," radio, BBC (1922), John Reith and Reithian values (inform, educate, entertain), John Logie Baird's TV, a 400-strong opening-night audience, BBC Four.
- WWII: Hitler (twice compared to Boris Johnson), "elite race, like the Grand Prix," "Win-ton Churchill," "finest hour," "never surrender," "we shall fight them bitches," RAF, Battle of Britain, dogfights vs. flying dogs, the Blitz ("aeroplane poos"), Oyster cards, Dunkirk, the Enigma "magic typewriter," D-Day, *Call of Duty 2*.
- Post-war: George Orwell's "One Nine Eight Four," Big Brother (later on Channel 5), a Labour government like "Corbyn and the Taliban," the welfare state "cradle to the grave," NHS (5 July 1948), 1948 Commonwealth citizenship ("reverse Brexit"/"Tixerb"), the *Empire Windrush* and 492 Caribbean migrants, race riots, Kent.
- 1950s: Anthony Eden, the Suez Crisis (misheard as "sewers"), teenagers, Mario 64, Morecambe and Wise, Cilla Black, Bob Monkhouse, David Frost.

### Continuity-relevant facts
- Edward died in 1910; WWI began in 1914 and ended at the 11th hour of the 11th day of the 11th month, 1918.
- The BBC started as a private company in 1922; TV emerged from Baird's shed; TV signals were switched off in 1939 for WWII.
- Britain won WWII but voted Churchill out; the Empire crumbled (India) while Russia and China rose.
- NHS founded and Windrush arrived in 1948; Elizabeth II crowned in 1953 before millions watching on TV.
- This is the penultimate episode: the next is billed as the final episode of *Cunk on Britain*.

### Jokes/references useful for later episodes
- Signature mishearings and mangled names: "Dovver," "Ed Sheer-han," "El-gar," "Norman's Land," "Win-ton/Winton Churchill," "One Nine Eight Four," "sewers" for Suez.
- Recurring motifs: Britain as a country "wearing a skirt," potatoes (Edward ruling "his fellow potatoes"), Cunk's crisps obsession ("I'd kill myself if I couldn't have crisps").
- Anachronism gags: Twitter in a tunnel, iPhone, Oyster cards, Netflix, *The Crown*, *Britain's Got Talent*, *Game of Thrones*, *Call of Duty 2*, YouTube, Facebook groups, TripAdvisor.
- Serial gags: "tune over, turn off and drop up" (a backwards twist on "turn on, tune in, drop out"), the 1960s TV line-up all "dead now," and a golden age "that sadly couldn't last."
- Production in-joke: "More research needed — make sure script amended before voiceover record."

### Unresolved threads
- Churchill "helplessly trapped inside this banknote" from the opening is never resolved on screen.
- "Why Elizabeth the First happened," promised in the opening, is not addressed here.
- The Suez Crisis is deliberately left blank with the "more research needed" note.
- The meaning of "tune over, turn off and drop up," the "shocks" teenagers will deliver, and what ends the 1960s golden age are all held over for the final episode.

## S01E02

# Episode plot

Philomena Cunk sets out to explain how Britain "came to rule the waves" and invented the "umpire," framed as a journey across the country ("from the white cliffs of Dovver" to "the Scottish high lands of the Scottish Highlands") in which she starts sentences in one location and finishes them in another. History is "a sort of rear view mirror for time." Because the story happens beyond the coastline, she announces she'll be using the "C word" a lot — sea.

The episode walks through the Tudor and Stuart monarchs, the birth of Great Britain, the naval wars with Spain and France, the Civil War, plague and fire, witch-hunting, and Newton's gravity, ending with the American War of Independence and Nelson at Trafalgar. It closes by teasing Queen Victoria and the 19th century for the next episode.

Recap of S01E01 at the opening: how God invented Britain, who the Romans were, and why "we went to war with the roses."

# Important character actions

- Henry VIII ("Henry of Eight"): married six wives (all "called Catherine"), invented divorce to dispose of them, broke with the Pope and Europe, founded the Church of England using bishops rather than a British Pope, and finally produced a male heir, Edward.
- Edward: became king at nine, died at fifteen — "the youngest anyone had ever died of old age."
- Lady Jane Grey: ruled for "nine glorious days — almost a week."
- Queen Mary ("Bloody Mary"): burned Protestants at the stake for religious intolerance.
- Elizabeth I: crowned at Westminster Abbey; permitted private worship so long as subjects "pretended to be Church of England when asked"; sponsored sailors to steal treasure from the Spanish and countries from "brown people"; beheaded Mary Queen of Scots; died unmarried and heirless — "the Vegan Queen," the "season finale of the Tudors."
- Walter Raleigh: first British explorer; debunked as potato-inventor.
- Francis Drake: first person to "circumcise the globe" in the Golden Hind; finished his game of bowls on Plymouth Hoe, then "thrashed the Spaniards" in war, not bowling.
- King James: became King of Scotland and England at once, united England, Scotland, Wales, Cornwall and Ireland, gave Britain the "Onion Jack"; padded his clothes against assassination but not Parliament, leading to the Gunpowder Plot.
- Oliver Cromwell: broke with Charles I, won the Civil War with the Roundheads; Charles was caught in a "big king net," executed at Whitehall, and wore two shirts so nobody could see him shiver.
- Charles II: came down from the tree he was hiding in; plague returned in 1665.
- Samuel Pepys: recorded the plague and fire in his diary; Christopher Wren rebuilt London and St Paul's.
- Matthew Hopkins: Britain's first and only Witchfinder General; ducked accused women — float = witch, drown = innocent "for two to three seconds."
- Isaac Newton: fled London in 1665 for Woolsthorpe Manor, had an apple dropped on his head, "invented gravity."
- Nelson: lost an arm and an eye, defeated Napoleon at "Trafalgar Square," was shot by a French sniper and kissed to death by Hardy.

# Newly established names and terms

- "Tudor": a Welsh family name from Henry VII's father's side; the Tudors barely used it themselves, and Richard III weaponised it to call Henry VII "some random Welsh person."
- "Henry of Eight," "Catholic" as a pun on "Catherine-aholic."
- "Church of England" / bishops in place of a "British Pope" (Matt Baker of The One Show suggested as a candidate).
- "Umpire" — promised as an invention of this period, never actually explained in the episode.
- "Will.i.am Shakespeare"; "engineless wooden car" = boat; "basic paper sat nav" = map.
- "The Golden Hind" = Tudor for "Arse of Gold"; such ships called "clippers."
- "Spanish Amanda" (the Armada); King Philip of Spain.
- "Mary Queen Offscots."
- "heirs" = "the olden word for children."
- "Onion Jack" (Union Jack): England's white and red, Scotland's blue, Wales's red dragon "but not the actual dragon."
- "Swear jar" as the origin of the term "Civil War"; Cavaliers versus Roundheads.
- BBC FOUR as the effect of Cromwell's ban on entertainment.
- "immigrant rats and fleas" and European free movement as the cause of the plague.
- Pudding Lane, "Sir Paul's Cathedral," "the most significant bird in British history since Francis Drake," "Cathedral Review Monthly."
- Witchfinder General; "Vice Admiral Viscount Lord Horrorshow Nelson" (Horatio); "Napoleon Cumberbatch."
- "Queen Victorian Era."

# Continuity-relevant facts

- The "rear view mirror" framing and the sentence-spanning-across-locations device are the show's running format.
- The Tudor sequence ends with Elizabeth I and the Stuart succession begins with James I/VI, setting up the next instalment's 19th-century focus.
- The Armada, Trafalgar and Nelson establish Britain's naval dominance — the "rule the waves" thread of this episode.
- The loss of the American colonies in 1776 is the turning point immediately before the Nelson section.
- The next episode (S01E03) is explicitly teasered: 19th century, Queen Victorian Era, Albert Hall, Oliver Twist, Wordsworth.

# Jokes and references useful later

- Running wordplay: "fork in its crossroads," "skepterred isle," "King Queen Elizabeth," "Vegan Queen," "Sir Paul's Cathedral," "Wills/i.am Shakespeare."
- Celebrity analogies likely to recur as a device: the Tudors as the Kardashians with Henry as their Kim; Cameron Diaz in There's Something About Mary; Matt Baker; Simon Cowell and One Direction; Wayne Rooney versus Noel Fielding; Rod Hull and his emu; Noel Edmonds and Deal or No Deal; Tipping Point; Anne Frank; Snapchat; Brush Strokes; Pret A Manger; Sainsbury's Local; First Dates.
- Cunk's method of interrogating experts with absurd literal questions (waves as "moving hills with the white bits," sailing into the sky, sailor "kissing him to death" as necrophilia, gravity and kites, "the best game show version of gravity").
- Expert's aside: "We're trying to attract younger viewers" — the Snapchat answer.
- The circular logic on whether other cities besides London burnt down, and "Made you think."
- The running gag that the Witchfinder General "went to my school," is now an IT consultant and "is going through a divorce."
- "Queen Victorian Era" personifies an era as a monarch, consistent with the show's habit of treating abstract names as people.

# Unresolved threads

- The promised "umpire" invention is never explained.
- Whether any other cities besides London burnt down remains unanswerable because, as Cunk argues, any evidence burnt with them.
- Matthew Hopkins the IT consultant and his divorce are left dangling.
- Nelson being "banished" high on his column, out of eyeshot and "shat on by birds," is posed but not resolved beyond "it's just like a joke."
- S01E03 questions left open: "Who was Albert Hall?", "Why did Oliver Twist?", "What are Words Worth?", and how Wordsworth got away with a cloud that has no legs.

## S01E03

Episode plot — Cunk on Britain, S01E03 (the 19th century)

Philomena Cunk presents the 19th century of "The United Britain of Great Kingdom," framing history as a rear-view mirror for time. The episode moves from the Georgian era (when every English king had to be called George) through the Romantic poets, Queen Victoria's reign, the Industrial Revolution, the expansion of the British Empire (including India and the Raj), Victorian culture and crime, and the suffragette movement. It closes with Victoria's death in 1901 and a teaser for the early 20th century: TV, two World Wars, and no World Cups.

Important character actions

- Philomena Cunk (host/interviewer): interviews a Romantics expert, asking who "Ron" is (the poet who signed works "By Ron"), where "exile" is, and whether clouds are made of boiling water; interviews computing historian Doron Swade about Charles Babbage's computer, asking where its games and screen are; interviews a Darwin expert, asking if The Beagle was a dog, whether ships evolved into dogs, how long a zoo monkey takes to turn human, and how fast a pig could become a cow; repeatedly misnames Charles Dickens as "Sir Charles Dickings."

Newly established names/terms

- Georgian Era — period when all English kings were called George (George One, Two, Three, Four, not necessarily in that order).
- The Romantics — Wordsworth, Shelley, Blake, and Lord George Byron ("By Ron," who signed poems "By Ron").
- Jane Austen — Hampshire novelist whose books Cunk wants translated "into proper English."
- Queen Victoria (born 1819, crowned 1837) and Prince Albert (her German cousin/husband, d. 1861).
- Industrial Revolution — called the "Industrial Revelation" and a "changequake"; powered by coal-made steam.
- Isambard Kingdom Brunel — "half-human, half-train" super-engineer; built the Clifton Suspension Bridge.
- Charles Babbage — invented the computer too early; machine the size of a Transit van, no screen or games; built by Doron Swade.
- HMS Beagle — Darwin's ship (which Cunk mistakes for a dog).
- Darwin's evolution theory — garbled as "The Oranges Of The Peaches," claiming oranges evolved from peaches and men from monkeys.
- Sherlock Holmes — invented by "Sir Arthur Coning Roddy Doyle"; 55 stories, "four more than The Fast And The Furious."
- Sir Charles Dickings (Charles Dickens) — Oliver's Twist, Great Defecations, Nicholas Nickelback, David's Copper Field, The Picnic Papers.
- Oscar Wilde — persecuted for being gay, imprisoned in Reed-ing Gaol.
- British Empire / British Raj — India as the "Jewel in the Crown"; East India Company replaced by the Crown in 1858; Victoria made Empress of India in 1877 but never visited.
- Jack the Ripper — uncaught Whitechapel serial killer of 1888.
- Emmeline "Emmerdale" Pankhurst — founded the suffragette movement; Emily Davison threw herself under a horse for the vote.

Continuity-relevant facts

- Last-time recap (S01E02): the British leaped out of Britain and sailed the world in boats.
- Cunk on Britain is presented as the history of "The United Britain of Great Kingdom."
- Victoria died in 1901; women had no vote, though "half the men in Britain were women" (Cunk's logic).
- Next episode (teaser): early 20th century, television invented, two World Wars but no World Cups, and why WWI soldiers were all called Tommy.

Jokes and references useful for later episodes

- Byron described as a rock star: sexy like Mick Jagger, brooding like Kurt Cobain, brown hair like Harry Styles; recurring gag that he slept with his sister.
- "Noel Gallagher's Islands" (Galápagos misnomer).
- Babbage's name autocorrected to "cabbage."
- Trains described as having a big smiling face and the voice of Ringo Starr; "mankind bred with trains" producing Brunel.
- Darwin "inventing the monkey"; pig-into-a-cow evolution question.
- Dickens's titles deliberately misnamed; Oliver's Twist "has no twist" called a brilliant twist.
- Wilde's prison song misspells "jail" on the cover; Victorian moral hypocrisy about ankles and bottoms.
- Royal Albert Hall said to hold Adolf Hitler's missing bollock.
- Victoria's funeral captured by moving pictures, too late for her to enjoy the footage.
- Suffragettes as "Wonder Woman, but with sleeves"; hunger strikes starting the "food detox craze"; voting booth as a pencil on a shoelace with no games or sandwiches.
- Jack the Ripper possibly still alive, maybe your friend or neighbor; his nickname compared to 50 Cent.
- BBC comedy Brush Strokes referenced as premiering 125 years after Albert's death.

Unresolved threads

- Jack the Ripper's identity, motive, and fate — never caught; possibly still alive today.
- Whether women actually get the vote — deferred to the next period of history.
- Why all British WWI soldiers were called Tommy (set up for S01E04).

## S01E01

# Cunk on Britain — S01E01 summary

## Episode plot
Philomena Cunk presents "Cunk on Britain," a journey through British history from the Big Bang to the Tudors, framed as looking into "history, a sort of rear view mirror for time" after Brexit ("Now we've got our country back, what actually is it?"). She travels from Dover to the Scottish Highlands, covering: the Big Bang and Pangea; dinosaurs; the Stone, Bronze, and Iron Ages; Stonehenge; the Roman invasion (Hadrian's Wall, Colchester, Roman Bath, roads); Boudicca's revolt; the Dark Ages; King Arthur and Camelot; St George; Anglo-Saxons and Vikings; Alfred the Great; 1066, Harold, and the Battle of Hastings; William the Conqueror; Norman Architecture; the Domesday Book; Magna Carta; and the War of the Roses, ending at Bosworth and Richard III. The Tudors are explicitly deferred to a later episode, and the closing teaser questions whether "Henry of Eight" is really who he was.

## Important character actions
- Cunk shouts at helicopters, walks somewhere impressive with her mouth shut while her voiceover speaks anyway, and interviews experts while misunderstanding basics (e.g., she thinks Camelot means Arthur "came a lot," wonders if souls live inside the Domesday Book, and hopes the book is cursed like Raiders of the Lost Ark).
- Experts patiently correct her on Arthur/Camelot, St George's non-English origins, and the Domesday Book's actual contents.
- William the Conqueror invades in 1066 and wins at Hastings; Richard III hides under a car park for centuries before "emerging in 2013."

## Newly established names/terms
- Philomena Cunk; "Cunk on Britain"; Cunk's malapropism "the United Britain of Great Kingdom"
- Big Bang; Pangea; "Across ones" and "Up-and-Down ones" dinosaurs; "Tyrannical Sawdust Rex"
- Stonehenge ("the only clock you can see from space"); Cerne Abbas Giant ("second crudest hill in British history, after Benny")
- Hadrian's Wall ("Hard Ian's wall"); Colchester (Roman capital); Roman town of Bathe/Bath
- Queen Boudicca; Dark Ages (Romans "took the last pens"); King Arthur, Camelot, Round Table ("a sort of lazy Susan")
- St George (father from Turkey, mother from Palestine, never came to England; dragon story made up)
- Angles, Saxons, Vikings; Alfred of Great; Harold; 1066; Battle of Hastings; William the Conqueror
- "Baywatch Tapestry" (Cunk's name for the Bayeux Tapestry); "Norman Architecture" (treated as a person's name)
- Domesday Book ("the internet of its day"); Magna Carta ("terms and conditions notice")
- War of the Roses; Battle of Bosworth; Richard III; the Tudors ("the Kardashians of British history"); "Henry of Eight" (next-episode teaser)

## Continuity-relevant facts
- S01 = Britain (5 episodes); S02 = Earth (5 episodes), per the series skill file; episode notes live at S01.md/S02.md/S00.md.
- The Tudors arc and Henry VIII ("Henry of Eight") are deliberately pushed to future episodes.
- The episode's Brexit framing ("took back control from the unelected bureaucrats of Rome," "William winning was like Brexit backwards") sets the show's comic lens.

## Jokes or references useful for later episodes
- "Now we've got our country back, what actually is it?" (post-Brexit thesis)
- John Major as "Prince of Onions" — Cunk draws a blank ("Ummmmmm...")
- Iron Man lacked superpowers and "couldn't tolerate Gwyneth Paltrow"
- Cerne Abbas Giant "still young enough to get wood"
- Steve McFadden boarding Concorde; Halley's Comet last seen in 1986, the year Brush Strokes started
- Braveheart / Mel Gibson playing a "Scottish Apache"; Raiders of the Lost Ark face-melting
- Martina Cole's The Runaway vs. the Domesday Book
- "Henry of Eight" identity doubt as a running teaser

## Unresolved threads
- Why John Major was called the Prince of Onions (unanswered)
- Whether King Arthur "came a lot" (Cunk unconvinced; one-child evidence suggests probably not)
- Whether the Domesday Book is cursed (expert denies it; Cunk disappointed)
- Whether people's souls are trapped inside the Domesday Book (denied)
- Whether Henry of Eight is really himself or someone pretending (teaser, unresolved)
- The Tudors story, deferred "for now"

## S01E05

# Cunk on Britain S01E05 — "The Arse End of History"

## Episode Plot
Philomena Cunk concludes her odyssey through British history, covering the 1960s to the 2016 Brexit referendum. The episode traces the Swinging Sixties (Beatles, sexual revolution, miniskirts), the turbulent 1970s (economic crisis, three-day week, Winter of Discontent, punk), the Thatcher 1980s (Falklands, miners' strike, privatization, yuppies), the 1990s (Cool Britannia, Blur/Blair confusion, Diana's death, Good Friday Agreement), the 2000s (Iraq War, financial crisis), and the Cameron era (Scottish independence referendum, Brexit).

## Important Character Actions
- **Philomena Cunk**: Narrates, interviews experts, deliberately confuses names/terms for comic effect
- **The Beatles**: "Discover colour" via LUZZD (LSD), spark Beatlemania
- **Edward Heath**: Takes UK into European Common Market ("Brentrance")
- **Margaret Thatcher**: Wins Falklands War ("Onion Jack" flies again), crushes Miners' Strike (led by "Arthur Scarface"), privatizes state assets
- **Tony Blair / "Blur"**: Running gag confusing PM with band; "guitarist and D:ream fan"; Cool Britannia party; Iraq War; becomes "living saint like Bono or Holly Willoughby"
- **Gordon Brown ("Gorgon Brown")**: "Stuck a plaster on the economy" during financial crisis
- **David Cameron ("Davis Cameron")**: "Skilfully almost won" 2010 election; forms "Brokeback Mountain style coalition" with Nick Clegg; calls Brexit referendum to "stop anyone arguing about Europe ever again"
- **Theresa May / Jeremy Corbyn**: Appear in final moments post-referendum
