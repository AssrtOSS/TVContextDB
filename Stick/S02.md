## S02E02

## S02E02 — Episode Summary

### Plot
The group travels to Los Angeles for **The Founders Invitational** (a PGA Tour signature event) after Jeremy Pierce secures Santi a sponsor exemption. Santi plays spectacularly as an amateur — makes the cut, contends all weekend, finishes **T3** (best-ever amateur finish at a PGA Tour event). The episode centers on the **pro-vs-college decision**: Jeremy and Elena push Santi to turn pro immediately; Pryce insists he's not ready. After a heated confrontation where Santi accuses Pryce of projecting his own regrets, Santi declares he's turning pro *without* Pryce. The episode ends with Pryce and Mitts navigating their new living situation (Elena and Mitts moving in together).

---

### Important Character Actions
- **Clark Ross** — Ambushes Pryce in Indiana with a "cockamamie" pitch: play the U.S. Open at Silver Pines in 10 weeks, become oldest major winner ever. Later in LA, gives Pryce a raw confession: he resented Pryce's talent, but always wondered what he could've been. "Fuck you for not trying."
- **Jeremy Pierce** — Agent friend; secures Santi's Founders exemption; aggressively recruits Santi to turn pro ("fortune favors the bold"; Billy Bowman cautionary tale).
- **Santi Wheeler** — Lights-out golf (T3, led field in GIR). Defies Pryce, announces he's turning pro *now*, cuts Pryce from his corner: "I can't have somebody who doesn't believe I can do this."
- **Pryce Cahill** — Rejects Clark's comeback pitch; tries to protect Santi from rushing; ultimately accepts Santi's decision ("Fine. Let's go pro") but Santi leaves anyway.
- **Mitts & Elena** — Announce they're moving in together; Mitts tells Pryce he needs to find a new place (offers the RV/trailer as interim).
- **Zero** — Present in LA; bonding with Santi (tacos, concert plans); supports Santi but stays out of the pro-decision fight.

# Stick S02E04 Summary

## Episode Plot

**Pryce's comeback bid takes shape** — At 55, Pryce Cahill insists on chasing a U.S. Open spot. He courts Amanda (his past flame, Butch's daughter) as swing coach. She initially refuses — "I'm spending time with my dad… I don't have time to chase some fairy tale" — but Pryce leverages her father's condition (referencing *Awakenings*, Robin Williams/De Niro) as emotional pressure. Amanda agrees: **$2,000/day + 20% of winnings**, plus a "Neon Rodeo" Trifecta rematch (Skee-Ball, Air Hockey, Step Maniax). She beats him fair and square, sealing the deal.

**Clark Ross goes bust** — Mitts discovers Clark's Corvette being repossessed. Clark admits total financial collapse: restaurant failures, meme-stock losses, **$2M spent on an NFT** ("a piece of digital art you can only look at on your phone"), ReadySafe buyout with no real payout, and a Ross Academy that hasn't produced a star in nine years. "The Ross Empire has fallen." Mitts talks him off the ledge, reframing Clark's gift of gab as an asset: "you're the da Vinci of bullshit."

**Santi's pro debut flames out** — At the Charles Schwab Classic (Round 2), Santiago Wheeler misses the cut at +5. He hits it "in the piss," swings out of his shoes, loses rhythm. His putter "literally just disappeared." Elena is barred from the men's locker room ("You're a woman… And still a woman") and can only watch him unravel. Hydro Flask sponsorship added to his gear overnight without her knowledge — Jeremy admits "last second" timing.

**Mitts vs. pride** — Clark needles Mitts about not calling Elena ("Her name is Elena, his Latin lover"). Mitts insists "she wanted a beat" and "the phone rings both ways." Clark, drawing on two annulments and three divorces, warns: "Your ego is not your amigo." Mitts eventually concedes: "He's right."

## S02E05

## Episode plot (S02E05 — “Birdie Machine”)

The episode opens with a 1981 flashback in Dayton, Ohio: a young Pryce Cahill bonds with his son over a round of golf, insisting one win paid for their whole Lake Geneva vacation (“You got a God-given talent”). Back in the present, Pryce and his new coach Amanda walk Silver Pines, the brutal 7,913-yard US Open venue. Amanda pulls his stats from The Dove — every drive and iron below tour average, yet he shot 61 on a course built for celebrities to feel good about themselves. Their strategy: “shorten the course” by bombing through the trees on holes 10, 13, and 18, relying on Pryce’s elite shot control and spin rather than power.

Meanwhile, Santi’s career is accelerating. EverCorp wants to build a skincare signature line around him (“No grease, no shine. Just clutch hydration.” “Blocks the burn, built for birdies.”), inviting him to Miami in August to meet and talk deal terms — with Kendrick Lamar in the mix. Clark Ross, vacationing in Saint Barth’s, pitches Pryce’s comeback narrative to Nike’s Bobby Kroft and gets rejected, though he keeps championing Pryce privately. Santi’s agent Jeremy sends assistant Keith to a meeting in his place; Santi meets Dominique, who wants Zero to launch a PTCHWRK capsule line using Japanese selvedge denim from a Boyle Heights warehouse, and wants Santi to model.

The emotional core: Jeremy replaces Zero with Todd Seaver, Rory McIlroy’s former caddy, as Santi’s pro caddy. Zero accepts it pragmatically but calls out that they only see each other on the course now; Santi promises to make time for them. Mitts visits his father’s dementia-preserved room and listens to his dad’s old “Go time” pre-tournament tape, then gets confronted by Clark, who asks for startup capital to buy a house and fund his Open campaign. Mitts refuses, and they erupt into a fight about commitment — Clark accuses Mitts of the same fear that’s stalling his relationship with Elena; Mitts insists he’s “giving her an out.”

Amanda’s coaching is tough love: she mocks Pryce’s showy shot as “razzle-dazzle street magic,” says he’s afraid of showing up, and dredges up Sapphire Lake, 1995 — Pryce stood her up the night before the Scottish Open. Pryce admits he was a jerk; she coldly notes 20-year-old Pryce in his Tacoma (not a Ford Ranger) never broke her heart — at 18 she knew he wasn’t worth investing in. Pryce ends the episode hitting the “Ole Ginny” bell at Silver Pines — hung there by Fuzzy Zoeller and his dad in ’96, previously rung by Mickelson and Bubba Watson — making it ring on command.

## Important character actions

- Pryce: practices the tree-cutting shortcut with Amanda, takes her brutal coaching, confesses to the 1995 Sapphire Lake stand-up, and rings the Ole Ginny bell.
- Amanda: walks Silver Pines with Pryce, delivers tough-love criticism (“You’re afraid of showing up”), reveals the 1995 history, storms off after their first session.
- Santi: gets the EverCorp skincare pitch, meets Dominique, agrees to let Todd Seaver replace Zero as his caddy, promises Zero time off the course.

- Elena: not speaking to Mitts (“on a break”); leaves a voicemail about The Schwab.
