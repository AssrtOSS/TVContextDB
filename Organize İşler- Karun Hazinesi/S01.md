## S01E06

# Episode Summary

## Plot

The episode opens with Lisa Wilking (pen name Lisa Konrad), a novelist, being driven into town to visit the parents she has not seen in ten years; their estrangement stems from her sister's murder. After a brief stop to care for her dog Bukowski, the on-screen titles "THE TRAP" / "THE TRUTH" play, after which Lisa arrives at the family home.

Her parents receive her coolly. They reveal that a journalist (from a reputable paper) visited them earlier, knew her real name, and showed them an excerpt of her novel that fictionalises Anna's murder. Her father politely threw the man out. The chapter in question describes Anna being "murdered"; Lisa insists the novel will never be published and was written only for herself.

Meanwhile at Reflekt magazine, Robert Lenzen phones Dr. Katrin Solms: his interview with Lisa has to be cut short because he is flying out to Syria and also meeting potential buyers for his parents' house (24 Dalantstraße, a Munich bungalow with a large garden). Robert suggests Katrin handle the interview solo; she agrees to call Lisa.

At Lisa's parents' house, music plays — the same kind of music from a previous visit. Katrin calls Lisa to reschedule the interview. A woman also calls the parents asking for Robert; the father says Robert is at his parents' house running errands. Robert left an expensive lighter behind. Outside, publisher Rudow and his companion discuss whether their joint arrival at the interview will look odd; they decide to play Lisa's concerned agent and publisher.

Lisa is called away from her parents by news that someone is hurt: a figure lies pale and possibly bloody. Katrin and Rudow tell Lisa to look downstairs while they go upstairs. Lisa receives a phone call and must leave abruptly, asking her father for his phone and €50.

A GPS tracker logs a 21.9 km active signal. Katrin's voice from the office confirms Rudow never believed Lisa killed her sister.

The interrogation scene begins. Lisa is searched, made to empty her pockets and show her waistband. Robert is direct: "I want the truth." When Lisa snubs him for skipping the usual friendliness, she lights up — even though she does not smoke — because her novel's main character does. Robert asks where she was on July 5, 2015. She calls the bluff "a dumb interrogation technique." She admits she agreed to the interview out of curiosity; when she heard "Lisa Konrad" was a pseudonym, she said no; once she realised who Robert was, she accepted to gauge whether he had evidence on her. She spent ten years not knowing whether she'd killed her sister.

Robert reveals the sound design: the first music was from a portable player in his colleague's equipment case; the second from a phone hidden in his jacket. He explains he researched her medical files. She has PTSD, panic disorder, and severe depressive episodes on a high prescription load; her therapist (Ms. Wilking's case) is meticulous. Her tolerance is high, so he overdid her dosage: GHB in her water, plus bufotenine in her coffee — a psychoactive hallucinogen that induces thirst. He deceived the photographer by framing the approach as an efficient strategy; he also promised him the trip to Syria. He also wants to confirm she didn't simply go to the police station — the police consider her statements unreliable.

Lisa asks how he knew she would fall for any of it. Robert replies: "What did Anna tell you?" Everything that is not in the book. Her book only describes a wonderful, close sisterly love; even her therapist only believes that. Robert quotes her therapist's note: her traumatic memories are distorted, and her suffering is so severe that her guilty feelings are pathological. He asks her to choke on her loneliness; she tells him to stop, accuses him of meanness and years of shouting.

Lisa finally speaks: Anna found Lisa unbearable, considered her artistic act over the top, and was hurt by Lisa's success. But since the moment she saw Anna lying down, she felt only love. Until yesterday.

Lisa demands to know what happened that night. Robert gives a long confession: at the time he had just become a father and editor-in-chief; people said he got the job because of his wife, whose family owns the publisher, but that is untrue. He loved his wife and had found his place. Then he fell in love with Anna; it was intoxicating; she made everything brighter. They kept it secret, using burner phones; Anna thought it fun at first. He tried to break it off several times and couldn't — a cliché he admits. They fantasised about a few weeks on an island where nobody knew them. Then his wife got pregnant again and he told Anna they had to stop.

Anna threatened to go see his wife and introduce herself. Robert points out the child is his, not a miracle; Anna retorts that she can already picture his life with her and the new baby in the fancy house with the huge pink hydrangea bushes. Robert is shocked: Anna went there. She wanted him to meet her sister so the sister could see how happy she'd been. Robert refuses. Anna promises there'll soon be enough money for both of them to spend months on the island.

Robert's wife is pregnant; he is leaving. Anna says his father-in-law will throw him out as soon as he learns he's been cheating on his beloved daughter. Robert snapped — he saw red, only for seconds — and stabbed her.

Lisa, through the doorway, asks: "Anna? I'm coming in." Music from "The Big Child" plays. Robert continues: he left the way he always came, so no one would see him entering her building; he took a nearby hotel room, then his car, and drove home with a birthday present for his wife. He feared police waiting at home. Then he realised Anna had kept her word: she told no one, not even Lisa. No one ever saw them together; in Anna's life, he didn't exist.

Lisa asks if the pregnancy was real or just an excuse. Robert asks his counter-question: why did her book say Anna was stabbed eight times? "That's how many, eight." Lisa: "It was six. It was six times." Robert: he counted even in his rage. He's telling the truth.

Lisa accuses him of provoking her, shifting blame onto self-defence. "Why didn't you just stay there in your house, Lisa?" He begs her: "I never would have hurt you. Close your eyes."

Then: "Lisa? Lisa? Can you hear me?" Police arrive at "24 Dalant Street. The Lenzens' house." Robert's recorded voice is heard: "I only stabbed her six times. You know that." Then a gunshot. Lisa screams "No!"

Lisa ("Ms. Wilking") is questioned. The gun has only her fingerprints. She and another present attest she did not shoot him. They clarify she was brought in along with Ms. Wilking. Robert — "Rudow" — says he is the publisher of Lisa Konrad / Lisa Wilking and wants to make a statement. Police note the gunshot was from Robert with her unregistered firearm. The pistol had been hidden behind some books at the publisher; a previous frightened guest probably stashed it. Lisa asks to use the phone to call her parents.

Rudow thanks the officers. Outside, with his injured companion, he says he'll be in touch.

The episode closes with Katrin alone: "Are you sure you want to delete the voice recording?" She nods. The recording continues. Lisa: "I had a sister three years younger than me. Her name was Anna. And I loved her. More than anything."

## Important character actions

- Lisa Wilking returns to her parents' home after a ten-year absence, then leaves abruptly after a phone call, asking to borrow her father's phone and €50.
- Katrin Solms takes over Robert Lenzen's interview at Lisa's parents' house.
- Rudow (Lisa's publisher/agent) and Katrin coordinate the visit as her "concerned agent and publisher."
- Robert stages an interview designed to destabilise Lisa: he plays specific music, drugs her with GHB (water) and bufotenine (coffee), and uses information from her medical files.
- Robert confesses to killing Anna Wilking in 2015 after she threatened to expose the affair.
- Robert shoots himself with Lisa's unregistered gun found hidden in the publisher's house.
- Rudow tells Lisa he never believed she killed her sister; he makes a statement to the police.
- The police clear Lisa on the shooting.

## Continuity-relevant facts

- Anna was murdered July 5, 2015 by her secret lover, an editor-in-chief married into the family that owns a publisher.
- Anna's novel counts six stab wounds; Lisa's novel counts eight — Robert later says six.
- Robert and Anna used burner phones; no one saw them together; Anna confided in no one, not even Lisa.
- Lisa has PTSD, panic disorder, and severe depression; takes a heavy course of medication; her therapist believes her traumatic memories are distorted and her guilt is pathological.
- The unregistered firearm was hidden behind books in the publisher's house (probably by a previous guest) and found there years before.
- Lisa's parents had previously been ambushed by a journalist who exposed Lisa's real name and the murder-fiction content; they rejected Robert "far too politely."
- GPS tracker logged 21.9 km with active signal.
- Robert has reported leaving for Syria the next day.
- Rudow positions himself as Lisa's concerned publisher.
- A lighter Robert left at his parents' house is mentioned.
- Police file: only Lisa's fingerprints are on the gun; witnesses attest she did not shoot; pistol classified as unregistered.

## Jokes / references useful for later episodes

- "Family man stabs younger mistress" — the imagined tabloid headline Robert cites as his "perfect crime" framing; could recur.
- Death-row analogy: a 65-year-old who served 40 years and became a Buddhist children's-book writer — Robert's philosophical dodge about whether one bad moment defines a life; the question of whether identity persists after the worst act will likely come back.
- "What if I were to go see your wife?" "She didn't get pregnant by God or some miracle!" — Anna's line captures the core conflict.
- The pink hydrangea bushes — a recurring visual motif at the family house, later used by Anna to describe Robert's life.
- Light and Sounds' "The Big Child" / "No Longer You" — diegetic songs played during manipulation; could be referenced again as Lisa recovers.
- Music cues (Mendelssohn "On Wings of Song" / Tchaikovsky "Andante cantabile" implied) functioning as triggers; therapists' notes on Lisa's sensory vulnerability may resurface.
- Lisa's line "There's no way all of that is in my medical records" — planting a future thread about what Robert actually knows vs. what he found.
- The journalist-awards "inspirational speech" clip and the listing for the Lenzens' house could be callbacks.

## Unresolved threads

- Stab count discrepancy: Robert says six; novel says eight — Robert explicitly notes the difference. Why does Lisa's novel miscount? Possible distortion / cover-up still unclear.
- Did Lisa ever actually go to the police that day? The episode ends with her asking for the phone to call her parents, but no contact with police is shown.
- Status of the novel: Lisa insists it will never be published; will the publisher (Rudow) push it?
- The GPS tracker of 21.9 km — who is being tracked, and for whom?
- Robert's Syrian trip and the journalist-awards speech — tied to his publishing role; was his plan to flee?
- Photographer baited into attending ("could come to Syria as well") — what does the photographer know?
- The expensive lighter Robert left behind — a potential link to his parents' house.
- "Light and Sounds" band / the specific recordings — how much more of Lisa's past is on file?
- Why did Lisa's therapist conclude the false memories in the first place? The "trap" was designed with that in mind.
- Does Katrin actually delete the voice recording? The last shot leaves it ambiguous.
- "Who is Yuma?" — name drops but not yet contextualised.
- The Lenzens' house was listed for sale — what happens to that property now that Robert is dead?
- The unregistered firearm at the publisher: who planted it and when? Mention of an earlier frightened guest points at a deeper publisher's-house history.
