# Strictly Come Dancing — Translation Background

## Title verification
- Exact title: Strictly Come Dancing (informal short: Strictly)
- Type: British live-action television dance contest / celebrity reality competition
- First aired: 15 May 2004 on BBC One (Series 1 ran May–July 2004; from Series 2 onward the show airs each autumn in the run-up to Christmas)
- Broadcaster: BBC One; produced by BBC Studios Entertainment Productions
- Format exported to 60+ countries as Dancing with the Stars (the show's Guinness-certified "most successful reality television format" record, awarded 2010). The Hunan-TV Mainland China licence-version also carried the title 舞动奇迹; it is a distinct show from this UK original.
- Companion weekday spin-off: Strictly Come Dancing: It Takes Two (BBC Two, premiered 25 October 2004).

## Series / episode structure
- 24 regular series as of 2026; 490 episodes in total (BBC One).
- 18 stand-alone Christmas specials and 19 charity specials are also produced.
- Each main series: Launch Show (couple reveal) → weekly Saturday live show on BBC One (each couple performs one or two routines, scored out of 40 by four judges) → Sunday results show (pre-recorded Saturday night; bottom-two couples dance again in a dance-off; one is saved by the judges) → Grand Final live on a Saturday.
- Viewers vote by telephone or online; votes are combined with judges' scores (ranked points, e.g. 10 contestants ⇒ 10 points for the favourite down to 1 point for the bottom couple) to determine the bottom two.
- Phone-line profits were donated to Sport Relief in Series 1, and to BBC Children in Need from Series 2 through Series 8.

## Production locations and on-screen setting
- 2004–2012: BBC Television Centre, London (mainly Studio TC1).
- 2013–present: Elstree Studios (George Lucas Stage 2), Shenley Road.
- Annual recurring location: Blackpool Tower Ballroom (Blackpool Week / selected finals; used in S1–2, then 2009–2019, 2022–present).
- 2011–2012: Wembley Arena used for special themed weeks.

## Relationships and recurring on-screen dynamics
- Strictly couple 舞伴 (1 celebrity + 1 pro) is the central on-screen unit each series; they rehearse daily, perform weekly, and may stay paired from the Launch Show through to the Final or elimination.
- The four judges each score every performance (out of 10). The head judge holds the casting vote in tied dance-offs; since S23 the casting vote rotates among the four.
- Several pros have met their life partners through the show: James & Ola Jordan (married 2015), Darren Bennett & Lilia Kopylova, Aljaž Škorjanec & Janette Manrara (married 2022), Karen Hauer & Kevin Clifton (later divorced), Neil & Katya Jones (later separated). Sibling pros Kevin & Joanne Clifton have often been confused by viewers.
- Winners across the run include Natasha Kaplinsky & Brendan Cole (S1), Jill Halfpenny & Darren Bennett (S2), Darren Gough & Lilia Kopylova (S3), Mark Ramprakash & Karen Hardy (S4), Alesha Dixon & Matthew Cutler (S5), Tom Chambers & Camilla Dallerup (S6), Chris Hollins & Ola Jordan (S7), Kara Tointon & Artem Chigvintsev (S8), Harry Judd & Aliona Vilani (S9), Louis Smith & Flavia Cacace (S10), Abbey Clancy & Aljaž Škorjanec (S11), Caroline Flack & Pasha Kovalev (S12), Jay McGuiness & Aliona Vilani (S13), Ore Oduba & Joanne Clifton (S14), Joe McFadden & Katya Jones (S15), Stacey Dooley & Kevin Clifton (S16), Kelvin Fletcher & Oti Mabuse (S17), Bill Bailey & Oti Mabuse (S18), Rose Ayling-Ellis & Giovanni Pernice (S19), Hamza Yassin & Jowita Przystał (S20), Ellie Leach & Vito Coppola (S21), Chris McCausland & Dianne Buswell (S22), Karen Carney & Carlos Gu (S23).

## Overall plot premise
A fixed cohort of British and international celebrities (actors, broadcasters, comedians, sportspeople, politicians, athletes) is paired with professional ballroom and Latin dancers. Each week they learn and perform a different dance drawn from the international ballroom and Latin syllabus. Couples are progressively eliminated through combined judge and public-vote rankings plus a dance-off, culminating in a Grand Final where one celebrity–pro pair wins the Glitterball Trophy. Each series is a self-contained annual tournament; recurring annual traditions include Blackpool Week, Halloween Week, Movie Week, Musicals Week, and Icons Week.

## Speaking style and cultural context for translation
- Hosts and judges use warm British primetime entertainment register: polite but playful, with cheeky innuendo (especially Bruce Forsyth in S1–11) and double entendres that subtitle cues should preserve without flattening.
- Judges' feedback is a fast-paced, character-driven mix of British ballroom jargon and personal catchphrases — keep each judge's voice distinct: Craig Revel Horwood is famously sharp withering ("a disaster", "dreadful", pronounced with Australian diction); Len Goodman is avuncular and uses "seven" gestures; Bruno Tonioli is exuberant and Italian-accented; Arlene Phillips is technical and direct; Alesha Dixon is warm and supportive; Darcey Bussell is technical and dancer-focused; Shirley Ballas is technical, anatomically precise; Motsi Mabuse is warm and emotional; Anton Du Beke is insider-pro perspective, fond of cosy British phrasing.
- The Sunday results show is the emotional centrepiece: contestants are regularly tearful, judges deliver verdicts, and Tess Daly / Claudia Winkleman / Emma Willis (S24+) close the show with the formal "lines are now closed" ritual.
- Cultural / national references likely to surface in subtitles: BBC Children in Need (for charity weeks), Royal Albert Hall, Blackpool Illuminations, British sports personalities (rugby, football, cricket, athletics), pantomime tradition, ballroom-dance heritage (Blackpool Tower Ballroom originally hosted Come Dancing from the 1950s–90s), Strictly Live arena tour, "Strictly fever" / "Strictly-mania" media coinages.
- Pre-recorded Sunday results show preserves the dance-off format; finals are broadcast live on Saturdays from Series 14 onward. Subtitle timing cues should expect applause, live band music, and judge deliberation pauses to fill dialogue gaps.
