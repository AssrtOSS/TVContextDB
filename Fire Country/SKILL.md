# Fire Country — Background

About the show
- Fire Country is an American action-procedural drama television series created by Max Thieriot, Tony Phelan and Joan Rater for CBS. It is produced by Jerry Bruckheimer Television and CBS Studios, with Tia Napolitano as showrunner.
- Premise: Bode Donovan (later Bode Donovan-Leone / Bode Leone) is a young convict with a troubled past. To shorten his sentence he volunteers for the California Conservation Camp Program, in which prisoners assist the California Department of Forestry and Fire Protection (Cal Fire). He is assigned to his rural hometown, the fictional Northern California town of Edgewater, and to a Cal Fire station called Three Rock — where inmates are partnered with elite firefighters to battle massive, unpredictable wildfires. Inspired by star Max Thieriot's own childhood growing up in Northern California fire country.
- Broadcast and structure: The series premiered on CBS on October 7, 2022. Each season runs weekly on Fridays in the 9:00 PM (ET/PT) slot, with episodes about 42–44 minutes. As of writing, four seasons (72 episodes) have aired, and a fifth season is scheduled to premiere on CBS on October 9, 2026. Episodes are also available on Paramount+, with the first season on Netflix and some catalog on Pluto TV. Filming takes place in Vancouver, British Columbia (the village of Fort Langley doubles for Edgewater), with establishing shots filmed in Rio Dell, Humboldt County, California.
- Themes: redemption and second chances; the Leone "first family of firefighters"; competing loyalties between Station 42, Three Rock Conservation Camp and the wider Edgewater community; the human cost of wildfire on a small, tinder-dry town.
- Related: back-door pilot "Alert the Sheriff" in Season 2 introduced Sheriff Mickey Fox (Morena Baccarin); it spun off into the CBS series Sheriff Country.

Main characters and relationships
- Bode Donovan / Bode Leone (Max Thieriot) — protagonist; former inmate firefighter of Three Rock Conservation Camp, later a Cal Fire firefighter at Station 42. Son of Sharon and Vince Leone. Love interest of Gabriela Perez across the first three seasons; briefly paired with Audrey James in S3. Adopted surname "Leone" reflects his reconciliation with his biological family.
- Vince Leone (Billy Burke; recurring in Season 4, deceased) — Bode and Riley's father; Sharon's husband; Cal Fire Battalion Chief at Station 42 across Seasons 1–3. Killed in the Season 3 finale when the Edgewater memory-care facility collapses during the Zabel Ridge fire; his death is confirmed at the start of Season 4.
- Sharon Leone (Diane Farr) — Bode and Riley's mother; Vince's widow; Cal Fire Division Chief. Becomes central authority figure in Edgewater's fire service after Vince's death.
- Riley Leone — Bode's younger sister; her earlier death is the foundational tragedy the Leone family carries through the series.
- Manny Perez (Kevin Alejandro) — Gabriela's father; Cal Fire firefighter and former Captain of Three Rock; promoted to Station 42 Battalion Chief after Vince's death. Briefly serves time as an inmate at Three Rock himself in Season 3.
- Gabriela Perez (Stephanie Arcila; seasons 1–3 main, S4 guest) — Manny's daughter; Cal Fire firefighter and paramedic at Station 42. Bode's main love interest; briefly engaged to Diego Moreno. Leaves Edgewater at the start of Season 4 to become a Cal Fire recruiter.
- Jake Crawford (Jordan Calloway) — Cal Fire firefighter, Bode and Eve's best friend; Station 42 Captain; later Battalion Chief of a Montana station before returning. Sometimes a romantic rival to Bode over Gabriela. Half-brother of Malcolm Crawford.
- Eve Edwards (Jules Latimer) — Bode and Jake's best friend; Cal Fire firefighter; Captain of Three Rock Conservation Camp across Seasons 2–4. Partner/girlfriend of Francine in later seasons.
- Luke Leone (Michael Trucco) — Vince's brother; Cal Fire Division Chief. Often serves as Sharon's counterpart in command disputes.
- Walter Leone (Jeff Fahey) — Vince and Luke's father; Sharon's father-in-law; former Station 42 Battalion Chief; placed in a memory-care facility as his health declines.
- Ruby Quinn (Christine Lahti; season 4) — Sharon's mother; Bode's maternal grandmother.
- Brett Richards (Shawn Hatosy; season 4) — interim Battalion Chief at Station 42 following Vince's death; an old antagonist of the Leones whose appointment forces Station 42 to rethink its identity.
- Recurring cast of note: Diego Moreno (Rafael de la Fuente; seasons 2–3, Gabriela's ex-fiancé and former paramedic instructor), Audrey James (Leven Rambin; seasons 3–4, ex-con turned Cal Fire firefighter, Bode's S3 girlfriend), Camden Casey (Jared Padalecki; season 3, SoCal fire captain who spots Bode's talent), Finn (Blake Lee; season 3 antagonist, photographer turned stalker who dies after a confrontation at the Leone house), Cole Rodman (Tye White), Violet / Margot (Nesta Cooper; seasons 3–4, Oxalta general counsel who becomes Jake's wife), Francine (Katie Findlay; Eve's partner), Landon and Chloe Mackenzie and their son Tyler (Josh McDermitt, Alona Tal, Conor Sherry; season 4, Bode mentors Tyler), Camille Thurston (Natalie Zea; season 4), Dr. Roberta (Paola Núñez; season 2 onward, Manny's ex-wife), Boone / Danny / Mickey Fox (ties into Sheriff Country crossovers in seasons 2 and 4).

World setting and terminology
- Edgewater — the fictional small town in Northern California where the show is set; treated as a "tinderbox" by the fire crews.
- Cal Fire — California Department of Forestry and Fire Protection; the state agency that fights wildfires. Refer to as "Cal Fire" (not "Calfire" or "California Fire Department"). Render 林业消防局 / 加州消防局 if a Chinese gloss is needed; the on-screen org is consistently called "Cal Fire".
- Station 42 — the Leone family's Cal Fire station in Edgewater; commanded by Vince Leone as Battalion Chief across Seasons 1–3, then by Manny Perez from Season 4 onward; new interim BC Brett Richards arrives in Season 4.
- Three Rock / Three Rock Conservation Camp — the inmate firefighting camp near Edgewater. Inmates here are partnered with civilian Cal Fire crews on the line. Eve Edwards is Captain. The camp faces a possible shutdown in Season 3 due to mismanagement and chemicals in the well water; it is also where Manny serves a brief stretch as an inmate.
- Conservation Camp Program / inmate firefighter / prison-release firefighting program — the California scheme that lets convicts reduce their sentences by working on the fire line. Render as 消防惩教项目 / 囚犯消防员 / 释囚消防计划 as needed.
- Battalion Chief (BC), Captain, Division Chief, paramedic, fire line, brush, engine, structure fire, knockdown, contain — standard U.S. wildland and structural firefighting terms used throughout. Wildfire classification terms include prescribed burn, controlled burn, fire tornado, zombie fire (used in S3E8 "Promise Me"), and incident command.
- Edgewater landmarks: memory-care facility (where Vince and Walter are caught in the S3 finale), Cal Fire Station 42, Three Rock camp grounds, Edgewater Rodeo, the dam-reservoir system referenced in S4 ("Pineville Dam").

Plot arc across the series (do not summarize a single episode or season)
- Season 1 (2022–2023): Bode enters the Cal Fire Conservation Camp Program, is assigned to Three Rock and Station 42 back in Edgewater, works to redeem himself with his estranged family (the Leones), earns his fire-cadet credentials, and ends the season with the team battling a plane-crash fire.
- Season 2 (2024): Bode, Gabriela and the rest of Station 42 respond to a record-breaking campaign fire; the back-door pilot "Alert the Sheriff" introduces Sheriff Mickey Fox, which seeds the Sheriff Country spinoff. The season deepens the Bode/Gabriela tension and tests Station 42's discipline.
- Season 3 (2024–2025): Gabriela's wedding to Diego is interrupted by a helicopter crash; a love triangle between Bode, Gabriela and Diego plays out; a contamination crisis at Three Rock (Oxalta pesticides in well water) and Gabriela's stalking by photographer Finn culminate in Finn's death at the Leone house, the loss of Three Rock's facilities, and the collapse of the memory-care building in which Vince (and Walter) perish; Bode is left outside the burning structure while Sharon is rescued by Walter. Jake accepts a Battalion Chief post in Montana.
- Season 4 (2025): Opens months after Vince's death. Bode vows to protect "my father's town, my father's station, my father's mission"; Gabriela departs Edgewater for a Cal Fire recruiting role; Sharon holds Station 42 together while grieving; Brett Richards is appointed interim Battalion Chief and shakes up the station. Subplots include Bode's sobriety risks, Sharon's survivor's guilt, Jake's grief over losing Vince as a father figure, a Pineville Dam flood, and the arrival of Sharon's mother Ruby Quinn.

Speaking styles and tone
- The dialogue uses standard contemporary American English with frequent fire-service jargon (knockdown, structure protection, hose lays, division, IC, etc.) and small-town Northern California idiom.
- Family and crew banter is warm and teasing; command scenes (Vince, Sharon, later Richards) are terse and military-cadenced.
- Spanish/English code-switching appears in Manny and Gabriela's dialogue and in community scenes; keep it intelligible, do not over-translate flavor.
- The series balances high-stakes disaster set-pieces with character-driven melodrama about grief, addiction and redemption; lines often lean emotionally earnest rather than quippy.

Cultural context
- Based on Thieriot's own upbringing near the Cal Fire crews of Northern California; the show romanticizes the inmate-firefighter program that is a real feature of California wildfire response.
- Wildfire season in Northern California is treated as a defining civic reality — evacuation orders, red-flag warnings, fire weather, smoke days, prescribed burns and the displacement of small communities.
- The Leone family archetype — multigenerational first-responder clan — is a recurring small-town-Americana motif.
- Mental health and addiction (Bode's sobriety, Sharon's survivor's guilt, Walter's dementia) are recurring through-lines.
- Officer ranks in the show mirror real Cal Fire / U.S. fire-service hierarchy: Firefighter → Firefighter II / Engineer → Captain → Battalion Chief → Division Chief.
