# S01E05 — Episode Summary

## Episode plot
- Cold open: Sybylla is delivered to the McSwats' place, sent for a year to work off her father's loan. Mrs McSwat had expected the "nice," prettier sister; Sybylla is "the smart one" — unmarried, teased as a "spinster." She explains she was once engaged, but it ended in "drought-related financial collapse" (he broke it off).
- She is put to work as governess and servant: breakfast, sweeping, chickens, vegetable garden, plus the children (Peter, Jimmy, Tommy, Liza, Lucy, Sarah, Mary-Anne). The kids mob her and chant "Spinster!"
- Sybylla writes a lovesick letter to Harry ("Dearest Harry… Like Mama's stolen piano, my keys exist, but I've long since lost my tune") — but Harry never replies; Nell keeps asking whether he's written, and he hasn't.
- She clashes with Mrs McSwat after Jimmy falsely claims she thrashed him ("he's disobedient, boisterous, and obstreperous"; "You're all feral. And this place is like hell!"). She slowly wins the children over and teaches them big words and spelling.
- Susie Duffy visits; Peter confesses she broke it off with him ("she's worried that we'll end up like my parents — common"). Sybylla writes love letters plus three poems for Peter to send her, in exchange for his help.
- Part two of the plan: Peter stages a "romance" with Sybylla. Mrs McSwat calls her "a snake in the grass… Jezebel," and Sybylla blackmails the family into clearing her father's loan — with the condition that Papa never borrows from McSwat again — in exchange for her silence.
- Meanwhile at Caddagat, Aunt Helen leaves for Sydney to get a job, over Granny's protests ("Times are changing, Mother"); Julius drives her to Gool Gool and Augusta will meet her. Helen refuses to keep carrying her runaway husband's shame.
- Sybylla returns home debt-free — "Free of the McSwats forever. Because of me. Definitely my best work." Her mother has sold the Bossier heirloom brooch ("found an emergency"); a flashback shows Granny giving the brooches with the motto "Bossiers bend, but we do not break."
- Her bushranger story (with accomplice Cora) came back rejected for "veracity issues" — "So write your truth, Sybylla." Harry arrives at the farm, repentant ("I've been an idiot, Syb. I should've written"), reads her writing, and proposes, having saved droving money for a farm near Gool Gool. Sybylla refuses: "I want to be a writer… If I marry you, I'll never know if I can." The episode closes on her new manuscript — "The Bush Girl, by Sybylla Melvyn" (《丛林少女》西比拉·梅尔文著), opening: "The summer of 1900 scorched the earth with the same careless brutality as the banks."

## Important character actions
- Sybylla: endures the McSwat year, writes to Harry (unanswered), stands up to Mrs McSwat, wins the children over, engineers the Peter scheme to clear her father's debt, returns home, refuses Harry's proposal, and writes "The Bush Girl."
- Peter McSwat: heartbroken over Susie, accepts Sybylla's letters and poems, plays his part in the staged romance, and honors the deal.
- Mrs McSwat: hostile throughout ("as hopeless as your father and as stuck-up as your mother"; "Jezebel"), then forced into the payoff.
- Mr McSwat: breaks the news that Papa defaulted and Sybylla must stay a second year; a merchant who sells gold watches and orders from catalogs.
- Harry: bosses a droving crew all year and saves for a farm; never writes to Sybylla; arrives to propose and is refused.
- Nell and Charlie: press Harry to write to Sybylla; plan to head back to Queensland (Charlie "wants to find his mob").
- Helen: leaves Caddagat for Sydney; Julius backs her ("I fully support Hels leaving"), Granny pleads ("No, Helen, please don't go").
- Granny (Winifred): tries to keep Helen; flashback shows her giving debutante brooches with the "we bend, but we do not break" motto; sends Sybylla off with "Chin up."
- Mama (Mrs Melvyn): sells the heirloom brooch to get by; tells Sybylla "How do you know if love is real? You sacrifice everything for it."
- Gert: now Granny's charge at Caddagat; warned not to marry "any old suitor"; a Gool Gool schoolteacher keeps asking her for walks.

## Newly established names/terms
- The McSwat family — Mr. and Mrs. McSwat; adult son Peter; kids Jimmy, Tommy, Liza, Lucy, Sarah, Mary-Anne. A tradesman household (catalogs, gold watches) that Sybylla is indentured to.
- Susie Duffy — Peter's intended; her father owns 1,000 acres; "the best singer in the district"; "a catch."
- Possum Gully — where the children's school desk comes from.
- Gool Gool — the nearby town (mail coach and train to Sydney depart from there; Harry wants a farm nearby; Gert's schoolteacher is from there).
- Archibald Butler — "world-renowned traveling photographer" who shoots the McSwat family portrait.
- Caddagat — Granny Bossier's station (the letter from home comes from there).
- "The Bush Girl" (《丛林少女》西比拉·梅尔文著) — Sybylla's new manuscript; her earlier bushranger story (with Cora) was rejected for "veracity issues."
- Terms: spinster, "common" (class slur), bunyip, school primer, "mob" (Charlie's people), "old boozy Melvyn," "Jezebel," "Hels" (Helen), "J." (Julius).
- Minor names: Eddie (greets Sybylla on her return — "You look like cow shit"), Florence (at the Melvyn table), Horace (shouted during the confrontation; identity unclear).

## Continuity-relevant facts
- The Melvyns' debt to the McSwats is fully cleared, with the condition that Papa never borrows from them again; the family is debt-free but still poor (Mama sold her brooch; the piano remains repossessed — "Mama's stolen piano").
- Sybylla is home for good — her scheme ends the McSwat year early.
- Harry's long silence hurt her badly; he wants to make it up to her and has farm savings, but she refuses marriage a second time to pursue writing.
- Helen has left the Bossier household for Sydney; Gert is now Granny's charge at Caddagat.
- Nell and Charlie intend to leave Harry's droving crew for Queensland.
- The bushranger story's rejection pushes Sybylla toward autobiographical writing.
- Sybylla was once engaged; it ended over "drought-related financial collapse" (he broke it off) — her stated reason for spinster status.

## Jokes or references useful for later episodes
- Jimmy's arithmetic: two shillings minus one-and-six = "None. 'Cause your family don't have two shillings to rub together no more."
- "Finnest leather" — the miscatalogued gloves Mrs McSwat and Liza covet.
- The dead-rat sandwich story ("First of all, it wasn't dead!").
- "Like a bunyip?" — the kids' description of scary Sybylla.
- "You've come on like a haystack. You look like cow shit." — Eddie's greeting.
- Sybylla's blackmail scheme as "my best work" — writing as weapon and art.
- "I hope you don't look at this photograph next year and think, who is that girl?"
- Localization note: Susie's father's "1,000 acres" is rendered 六千亩 (≈6 mu per acre) — keep this conversion consistent in later episodes.
