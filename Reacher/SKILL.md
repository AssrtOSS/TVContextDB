# Reacher — Translation Background

## Title verification
- **Title:** Reacher
- **Type:** American action/crime/detective television series (NOT the Tom Cruise films; NOT the novels alone)
- **First aired:** February 4, 2022
- **Broadcaster:** Amazon Prime Video
- **Developer/Showrunner:** Nick Santora
- **Based on:** Lee Child's "Jack Reacher" novel series
- **Starring:** Alan Ritchson as Jack Reacher

## Season/episode structure
- Season 1 (2022): 8 episodes, based on novel *Killing Floor* — set in Margrave, Georgia
- Season 2 (2023–2024): 8 episodes, based on *Bad Luck and Trouble* — reunites Reacher's old 110th Special Investigations Unit
- Season 3 (2025): 8 episodes, based on *Persuader* — set in Boston/Maine, against arms dealer Xavier Quinn
- Season 4 (2026): 8 episodes, based on *Gone Tomorrow* — premieres August 12, 2026
- Season 5: announced May 2026, based on *Make Me*
- Spin-off: *Neagley* premieres September 16, 2026

## Key relationships
- **Jack Reacher**: former U.S. Army Major, military police; drifts across America; towering build, fights multiple opponents bare-handed
- **Frances Neagley**: closest ally; ex-110th SIU; Reacher's most trusted partner
- **110th Special Investigations Unit (第110特种调查组)**: Reacher's former army unit; core members include Neagley, Dixon, O'Donnell
- **Joe Reacher (乔·里彻)**: Jack's late brother; his murder in S1 draws Jack to Margrave
- **Xavier Quinn**: recurring antagonist from Reacher's military past, arms dealer
- **Kliner family (克莱纳家族)**: S1 antagonists running a counterfeiting operation
- **Beck family (贝克家族)**: S3, front company Bizarre Bazaar (奇珍集市) for arms trafficking

## World setting
Contemporary United States, traveling locations. Tone is grounded, gritty, realistic crime-thriller. S1: small-town Georgia; S2: New York / Atlantic City; S3: New England (Maine coast, Boston); S4: Philadelphia. Reacher is a true drifter — no fixed address, no phone, no bank cards, buys only what he needs. Reacher carries **no weapon** in the show; he fights hand-to-hand. He stays in cheap motels, eats diner food, takes buses. The show deliberately contrasts with the 2012/2016 Tom Cruise films.

## Overall plot premise
Drifter and former Army MP Jack Reacher wanders into a town or situation, encounters violent crime or corruption, and uses his investigative experience, physical strength, and deductive mind to dismantle criminal operations — typically involving corruption, conspiracies, or trafficking at scale. Each season is a self-contained case adapted from one Lee Child novel.

## Speaking styles
- **Reacher**: laconic, blunt, deadpan; short sentences; rarely raises his voice; tends to use silence. Speaks longer only to people he respects (showrunner's stated rule). Sarcastic one-liners.
- **Neagley**: efficient, professional, military-speak, dry humor; mirror image of Reacher's manner.
- **Villains (Quinn, Langston, A.M.)**: polished, menacing, verbose monologues; treat violence casually.
- **Cops (Finlay, Roscoe, Duffy)**: procedural, by-the-book, often exasperated by Reacher's methods.
- **Everyday civilians**: regional American dialects — Georgia drawl (S1), Boston accent (S3), New York cop talk (S2).
- Notable catchphrase motif: "Reacher said nothing" / a person who "says nothing" — recurring narrative beat.

## Cultural context
- The show's selling point is correcting the 2012/2016 Tom Cruise films by casting a 6'5" actor matching Lee Child's descriptions of the character.
- Frequent references to **diner culture, Greyhound buses, Motel 6, coffee in paper cups, cheap pie** — Americana drifter iconography.
- S1 addresses **rural Southern corruption, KKK-era racial legacy in small-town Georgia**.
- S2 features **Wall Street / private military contractors / post-9/11 defense industry** themes.
- S3 explores **the opioid / fentanyl trade** as a backdrop to arms trafficking.
- Military references: **MPs, special investigations, Afghanistan/Iraq deployments, military intelligence** (Quinn).
- Hand-to-hand combat is brutal and graphic (Reacher incapacitates multiple men); emphasize bodily impact in subtitles (e.g., "撂倒", "砸", "扭断").
