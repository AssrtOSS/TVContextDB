## Overall Plot Premise

Two Black women from opposite ends of the social spectrum cross paths and become entangled with the same powerful family. Kimmie is a young stripper trapped under the control of a violent pimp; Mallory "Mallory" Bellarie is the Black executive heir of a wealthy hair-care (relaxer) empire, married into a dysfunctional, scheming family. Their collision sets off blackmail, a murder cover-up, a corporate lawsuit, gang violence, and a slow-burn power war inside the Bellarie family and the strip club. The series is a Tyler Perry-style melodrama mixing crime thriller, family soap, and Black bourgeois intrigue; it is not a detective show, not a legal procedural, and not a comedy despite occasional dark humour.

## World Setting

- Contemporary United States, mostly an unnamed Southern city (production set in Atlanta, Georgia)
- Two parallel social worlds: the underclass strip-club / sex-work underworld, and the Black upper-middle-class corporate world of a hair-care ("relaxer") manufacturer
- A hair school called "Beauty in Black" (also the brand and company name) is the symbolic link between the two worlds
- Recurring locations: the strip club run by Jules (a violent pimp), the Bellarie family mansion and company headquarters, a hospital, a private road where a fatal hit-and-run occurs in the pilot arc

## Speaking Styles and Register

- Tyler Perry's dialogue is heavy on Black American Vernacular English (AAVE), which in Chinese subtitle translation should be rendered as natural conversational Chinese, not academic or stiff. Avoid translating AAVE sentence rhythm literally.
- Family and corporate scenes lean toward formal, often Southern-inflected English; translate as 端庄 / 上流社交语体.
- Strip-club and underworld scenes are profane, slang-heavy, and physically threatening; keep 粗口 calibrated (e.g. "bitch" → 贱人/婊子 only when character is in attack mode, otherwise 女人; "son of a bitch" → 混蛋/狗东西).
- Menacing, slow-burn threats are common; characters like Jules and Olivia speak in measured, controlling cadences — keep lines clipped, never over-explanatory.
- Kimmie's voice shifts across the series: from fearful victim (S1 Part 1) to schemer (S1 Part 2) to reluctant power-broker (S2). Track the change.
- Mallory speaks as an upper-class Black Southern businesswoman — poised, cutting, occasionally dropping formal-mask sarcasm.

## Cultural Context for Translators

- Hair and Black hair politics: "relaxer," "natural hair," "weave," and the "Beauty in Black" brand all sit inside a real-world Black hair-culture discourse. Translators should not reduce "relaxer" to a generic 美发产品.
- BBL (Brazilian Butt Lift) surgery: referenced directly in S1E2 as a botched procedure; brief but graphic — keep clinical terms.
- "Hood" / "the streets" / "trap" lexicon: standard Black-American urban slang used by Jules, Body, Angel, and Kimmie's circle.
- "Church" / "choir" / "Sunday best" imagery: occasional backdrop for Black bourgeois scenes; render culturally, not literally.
- Cops are an ambient presence (Officer Alex, Officer Trackson); police interactions are not the focus but recur as pressure.
- Tyler Perry's signature is melodramatic plotting and family secrets erupting in public; this show is more noir-leaning than his BET+ work, but the soap DNA is constant — keep the over-the-top betrayals and confrontations, do not flatten them.
