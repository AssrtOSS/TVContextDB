# Youth S01E02 "Wagon Burner" — Episode Summary

## Episode Plot
Alex and Sam attend the **Frankfurt Book Fair** with Ruari in tow. Alex pitches her client Charlotte Arbor's controversial new manuscript *Wagon Burner* (explicit sexual content, 90% anal scenes) while secretly exploring a move to Random House after Karen Saville-Stewart (HarperCollins) rejects the manuscript. Patrick unexpectedly appears at the fair. Ruari spirals — drunk, hooking up with a German man (Lars), and confessing his university trauma: a drunken encounter with Bridget where his hand was in her jeans when she woke up, plus erectile dysfunction with Mia that led to campus rumors branding him a "pervert." Alex comforts him, promising no pressure to return to uni. The episode ends with Alex and Patrick hooking up in a sauna/bathroom, interrupted by her sudden heavy period (perimenopausal flooding).

## S01E05

# Episode 5 — The Holiday

## Plot
A couples' holiday in Ibiza to celebrate Sam and Frank's 10-year wedding anniversary gathers the full ensemble: Alex and her 18-year-old son Ruari, Sam and Frank, Lorcan (Sam's old friend) and his girlfriend Theresa, Patrick and his new girlfriend Cara. The trip is dominated by awkwardness, sunburn, drinking and simmering friction.

Alex and Ruari arrive on the dreaded 5:45am Stansted–Ibiza Ryanair; Alex openly resents the airline and blames Ruari for booking it. She and Ruari are relegated to the "outhouse," a small cottage apart from the main villa, which Alex calls "future-issues territory." The villa owner jokes their "marriage doesn't look a day over five."

By the pool, Cara picks outfits and Patrick joins the group alone. Lunch in town becomes a low-grade farce over ordering paella: Theresa is on appetite suppressants ("only for this one meal"), Lorcan is starving, Ruari offers to share, Alex refuses ("it gives me acid reflux") and orders ceviche instead. Alex is caught looking at flights and admits she "came on a couples' holiday by mistake"; Ruari tells her to do single-woman-on-holiday things (Wayne Lineker's bar, dolphin-watching, getting her nails done).

The couples play "Heads Up." Alex partners with Theresa after Ruari declines ("we'd be useless"). Frank and Sam pair up. Frank's guesses derail the round ("Shrek's dick" for Shrek; "a big poo" for a morning routine); Theresa and Alex win. One clue chain ("poem, rhymes, Irish") leads Theresa to answer "Kneecap," and Frank completes the joke with their catchphrase "Tiocfaidh ár lá."

That night the boys head out ("lads' night"); the girls stay in. Alex pulls Lorcan aside and explicitly asks him to keep an eye on Ruari ("I trust you more than I trust his father"). At the bar, Ruari opens up to Frank about his absent father — fear of "ending up like him," being "paranoid around girls." Frank reassures him. Ruari then chats up a Spanish girl using his only German phrase, "Nussgebäcks" (nut pastries). Cara abandons the group to go to Klërrb alone.

Among the women, two parallel cracks widen. Theresa reveals Lorcan has a Toronto job and hasn't asked her about her plans. Sam hasn't given Frank oral sex in a week and spirals into paranoia about who else might be; she stalks him via texts and nearly forces an early exit. Alex proposes a "head holiday"; Sam rejects the perimenopause read ("I'm barely in my early late 40s").

Final day, the group goes on a boat rave. Frank delivers his anniversary toast — a sarcastic "to the woman who made me the twat that I am today." Ruari is hungover (MDMA the night before, slipped to him by the Spanish girl). Patrick goes in the water, is stung by a jellyfish and shouts for someone to pee on him. Frank jumps in to help and gets into trouble (jellyfish sting plus what looks like a cardiac event); Alex and Patrick haul him out. Sam screams.

After the rescue, Alex runs through Ruari's hangover cure (full-fat Coke, Rick and Morty), then turns to Patrick on the dock: "life's fucking short… I didn't try hard enough — for us… I miss you." Patrick deflects ("maybe we don't get into all that"), then hedges with "well, you've said that before," and Alex answers "yeah, but this time it hurts." Patrick insists he didn't take drugs the night before. The episode closes on Patrick offering water: "¿sin gas or con gas? You choose."

## S01E06

# Desperate Measures

## Plot

The episode opens at Frank's post–heart-attack gathering at the house, where Sam is sober in solidarity with her husband and Frank obsessively serves Jamaican food while still on cardiac meds. Upstairs, Patrick admits he has been sleeping over regularly, but signals he wants a casual "see other people" arrangement; Alex agrees while privately miserable. Sam texts that a man named Lars has asked Alex on a date, which Alex then mentions to Patrick, sharpening their unspoken tension.

The next morning Ruari starts an internship at the literary agency. At a contentious restaurant lunch, Alex and Sam compete with Grayson to win back their star author Malcolm. Grayson stages a fake phone call announcing that his senior partner Harriet has died, then abandons the table. Alex uses a HarperCollins pitch ("you could be the next J.R.R. Tolkien") to lure Malcolm, who finally says yes — then disappears without paying the £670 bill.

Sam, drunk and increasingly volatile, reveals she is in perimenopause ("peri-peri bloody merry meni," "Pussy's dead"), has zero sex drive, and is half-popping Frank's Xanax; Alex collapses into a cab and is dropped at the wrong address (Bloomsbury Hotel) for £130. At the Queen's Arms pub, she meets Patrick's war-zone journalist friends Saleh and Ed. Lars unexpectedly turns up because Alex shared her location by accident, and Patrick confronts him furiously, calling him "Von Triers" and a "reformed boy band" leftover.

A late-night subplot: Alex's father accidentally activates an "IRA podcast," and a panicked Alex asks Patrick to phone him through the fix. Patrick instead drives Ruari over to the house, plays video games with the grandfather, and tells Alex he did it to feel closer to her.

The episode's two strands collide in the office: Sam has secretly told publisher Blue Hour she already reps Yafa Barghouti — a writer Alex was told not to sign — and is furious when Alex walks into a meeting already in progress. Alex goes home and finally has the conversation with Patrick that has been building: both agree they don't want to see other people, ending the "what are we" ambiguity. The episode closes with Ruari still gaming with Alex's father and an "IRA podcast" still pending.
