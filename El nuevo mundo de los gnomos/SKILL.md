## Overall premise

Each episode uses the same narrative template: David the Gnome is alerted to an environmental emergency somewhere on Earth and travels there to solve it, usually accompanied by his nephew Tomte. Episodes cover habitats worldwide — African savanna, Amazon rainforest, Bengal tiger reserves, Mediterranean coast, Himalayan panda habitats, Australian Outback, Arctic, tropical reefs, wetlands and rivers. Conflicts pit gnomes and local wildlife against poachers, illegal hunters, polluters and careless campers, while three trolls cause parallel damage.

## World setting

Gnomes are a benevolent species standing 15 cm tall and weighing 250–300 g, identified by their omnipresent conical red cap. They are seven times stronger than humans relative to size but risk being stepped on in human spaces. They mate at age 100 and live exactly 400 years. Habitat-based subspecies include forest gnome, garden gnome, farm gnome, house gnome, dune gnome and Siberian gnome. They live as couples in caves or burrows beneath a large tree, accompanied by a pair of mice and a guardian cricket. Their diet is ovovegetarian (nature's produce plus surplus bird eggs). They are telepathic, know nature's secrets and oppose human damage to nature. Their natural enemies are trolls, depicted as smelly and clumsy. Gnomes ride birds and enlist forest animals for long-distance travel.

The series' headquarters for global environmental coordination is an unnamed council center led by Millius, who receives distress reports from around the world and assigns them to David.

## Speaking styles

- Narrator (Teófilo Martínez) — warm, didactic third-person; sets the ecological lesson for each episode. Render as 旁白 with a calm, slightly storybook register.
- David — measured, paternal, authoritative; uses formal "we" and "the gnomes" when acting as Royal Knight; otherwise conversational and gentle with animals. Mix of usted and tú.
- Tomte — youthful, eager, occasionally clumsy; short exclamations and slightly awkward phrasing.
- Lisa — warm, maternal, calm; polite direct address.
- Millius — official, dispatch-style, short clauses, urgent but composed; mission-briefing voice.
- Trolls (Babas, Bruto, Tufo) — gruff, crude, malaprop-prone; coarse second-person; interjections like 呸, 嗷呜, 啊哈.
- Animals — anthropomorphized but species-faithful; chirps, hoots and growls as interjections rather than full sentences.
- WWF-flavored conservation messaging — educational register, declarative, occasionally rhymed in the original; keep dignified and accessible.

## Cultural context

- The show is the third and final BRB Internacional adaptation of the Wil Huygen / Rien Poortvliet gnome book cycle, succeeding David, el Gnomo (1985) and Wisdom of the Gnomes (1987). David and Lisa return from the original; the franchise's environmental-mission framing is preserved and modernized.
- The David el Gnomo franchise is a staple of Spanish-language children's television and was widely syndicated across Latin America and US Hispanic markets via Mi Tele on TeleFutura.
- In mainland China, Taiwan and Hong Kong the original aired under multiple Chinese titles; common franchise labels include 《大卫小矮人》, 《小矮人大卫》, 《聪明的小矮人》. There is no single dominant Chinese title for this third series; preferred renderings are 《小矮人的新世界》 or 《大卫小矮人新篇》, with subtitles clearly distinguishing it from the 1985 original.
- WWF co-production status means ecological accuracy (species names, habitats, threats) is treated as factual and should not be paraphrased away.
- Honorifics: David is addressed as "don David" in ceremonial contexts; preserve 大卫先生 or 阁下 when formal. Trolls never receive honorifics.
- Music: Marta Sánchez and Albert Hammond contributed to the soundtrack; the opening theme continues the franchise's signature "Soy un gnomo" lineage and benefits from rhymed Chinese rendering.
- Measurement conventions: heights in cm and weights in grams per the original; do not auto-convert to imperial units.
