## S05E03

### S05E03 summary (Trying)

- Nikki and Jason juggle family optics and stress. With Kat back in the family orbit, they try to keep a stable front for Princess and Tyler, including a “pizza night” plan and repeated attempts to treat this as a “strong family unit.”
- Jason has a demanding new job and discusses work metrics with his team, while also dealing with morning tension at home. He heads out despite Nikki’s emotional pressure, reinforcing the show’s work–family balancing theme.
- Scott’s Atlantic rowing arc continues with his dramatic “I’m rowing the Atlantic” routine and preparation details (including image-conscious sunscreen/tanning logistics), played as a comic subplot.
- The episode links family chaos with domestic practicality: Stevie is sick, Nate? (Nadya) is sent away from cleaning, and household logistics are pushed to the edge in the name of flexibility.
- A major comic thread comes from the office mice: pest control, weird trap failures, and a chaotic discussion about “root causes” (including people removing traps and dog traffic in the office). SnapCore9X is treated as both absurd product and practical joke.
- Jason still goes to the college “mixer,” despite not feeling invited, becoming a mirror for how the adult characters try to escape stress through nightlife.
- In the night-out scenes, the series leans on awkward-mentor comedy (health/self-care lectures, phone confiscation, “big night out” rules, dance-floor bonding), ending with a late-night kebab routine that becomes emotional debrief space.
- A major emotional center is Nikki/biological-mother anxiety: the fear of Kat returning/remaining, Princess wanting contact with her bio-mum, and Nikki spiraling into panic. A friend character offers perspective (“not every moment is crisis; let the relationship breathe”).
- The episode closes with morning near dawn; the adults are physically exhausted but still tied together through the shared “kebab therapy” coping cycle.

### Important character actions / beats

- **Nikki:** emotional anchor; manages children, attempts family unity, and shows rising anxiety about Kat’s re-entry.
- **Jason:** tries to split attention between job, partnership, parenting image-management, and social escape.

- **Office staff/social circle:** repeatedly turns practical crises (mice, traps, labels, missing dessert, event plans) into social comedy and delayed emotional revelations.

## S05E02

### 剧情概述
Jason 和 Nikki 同时迎来新阶段：Jason 开始社会工作理论与实践课程，Nikki 接任旅游公司的销售总监。两人都遭遇适应困难，Jason 因年长学生身份和笨拙表现频频尴尬，Nikki 则发现销售团队因落选职位而对她心存芥蒂。

与此同时，Kat 计划带 Princess 前往泰国芭东海滩参加满月派对。Nikki 得知后担心未满17岁的 Princess 独自远行会影响她的安全与心理状态，也害怕 Princess 因重新接近生母而逐渐离开自己。

Scott 原本试图划船横渡大西洋，为动物保护筹款，却因无法继续而偷偷躲在 Karen 家地窖里九天，靠剩余口粮和马卡龙度日。他打算继续伪装自己仍在海上，并谎称船沉后游到美国，以免失去赞助及承担船只费用。

## S05E06

## S05E06 Summary

**Plot**

It is Halloween night. Nikki, Jason, Princess, and Tyler prepare at home. Nikki and Jason are heading to a fancy-dress screening of *The Ring* at the cinema — the film they watched on their third date, which holds sentimental value despite Nikki hating horror films. They debate the merits of horror films versus art-house films en route. Meanwhile, Princess and Maddy sneak off to a party at Maddy's house, telling the parents it was approved by the other parent when neither actually agreed.

At the supermarket next to the cinema, Nikki accuses Jason of checking out a woman in a low-cut top. Jason denies it, claiming he was looking at a ghost-costumed child next to her. They spend the trailer period searching the store for the supposed child — asking staff, reviewing phone footage, and generally embarrassing themselves — before admitting the child doesn't exist.

Back at the cinema, the argument escalates into a surprisingly honest conversation. Jason admits he did find the woman attractive but hid it because Nikki was right there. Nikki then pivots to her real confession: she has been feeling like "furniture" in the relationship — supportive but invisible, like an armchair that blends into the background. She had developed a crush on a work colleague named Kerry (a man whose name she had been calling "she"), and the crush itself made her feel alive rather than like a piece of furniture. The emotional core of the fight is not the "checking out" incident but Nikki's sense that their relationship has become so secure and routine that neither feels like an exciting new person to the other. They reconcile, with Jason calling himself a "bed" (comfort and excitement in equal measure) and Nikki calling herself a "sofa" (welcoming, nonjudgmental, everyone's favorite).

At Maddy's party, Princess and Maddy encounter older college-age men. Princess gets separated from Maddy and is picked up by a guy named Rory, who takes her to meet his friends. Erica (Nikki's best friend) tracks Princess down and delivers a sharp speech about predatory men who camouflage themselves as feminists with tote bags and nail polish. She escorts Princess and Maddy out.

Parallel scenes at Scott and Karen's home show Scott in a depressive episode after starting therapy. His Ukrainian housekeeper/caregiver Nadya tends to him and makes dry observations about his situation. Karen expresses regret about suggesting therapy.

The episode closes the next day with everyone back at home. Princess and Tyler report their party was "fine." Barbara returns to the household, now revealed to be a goth (wearing a necklace and darker aesthetic). Jason admits to Nikki he was "a hundred percent" looking at the woman in the store after all, and Nikki says "I know."

**Important Character Actions**

- Nikki lies about Jason approving the party and about having seen a ghost-costumed child to mask the real jealousy argument.
- Princess lies to both parents about who gave permission, exploiting a communication gap.
- Erica demonstrates her protective maternal instinct toward Princess, delivering the episode's most morally grounded speech about men's behavior.
- Scott's depression arc deepens; he has stopped getting out of bed and is being cared for by Nadya.
- Karen begins to reckon with the unintended consequences of pushing Scott into therapy.
- Jason's final admission — that he was genuinely looking — recontextualizes the entire episode's argument as one where both parties were partially dishonest.

**New Names and Terms**

- **Barbara** — Jason's new girlfriend/partner who is spending time with the family; revealed to be a goth.
- **Kerry** — Jason's work colleague, a man Nikki developed a crush on.
- **Nadya** — Scott and Karen's Ukrainian housekeeper/caregiver; dry-witted and practical.
- **Maddy** — Princess's friend who hosts the party.
- **Paul** — a young man at the party Princess and Maddy talk to (not uni-age).
- **Rory** — an older man at the party who approaches Princess after Maddy wanders off; met at "Izzy's party."
- **Izzy** — mentioned in passing as someone whose party Rory and Princess may have both attended.
- **Suki** — a friend who invites the family to a roller-skating-and-burgers party on Friday.

**Continuity-Relevant Facts**

- *The Ring* is established as Nikki and Jason's third-date film, making it symbolically important to their relationship history.
- Jason is described as a former English teacher; he references his teaching background when discussing the word "Kerry" and gendered names.

**Unresolved Threads**

## Episode Plot

Nikki and Jason discover they were never actually invited to Laurie's wedding.
