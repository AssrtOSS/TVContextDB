## Overall Plot Premise
Years after her mother vanished without a trace, **Sabina Tejeros** follows a cryptic clue to an uncharted island. There she uncovers terrifying secrets binding her bloodline to the land — dark Filipino folklore, generational trauma, and oceanic mystery intertwine as Sabina confronts the truth behind her family’s past. The series blends investigative thriller structure with supernatural horror rooted in local myth.

## World Setting
- **Primary locale**: A remote, uncharted Philippine island (fictional), shrouded in folklore and isolation.
- **Timeframe**: Contemporary (2020s), with flashbacks to the mother’s disappearance era.
- **Mythological layer**: Indigenous Filipino beliefs — *aswang*, *manananggal*, *babaylan* traditions, blood-oath lineages — are treated as lived reality rather than superstition.
- **Social context**: Urban Manila (Sabina’s starting point) vs. insular island community with its own hierarchy, rituals, and secrets.

## Speaking Styles & Tone Guide
- **Sabina**: Urban, educated, analytical — crisp Tagalog with English code-switching; grows raw, visceral as trauma surfaces.
- **Rebecca (flashbacks)**: Softer, lyrical, laced with folk idioms; contrasts with Sabina’s modernity.

- **Island Elders (Lazaro, Buencamino)**: Measured, authoritative; mix formal Tagalog with local dialect markers; deliberate pauses.
- **Supporting islanders**: Colloquial, superstitious undertones; may use *probinsyano* accent/dialect.
- **Narrative voice (if any)**: Somber, mythic; present-tense legend tone.

## Cultural Context for Translators
- **Filipino horror DNA**: Not jump-scare Western horror; rooted in *kwentong bayan* (folk tales), bodily horror (*manananggal* separation), and communal guilt. The horror is moral and ancestral.
- **Family as horror unit**: Filipino *pamilya* extends beyond nuclear; *utang na loob* (debt of gratitude), *hiya* (shame), and *kapwa* (shared self) drive character motives.
- **Syncretism**: Catholic imagery (crosses, rosaries) coexists with *anting-anting* (amulets), *babaylan* chants — characters move fluidly between.
- **Geography as character**: The sea (*dagat*) as barrier and connector; the island as womb/tomb. “Blood Island” is metaphor and literal geography.
- **Language**: Primary audio is Filipino (Tagalog-based). Expect *Taglish* (Tagalog–English mix) in urban scenes; deeper Tagalog/dialect on the island. Subtitles should reflect this register shift.
- **Production pedigree**: Director Rae Red (*Babae at Baril*) brings neo-noir grit; producer Bianca Balbuena (Project 8 Projects) champions genre auteurism. Expect visual density — pay attention to background details (tattoos, altars, graffiti) for subtitle cues.
