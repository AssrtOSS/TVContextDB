# Chicago Med — Subtitle Translation Background

## Series Identification (verified)

- English title: Chicago Med
- Type: American medical-drama television series (single-camera, ~40–44 min per episode)
- Creator: Dick Wolf, with Matt Olmstead. Developers: Derek Haas, Michael Brandt
- Production companies: Wolf Entertainment and Universal Television
- Broadcaster: NBC (USA). Canadian run originally on Global Television Network, then Citytv. Hong Kong: NOW TV- First aired: November 17, 2015 on NBC
- Franchise standing: third installment of Dick Wolf's "One Chicago" / "Chicago franchise," after Chicago Fire (2012) and Chicago P.D. (2014); sibling series Chicago Justice (2017)
- A backdoor pilot aired inside Chicago Fire S03E19 "I Am the Apocalypse" (April 7, 2015); the standalone pilot under the title Chicago Med also aired that night

## World Setting and Overall Premise

The series is set in Chicago, Illinois, primarily inside the Emergency Department of the fictional Gaffney Chicago Medical Center (加夫尼芝加哥医疗中心), with frequent crossover to neighboring Gaffney units (OR, psych, pediatrics) and occasional crossover to the Chicago Fire Department and Chicago Police Department. The premiere episode "Derailed" takes place on opening day of a brand-new state-of-the-art ED and opens with a mass-casualty L-train derailment that immediately tests the newly assembled trauma, ED, pediatrics, psych and nursing teams.

Across seasons the show braids:
- High-stakes medical cases (trauma, ED, pediatrics, infectious disease, psych, OB, oncology)
- Ethical dilemmas (DNR, end-of-life, capacity, organ donation, addiction treatment, medically assisted treatment)
- Workplace politics among clinicians and the executive administration, including ownership-arc pressure under Jack Dayton in S8
- Personal lives and romantic arcs of the staff
- Periodic crossover events with Chicago Fire and Chicago P.D.

## Speaking Styles and Cultural Context

- Dialogue is rapid-fire American clinical English with frequent medical shorthand layered onto emotionally charged personal scenes. Doctors and nurses often speak in clipped imperative sentences ("Page Dr. Choi. Trauma panel. Let's move.")
- In professional exchanges, characters default to title-and-surname ("Dr. Manning," "Dr. Choi," "Dr. Halstead"); nicknames (Will, Big Red) appear off-shift or with close colleagues
- Sharon Goodwin uses institutional register and legalistic phrasing; Maggie Lockwood uses warm, working-class Chicago cadence; Dr. Daniel Charles uses contemplative, philosophical speech for psych consults; Dr. Crockett Marcel drops into brash New Orleans-flavored English; Dr. Dean Archer is sardonic and blunt
- Chicago is treated as a recurring character: Lake Michigan, the L (CTA), Lake Shore Drive, Cabrini–Green references, deep-winter street scenes, sports references (Bears, Cubs, Bulls) and the city's neighborhood politics all appear regularly
- The series inherits procedural rhythms from Chicago Fire and Chicago P.D., blending case-of-the-week medical stories with serialized workplace arcs; the "crossover" event is treated as in-universe normal — a gunshot wound may pull in a P.D. detective, a structure fire pulls in Chicago Fire's 51
- Real headline-driven ethical debates (vaccination, end-of-life, addiction, mass-casualty triage, mental-health crisis, gun violence, organ donation) often serve as episode backbones; subtitles should preserve the moral register and not soften medical or ethical terms
- Several actors retain native-accented English (Brian Tee for Ethan Choi, Ato Essandoh for Isidore Latham, etc.) and the dialogue occasionally shifts into Spanish or Korean in the ED; the show does not subtitle these internally, so subtitle groups must decide whether to flag or translate the original line

## Notes for Subtitle Translation

- This is a continuing series across 11+ seasons; many character-name and term choices are fixed through mainland subtitle groups. Where two renderings circulate, prefer the YYeTs / 人人影视 mainline to maintain continuity across seasons; for Taiwan / Hong Kong releases use 芝加哥醫情 and the traditional-character pairings (威廉·霍斯泰德, 莎朗·古德溫, 瑪姬·洛克伍德)
- Hospital jargon should be rendered in conventional Chinese medical terms rather than literal English ("trauma bay" → 创伤抢救室; "OR 2.0" → OR 2.0 手术系统; "Patient and Medical Services" → 患者与医疗服务部)
- "One Chicago" / "Chicago franchise" can be left as芝加哥系列 or 芝加哥宇宙 depending on context; the phrase 一芝加哥 is not in active use in subtitle files
- When characters reference real Chicago landmarks or institutions (CTA, Lake Shore Drive, Bears, Cubs), keep them recognisable in Chinese and add brief parentheticals only when essential for comprehension
- Keep professional-formality register intact in clinical exchanges (surname + 医师 / 医生), and switch to first names in personal scenes to mirror the source register
- For recurring nicknames, retain them in Chinese (大红 for Big Red, 红发辣妹 for Ginger Spice) only on second reference after the English nickname has been introduced
- When a crossover episode involves characters from Chicago Fire or Chicago P.D., verify against the cross-show source list above before translating their first-name references
