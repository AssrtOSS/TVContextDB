# Eternally Yours — Translation Background

## Series Identification

- **Exact title:** *Eternally Yours* (no subtitle; do not confuse with the 1939 Barbara Stanwyck film of the same title, nor with the earlier anime/drama of similar name)
- **Type:** Single-camera sitcom, supernatural comedy
- **Creators:** Joe Port and Joe Wiseman (the same team behind CBS's *Ghosts* adaptation)
- **Country / Language:** United States, English
- **Broadcaster:** CBS (Thursdays 8:30/7:30c); streams live on Paramount+ for Premium tier, on-demand the next day for Essential
- **Premiere:** October 8, 2026 (Pilot); Season 1 ongoing as of writing
- **Production:** CBS Studios, The Tannenbaum Company, Joe vs. Joe
- **Format:** Half-hour comedy, ~30 min per episode
- **Known Season 1 episodes:** "Pilot" (Oct 8, 2026), "Baby Vampire" (Oct 15, 2026), "The Mistake" (Oct 22, 2026), "Halloween" (Oct 29, 2026) — production codes EY101–EY104

## Setting & Premise

A 500-year vampire marriage has gone stale. **Charles** and **Liz**, an undead couple who fell for each other in 16th-century Austria, now live in a present-day Seattle house shared with their oddball coven. Their adopted daughter **Emma** brings home **Max**, a sincere human IRS investigator. His arrival forces the household to confront whether eternal love can survive — or whether eternity itself is the problem.

The show's comedic engine is the collision of immortal domesticity with mundane mortal life: tax forms, school, dating, Halloween, blended-family awkwardness — all complicated by fangs, sunlight rules, and the politics of a vampire coven pretending to be a normal Seattle household.

## Speaking Style & Tone

Sitcom dialogue: fast, witty, dry. Vampire idioms (forever, eternal, undying) are deliberately overused as relationship metaphors. Expect: meta-jokes about immortality, pop-culture references to other vampire properties (*What We Do in the Shadows*, *Twilight*, etc.), Seattle-local color, and IRS procedural flavor whenever Max is on screen. Charles speaks with a dry, English-tinged cadence (Ed Weeks is British); Liz is more direct and contemporary-American; Max is earnest and slightly neurotic.

## Cultural Context

- Seattle setting: rain, coffee culture, Pacific-Northwest liberal milieu, tech-industry backdrop.
- IRS investigator Max = the classic bureaucratic outsider trope.
- Adopted trans-species family: vampires raising a human daughter nods to both *What We Do in the Shadows* household energy and long-running *Ghosts* blended-family DNA (same showrunners).
- "500 years married, fell in love in Austria" → vague European-historical glamour, not tied to a specific real historical figure.
- Halloween episode aligns with U.S. broadcast tradition; the show replaced *Ghosts* in its Thursday slot.

## Subtitling Notes

- Keep vampire-status reveals (Max learning the truth, sunlight rules, no-reflection) as earned moments — do not front-load exposition.
- "Coven" should be rendered as a single consistent term across all episodes; do not switch to "clan" or "brood" mid-series.
- Title *Eternally Yours* may carry double meaning (romantic vow + immortal promise). Standard subtitle convention: leave series title untranslated; translate episode titles literally ("Pilot" → 《试播集》or keep《Pilot》; "Baby Vampire" → 《吸血鬼宝宝》; "The Mistake" → 《那个错误》; "Halloween" → 《万圣节》).
- Charles's dry wit often relies on understatement — preserve the flatness rather than embellishing.
- Number, currency, and date formats: localize to target audience (CST is +8h from U.S. Eastern; release dates fall one day earlier in China).
