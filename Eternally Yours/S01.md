## S01E01

# Eternally Yours S01E01 — Pilot

## Plot

Cold open: 16th-century Austria. Liz begs Charles to turn her so they can be together forever; he refuses, she accepts. A cut reveals the "way afterward" — five centuries of bickering over his fake throat-clear.

Present-day Seattle. Max Marshall, an SSA (Social Security) fraud investigator and grad student in psychology, meets his future girlfriend Emma when he brings his rescued therapy dog to her veterinary clinic. They date for two months, but Emma refuses to let him visit her home. His skeptical coworker Benji jokes she's married; Max deflects. At work Max flags a case: Jesse Blythe at Emma's address has been collecting Social Security since 1935 — impossibly, ~150 years old.

The household: Charles and Liz's vampire coven includes buff personal trainer Jesse, older Phyllis (who frequently needs her memory wiped), and teen half-vampire twins Henry (basketball star, dreamy) and Mort (not on the team, reads the dog Argus's mind). They buy bagged blood from a Craigslist seller named Steve. They hate moving every few years to hide their non-aging. Vampires can't divorce — the Council of Elders would stake them. Charles pines for the old days of hunting humans.

Max shows up posing as an SSA investigator. Charles attempts the vampiric memory-wipe hand gesture; Max is unusually resistant. Inside, Liz and Charles roleplay a normal couple: Charles is "in financial planning," Liz a Seattle College history professor. Charles jokes most retirements last 20 years "and then you get to die." Max tells them to "find their dance" — rekindle what they loved early on. Charles picks Liz as "right."

Watching Twilight on TV, the coven debates killing Max to avoid moving. Then Liz reframes Max's advice: hunting and feeding on humans is *their* dance. They prepare to kill him anyway — Emma walks in and introduces Max as her boyfriend, blowing the "we're Canadian" cover.

Charles forbids the relationship. Emma argues she's dated for two centuries and Max is special. Liz visits Max at his apartment (Max formally invites her in; Liz scolds him about the garlic stereotype) and coaches him to stand up to Charles. Max returns and confronts Charles at the door. Liz reminds Charles that *he* once defied a coven elder to be with her. Charles relents: Max and Emma can date, but if he hurts her, "we eat him."

While training telekinesis (Mort is trying to move a pencil), Charles's marble bust flies across the room — Mort's power surging — and strikes Max, who stops breathing. Charles, having just grown, agrees to turn Max. Cut: Max narrates "the day I died."

Final beat: Phyllis walks in on a stranger on the floor with Charles bearing fangs. Jesse wipes her memory. She wanders off smiling.

## Character actions

- Max: Falls for Emma; investigates Jesse; resists vampiric mind control; is fully accepted only after being fatally wounded by Mort's accidental telekinesis and turned.
- Emma: Two centuries of dating; lied about being Canadian; stands up to Charles over Max; previously dated Ivan.
- Charles: Head of the coven, patriarch, dry wit, possessive; only bends because Liz appeals to who he used to be; ultimately turns Max.
- Liz: The conscience of the marriage; engineers Max's acceptance by recalling her own courtship.
- Jesse: Buff spray-tanned personal trainer; SS-fraud case subject; untrained mind-control wipes Phyllis.
- Phyllis: Memory-wipe sponge; walks in at the worst moment.
- Henry: Basketball-team twin, dreamy philosopher type; first suggests turning Max.
- Mort: Twin not on the team, reads Argus's mind, struggling with telekinesis — his power surge causes Max's death.
- Argus: Therapy dog; mind-readable by Mort.
- Benji: Max's skeptical SSA coworker.
- Steve: Craigslist blood supplier; socially awkward.
- Ivan: Ex-boyfriend, vampire, ate a mailman and a guy for cutting him off in traffic; praised for "great hair."

- **Covens** — vampire households (Charles's rule: vampires can't divorce; Council of Elders would stake them).
- **"Find your dance"** — Liz's framing for rekindling a marriage.
- **"Way afterward"** — what Max thinks vampire-romance films skip.
- **Wildcats** — Henry's and Mort's basketball team (only Henry actually plays).
- **SSA** (not IRS) — Max's actual employer, U.S. Social Security Administration.
- **Memory-wipe hand gesture** — vampire power; only works on weak-minded targets (Max is resistant; Phyllis is highly susceptible).
- **Invite-in rule** — vampires must be formally invited into a home.
- **Garlic stereotype** — explicitly framed as offensive in this household.
- **Council of Elders** — vampire authority that punishes divorce with staking and dismemberment.

## Continuity facts

- Emma is an adopted orphan whom Charles and Liz turned into a vampire to save her from diphtheria (she's now a half-vampire only by heritage; she's full vampire).
- Henry and Mort are Liz's teen half-vampire twins (one parent vampire, the other undead — implying a previous relationship).
- Argus the therapy dog was trained by Max and previously visited a children's hospital.
- Max attended Michigan for undergrad; is pursuing a master's in psychology.
- Phyllis has reading glasses; Charles keeps a marble bust of himself in the home.
- Charles's cover job is "financial planning"; Liz's is Seattle College history professor.

## Jokes and references for later episodes

## Unresolved threads

## S01E02

# Episode S01E02 — "Baby Vampire"

## Plot
Max wakes up in the coven's house with no memory of being attacked by a flying marble bust (the result of Mort's still-unreliable telekinesis). With the family unable to let him die, Charles bit him, and he is now a freshly turned vampire. A hung-over morning of bad news turns into Max loudly refusing to believe it: no fangs, no powers, blood makes him nauseous, his 12-hour skull heal is dismissed as "calcium chews." Charles grounds him indefinitely; Jesse is assigned babysitter.

Max sneaks out anyway to make a giant IRS-facing presentation at the Tacoma office, leaving Benji, his PowerPoint-clicker coworker, holding the bag. Mid-pitch, Max cuts himself on a paper-towel dispenser, gets hit by the smell, and lunges at Benji, knocking them both down. Liz, Jesse, and Emma trail him to his workplace, blunder through vampire etiquette (formal invitations, almost-wiped a bystander, telepathy accidentally overheard), and learn he is in Tacoma, not Seattle.

Charles catches wind of the escape. Liz argues that banishment violates the coven's "we broke him, we bought him" rule; Charles invokes the Council's law requiring accommodation only, and arranges a transfer to the Winnipeg coven for six pints of Steve. Emma counters with an ultimatum — "if he goes, I go" — and lets slip that she burned down Chicago, blamed it on a cow, and is in general commitment-phobic. Charles backs down. Max finally drinks blood on camera, finds it sweet, and gets welcomed in (though Charles still wants "one of us" buy-in). The episode closes on Charles over-texting his tentative work friend Eric and almost doubling down on a literal carrier raven.

## Important Character Actions
- **Max**: Denial -> escape -> feeding accident -> apology -> first sip. Goes from "I'm not a vampire" to "100%, one of us" within the hour.
- **Charles**: Bites Max on Liz's insistence to save him; grounds Max; ready to banish him to Winnipeg; ultimately folds in the face of Emma's threat.
- **Liz**: Defends Max, argues against banishment ("a bit cruel"), front-lines the search.
- **Emma**: Holds the line with Charles via ultimatum, and finally puts a stake in the relationship without physically proposing.
- **Jesse**: Quarantines Max on the couch, gets distracted by Phyllis returning from the salon, joins the manhunt that ends in Tacoma.
- **Phyllis**: New functional beat — "the hair is looking fire," a TJ Maxx sweater set, telekinetically cracking floor tiles. Also revealed to be Jesse's wife, and crucially does not know the family is vampires (Jesse slips, then Charles says "you didn't hear anything").
- **Mort**: Comedy narrator of the denial beat; the "sucks" pun is finally delivered. Sharpens the running gag of being the buffoon who says the quiet part out loud.
- **Henry**: Youngest-looking coven member; light presence; throws in "doesn't he have to stay with us?" — small but useful.
- **Charles's "friend" Eric**: New workplace-only human contact; Charles has never mentioned him, doesn't actually have lunch booked, and after meeting tries to bond over "Prussian" copy machines.

## Newly Established Names / Terms
- **Benji** — Max's IRS coworker, runs the clicker during Max's presentations, becomes Max's first "vampire snack" casualty. Plays the integral-but-expendable sidekick.
- **Eric** — Charles's awkward work "friend"; loves barbacoa; hates Prussians (joke-in-joke running gag).
- **Nathaniel the candlemaker** — Charles's dead friend of two centuries; brought up by Liz to needle him. Useful as proof Charles has essentially no living friends outside his wife.
- **Steve** — the guy from Craigslist whose blood the coven buys. Brand-name shorthand ("a nice tall glass of Steve"). Six pints of Steve become the bribe currency for Max's transfer.
- **The Council** — vampire governing body that mandates a coven must find accommodation for anyone they turn. Adds an institutional layer above Charles.
- **Winnipeg coven** — Canadian vampire family willing to take Max for the right price.
- **BV / "Baby Vampire"** — Jesse coins as Max's nickname; quickly sticks.
- **"Vamp"** — slang for ad-lib/improvise ("I can't vamp"), rebuilt as vampire pun. Max finds it clever; Liz does not.
- **"V8 juice"** — the family cover story for blood, used in front of outsiders.
- **Claude** — implied past associate who replies by raven; used to mock Charles's analog tendencies.

## Continuity-Relevant Facts
- The Pilot's cliffhanger — the marble-bust accident and the emergency bite — is resolved: Max was turned by Charles on Liz's order ("at your mother's request") because he was going to die.
- Max's six-week relationship with Emma is now a fully cohabiting situation (Liz calls moving in fast; Max slept over that night).
- Vampire rules / worldbuilding rolled out this episode: (1) Must be formally invited into a building; (2) sunlight exposure endangers the coven; (3) vampire pee is still red; (4) super-speed is "cheetah-like short bursts," not long-distance travel; (5) telepathy exists but is leaky and audible to humans nearby; (6) memory wipe is a tool they sometimes panic-deploy on bystanders; (7) offspring/turn-responsibility is governed by Council fiat, not just etiquette.
- Fangs and major powers don't appear on day one — Liz confirms "can take some time to develop."
- Charles's grudge-holding is canonized: the 1893 Chicago World's Fair ascot incident (Jesse borrowed it, lost it on the Ferris wheel) is invoked twice — once by Jesse as a warning sign of what's coming, once by Charles himself.
- Liz burned down a city, which the coven explains away as "the cow" did it. The comic self-portrait of an immortal with a checkered past.
- IRS / Max's day job: he had a presentation with "all the department heads" and a Tacoma office approximately 45 minutes from Seattle. Likely ongoing workplace complications in future episodes.
- Charles has no real friends in 2026 except Liz and a coworker whose name he hadn't spoken out loud before this episode.

## Jokes / References Useful for Later Episodes
- "Sucks" pun runway (Mort) — keep available for any new vampire double-meaner beats.
- "There's no 'I' in coven" — Henry/Liz earnest maxim, ripe to come back as a gag when the coven fractures.
- "Baby Vampire" / BV — assignable nickname any time Max fumbles a vampire skill.
- "Vamp" the verb — easy callback for any improv/awkward moment.
- "V8 juice" cover story — keep consistent as the household code for guests or delivery people.
- "Prussians" / French-era Charles one-liner — surfaces again in any old-country episode.
- "Booyah guy" — Emma and Max's running agreement from their Scrabble game, sets the tone for how she manages him.
- The 1893 Chicago World's Fair / ascot — evergreen threat, can be invoked whenever someone misplaces Charles's things again.
- The cow that took the fall for the Great Chicago Fire — straight-faced family lore; high reuse value.
- "Double raven" — Charles's instinctive, almost-comic analogue communication; Liz as the corrective voice.
- "We broke him, we bought him" — coven responsibility ethos; Charles abandons, Henry + Emma enforce.

## Unresolved Threads
- Max's first real vampire skills (telekinesis, speed, mind-reading) — clearly to come.
- Whether Charles will actually host a lunch with Eric, and what Eric is. (Strong "secret also-a-vampire-or-supernatural" hook given the writers' pacing.)
- The Winnipeg coven offer — left suspended. Pressure lever for any future Max-misbehavior beat.
- Emma / Max escalating to official cohabitation, and the unspoken question of turning Emma further or accepting her half-vampire status going forward.
- Liz's "Council" mention hints at a broader vampire politics / bureaucracy arc; the Winnipeg transfer is the first visible exercise of it.
- Phyllis still does not know Jesse's secret. Open detonator.
- Henry is barely used — likely a sleeper setup.
- Jesse's cluelessness about adulting plus stealth infidelity-grade interest in a much older vampire (Phyllis) reads like a near-term subplot.
- The vampire-hunter threat that Liz name-drops but never explains — flagged as the next external-danger lever.
- Max's IRS career: his cover story is now "ill," he attacked Benji, he left Tacoma mid-presentation. Real-world consequences queued up.
- Mort's telekinesis is still rogue; the marble bust was the canon accident, the "floor thing" with Phyllis implies recurring property damage.
- Charles's raven vs. text dynamic and his almost-stalker-level monitoring of Eric — long fuse for character growth or embarrassment.
- Max's lingering "maybe I'm not really a vampire" theory — the show has not foreclosed on the half-turn hypothesis; could be a season-arc reveal.

## S01E03

# Eternally Yours — S01E03 "The Mistake" Summary

## Episode Plot
Max's *hospitio vampirus* (vampire initiation ceremony) becomes the backdrop for a long-buried secret: Liz tried to kill Jesse in 1876 but accidentally turned him instead. The revelation fractures the coven's trust, especially Jesse's, while Charles's centuries-old grudge against local vampire Celeste Bonafour erupts into a petty magical feud. The episode ends with a joint ceremony welcoming both Max and Jesse, repairing some bonds.

---

## Important Character Actions

| Character | Action |
|-----------|--------|
| **Liz** | Confesses to Max (then accidentally broadcasts via his telepathy) that she *tried to kill* Jesse in 1876 but botched the feeding, turning him instead; concealed the truth for 150 years. |
| **Jesse** | Learns his vampirism was a mistake, not a choice; feels betrayed; initially refuses to forgive Liz; later accepts a belated *hospitio* and the name "Jesse the Iceman." |

## Eternally Yours — S01E04 "Halloween" Summary

### Episode Plot
Halloween night. The coven refuses to celebrate (Charles: "my culture is not your costume"), but Liz hosts her department head **Dr. Geltzer** and his wife **Susan** to schmooze for her pending **full professorship**. Meanwhile, **Max** unknowingly places the **Wolfsbane Chalice** — an ancient relic that protects the domicile from werewolf incursion — on the porch as a candy bowl. **Mort**'s date **Mackenzie** arrives; the coven suspects she's a werewolf (werewolves are emboldened on Halloween, can blend in). They run the **Lupinian Trials** (ancient werewolf-detection tests), kick her out, then discover **Phyllis** had the real chalice in her bedroom all night — meaning the house was protected and Mackenzie *couldn't* have been a werewolf. ## S01E05

# Eternally Yours S01E05 Summary

## Episode Plot
Steve (the household's blood supplier) leaves town after Max's motivational pep talk at his 50th birthday game night inspires him to chase his dream—walking onto the Notre Dame football team at age 50. The coven loses its blood source and faces starvation. Desperate, they consider "The List" (killable people: a Target parking-spot thief and elderly neighbor Mrs. Kellerman). Max proposes robbing a blood bank instead. The heist fails when vampires realize they need a formal invitation to enter. Steve returns, dream crushed by a security guard. Max improvises a lie: Steve's blood contains a rare protein used by a secret government program to synthesize cutting-edge medical treatments, making him a hero who saved hundreds of lives. Steve stays. Meanwhile, Phyllis manipulates Liz into banishing her giraffe painting to the basement; they later reconcile when Phyllis repairs Liz's slashed Renoir and adds a lizard "zhuzh."

## Important Character Actions
- **Max**: Organizes game night, invites Steve → causes crisis; improvises "government program" lie to save Mrs. Kellerman and restore Steve's self-worth; reveals he was college improv co-captain ("Gag Reflex") and is studying to be a therapist.
- **Charles**: Initially dismissive of Steve ("walking blood bag"); gets competitive at Minutia Battle; admits Max's recent humanity gives him a unique perspective and thanks him for teaching empathy.
- **Liz**: Defends Steve as a person; clashes with Phyllis over decor; accepts Phyllis's repaired Renoir.
- **Phyllis**: Manipulates Liz into moving giraffe painting to basement (30-year pattern revealed); later repairs Renoir as peace offering; demands gnomes on mantel year-round.
- **Jesse**: Plans blood bank heist (Old West background); assigns roles using vampire powers (Charles: telekinesis, Emma: lockpicking/Houdini reference, Max: bolt cutters).
- **Emma**: Picks blood bank lock; mediates Charles/Max dynamics.
- **Steve**: Leaves for Notre Dame dream (copies *Rudy* exactly); returns defeated; believes hero narrative.
