## Overall Plot Premise
Twenty years after a gambling scandal ended his NFL career and got him banned, **Reggie Dinkins** (Tracy Morgan) hires **Arthur Tobin** (Daniel Radcliffe), a fallen documentary filmmaker, to shoot a rehabilitation documentary. The project becomes a chaotic household fixture as Reggie's ex-wife/agent **Monica**, fiancée **Brina**, son **Carmelo**, and deadbeat best friend **Rusty** all navigate the cameras — and each other. The season-long arc: **can Reggie claw his way into the Hall of Fame while his past, his rival Jerry Basmati, and his own ego sabotage every step?** The mockumentary lens exposes the gap between Reggie's self-mythologizing and the messy reality.

## Speaking Styles & Translation Notes

| Character | Style | Translation Approach |
|-----------|-------|---------------------|
| **Reggie Dinkins** | Tracy Morgan's surreal, non-sequitur delivery; grandiose self-mythologizing; sudden vulnerability; malapropisms | Keep rhythmic absurdity; don't over-explain jokes; preserve "Reggie logic" — e.g., "I didn't gamble *on* football, I gambled *near* football" |
| **Arthur Tobin** | British neurotic; pretentious film-school jargon; panic spirals; self-deprecating but oblivious | Use slightly formal/academic register for his "director speak" (幕后花絮, 视觉语言); contrast with Reggie's vernacular |
| **Monica** | Sharp, rapid-fire, corporate-speak mixed with maternal bite; code-switches between agent & mom | Crisp, professional tone for business scenes; warmer but still clipped with family |
| **Rusty** | Simple, literal, sweetly dim; unexpected wisdom; deadpan non-reactions | Plain vocabulary; literal translations of idioms land as jokes |
| **Jerry Basmati** | Smug broadcaster polish; passive-aggressive; "I'm just saying" cruelty | Smooth, slightly formal Mandarin; layered insults under polite surface |
| **Brina** | Hip-hop adjacent slang; confident, unbothered; switches to business mode for money | Contemporary youth vernacular; keep English loanwords (flow, bars, royalties) where natural |
| **Carmelo** | Teen speak; TikTok/Gen Z references; manipulative when needed | Light internet slang (整活, 破防, 躺平); don't over-localize memes |
| **Narcissa** | Performative wellness-speak; manipulative vulnerability; British posh | Soft, breathy tone masking control; "healing journey" vocabulary |

## Cultural Context for Translators
- **30 Rock / Tina Fey comedy DNA**: Fast dialogue density, cutaway gags, celebrity cameos playing twisted versions of themselves, meta-showbiz satire
- **Sports mockumentary tropes**: *The Office* (UK/US), *Parks & Rec*, *Ted Lasso* — talking heads, "documentary crew" as character, cringe comedy
- **NFL culture references**: Hall of Fame voting politics, ex-player media pipeline, youth camp circuit, gambling scandal stigma (Pete Rose parallel)
- **British vs. American humor clash**: Arthur (Radcliffe) embodies awkward British cringe; Reggie embodies loud American absurdity
- **Celebrity cameos as themselves (heightened)**: Megan Thee Stallion, Craig Robinson, Ronny Chieng, Heidi Gardner — treat names as known entities
- **2026 tech/media landscape**: TikTok dances, viral clips ruining careers, streaming docs, reality TV crossovers, music royalty disputes
- **Family dynamics**: Divorced co-parenting as business partnership, blended family friction, teenage secrecy under surveillance
