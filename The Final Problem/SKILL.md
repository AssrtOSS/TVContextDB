## Key Relationships
- **Basil ↔ Foxá**: Reluctant investigative partners; Basil provides Holmesian deduction, Foxá provides literary genre savvy and local access.
- **Basil ↔ Mrs. Ausländer**: Adversarial tension; she controls the hotel, he probes its secrets.
- **Adela Figueroa ↔ Elisa Mander**: Traveling companions; Adela's true connection to Elisa is a mystery.
- **All guests/staff ↔ Each other**: Closed-circle suspects; every relationship conceals motive or secret.

## World Setting
- **Time**: Spring 1959
- **Place**: Small islet (Santa Águeda) off the coast of Mallorca, Balearic Islands, Spain
- **Primary location**: Isolated luxury hotel cut off by a violent storm — no one can leave or arrive
- **Atmosphere**: Period-authentic 1950s Spain under Franco regime (background tension), post-war European glamour meeting claustrophobic paranoia
- **Visual palette**: Storm-lashed Mediterranean, art-deco hotel interiors, black-and-white film-noir aesthetic echoes

## Overall Plot Premise
Thirteen strangers are stranded on a remote Mallorcan islet by a catastrophic storm. When a quiet English tourist, Elisa Mander, is found dead in the hotel — initially dismissed as suicide — retired actor Benjamin Basil, once famous for playing Sherlock Holmes on screen, is pulled into a real investigation. As the storm traps everyone, Basil partners with Foxá, an aspiring thriller novelist, to dissect alibis, expose lies, and confront the hotel's steely manager, Mrs. Ausländer. Each episode peels back layers of deception, revealing that every guest and staff member harbors a secret. The "final problem" is not merely the murder, but the meta-puzzle of an actor becoming the detective he once pretended to be.

## Speaking Styles
- **Basil**: Measured, theatrical precision; slips into "Holmes voice" (deductive monologues, archaic phrasing) when reasoning; weary but sharp. Spanish original likely uses formal *usted* and literary register.
- **Foxá**: Youthful, energetic, peppered with writerly metaphors and genre references; more colloquial, aspirational tone.
- **Mrs. Ausländer**: Controlled, polite but guarded; bureaucratic professionalism masking authority. German surname suggests slight accent or formal Spanish.
- **Adela Figueroa**: Enigmatic, evasive; shifts between vulnerability and calculation.
- **Fonseca**: Director's jargon, ambitious, performative charm.
- **Hotel staff**: Functional, subservient surface; underlying resentment or complicity.
- **Narration / Episode descriptions**: Noir-inflected, omniscient, slightly literary ("The shooting of a new film reveals everyone's true colors...").

## Cultural Context
- **Francoist Spain (1959)**: Censorship, secrecy, international isolation — parallels the locked-room mystery. Foreign tourists (English, German) contrast with Spanish nationals.
- **Sherlock Holmes in Spanish pop culture**: Basil's film series evokes the Rathbone-Bruce era; Spanish audiences would recognize the detective archetype.
- **Arturo Pérez-Reverte**: Bestselling novelist known for historical thrillers, intellectual puzzles, and meta-literary play. Adaptation fidelity expected to be high.
- **Mallorca as setting**: Not yet mass-tourism destination; exclusive, aristocratic, insular — heightens class tensions among guests.
- **Netflix Spanish original**: High production values, international cast, subtitles/dubs in 30+ languages. Translation must balance period flavor with global accessibility.
