## S01E07

### 剧情

- 两百五十万年前，南北美洲逐渐靠近，海床隆起并形成潮汐陆桥，开启南北美洲生物大交换。
- 中美洲持续干旱，一头四十岁的嵌齿象首领母象生下罕见的双胞胎。
- 剑齿虎夜袭象群。成年象在首领母象带领下组成防线，双胞胎最终都活过首夜，但小公象与母亲受伤。
- 为寻找水源，首领母象带领象群向南迁徙。她的伤口感染，在旅途中死亡；曾失去幼崽、仍有乳汁的另一头母象继任首领。
- 新首领继续南行，带领象群穿越只在退潮时出现的陆桥。象群被涨潮困住，小母象幼崽一度被冲散，却奇迹般幸存并抵达南美洲。
- 南美洲拥有淡水和茂密植被，但象群成了陌生大陆上的“外来者”。它们先后遇到巨型树懒、箭齿兽和恐鸟；新首领挺身保护象群，成功击退恐鸟。
- 随着永久陆桥在未来的巴拿马形成，南北物种大规模混合。许多物种灭绝，也有新物种演化，南美洲最终出现地球历史上最丰富的巨型哺乳动物群落之一。

### 重要角色行动

- 首领母象：夜间守卫象群；凭脚底感知远方雷暴；冒险离开祖传领地并率群南迁；临终前完成了通往新大陆的关键旅程。
- 失去幼崽的母象：救下走散的小公象；继任首领；凭借前任留下的路线继续带群寻找水源，并建立新族群秩序。
- 双胞胎幼崽：小公象曾靠近雕齿兽并被母象救回；小母象在渡海时被潮水卷走，最终幸存。
- 恐鸟：将箭齿兽驱赶出弱者后，试图把新来的嵌齿象当作猎物；首次面对大象时遭到首领母象阻挡。

### 新名称与术语

- Gomphotheres：嵌齿象类
- Matriarch：首领母象
- Smilodon：剑齿虎
- Glyptotherium：雕齿兽
- Giant sloth：巨型树懒
- Toxodons：箭齿兽
- Terror birds：恐鸟
- Great American Interchange：南北美洲生物大交换
- Isthmus of Panama：巴拿马地峡
- Clash of Species：物种大碰撞

### 连续性相关事实

- 南北美洲此前被海洋隔开，南美洲曾与世隔绝约五千万年。
- 大陆漂移改变洋流，使中美洲逐步干燥；水源消失成为象群迁徙的直接原因。
- 嵌齿象类源自非洲，至此已扩散到第五块大陆。
- 南美洲本土捕食者长期独立演化；孤立结束后，北方物种与南方物种发生剧烈竞争。
- 生物大交换最终令南美洲拥有三十二种体重超过一吨的哺乳动物。
- 更近代的人类重新连接各洲，推动家畜、昆虫、植物和病毒跨洲传播，造成新的物种混合与灭绝。

### 笑点与可回指内容

- 象群幼崽把首次见到海洋当成“海滩欢乐日”，成年象却因陌生环境紧张。
- 小公象把长相古怪的雕齿兽视为潜在玩伴；雕齿兽显然不习惯被轻易掀翻。
- 旁白调侃雄性嵌齿象“体内充满睾酮”，随后强调连顶级捕食者也知道何时撤退。
- “外来者”视角可在后续涉及物种迁徙、生态入侵或新捕食者出现时回指。
- 哥伦布抵达被旁白称作另一场“大陆碰撞”，把史前生物大交换与人类时代的全球物种混合联系起来。

### 未解决线索

- 北方捕食者已经跨越陆桥，后续将改变南美洲生态格局。
- 南北物种持续扩散，竞争与灭绝仍会继续。
- 人类推动的全球物种混合将成为地球生命未来形态的重要影响因素。

## S01E08

## Surviving Earth S01E08: When America Flooded

### Episode Plot
12,000 years ago at the end of the last Ice Age, the **Laurentide Ice Sheet** covering northern North America collapses. A massive glacial lake (larger than all Great Lakes combined, holding 2,300 cubic miles of meltwater) bursts through ice dams, unleashing one of Earth's greatest flash floods. The deluge tears across the continent, dumps freshwater into the Atlantic, and **collapses the North Atlantic Current** — plunging the Northern Hemisphere back into ice age conditions for 1,000 years (the Younger Dryas).

The episode follows a **giant short-faced bear mother** (North America's largest bear ever) raising two cubs. She emerges from hibernation ⅓ lighter, marks territory, hunts, and teaches cubs to forage. When the megaflood hits, her instinct drives them to their high hillside den — saving their lives. But the post-flood world is barren. A **large male short-faced bear** begins hunting her cubs; she fights him off but loses one cub days later. Desperate and unable to produce milk in the returned deep freeze, she risks stealing caribou from a human encampment, takes a spear to the flank, and dies — her final act securing a last meal for her surviving cub.

A millennium later, warming returns violently (18 °C rise in decades). The climate whiplash finishes off the ice-age giants: the last **mastodon** and **mammoths** fall to human hunters who have become coordinated apex predators. The episode closes drawing a direct parallel to today: Greenland Ice Sheet meltwater pouring into the North Atlantic, with the critical difference that **humans now control the causes of change**.

### Important Character Actions
- **Mama Bear**: marks territory by scent-rubbing; denies cubs den exit until spring; removes porcupine quills from curious cub; leads cubs to high den ahead of flood (saving them); fights larger male to the death for cubs; steals human-hunted caribou while mortally wounded.
- **Male short-faced bear**: ignores mating opportunity, targets cubs; kills one cub after truce.
- **Dire wolves**: form a "super pack" after desperate winter; hunt stag-moose cooperatively.
- **Stag-moose**: escapes wolves by entering swollen river, survives plunge over falls, loses one antler.
- **Humans**: hunt caribou cooperatively; share meat; later drive mammoth/mastodon into traps with flint-tipped spears; become apex predators capable of killing any animal as a group.

### Newly Established Names / Terms
- **Giant short-faced bear** (*Arctodus simus*) — North America's largest bear ever
- **Dire wolf** — ⅓ larger than modern gray wolf
- **Stag-moose** (*Cervalces*) — six-foot antlers
- **Mastodon** (乳齿象) — shorter, stockier than mammoth/elephant
- **Laurentide Ice Sheet** — millions of sq mi covering Canada north of Great Lakes
- **Lake Agassiz** (implied) — glacial lake > Great Lakes combined, 2,300 cu mi
- **North Atlantic Current / AMOC** — transports warm water northward = 100 Amazon Rivers
- **Younger Dryas** (implied) — 1,000-year return to ice age after megaflood
- **Greenland Ice Sheet** — modern analog melting into North Atlantic

### Continuity-Relevant Facts
- Final episode of Season 1; bookends the series' "life survives catastrophe" thesis with a direct modern warning.
- The megaflood → AMOC collapse → Younger Dryas → rapid warming sequence explains the extinction of North American megafauna (short-faced bears, mastodons, mammoths).
- Humans numbered only ~10,000 in North America but cooperative hunting made them the decisive pressure.
- Large animals (mammoths) could migrate; bears could not — a key extinction filter.
- Climate whiplash (rapid freeze → rapid thaw) exceeded adaptation capacity of ice-age giants.

### Jokes / References for Later
- "A mother's work is never done" / "当妈的永远没个停歇" — cubs wander off.
- "Rude" / "真是无礼" — porcupine rebuffs naive cubs.
- "This little one won't be messing with porcupines again" — quill removal lesson.
- "They are playing hooky" / "它们在开小差" — cubs sneaking away.
- "Miracles can happen and this old timer is proof" — stag-moose survives falls.
- "It's a small price to pay for cheating death" — antler lost vs. life saved.
- Louis Armstrong's "What a Wonderful World" plays over the final montage — ironic/fitting close to the series.

### Unresolved Threads
- Fate of the **surviving cub** (mother dies, cub alone in returning deep freeze).
- Fate of the **last mastodon** ("lucky to make it through the fall").
- Whether the **Greenland Ice Sheet melt** will trigger another AMOC collapse — the episode leaves this as an open warning.

## S01E02

## S01E02 — When the Climate Broke / 当气候崩溃时

**Setting:** 232 million years ago, Late Triassic (Carnian Pluvial Episode). The supercontinent Pangaea has endured a million years of megamonsoons — the wettest period in Earth's history. Ancient reptiles rule; dinosaurs have not yet risen.

### Plot

A million years of monsoon rains have turned Pangaea into a lush world of swamps, fern forests, and vast estuaries. Two massive male **Ischigualastia** — the planet's largest herbivores and early mammal relatives — fight for dominance, trampling a communal nest and scattering eggs. A tiny hatchling female, no bigger than a rabbit, survives the chaos and reaches the nursery run by young females.

She learns two essentials: how to identify the sole plant her herd eats, **Dicroidium**, and never to stray far. But she wanders off and is stalked by **Panphagia**, a small but skilled early dinosaur. Too large to kill quickly, the predator tries to tire her out. She fights back, cries for help, and the herd's babysitters arrive in time. Lesson learned — she now knows how to call for aid.

Then the rains stop. The volcanoes driving the monsoons go silent. For the first time in a million years, drought threatens Pangaea. Swamps vanish, lakes shrink, forests die. The herd must roam farther each day to find Dicroidium. At a grove where the plants should grow, they find a troop of **Rhynchosaurs** have already eaten every shoot down to the root. The rivals are small but aggressive; the herd moves on.

After days of trekking, the herd finds a small lake — the only water for miles. Many have already perished. The curious youngster wanders off again, this time into coastal plains stalked by **Saurosuchus**, a 20-foot apex predator distantly related to crocodiles, fully capable of killing an adult Ischigualastia. She calls for help and survives once more.

The herd follows dry riverbeds where Dicroidium still grows, but this leads them hundreds of miles from their forest home into unfamiliar territory. Conifers and woody shrubs replace the soft ferns they know — vegetation they cannot eat. Months become years of drought. Half the herd dies.

Two years after hatching, the young female has grown into the herd's sole babysitter. Far fewer hatchlings were born this year, and she carries the weight of the herd's future. The herd's keen sense of smell leads them to a hidden pool inside a cool cave — relief turns to terror when a Saurosuchus appears. The cave is a trap. The herd panics; some flee. She stays inside, guarding the hatchlings. She holds back, protecting the youngest for now.

Then, after two years of drought, the monsoon returns — but the parched ground is rock hard. Water rushes into empty river valleys, causing violent flash floods. The herd, feeding at the bottom of a river canyon, is right in the flood's path. Older adults sense danger and scramble for higher ground. The young babysitter frantically tries to push the hatchlings up the riverbank. A Saurosuchus appears. In an instant, predator and prey are both swept away. But her hatchlings survive. She is a born fighter, and today she has ensured the future of her herd.

**Time jump:** Thousands of years of climate chaos follow — landscapes lurch from rainforest to desert and back again. Half of all species perish. The world stabilizes much drier. The dinosaurs, able to survive on far less water, take over as the dominant dynasty. Many ancient reptile types vanish.

**Epilogue:** A brief glimpse of a drier world 15 million years later introduces **Chaliminia**, a primitive mammal. A father leaves his underground nest of 34 babies to hunt crickets. He accidentally blunders into a **Zupaysaurus** (predatory dinosaur) nest, is surrounded, and seems doomed — until a passing **Lessemsaurus** herd disturbs the predators. The mother defends her young. He escapes with one cricket, but his mate needs twenty every day. Mammals have claimed the undergrowth; above ground, dinosaurs will rule for the next 160 million years.

### Character Actions

- Hatchling female survives the male fight, reaches nursery
- Wanders off, attacked by Panphagia, fights and learns to call for help
- Stalked by Saurosuchus, calls for help again, survives
- Grows into sole babysitter during the drought
- Guards hatchlings in the cave trap, holds back from panic
- Sacrifices herself (apparently) to save hatchlings from the flash flood

| English | Chinese | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Ischigualastia | 伊氏古阿斯塔利亚 | Main herbivore, early mammal relative, largest herbivore on Earth |
| Dicroidium | 双叉叶 | The herd's exclusive food plant |

### Continuity-Relevant Facts

- Dinosaurs do NOT dominate yet in this era — ancient reptiles rule
- Ischigualastia are early, distant relatives of mammals
- The herd's extreme dietary specialization (only Dicroidium) becomes a fatal weakness during climate chaos
- Dinosaurs' ability to survive on less water is the key to their later dominance
- Mammals first appear as tiny, water-efficient creatures in the undergrowth
- Water and rainfall remain the central determinants of life across all episodes
- The series-long theme is reinforced: out of chaos, the most adaptable dynasty rises

### Jokes, References & Tone

- "What a time to hatch!" — dry irony as the nest is trampled during a dominance fight
- "Okay, it's time to go" — the herd's collective shrug when faced with aggressive Rhynchosaurs
- The closing song "What a Wonderful World" plays over footage of humans engineering water systems, then cuts to the narrator noting that Earth still decides where it rains — a pointed, ironic juxtaposition
- The Chaliminia father needs 20 crickets a day and only has one: "Unfortunately, he's got to get out there and do it 19 more times" — a rare moment of documentary humor about the unrelenting grind of parenting

### Unresolved Threads

- The main protagonist's fate after being swept away in the flash flood is left ambiguous — her hatchlings survive, but hers is not confirmed
- Whether the Ischigualastia lineage persists into the drier world, or goes extinct with the other ancient reptiles, is not addressed
- The dinosaurs' takeover is declared but not yet shown in action — a transition the series will explore in later episodes
- The epilogue's closing question — whether humanity's technological control over water has truly freed us from nature's rule — is posed as a lingering challenge that echoes across the series

## S01E06

# S01E06 "When the Oceans Shrank" — 当海洋退缩时

## Plot Summary

Set 444 million years ago during the Late Ordovician extinction event. A young male **Sacabambaspis** (萨卡班甲鱼), one of the first vertebrates, navigates a coral reef ecosystem that is about to collapse. The episode opens with the reef thriving in warm shallow seas, but land erosion feeds massive algae blooms that absorb CO₂, triggering rapid cooling. A polar ice cap forms, sea levels drop, and 85% of all life dies out.

The young fish endures a series of misfortunes: stuck in a shell, chased by squid-like predators, taking refuge in a cave occupied by a molting **giant sea scorpion** (巨型海蝎), and swept a mile offshore by rip tides. He catches a ride on a **giant orthocone** (直角石) during a supermoon mass-mating migration, which carries him back to the reef just in time to find a solitary female and fertilize her eggs before dying. An iceberg drifts into the tropics, releasing freezing freshwater that devastates the reef. The eggs hatch at the worst moment, but at least the newborns are mobile and escape. Eventually volcanic activity warms the planet, seas rise, and life recolonizes — now with jawed, toothed fish as apex predators.

## Important Character Actions

- The young male Sacabambaspis searches for food, fails to compete for prime nesting spots, gets swept away, and ultimately succeeds only through persistence and luck.
- The giant sea scorpion molts in a cave (vulnerable), then returns to hunting with renewed size and hunger.
- Hundreds of orthocones gather under a supermoon to mate; males perform bioluminescent courtship dances.
- A female orthocone lays eggs and stands guard without eating for six months, dying before they hatch — "a mother's ultimate sacrifice."
- Baby Sacabambaspis hatch as freezing water hits the nesting site.

| Orthocone | 直角石 | 30-foot shelled cephalopod, ocean's top predator |

| Supermoon | 超级月亮 | Triggers mass mating events |

## Continuity-Relevant Facts

- **85% extinction rate** — matches the Permian-Triassic event in scale.
- Sea levels drop by **300 feet**; polar ice cap grows to **8× modern Antarctica**.
- Volcanic activity under the ice eventually reverses the cooling — a pattern echoed in later episodes.
- **Post-extinction innovation**: fish evolve jaws and teeth, becoming fast apex predators.
- Sea scorpions are the **first complex predators to move onto land**, foreshadowing terrestrial ecosystems.
- Prototaxites fungi create the **first forests**; the first forest fire is called "a sign of life's success."
- Closing narration draws explicit parallel to **modern sea-level rise** (~3 feet predicted by 2100) and human coastal cities.

## Jokes & References

- "This guy, a fish" — narrator's casual tone about the protagonist.
- "It is just not his day" / "Looks like it's this guy's lucky day" — running commentary on the fish's rollercoaster of luck.
- "Here be monsters" — classic cartographic reference when the fish reaches open ocean.
- "Sometimes, it pays to be tiny" — the fish survives precisely because he's too small to be hunted.
- Closing song: Louis Armstrong's **"What a Wonderful World"** plays over images of modern reefs and human adaptation.

## Unresolved Threads

- The fate of the specific Sacabambaspis eggs is ambiguous — they hatch, but the iceberg's cold pulse hits immediately; survival of this lineage is implied but not confirmed.
- The episode ends with a modern coda: rising seas threaten coastal cities, and the question of whether humans will "adapt" as life has done remains open — a thematic thread for the entire series.

## S01E05

## S01E05 — When the Forests Collapsed / 当森林崩塌时

### Episode Plot

3.05亿年前，赤道地区覆盖着以鳞木为主的茂密沼泽森林，大气含氧量达到史前巅峰。森林的成功反而埋下毁灭的种子——巨量植被吸收二氧化碳，触发不可逆转的全球降温。气候变冷导致蒸发减少、旱季延长，曾经湿润的沼泽逐渐干涸。

**笠头螈**错过筑巢时机，自行挖掘洞穴产卵，并在数周内悉心守护。然而水位持续下降，她被迫将幼崽含在口中送往溪流，随后织出黏液茧转入休眠，但属于她的时代已经结束。

**林龙**在大火中奋力抢救巢中幼崽，成功脱险。爬行动物凭借有防水壳的卵，得以摆脱对水的依赖，在干旱中占据优势。

**埃里欧普斯**的求偶季到来，雄性以沼泽相扑角力争夺配偶，胜者紧紧缠绕数小时完成受精。双亲随即弃卵而去，500枚卵中仅约10枚能存活至成年。

**节胸蜈蚣**（8英尺长的巨型千足虫）发现笠头螈的洞穴，但这只温和的巨兽对幼崽毫无威胁，它真正的目标是埃里欧普斯的粪便。

随着干旱加剧，淡水鲨鱼**奥氏刺软骨鲨**在浅水区追踪笠头螈，却因贪功冒进送了命。

森林崩塌数百万年后，针叶树取代鳞木，爬行动物进入鼎盛时期。**二齿鳞龙**成为顶级掠食者，**圆颅龙**作为首批大型草食动物之一出现——它们以群体防御抵御天敌，却因误食有毒植物而中毒倒下。

### Important Character Actions

| 角色 | 行动 |
|---|---|
| 笠头螈 | 自行挖洞产卵 → 守护数周 → 水位下降时转移幼茧 → 织茧休眠 |
| 林龙 | 大火中返回救幼崽 → 成功带领幼崽逃生 |
| 埃里欧普斯 | 雄性以低频轰鸣和水面震动展示 → 沼泽相扑角力 → 胜者受精后双亲弃卵 |
| 节胸蜈蚣 | 探查森林地面 → 被粪便吸引离开洞穴 |
| 奥氏刺软骨鲨 | 在浅水追踪笠头螈 → 冒进捕食被反杀 |
| 二齿鳞龙 | 嗅到地下冬眠的两栖动物 → 追踪圆颅龙 → 被群体防御逼退 |
| 圆颅龙 | 误食毒叶 → 出现头晕、流涎、胀气 → 倒下昏睡 |

### Newly Established Names & Terms

- **笠头螈** (Diplocaulus) — 小型两栖动物，体外鳃，水中呼吸
- **林龙** (Dendromaia) — 最早永久登陆的脊椎动物之一，产防水壳卵
- **埃里欧普斯** (Eryops) — 体长可达八英尺的巨型两栖掠食者，满嘴弯曲獠牙
- **节胸蜈蚣** (Arthropleura) — 八英尺长的巨型千足虫，以粪便和腐殖质为食
- **奥氏刺软骨鲨** (Orthacanthus) — 淡水鲨，干旱时被迫进入浅水区
- **二齿鳞龙** (Dimetrodon) — 自卸卡车大小的顶级掠食者
- **圆颅龙** (Cotylorhynchus) — 首批大型草食动物，祖先为肉食者，具群体防御行为
- **鳞木** (scale trees) — 树干为空心管，不到15年可长至14层楼高
- **针叶树** (conifers) — 以种子而非孢子繁殖，取代鳞木成为新优势树种
- **巨脉蜻蜓** (Griffinfly / Meganeura) — 翼展如乌鸦，因高氧环境而巨大化

### Continuity-Relevant Facts

- 沼泽森林是"自身毁灭的建筑师"——吸收过多二氧化碳导致全球降温
- 这个地质时期形成了后世90%的煤炭储量
- 爬行动物的崛起归功于防水卵，使其摆脱对水体的依赖
- 草食动物出现后，植物开始产生毒素防御——圆颅龙中毒即为此例
