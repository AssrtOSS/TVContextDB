## S18E01


Plot

Frank throws a grand, combined funeral for two women: Bonnie, Charlie's mother, and Sam, Frank's (late) bride. To fund an outsized memorial with clowns, musicians, Mummers, Shriners, and a cannon, Frank sells the family house over everyone's objections. Dennis and Dee spend the episode trying to collect on inheritance agreements they each secretly signed with Sam — a deal that requires Sam's official marriage certificate (with both spouses' signatures and thumbprints), which Uncle Jack the lawyer has but forgot to bring, and which ultimately dies with Sam. Meanwhile Mac schemes to free his father Luther from prison, and Charlie quietly carries his cremated mother's ashes through it all.

Important character actions

Frank plans and funds a "calliope-and-cannon" Mummer's funeral for Bonnie and Sam, sells the house to pay for it, and delivers the eulogy — capped by a cannon blast sending Sam's ashes (and everyone's hoped-for thumbprints) skyward. Charlie mourns his mother's ashes in a small urn, joins Frank's circus-themed tribute, and celebrates the "slam dunk" cannon shot. Dennis and Dee work the inheritance angle with Uncle Jack, picking up a death certificate, needing a signed/thumbprinted marriage certificate, and trying to get Sam's correct thumbprint — only to be foiled when Frank has already claimed Sam's remains. Mac takes his mother to Uncle Donald, brings Luther in on a 24-hour compassionate leave, tries to protect Donald at the last moment by calling out the switch, and is ultimately outmaneuvered by his father.

Newly established names and terms

Bonnie — Charlie's mother, who has died; her ashes are placed in a small urn and honored at a Mummer's funeral. Sam — Frank's bride, also deceased; her fortune is the prize Dennis and Dee are chasing, and her ashes are shot from a cannon. Luther — Mac's father, in prison, attending the funeral on 24-hour compassionate leave. Uncle Donald — Luther's brother, effeminate and a lifelong Mummer enthusiast, who hosts the family with rosé, branzino, and crème brûlée. Mummers, Shriners (in fezzes), VFW, and Elks Lodge — the men's-group scene Bonnie was "super active" in; she was a "Mummer hummer."

Continuity-relevant facts

Charlie's mother Bonnie is dead and cremated (kept in a chained urn he carries). Sam, Frank's bride, is dead, leaving Frank a widower.

Dennis and Dee had each separately signed inheritance agreements with Sam — both now appear void. Frank is selling Charlie's house to pay for the funeral. Mac's father Luther is in prison; his mother (Mac's mom) was living at the house and is now effectively homeless. Uncle Jack remains the family lawyer, and is famously sloppy (he brought the wrong paperwork, left the signed marriage certificate at his place, and is stuck in a waterless basement). The Gang previously helped Frank "throw a man over a cliff."

Jokes and references useful later

Frank's eulogy — "I should know, I banged both of 'em." The recurring "slide the body in and slam the door" mortuary bit, including repeatedly busted $60,000 doors. The "wrong body / wrong thumbprint" gag stemming from ripped-off thumbs. Scrooge McDuck money-vault-swimming fantasy. Musical cues "When The Saints Go Marching In," "I'm Looking Over a Four Leaf Clover," and "Temptation Sensation." Dennis and Dee's running classist tirades about "breathing in the poor."

Unresolved threads

With Sam's ashes fired from the cannon, both Dennis's and Dee's inheritance deals appear lost. Luther is on the loose after using the funeral procession to escape, and vows to "come back to kill you someday" (directed at Mac); he may be living as Donald with Mac's mother. Mac's mother's housing is unsettled, and Charlie's house was sold for the funeral. Frank remains a widower with a costly, spectacular funeral behind him.

## S18E03

S18E03 — Back to School

## Plot

After Dennis and Dee get positive ADHD results from a full neuropsych battery at Penn (Charlie's comes back "inconclusive"), they decide to return to school to prove they could have graduated. Charlie insists they "declined to graduate" rather than flunked out, with Frank reminding them he paid the tuition. Mac, by contrast, is actually at Penn grinding, paired with a study buddy.

Dennis and Dee plan to buckle down and study. Frank wants to party instead and "pull a Back to School," Rodney Dangerfield–style, dragging along a bewildered Chinese international student, "G" (real name Jie). In rapid succession Frank (a) trashes G's dorm as lacking bongs and lava lamps, (b) recruits G to defecate in the dean's trash can — a plan aborted when G notes campus cameras and uses his key card, forcing Frank to break a window and then confess to the dean while covering for G.

While Frank mentors G, Charlie is administered a CAPTCHA-style "which image has grass" test, baffles the professor by insisting a man's hair is "the grass of the head," claims he can smell every color in the room, and is later invited to a panel of professors assembled to announce "the most extraordinary scientific discovery in the last 100 years" about him. He walks out mid-lecture the moment the professor puts his folder down, declaring he already knows what he is.

Mac's confidence is destroyed when Dennis stuffs a Tootsie Roll (previously stored in his own anus) into Mac's study partner's ear. Dee abandons Ritalin for cocaine and then crack. Frank convinces G to attempt a synchronized high-dive off the college board — Frank backs out at the top. G jumps anyway and the episode cuts on the Gang's horrified reaction as he falls.

After the dive, Dennis and Dee check the grade portal and discover they never actually enrolled — Dennis admits he "skimmed the website" but there were "so many words." The episode ends on Jude Cole's "Back to School" with Frank declaring G failed "the most important test in life" — listening to a stranger.

## Important Character Actions

- Dennis: ADHD-positive; returns to school, never enrolls; gets high with Dee; torments Mac's study partner with a butt-stored Tootsie Roll; at the end asks Mac if anything was put in his ear.
- Dee: ADHD-positive; returns to school, never enrolls; escalates from Ritalin talk to actual cocaine, then crack; her crack-fueled chaos ("rocket surgeon" malapropism, breaking pencils, shouting "Bitch" at Dennis).
- Mac: actually studying at Penn with a partner; gets his partner's focus destroyed by Dennis; later asks if Dennis put something in his ear.
- Charlie: inconclusive test; baffles faculty with the CAPTCHA, a word-analogy question ("stab infection" for staph, "cummerbund" for cumbersome), and knowledge that a can holds 627 beans; walks out on the panel's diagnosis; declares "I'm Charlie" and that he doesn't need a test to know he's smart.
- Frank: hijacks G's dorm life, attempts dean's-desk crap prank, stages a synchronized dive that ends with G's apparent injury, philosophizes that "life is the only education" and you don't want to fail it.

## Newly Established Names / Terms

- G (Jie) — Chinese international student Frank adopts as a sidekick; mispronounces his own name as "G."
- "Rocket surgeon" — Dennis's malapropism for "rocket scientist."
- "Stab infection" — Charlie's mishearing of "staph infection."
- "Cummerbund" — Charlie's misuse of "cumbersome."
- "Ritterall" — recurring mispronunciation of Ritalin.
- "The China Express to sexy town" — G's pickup line under Frank's coaching.
- The dean's-office / synchronized-dive schemes — variants of the Rodney Dangerfield Back to School bit.

## Continuity-Relevant Facts

- Dennis and Dee attended Penn on Frank's money; their long-standing claim is they "declined to graduate," not failed.
- Charlie's childhood Tootsie Roll-in-the-ear memory (told as a gag earlier in the episode) is treated as real — Dennis weaponizes it against Mac's study partner.
- The school-shooter "front row so we can scramble" gag from earlier in the episode is echoed later when G assumes surveillance cameras exist "because of the shooters."
- Dennis drops a slipped reference to "that old bitch" professor "taking advantage of me," echoing the season's pattern of Dennis's predatory academic-romance history.
- The professor panel explicitly frames Charlie's case as "the most extraordinary scientific discovery in the last 100 years" — the actual diagnosis is never delivered on-screen.
- The episode closes on Jude Cole's "Back to School," suggesting it functions as the season's title/theme for this installment.

## Jokes and References Useful for Later Episodes

- Rodney Dangerfield's Back to School (1986) is the central conceit — Frank keeps invoking it and the closing song is its theme, so any future "Dangerfield did it" callback lands here.
- Timbuk 3's "The Future's So Bright, I Gotta Wear Shades" plays over the montage of G's integration into campus life.
- Charlie's idiosyncratic senses (smelling colors, hearing bean counts, knowing pencil-pocket volumes) are treated as legitimate diagnostics by the panel — establishes him as a continuing "savant" thread.
- Frank's "life is the only education / you don't want to fail at that" line, plus his "rocket surgeon"-adjacent mentorship of G, is the kind of quote that gets recycled in later clip-show-style episodes.
- The dean's-office crap prank and the synchronized high-dive are classic "Frank pulling a younger, stupider guy into criminal stupidity" set pieces that may be referenced.

## Unresolved Threads

- G's condition after the dive — cut on horrified reactions; status unknown.
- Charlie's official diagnosis — the assembled panel never delivers it; he walked out because the professor set his folder down.
- Mac's study partner, who was sabotaged via the Tootsie Roll and may be compromised academically.
- Dennis's hinted encounter with "the old bitch" professor — explicitly cut off mid-sentence ("The Noth— Nobody.") and likely a running thread.
- Dennis and Dee never enrolled — their "Back to School" arc is fully deflated; no resolution.
- Dee's escalation from Ritalin-talk to cocaine to crack, plus her "rocket surgeon" slip, hint at a substance thread the season may pick up.
- The crew-team jock who told Frank and G to "be there or be square" for the dive — never seen again.

## S18E04

# S18E04 — "The Gang Gets Lost in a Homeless Encampment" (working title)

## Episode Plot
The Gang drives to a Jamiroquai concert in an RV, having left their phones at home as a "'90s tribute" and ripped out the console (breaking the gas gauge). They run out of gas in a sprawling homeless encampment. While Mac goes for gas, the others encounter Cricket, now living among Puerto Ricans in the camp. Cricket reveals the Gang is his "backstory" — he's homeless because of them. Charlie invents a "fentanyl stand" to prop up people "folding" from fentanyl, and they scheme to monetize it via government contracts. Frank accidentally shoots Cricket, Cricket locks them out of the RV, Mac returns pantsless in a trash bag, Dee is folded on fentanyl, Dennis's Jay Kay hat catches fire on a bug zapper, and Frank gets a phone by trading teeth. They ultimately drive off to the concert singing "Virtual Insanity."

## Important Character Actions
- **Dennis**: Jamiroquai superfan; insists on "Jay Kay" corrections; frames the encampment as the future Jamiroquai warned about; makes the "virtually insane" pun to justify going to the concert.
- **Mac**: Volunteers as "enforcer" who "knows the streets"; chain wallet fails (pants fall down); returns in a trash bag after failing to find gas/RV.
- **Charlie**: Invents the "fentanyl stand" prototype; treats it as a million-dollar government-contract idea; sings wrong songs (Cypress Hill/Jumanji) thinking they're Jamiroquai.
- **Dee**: Takes fentanyl, spends episode "folded" (bent at waist); Charlie demos the stand on her.
- **Frank**: Paranoid about "holes" and tooth currency; shoots Cricket (grazes him); trades teeth for a cell phone; wants robot teeth implants.
- **Cricket**: Living with Puerto Ricans in the encampment; CD player is his prized possession; bluntly tells the Gang "You are my backstory"; locks them out of the RV after being shot.

## Newly Established Names/Terms
- **"Folding"** — physical state of fentanyl users bent at the waist, unable to stand straight.
- **"Fentanyl stand" / "fentanyl standing device"** — Charlie's prototype prop to keep folding people upright.
- **"Torso-based narcotic"** — Dennis's term for fentanyl only affecting the upper body.
- **"Virtual Insanity" future** — Dennis's interpretation: Jamiroquai predicted a tech-distracted, humanity-losing dystopia.
- **Jay Kay** — Dennis insists this is the singer's real name (not Jamiroquai), spelled J-A-Y K-A-Y.

## Continuity-Relevant Facts
- Cricket explicitly states the Gang caused his homelessness: "You are my backstory... I got pushed out of society because of you two."
- The RV is established as a "second home that drives" (acquired previously).
- Frank's phone addiction referenced — Gang replaced it with a calculator.
- Mac's "enforcer"/"knows the streets" self-mythology fails again.
- Dennis/Dee twin dynamic: Dennis mocks Dee's concert behavior (Dave Matthews callback: tossed under Astrovan, broken ribs).
- Jamiroquai fandom established for Dennis (superfan knowledge: portmanteau of "jam session" + "Iroquois").

## Jokes/References Useful for Later Episodes
- **"Fentanyl stand" government-contract scheme** — classic Gang "social play" for federal dollars; prototype exists.
- **Cricket's CD player** — his "most prized possession"; used as night pot alternative.
- **Tooth currency** — Frank's Mad Max confusion becomes semi-real when he trades teeth for a phone.
- **"Virtually insane" pun** — Dennis's closing wordplay on the song title.
- **Bug zapper as landmark** — "Left at the bug zapper, past the rat lot, right at the barrels."
- **Pigeon head painted like parakeet used as guitar pick** — encampment surrealism.
- **Jamiroquai lore** — Dennis's deep-cut knowledge (Jay Kay, hat, portmanteau) now canon.

## Unresolved Threads
- **Fentanyl stand prototype** — left in Cricket's tent/RV; Charlie wants to "whiteboard it" for government contracts.
- **Cricket locked in RV** — armed, shot, now in possession of the vehicle.
- **Mac's pants/trash bag situation** — unresolved wardrobe failure.
- **Frank's tooth trade** — missing teeth; plans for "robot kind" implants.
- **RV location** — Gang drives off but RV was unhoused; unclear if they reclaimed it or stole another vehicle.
- **Dee's fentanyl state** — last seen propped up by stand; no recovery shown.

## S18E05

## Its Always Sunny in Philadelphia — S18E05 Summary

### Episode Plot
The Gang attends a Renaissance Faire near the airport. Mac wants to earn a spot on the **"Quilt of Legends"** — a tapestry commemorating the most sensational moment from each year's Ren Faire. Frank attempts to buy the faire grounds to bulldoze it for a data center, but the land belongs to the **McPoyle family** (Liam, Ryan, and their father). Frank ends up in a high-stakes joust: if Mac wins, Frank gets the land; if Mac loses, the McPoyles claim Mac's eye. Meanwhile, Dee orchestrates an elaborate scheme to prove women *can* drive stories after Dennis claims they never have.

### Important Character Actions
- **Mac** volunteers to joust for Frank, then backs out when the stakes become "an eye for an eye." He never actually rides a horse.
- **Frank** negotiates with the McPoyle patriarch, bets the land on a joust, and tries to pawn the risk onto Mac.
- **Dee** masterminds the entire episode: she spies on Frank, learns Ryan McPoyle has fallen from his father's favor, convinces Ryan to switch places with Mac in the joust, and gets her parking ticket validated — driving every major story beat.
- **Dennis** claims women don't drive stories (citing Rapunzel as "passive"), then concedes Dee earned her spot on the Quilt.
- **Charlie** gets a lute/avocado guitar, sings improvised songs about Mac's (fabricated) sexual encounter, and hangs out with LARPers.
- **Ryan McPoyle** takes Mac's place in the joust and loses his eye.
- **Liam McPoyle** challenges Frank, demands "an eye" as collateral, and declares revenge after Ryan is injured.
- **Kevin** (Ren Faire employee) runs a "riddle for tent access" scam, charges 150 quid after Mac "goes off-menu" with his "wench."

### Newly Established Names / Terms
- **Quilt of Legends** — annual tapestry at this Ren Faire; each square = most sensational moment of that year.
- **"Driving story"** — recurring meta-debate: who actively propels narrative vs. passively experiences it.
- **Kevin** — Ren Faire worker who runs the "tent of pleasure" scam (riddle → entry → hidden fees).
- **"Quid"** — British slang for pound sterling; used throughout the McPoyle negotiation (150 quid ≈ $20 per the show's math).
- **"By the cabbage in my colon"** — McPoyle oath/swearing phrase.
- **Number one boy** — Ryan's status after his brother's failed investments (video store, bowling alley, now data center).

### Continuity-Relevant Facts
- McPoyle family owns the Ren Faire land; they've held it 100+ years.
- Ryan McPoyle previously made "foolish investments" (video store, bowling alley) — explaining family tension.
- Frank's data-center scheme continues his pattern of trying to monetize/destroy local institutions.
- The "gerbil" urban legend (from S03E07 "The Gang Gets Invincible" / Quilt square) is referenced as a cautionary tale about rumors becoming legend.
- Dee's fake horse-kick injury and limp were entirely fabricated for her scheme.
- Dee earns a spot on the Quilt of Legends (center square implied) for orchestrating the day's events.

### Jokes / References Useful Later
- **"Women don't drive story"** — Dennis's claim, dismantled by Dee's reveal.
- **Avocado guitar / lute confusion** — running visual gag; Charlie adopts the "bard" persona.
- **Mister Ed parody** — Dennis/Charlie sing "I'm annoyed at Dee / She's in my way / I'll kick her in her head."
- **"Bash, block, force field, backlash spell"** — LARP combat mechanics mocked as anachronistic.
- **Zelle payment at a Ren Faire** — anachronism joke; Kevin accepts Zelle.
- **Dragon = airplane** — recurring "Hold for dragon!" announcement is just planes flying overhead (airport proximity).
- **Dee's parking ticket** — she admits it was a boring addition to her otherwise perfect scheme.

### Unresolved Threads
- **McPoyle revenge** — Liam declares "Nobody messes with the McPoyles!" and vows retaliation.
- **Frank's data center** — deal collapses; land remains with McPoyles.
- **Mac's eye** — spared only because Ryan switched places; Mac still owes Frank a joust victory.
- **Dee on the Quilt** — confirmed she'll be featured this year; potential for future callbacks.
- **Kevin's tent** — still operating; Mac paid 15 quid for later use (unclear if collected).

## S18E06 — "The Gang Fights the War on Alcohol"

### Episode Plot

A Shallow Shade Lager sales rep pitches the Gang at Paddy's. They love it — until he reveals it is nonalcoholic, and they chase him out. Convinced a "war on alcohol" is underway (alongside wars on tobacco, meat, and cocaine in Coca-Cola), they split into teams to recruit drinkers: Dennis and Dee hit a white-collar office building, Mac and Charlie go to a factory for blue-collar workers. Both locations turn out to be nearly empty — offices run remote, factories are automated. Dennis and Frank hijack an empty marketing-agency conference room to create a pro-alcohol ad campaign; Mac and Charlie bring back Mitch (a lone factory intern) and later track down Buck Longwood, a legendary former worker from the factory's employee files. Buck turns out to be severely burned and wheelchair-bound from a lava-and-alcohol workplace accident, so they bail. Dennis and Frank present their half-baked ad concept to real marketing employees and stumble out. Back at Paddy's, the automated sales system shows they actually made good money — mostly from the NA beer — while they were gone. They briefly consider embracing modernity, then walk out together to "get a real beer."

### Important Character Actions

- **Dennis**: Co-leads the white-collar recruitment; co-creates the ad concept (penguin mascot, aspirational lifestyle); presents to the marketing meeting; narrates the final ad as a cinematic monologue about a "perfect secretary" that turns out to be a bottle of vermouth.
- **Frank**: Proposes the recruitment plan; reminisces about his old work routine (drink, meetings, goose the secretary, drink more); co-creates the ad; pours himself martinis in the office.
- **Mac**: Co-leads the factory trip; is obsessed with the idea of molten lava vats and muscular men bending steel; convinces Mitch to come to Paddy's; spikes Mitch's NA beer with real alcohol without telling him.
- **Charlie**: Brings sandwiches for the factory "beam lunch" he imagines; co-discovers Buck Longwood's file; pitches the Buck visit as a patriotic duty; repeatedly botches the pitch by saying the quiet part loud (mentioning Buck being "burned up").
- **Dee**: Refuses to be the group's secretary; leaves to exploit the empty office on her own terms; attempts to seduce/pressure the intern Darren (calling him "Derrick") using power dynamics, but he has low testosterone and a girlfriend.

### Newly Established Names / Terms

- **Shallow Shade Lager** — nonalcoholic craft beer brewed locally in Philly; 80 calories per bottle; the Gang samples it enthusiastically before learning it has zero alcohol.
- **Darren** — marketing-agency intern, the only person physically in the office; has low testosterone; rebuffs Dee's advances.
- **Mitch** — sole remaining factory worker; monitors all machines from a phone app; has done P90X since age five; gets his tie caught in machinery; gets secretly drunk on spiked NA beer.
- **Buck Longwood** — legendary former factory worker found in old employee files; severely burned and wheelchair-bound from a "lava accident" caused by drinking on the job; lives in a trailer park.
- **P90X** — the fitness program Mitch claims to have done since age five.

### Continuity-Relevant Facts

- Paddy's Pub now has an automated self-service sales system (tap card, phone pay, tip, self-serve) that can operate while nobody is physically present.
- The Gang has a traumatic shared memory of a monkey incident — the monkey "stuck its dick in their mouths," they all got sick for days, and it still haunts them at night.
- Frank's old work-life pattern is established: office, drink, meetings, goose the secretary, lunch, more drinks, bar until drunk enough to tolerate his family. Dennis and Dee confirm they were his children during this era.
- The "war on alcohol" framing — the Gang sees nonalcoholic beer, remote work, factory automation, and the decline of in-person socializing as a coordinated cultural assault.

### Jokes / References Useful for Later Episodes

- **Monkey trauma** — the Gang all caught something from a monkey; Dee says she wakes up terrified thinking about it; they agree to "forget the whole thing" but keep circling back. Strong callback potential.
- **Lava factory delusion** — Mac and Charlie genuinely believe every industrial warehouse in Philadelphia has "a giant vat of lava in the middle" for melting steel; Mac says lava was "America's most important export" and that's "why we took over Hawaii."
- **Penguin mascot pitch** — tuxedo, Rolex, Alfa Romeo, drink in one hand, babe in the other. The Gang loves it. Could resurface as a half-finished campaign.
- **"Goosing" as workplace culture** — Frank treats it as essential to office life; Dee weaponizes it against Darren; the entire office subplot is framed around the '50s Mad Men fantasy.
- **"No Asians" at the old bar** — Frank claims Asian people "kept to themselves" and it was "like an allergy"; Dee is surprised. Classic Gang casual racism played for discomfort.
- **"Perfect secretary" ad** — Dennis's cinematic monologue (Earth, composed, sophisticated, powerful, doesn't judge, doesn't talk back, waits patiently, not too sweet, not too bitter, smooth, reliable) is revealed to be about a bottle of vermouth. The ad was never finished.

### Unresolved Threads

- **Mitch's fate** — drunk on spiked NA beer, tie partially mangled in factory machinery, left behind at Paddy's; Mac planned to "mold him" but the Gang moved on.
- **Buck Longwood** — found but abandoned; Charlie and Mac fled without a real invitation.
- **The ad campaign** — never completed; the PowerPoint had only Earth imagery and the "secretary" reveal; the real marketing employees saw it and were confused.
- **Darren / the office** — Dee left without resolution; the office remains accessible; the Gang may return to the empty space.
- **Automated Paddy's sales** — the self-service system generated real revenue (mostly NA beer) while the Gang was absent. They briefly acknowledged this could be the future, then ignored it.
- **The monkey** — explicitly unresolved by mutual agreement, yet clearly a deep shared trauma that surfaces involuntarily.

## S18E07

## S18E07 — Episode Summary

### Plot

Mac and Charlie are assigned to Frank's annual "boiling day," the task of boiling and staining (with Kool-Aid) a year's worth of paperwork to destroy it. While boiling documents, Mac fixates on an internet conspiracy theory that the creators of *Gilligan's Island* deliberately stopped the show one episode short of 100 — the threshold needed for syndication money. He convinces Charlie that there's a cover-up involving the Howells (rich characters on the show), the Professor, government conspiracies, and even aliens stealing Bluetooth technology. Mac further spirals into the "birds aren't real" theory after Dee's offhand island-reenactment line ("You can't trust the birds") triggers a paranoid chain. He and Charlie find a plastic bird with a camera in its eye, then accidentally kill a real pigeon while destroying it.

Meanwhile, Dennis and Dee (with Frank) are secretly rehearsing a pitch for the 100th episode of *Gilligan's Island*, hoping to present it to Sherwood Schwartz's supposed grandson. They deliberately trapped Mac and Charlie in the boiling task to keep them away. Mac and Charlie catch on — they believe Frank, Dennis, and Dee are conspiring against them, using the paint job, Cricket (hired to wash windows), and surveillance pigeons as cover.

They burst in on the pitch meeting and confront the trio, but the "grandson" — who shares the name Sherwood Schwartz but whose grandfather simply owns a laundromat in Albany — reveals there's no relation. The entire pitch was built on a coincidence Mac and Charlie found "online." Dennis and Dee berate them for ruining the pitch. Mac and Charlie sheepishly conclude they shouldn't believe everything they read online, that not everything is a conspiracy, and that sometimes "there's just no 'there' there." The episode ends with Charlie apparently finishing a document — likely his own "expose" — and driving off.

### Important Character Actions

- **Mac** is the driving force of the conspiracy thread. He is the one who reads the Gilligan's Island theory online, assigns character roles for reenactments (Dennis = Professor, Frank = Skipper, Charlie and himself = "the rest"), and escalates from TV trivia to government surveillance to birds-not-real. He drinks both literal Kool-Aid (the stain drink) and metaphorical Kool-Aid.
- **Charlie** serves as Mac's skeptical but easily swayed sidekick. He initially tries to focus on the boiling task, gets pulled into the conspiracy, and provides absurdist commentary ("One less than 200," "Half a zebra"). He's the one who first notices the plastic bird and connects the Risk E. Rat logo on a boiled document to a long-shuttered restaurant.
- **Frank** is the instigator of the boiling-day scheme and also the secret organizer of the Gilligan's Island pitch. He hired Cricket to wash windows as a cover, took white paint (suspiciously matching the SS Minnow's color), and fed Mac and Charlie old Risk E. Rat receipts to keep them busy. He's the one who knows the Gilligan's Island TV-movie details (Harlem Globetrotters, robots, supremium mineral).
- **Dennis and Dee** are co-pitching the episode idea. Dennis casts himself as the Professor in the reenactment. Dee is forced into the Mrs. Howell role, which she resists (wanting Ginger or Mary Ann). Dennis delivers the final version of the pitch involving "wheelbarrow technology" applied to boat-building. Both are hostile toward Mac and Charlie's interference.
- **Cricket** appears briefly, hired by Frank to wash windows all day. He's paid by the hour and saving up to get his teeth sharpened "for the vibe."

### Newly Established Names/Terms

- **"Boiling day"** — Frank's annual tradition of boiling and staining all the year's paperwork with Kool-Aid to destroy it (an accounting scheme he calls "doubles" or "the oldest accounting trick in the book").
- **"Drinking the Kool-Aid"** — used both literally (Mac physically drinks the stain Kool-Aid) and metaphorically (Dennis means accepting conspiracy theories uncritically); Dennis notes Mac is doing both simultaneously.
- **Risk E. Rat** — a restaurant/establishment whose logo appears on one of the old boiled receipts; the Gang hasn't been there "for years."
- **Sherwood Schwartz** — creator of *Gilligan's Island* and *The Brady Bunch*; the Gang believes his "grandson" is a potential pitch contact.
- **SS Minnow** — the ship from *Gilligan's Island*; white in color, which is why the white paint raised suspicion.
- **"Supremium"** — a fictional rare mineral from the *Gilligan's Island* TV movies (compared to unobtanium from *Avatar* and vibranium from *Black Panther*).
- **"The rest"** — the unnamed characters in the *Gilligan's Island* theme song (Professor and Mary Ann), which Mac and Charlie fixate on as a deliberate erasure.

### Continuity-Relevant Facts

- Frank continues to run elaborate, layered accounting fraud schemes; the boiling of documents is framed as a long-standing annual tradition, suggesting years of tax evasion.
- The Gang maintains hidden cameras on each other constantly ("to keep the other guy from pounding off"), establishing a running surveillance dynamic.
- Cricket is still hustling odd jobs (window-washing); he previously worked for Frank and continues to be exploited by the Gang financially.
- Frank's pattern of secretly scheming behind the other Gang members' backs (the pitch) while assigning them menial busywork is well-established here.
- The episode references the Cybertruck and a water park as other Frank schemes whose paperwork was recently boiled.

### Jokes and References Useful for Later Episodes

- **Gilligan's Island conspiracy theory** — Mac's deep-dive into the show's 99-episode run, syndication math, the Howells preventing the Professor from fixing the boat, and the TV-movie plotlines (Globetrotters, robots, supremium) are all rich for callbacks.
- **"Birds aren't real"** — Charlie and Mac discover a literal plastic surveillance bird with a camera, connecting the real-world "Birds Aren't Real" conspiracy movement to the show's universe. The genuine pigeon they kill is a dark-comedy punctuation.
