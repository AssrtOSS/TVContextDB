## S01E03

# Episode plot

Three weeks before Val's vampire initiation, Victor announces a formal family dinner for Saturday 8pm to impress his rival Alexis, arriving that weekend. The same Saturday 8pm, Val has promised to take Gertie to the school prom. To handle both, Val recruits Clifford — a shapeshifter Victor banished to a "retirement home" three floors below ground for being a hopeless drunk — to impersonate him at dinner while he slips out to the prom.

Victor confides to Valeeta that years ago in Vienna he lost the castle deeds to Alexis in a game of "gummy"; Alexis gave him a century to vacate, and that deadline has now arrived. Victor refuses to beg, so he plans to win the house back in a post-dinner sword duel (double or quits). To impress Alexis, he demonstrates Wilf the werewolf's manacles on the dining-room wall, locking Wilf up on the pretext of full-moon safety.

Clifford drinks the wine cellar before the swap, gets drunk, and ruins the plan by going to the prom as Val. He tries to spike the punch bowl — which turns out to be a fish tank, killing the fish — then impersonates the wheelchair-using headmaster Mr. Henford to keep Val out of trouble. Clifford also tries to act as a tough guy ("boss daddy") in front of Gertie's father John, gets into a fight, and is thrown out by half nine. Val returns to find his double has been thrown out of his own prom date.

Back at the castle, Victor opens up to Alexis about why the home matters — the place where he raised his family, fell in love with Valeeta again, and "staged a mass execution of traitors" — and asks Alexis to let them stay. Alexis squeaks in fright and apparently gives up the house. The duel is called off; Victor declares the initiation ceremony back on track. Clifford has a brief epiphany about drinking being "just a mask."

## S01E02

## Episode S01E02 — As Val's curiosity for human life gets the better of him, he explores the village

### Plot
With one month until his vampire initiation, Val cannot bite a human neck. Viv proposes a fake-bite rig (a human effigy filled with blood-substitute that pours from a neck valve); Igon debunks it as a pig in a hat, last successfully worn by a pig-king in 1380. Val has a month to devise a plan. He follows Igon into the belfry via a waste pipe (Igon admits four bouts of dysentery) and is shown Igon's secret collection of human memorabilia. Victor summons Val for late-night hunting tuition; Vernon uses the window to draft Gary and Guy to deliver his marriage proposal to Countess Willa of Luxembourg when her family flies back from Crewe. Valeeta, sewing a hex doll of Auntie Janet, gently steers Val to stay home and keep his secret; Viv backs her up, but Val slips out in daylight anyway. He meets Gertie, who invites him to a schoolmates' Halloween party, then gets battered four times by the village windmill and concussed. John and Susan, the village vampire hunters, take him in, mock vampires at the table, and send him home with wrapped vegetables, onion, and garlic bread. Back at the castle the garlic reek sets Val off and Victor too; Gary tells Guy Countess Willa said yes; Vernon is ecstatic. Victor starts the sleeping-victim drill with Val and is genuinely impressed. The cut to the Halloween party shows the castle family already treating it as a buffet; Val's delayed garlic reaction turns into a public fit, and he screams "Vampire!" to spook the room. The episode ends on Val's identity question.

### Important character actions
- Val rejects the fake-bite plan and accepts Igon's belfry tour.
- Victor lectures Val on self-sufficiency and demands a hunting practice round.
- Vernon deputises Gary and Guy to carry his marriage proposal to Countess Willa's father when the family flies past.
- Valeeta tells Val to focus on home and keep the secret between them; Viv chimes in with her own story of being taken in as a frightened teen.
- Val breaks curfew, walks the village in daylight, meets Gertie, gets knocked into the windmill, and is concussed at John and Susan's.
- John and Susan feed Val, mock vampires, and pack him a takeaway.
- Gary reports Countess Willa's yes to Guy and Vernon; Vernon is triumphant.
- Victor begins the sleeping-victim drill with Val and is impressed enough to declare pride.
- The castle family descends on Gertie's Halloween party and treats the costumed villagers as a feast.
- Val screams "Vampire!" to mask his own garlic reaction; a girl in a Carrie costume mistakes him for a vampire.

### Newly established names and terms
- Auntie Janet (subject of Valeeta's hex doll, comments about Valeeta's hair).
- Countess Willa of Luxembourg and her family (Vernon's betrothed target), visiting a cousin in Crewe.
- Gizzacolly, the village grocer who scavenges thrown produce from the castle walls.
- The Vampire's Handbook (Igon and Viv's reference text on human behaviour).
- Elliot Davie, Gertie's schoolmate, delivered by his nan in a Freddy Krueger mask.
- Daisy, the cow at the milking shed who kicks Val.

### Continuity-relevant facts
- Val's initiation is one month away; the biting requirement is still unmet.
- Igon's belfry collection is secret; entry is via an external waste pipe; the belfry also holds dead wasps and the remains of a dead beekeeper.
- Ancient vampire lore: whichever royal-blood sibling is first betrothed becomes heir regardless of age. Vernon's betrothal plot is a direct attempt to leapfrog Val.
- Val's human identity remains secret from Victor and Vernon. Valeeta is confirmed as knowing; Viv and Igon also appear to know.
- Garlic is a confirmed vampire vulnerability. The handbook says a garlic reaction subsides within two to four nights.
- The village openly despises the castle vampires: the grocer scavenges thrown produce, John and Susan mock them at the table, and Lucy (Val) is embarrassed by her parents' vampire fixation.
- The castle household roster shown or named: Victor, Valeeta, Vernon, Val, Igon, Viv, Wilf, Gary, Guy; "the ogre" is mentioned but not on-screen this episode.

### Jokes and references useful for later episodes
- The1380 pig-king gag (vampires fooled by a pig in a hat once, briefly, with monarchy consequences).
- Igon's misinterpretations of human objects: a measuring tape as a "number rememberer" for mathematicians, a weathervane as a "chicken god" the humans obey, a brass wine dispenser as a ceremonial item.
- "Curiosity killed the cat" — Igon's stated reason humans are reduced to "big bags of blood" in the handbook.

## S01E06

S01E06 — Val realises he can no longer live a double life and is faced with the ultimate sacrifice.

## Plot

The night before Val's vampire initiation, the family fusses over preparations. Viv is being painted for a portrait, Vernon sulks and polishes goblets (the spare set still smells of fungus from an old loan to the ogre), and Victor boasts that King Valkar first demonstrated "the power of Zymonzes" at his own initiation — a forgotten ability to compel humans to obey spoken commands. Victor rigs an old theatre spotlight so guests can "see things clearly."

Valeeta admits to Val that the initiation requires him to feed from a human neck in front of the vampire peers, which is impossible because he is human. She has secretly hired Siegfried Henge, a world-renowned fixer who, according to legend, was behind the Trojan horse, the Great Fire of London, and Moses parting the Red Sea ("a system of glass partitions"). Henge sleeps on a bed of nails and arrives with a five-person decoy squad (Tess, Dale, Paul, Lara, and "grumpy" Jeremy) plus a plan involving lowered lights, a fake choking fit, and a napkin fire to create cover smoke. Igon quietly notices the extra horse and the ledger entry for Henge but doesn't connect it to the situation.

Wilf mentions off-handedly that the initiation was not scheduled on a full moon, and brags about his new life coach — a man who "turned his whole life around" but now sleeps in his car and has helped Wilf unlock his "inner fierceness."

Val spends his last day as a human saying goodbye without explaining why. He plays rock-paper-scissors for the last crisp ("nose-badger-spoon"), teaches Gertie Simon Says at the lake, and has a final supper with John and Susan (John's front-door locks are still broken — his running gag; Susan recalls the Windmill pub quiz won on the answer "Last Supper" and a lock-in that pub landlord Ted allowed). When Gertie presses him to open up about his life, Val snaps back that the responsibility on his shoulders would make her head explode.

Back at the castle, in despair, Val begs Valeeta to bite him. He reasons: if she bites him, he becomes a vampire, gains fangs, and can pass the initiation by biting a human himself — preserving Victor's pride. Valeeta resists because it would mean Val can never see Gertie again, but Val breaks down arguing that exposing himself as human would break Victor's heart forever. Valeeta agrees: "Don't expect a very big bite." Val spends the rest of the day tying up loose ends — saying a final goodbye to Gertie, who is hurt and suspicious that he won't tell her what's wrong.

At the ceremony the next evening, Vernon privately confronts Val and gloats that he knows the secret — "You're a human. You have a reflection" — and plans to expose him in front of the guests. Victor formally introduces Val; Val opens with the pun "FANGS for coming." Victor keeps boasting about Zymonzes. A human victim is wheeled out on the altar — it is Susan, who sleepwalked two miles from her unlocked home. Vernon has clearly engineered this.

The moment Val is supposed to bite, Wilf — it being a full moon — transforms into a werewolf mid-ceremony, pees on the floor, and sends the hall into chaos. Igon fetches the mop. Susan wakes up confused on the altar, asking "What have you done to your hair?" In the confusion, Val instead commands Susan with the spoken phrase "Zymonzes, moo like a cow" — and she moos from a distance. Victor is ecstatic: "My son has the power of Zymonzes!" Val declares he may not have bitten anyone, but the power is "way more impressive," and Vernon's exposure plot collapses.

## Important character actions

- Val: confides only in Valeeta, breaks down and asks to be turned, says a permanent goodbye to Gertie, deflects her with Simon Says, and improvises the Zymonzes display to pass initiation without biting anyone.
- Valeeta: hires Henge behind Victor's back, then agrees to bite Val — the ultimate sacrifice of taking his human future away.
- Victor: oblivious throughout, assumes Val will perform the biting, then reframes the Zymonzes display as his own fatherly triumph and mails the news to 416 contacts.
- Vernon: discovers the reflection secret, engineers Susan-as-victim to humiliate Val publicly, and is publicly outmanoeuvred — though still suspicious.
- Wilf: transforms at full moon despite the life-coach work, ruins the ceremony, and has to be taken out; his pride is wounded.
- Gertie: pushes hardest she's ever pushed for honesty; reads that something is wrong and is hurt by Val's silence.
- Igon: notices anomalies (extra horse, Henge ledger entry, full-moon timing, later the mop) but defers.
- Siegfried Henge: delivers an elaborate five-person decoy plan that is never executed because the werewolf chaos pre-empts it.

## Newly established names / terms

- Siegfried Henge — flamboyant world-historical fixer with a bed-of-nails bed and a multilingual manner ("Danke," "c'est wunderbar"); his decoy team Tess, Dale, Paul, Lara, Jeremy.
- The power of Zymonzes — a forgotten vampire ability to control humans with a spoken command; King Valkar the only previous master.
- Mary — referenced in Igon's Bloody Mary joke as a household resident "not too happy" about being served as a cocktail (likely the sleeping resident).
- Ted — the Windmill pub landlord who once let the Hunters have a lock-in.
- The lake — Val and Gertie's habitual swim spot; this episode's unfinished swim becomes a symbol of what he is giving up.
- Wilf's life coach — a man who "sleeps in his car" but claims results.

## Continuity-relevant facts

- Val is bitten by Valeeta and becomes a vampire; he can no longer see Gertie or any human friend without losing them. He has fangs now.
- Val can perform Zymonzes — the ability appears to work through him once he is a vampire (or perhaps always worked; the show leaves this open).
- Vernon now definitively knows Val's secret and has lost one round of exposure; he remains a threat and will keep watching for the reflection tell.
- Wilf's life-coach regimen has not cured his transformations — full moon still takes him.
- Victor's boast about Zymonzes predates the ceremony and lets Val pivot when the biting ritual fails; the family now believes Zymonzes is a hereditary Valkar power unique to Val.
- Victor's address book contains 416 recipients, a useful running joke if mail/news ever resurfaces.
- The Susan-on-the-altar incident means the Hunters now have direct physical evidence that vampires abducted her (she has been inside the castle), even if she cannot explain it.
- Igon's ledger system is detailed enough to flag a single guest horse.

## Jokes / references useful for later episodes

- "FANGS for coming" — Val's signature dad-joke register; recurring pun pattern.
- "Bloody Mary" — the cocktail gag leans on Mary being a recognisable household name; future episodes can mine more from her.
- Vampire bat physics debate between Val and Gertie ("where would all the molecules go?") — tonal marker for Val's outsider-on-the-inside voice and useful colour for any future "Val questions vampire lore" scene.
- "I prefer to play with myself… chess with myself… So I've heard" — Vernon double-entendre running gag with Valeeta; the pattern repeats.
- The "ultimate sacrifice" framing ties to the season premise: the sacrifice is giving up Gertie and the human world, not dying.
- Siegfried Henge's mythical CV (Trojan horse, Great Fire of London, Red Sea) is played as the canonical "eccentric genius fixer" trope and can be re-cited for comedy.

## Unresolved threads

- Val's new vampire status vs. his friendship with Gertie — he has just said goodbye and cannot reverse it; whether he sneaks back is open.
- The Zymonzes power is now public; Val says it's "a problem for another day" — he has to keep performing it convincingly while staying hidden in plain sight.
- Vernon has been humiliated but not silenced; he still knows the secret and is still watching.
- The life coach / Wilf control arc remains unfixed.
- John's broken front-door locks are an open household liability the Hunters keep ignoring.
- Susan's two-mile sleepwalk into the castle creates a new witness risk; whether she remembers anything is unclear.
- The "initiation gift" Val teased for Vernon was never shown on screen — possibly a deliberate dangling thread.
- Igon has noticed Henge's horse and the ledger but has not been told anything; his quiet awareness is a slow-burn thread.
- Whether any non-vampire supernatural (Wilf, the gargoyles, Henge's hired decoys) leak knowledge of Val's humanity is open.

## S01E01

# S01E01 Summary

## Plot

Vampire Prince Valentin ("Val"), heir to the vampire throne, is genuinely failing at being a vampire: his teeth are half the required length, he cannot turn into a bat, his flying amounts to inches off the ground, and he physically retches at the taste of blood. On a hunt with his father Victor, he ruins things by needing the toilet and letting the female victim go. Back at the castle, a letter from King Vladimir of the Urals — whose sons massacred a whole congregation and had a massacre named after them — is used to contrast Val's older but more accomplished brother Vernon. To cheer Val up after the failed hunt, Victor announces Val will be initiated at the next Blood Council session, then presents the initiation cloak (passed down through generations) and The Vampire's Handbook. Victor presses ahead despite Valeeta suggesting they wait until Val's fangs come in ("Carpe noctem. Seize the night!"). Val, eager to prove himself, announces he will secretly sneak to the village, retrieve the woman from the hunt, and deliver her to his father personally. On the ramparts, Gary and Guy the gargoyles explain why gargoyles are first line of defence, not rainwater spouts.

## Important Character Actions

- Val breaks initiation etiquette by letting the human victim go during Victor's hunt, lying in his write-up ("Hunt went well"), and hiding in the castle claiming invisibility.
- Victor announces Val's initiation at the next Blood Council convening, ignoring Valeeta's advice to wait.
- Valeeta gives Val the initiation cloak, hides the awkward puberty pamphlet chat, and later breaks the news that Val is human.
- Val sneaks to the village (after a careful toilet trip) to recapture the escaped human.
- Wilf approaches Val asking for an invitation to the initiation, then gets caught up in Trish the Maid taking offence at being called "fetching" like a dog.
- In the village, John and Susan — vampire-hunting parents — chase Val into a barn when B&B Ben mistakes him for a vampire because of his bat tie pin. Susan throws a stake and cracks a pun ("bloody or not").
- Val hides from the mob and meets Gertie, who has been practising kissing on a scarecrow/pumpkin named Mary to avoid social embarrassment at 15. Val fabricates a cover story about also being out at night "practising moves," giving both of them a reason to hide from their respective communities.
- The hunters call it a night after Susan says "you're not a vampire … because you have a reflection."
- Val returns to the castle; Valeeta confesses she rescued him as a baby from a burning house by the River Ouse while Victor was in Carpathia, swapped his blood for beetroot juice, and had the Invisible Woman carry him to fake his flying. She swears Val not to tell Victor.
- Val attempts to confess to Victor, but cannot get the words out; instead he wanders past Vanya (Great-Grandfather, framed as punishment for failing his initiation), and is interrupted mid-confession by Gary and Guy bickering about cloud-counting.
