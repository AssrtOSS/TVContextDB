## S02E01

## Las azules S02E01 Summary

### Episode Plot
Season 2 opens the day after the 1971 "El Halconazo" massacre (June 11, 1971), where paramilitary "Halcones" killed 150+ civilians at a student protest. Against this backdrop of shattered police-community trust, the Women in Blue unit navigates institutional resistance while secretly investigating the brutal murder of Lucio Lima (22), a gay bar employee found strangled, mutilated, and missing two fingers in his Colonia Juárez apartment. The official detectives (Gerardo Herrera, Plutarco Lozano) arrest Lucio's lover Mauricio Rubirosa—a married, closeted man with a violent record—framing it as a crime of passion. María, Val, Ángeles, and Gabina recognize the evidence doesn't fit (clean crime scene, trophy-taking, deliberate finger amputation) and push to join the investigation. María wins a coin toss against Gerardo, securing one week for her team to assist.

### Important Character Actions
- **María (María Elena Camacho de la Torre):** Reconnects with old friends (Fabiola, Tita, Esmeralda) after month of isolation post-separation from Alejandro; confronts Gerardo at poker night, bets coin toss for investigation access, wins.
- **Val (Valentina):** Suffers panic attack/flashback (implied trauma from S1 water tank incident); attends support group led by Jacinto; confronts Ángeles about rule-breaking; learns her brother Ulises was killed by police at 1968 Tlatelolco massacre.
- **Ángeles:** Pushes to investigate despite orders; analyzes crime scene inconsistencies (organized vs. crime of passion); shares case details with María at support group—breaking protocol.
- **Gabina:** Reluctant partner to Val; enforces deal (Val gets help, Gabina stays silent about bathroom incident); accompanies Val to Lucio's funeral.
- **Gerardo Herrera:** New father (daughter Violeta born); hosts poker night; refuses Women in Blue involvement initially; loses coin toss to María.
- **Lozano:** Arrests Rubirosa through intimidation; dismisses gay bar patrons as uncooperative; mocks case.
- **Mauricio Rubirosa ("Mau"):** Lucio's closeted married lover; confronts Val at bar in cold open (ex-relationship tension); arrested with hand injury.

## S02E02

## Las azules S02E02 — Summary

### Episode Plot
Set one day after the S02E01 coin-toss agreement, the episode expands the Lucio Lima case into a serial-killing investigation. María and Val track down a photograph from Lima's funeral showing multiple young people flashing a two-fingered salute, identical to the fingers severed from the first victim. Lozano pulls the women in formally; Gerardo confirms the murders are targeted and that any remaining member of the group — the Narciso Marxist Brigade, a 1968 student cell — could be next. While Gerardo and Romandía analyze the church crime scene (Lorenzo Vargas, the priest, found in the confessional beaten and mutilated; janitor Saúl Romero shot twice in the back from the confessional, with no casings — apparently an unplanned killing by the killer slipping in the victim's blood), María and Val are sent to interview Lorenzo's mother and then to locate the other people in the photo. Parallel scenes track Carolina at a bar (her cousin's "forsaken divorcée" warning comes home to her when Alejandro's infidelity is confirmed) and a new cashier-supervisor subplot. María and Val bluff their way through UNAM by claiming Olga's mother sent them, eventually tracking Olga Correa to a perfume shop where she works — and warning her in time that an unknown man had followed her the day before. Gerardo and Romandía trace the Brigade's government file to its handler, Filiberto Guerra, a cop discharged in 1954 and recruited as a government spy in 1960. Romandía reveals Guerra was a friend of her father and "not the most stable bastard." When Gerardo and Romandía reach Guerra's home, they find him shot dead inside. The episode ends on the cliffhanger: "Who the hell killed him?"

### Important Character Actions
- María and Val: present the salute photo to Lozano and get authorized onto the case; visit Lorenzo Vargas's mother (who denies knowing about his student group); then bluff their way onto UNAM campus in civilian clothes to find Olga; locate Olga at a perfume shop; extract her location via a perfume-saleswoman informant; get Olga out before the killer can act; deliver her to Gerardo for protection.
- Lozano: pulls the women into the investigation officially; sends them to the priest's mother's address.
- Gerardo: confirms targeted killings; assigns Lozano to deliver the priest-mother lead; works with Romandía on the church crime scene and later on the Filiberto Guerra lead; takes charge of protecting Olga once she's found.
- Romandía: identifies the two-finger salute; analyzes the bullet trajectory ("Tlatelolco. Forty-nine feet. Two clean shots") and floats the theory that the killer is a trained shooter, possibly a cop; recognizes Filiberto Guerra's name from her father's circle.
- Olga Correa: initially refuses to talk, then privately names four surviving Brigade members — Damián Ojeda, Enrique Saravia, Lola Hoffman, and herself — and recounts the Tlatelolco killings (César, Lino, Paolo, Manuel, Vera, and Gamboa who died four days later in the hospital), but admits she couldn't get a good look at the man who followed her.
- Filiberto Guerra: never speaks on screen; found shot dead at the close.
- An unknown superior + informant: scene shows someone reporting directly to a superior officer that "Filiberto Guerra" was the overheard name and the Women in Blue are "going to go see him" — strongly implying a mole inside the unit feeding information to a third party.
- Carolina (Carito): confronts her ex-friend circle at a bar; is revealed to have left Alejandro after he cheated ("three broken ribs and internal bleeding" — actually Aníbal, not Alejandro, in flashback context); advises María to "try other flavors" and hints someone at María's workplace is interested.
- Alejandro (briefly): calls his kids past bedtime; María negotiates a Saturday sleepover.
- Gabina: not present in the main UNAM/investigation beats; appears in the cashier-supervisor subplot and is told the others owe her 3,000 pesos for obtaining Guerra's file.

### Newly Established Names / Terms
- Narciso Marxist Brigade (Brigada Marxista Narciso) — 1968 student activist cell, named after a person called Narciso; the salute of two raised fingers is the group's signature gesture and matches the fingers amputated from the victims.
- Lorenzo Vargas — second victim; found inside the church confessional, beaten, strangled, mutilated; same fingers removed as on Lima.
- Saúl Romero — church janitor; second body on the scene, shot twice in the back from the confessional at roughly 49 feet (≈15 m); all ten fingers intact; death appears unplanned, the killer slipped on Lorenzo's blood.
- Filiberto Guerra — former police officer, discharged 1954, recruited by the government in 1960 to spy on student groups including the Narciso Marxist Brigade, released in 1969; friend of Romandía's father; found murdered in his home at end of episode.
- Olga Correa — survivor, Philosophy and Literature student at UNAM (dropped out two years ago); from Morelos; currently working selling perfumes.
- Mila — perfume-shop informant who gives the unit Olga's workplace and surname.
- Damián Ojeda, Enrique Saravia, Lola Hoffman — the three remaining surviving members of the Brigade named by Olga; locations still unknown.
- Tlatelolco casualties (Olga's recounting): César, Lino, Paolo, Manuel, Vera — killed during the massacre; Gamboa died four days later in hospital. The "rendezvous" was set for Enrique's grandmother's house; Olga didn't go and doubts anyone else did.
- "Ma'am" / "supervisor" — Gabina becomes the first female cashier supervisor at her store; her coworkers resist calling her anything formal.
- "Find your own music, Caro" — appears as a chyron/sign related to Carolina's bar subplot.

### Continuity-Relevant Facts
- Case status: two Brigade murders confirmed (Lucio Lima, Lorenzo Vargas), one accidental witness-killing (Saúl Romero); Olga Correa rescued; three Brigade survivors (Damián Ojeda, Enrique Saravia, Lola Hoffman) still at large and presumed next targets; Filiberto Guerra, the government's man who watched them, now dead — implying the killer has a state-tied background or is being silenced.
- Killer profile per Romandía: trained shooter (two clean shots at 49 ft/15 m in low visibility against a moving target), ammunition is expensive, possibly a cop or ex-cop. Right-wing "settling a score" theory floated and dismissed.
- Church forensics: gunpowder traces but no prints on the confessional; footprints from the killer toward the exit.
- The women do not officially wear uniforms at UNAM; being caught in uniform there would be a serious problem. UNAM's registrar refuses access to student files without the dean's order; the chief and the president are both off-limits because calling the governor's office would alert the President.
- Romandía's father appears at the station to say hello to her and the team — establishing her family tie to Gerardo's circle and explaining her connection to Filiberto Guerra.
- Gabina's supervisor pay raise does not take effect until next month.
- Carolina confirms she left Aníbal (not Alejandro) after domestic violence ("three broken ribs and internal bleeding"); she has since rebuilt her life and now owns the bar where she and María meet.
- Alejandro is described as a "romantic" who met Carolina's father three days after they met; Carolina uses this as the benchmark when advising María about Gabriel.
- A wiretap/informant scene confirms someone inside the station is reporting the unit's moves to a superior, specifically that they are about to visit Filiberto Guerra — this is established as an active surveillance/intelligence thread.

## S02E03

# Las azules — S02E03 Summary

## Plot

The episode opens with a flashback to 1968, ten days before the Mexico City Olympics. Members of the "Narciso Marxist Brigade" (Lola, Lucio, Enrique, Olga) chant against the games; Lola argues they should march anyway because the foreign press is in town. They pose for a brigade photo, then a title card reads "October 2nd" — the Tlatelolco massacre begins as shots ring out and someone screams "Grab Olga!"

In the present, an older María and her mother watch a televised confession by Filiberto Guerra, the former cop who hunted the survivors of the brigade. Chief Escobedo and Detective Herrera discuss burying Guerra's name and cop background; a city-wide manhunt is launched against the three remaining brigade survivors. Las azules (Gabina, Valentina, María, Chela) are pulled into the case: Gabina reveals her late father knew Guerra; Valentina objects to hunting activists as scapegoats.

Damián Ojeda and Lola Hoffman are publicly arrested by Detective Antonio Díaz. Inside the station, the women push for a softer approach; Lieutenant de la Torre and Officer Camacho, joined by Val, interview Lola, who confesses she's "glad he was killed" and confirms Filiberto had been hunting them one by one and that Val's team had found Olga to warn her. Lola gives up Enrique's address.

Enrique is found in a week-long coma at Santo Tomás Hospital, beaten by the "Halcones" while supporting Monterrey students. His mother Herminia confirms he and Damián were inseparable. Tox reports show Filiberto died of a sodium pentothal overdose — something a medical student like Damián could administer.

The stakeout at Damián's house goes wrong: María is pushed/attacked (she's shaken up but okay), Damián flees across a balcony, and escapes in a getaway car. A city-wide alert is issued but Damián remains at large.

The episode ends with Chief Escobedo on a live TV show boasting the case is closed. Mid-broadcast, a videotape arrives and is played on air: Filiberto's own confession naming his 28 years on the force, the bribes, the torture, the spying on communists, the killings in '68, the apartment and the church murders. Escobedo abruptly leaves the set. A final title card reads "POLICE LIEUTENANT," teasing the next case.

## S02E04

## Episode Summary: S02E04

### Plot
The episode intertwines the hunt for "The Executioner" (Damián Ojeda) with escalating personal crises. María and Valentina track the Portapak camera used to record Filiberto Guerra's confession to film director Gabriel Arizmendi. Damián contacts Valentina claiming innocence; his alibi is Elsa Palacios (Palacios de Vivanco), a married woman he met at a Book Fair. Elsa initially denies knowing him but later testifies at the station—revealing she is pregnant with Damián's child. Meanwhile, Customs Lieutenant Rómulo Quintanilla hijacks a live TV broadcast ("Mediodía con su Rorro") to confess systemic police corruption: stealing seized goods, splitting proceeds with bosses and gangs. The commander orders Damián taken to **Hotel Madrid** (an abandoned hotel used for torture) to force a confession. Gerardo and Gabriela intervene at gunpoint; Gerardo takes command and brings Damián to the station instead. María goes on a date with architect Octavio at a bowling alley; Alejandro confronts them, a fight breaks out, and María rejects Alejandro. The episode ends with Alejandro missing—María files a missing-person report.

- **Gerardo**: defies commander's illegal torture order, draws weapon on fellow officers to protect Damián's due process.
- **Elsa Palacios**: risks marriage, children, and safety to give formal statement exonerating Damián.

## S02E05 Summary

### Plot

The night before his daughter Lulú's quinceañera, Detective Plutarco Lozano (clean from gambling for three to four months) leaves home to finalize the party venue and wins big at the Jai Alai fronton. He never returns. His wife Esperanza calls María, who pulls the team together to search. Lozano's car and keys are found at the Jai Alai, and Chief Escobedo publicly declares Lozano was kidnapped by The Executioner (El Verdugo), the same person who killed Quintanilla and Guerra.

While the team searches, Escobedo forces the Women in Blue to shoot a national-TV marketing campaign. María is pulled off the set mid-take when her daughter Mar is rushed to a school disciplinary meeting: Mar dislocated a classmate's shoulder after the boy taunted her that the Executioner's next confession would name María. The principal pushes for expulsion; María and Alejandro negotiate a deal involving a family mediator.

Captain Romandía resigns after a personal incident involving María — Romandía was punched by María's estranged husband Alejandro after he saw María and Romandía together; Romandía's husband also discovered the situation, and Romandía blamed María for using her as cover for a date with architect Octavio. Escobedo takes direct control of assigning tasks and informs the Women in Blue they are now the face of the police marketing campaign.

María recruits Gabriel Arizmendi (the film director who sent her the premiere invitation) as a confidential source. Gabriel previously drove Lozano around during his night operations and can identify thirteen businesses Lozano extorted through protection rackets. The team sets up a covert investigation center in a park basement, using Val's car trunk as a research board, and traces Lozano's corruption to closures and land-use permits (17% of his closed cases, all outside his legal authority).

Lozano's body is recovered. Esperanza identifies him and breaks down in front of his family; Chela comforts Esperanza. Mar survives the school meeting; the family mediator agrees to recommend leniency. ### Plot

The episode opens at the premiere of Gabriel Arizmendi's film *Red Tide* at the Metropólitan Cinema. María conducts the interrogation using a psychological strategy: she needles Arizmendi about his fading box-office record and lack of public impact until he loses his temper, grabs her, and is formally arrested for assaulting a police officer. This arrest gives the team legal grounds to search his house.

During the search, police find a film-development lab and Lozano's belongings in Arizmendi's car trunk. Gerardo wants to close the case. María's team disagrees. Using the "Brighton method" (a UK comparative-profiling technique), they build two psychological profiles — one from the crime scenes, one from Arizmendi — and conclude they do not match. The crime-scene profile describes a cold, methodical, psychopathic personality who values control and anonymity. Arizmendi is a narcissist who craves the spotlight and cannot stay anonymous. A narcissist would never hide from public credit for the killings. The team formally presents their finding: Arizmendi is not The Executioner.

María also realizes during the interrogation that Arizmendi genuinely did not know why she was at the premiere — he did not send her the invitation. Someone else lured her there. The team re-examines the case and discovers that Eleazar, before working for Arizmendi, had a lengthy criminal record and spent three years at Lecumberri Prison. His lawyer and cellmate was Gregorio Cadenas — the same Cadenas quoted in the episode saying "The real criminals in our country are wearing a badge and a uniform" and describing the difference between cops and convicts as "the disguise they wear." The episode's final beat reveals the truth: Cadenas's motivation is not idealism but revenge.
