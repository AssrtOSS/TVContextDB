## Core Premise
**Season 1 (1971)**: The Mexico City government creates a female police corps (*las Azules*) as a public-relations stunt to distract from its failure to catch a serial killer dubbed the **Tlalpan Undresser** (*el Encuerador de Tlalpan*). Four recruits — a housewife, her younger sister, a police officer’s daughter, and an analytical loner — are denied real investigative roles and launch their own covert inquiry.

**Season 2 (post-June 1971 Corpus Christi massacre)**: María, now a lieutenant, leads the Azules into the murder of a student activist. The trail implicates the police itself and reaches back to the **1968 Tlatelolco massacre**. A shadowy figure executes vigilante justice against corrupt officers, forcing the Azules to choose between institutional loyalty and truth.

- **María ↔ Alejandro**: Marriage strained by María’s police work; Alejandro’s journalism parallels her investigation.
- **María ↔ Ángeles**: Professional respect → deep trust; complementary styles (intuition + analysis).
- **Gabina ↔ Valentina**: Close friends; class differences (Gabina police family, Valentina middle-class).
- **Gabina ↔ Gerardo**: Romance complicated by institutional loyalty.
- **María ↔ Romandía**: Antagonistic → grudging partnership; he represents male-dominated police hierarchy.
- **María ↔ Escobedo**: Direct superior; embodies institutional corruption / cover-up.
- **Azules as unit**: Solidarity forged by marginalization; each episode (S1) focuses on one woman’s perspective.

## World Setting & Cultural Context

- **Time**: 1971–1972 (S1: mid-1971; S2: late 1971 onward).
- **Place**: Mexico City (Ciudad de México / DF), primarily middle-class neighborhoods, police precincts, university areas, *vecindades*.
- **Political Backdrop**: Presidency of **Luis Echeverría** (1970–1976); “Dirty War” (*Guerra Sucia*) against student/leftist movements; state surveillance via DFS; media control (Zabludovsky’s televised news).
- **Historical Events Referenced**:
  - 1968 Tlatelolco massacre (pre-series trauma)
  - 1971 Corpus Christi massacre (S2 pivot)
  - 1968 Olympics (national prestige project)
- **Gender Politics**: Ultra-conservative norms; women barred from investigative police work; *Las Azules* created as token gesture. Themes: machismo, institutional sexism, female solidarity.
- **Police Culture**: Corrupt, hierarchical, male-dominated; *comandantes* protect power; detectives dismiss women’s leads.
- **Language Register**: 1970s Mexican Spanish — *usted* for superiors/strangers, *tú* among peers/family; police jargon (*expediente, sospechoso, declaración, cateo*); political rhetoric (*subversivo, agitador, orden público*). Avoid anachronistic slang.

## Translation Conventions for Subtitles

1. **Series Title**: Use **女警急先鋒** as main Chinese title; include *Las Azules / Women in Blue* in first-episode header if needed.
2. **Character Names**: Keep Spanish names in dialogue (María, Ángeles, Gabina, Valentina, Alejandro, Romandía, Escobedo, Gerardo, Goyo). Use Chinese in character lists / credits.
3. **Rank/Title**: Translate ranks (Teniente → 中尉, Comandante → 指挥官) but retain *Señor/Señora/Señorita* as 您/夫人/小姐 contextually.
4. **Historical Terms**: Use standard Chinese historiography (特拉特洛尔科大屠杀, 圣体节大屠杀, 肮脏战争).
5. **Period Vocabulary**: Avoid modern PRC police terms (e.g., 派出所 → use 警局/分局; 刑侦 → use 侦查/调查). Prefer 1970s HK/TW/overseas Chinese crime-drama lexicon where it matches Mexican context.
6. **Profanity / Slang**: *chingados, cabrón, pinche, no mames* → render force level, not literal (e.g., “他妈的”, “混蛋”, “去你的”, “别废话”).
7. **Songs / Poems / Radio**: Translate meaning; keep original lyric lines in italics if plot-relevant.
8. **On-screen Text**: Translate signs, documents, headlines (e.g., *EXPEDIENTE 1971/042* → 案件编号 1971/042).
