# Ghosts (US) / 鬼屋欢乐送 — Subtitle Translation Background

## Series Verification
- **Title**: *Ghosts* (American TV series) — 美版《鬼屋欢乐送》（亦译《古宅老友记》美版）
- **Format**: Single-camera fantasy sitcom
- **First air**: October 7, 2021 (CBS, back-to-back premiere)
- **Broadcaster**: CBS (US); Paramount+ (streaming); BBC Three / BBC iPlayer (UK, as "Ghosts US")
- **Seasons / Episodes** (as of Oct 2026):
  - S1: 18 eps (2021–22)
  - S2: 22 eps (2022–23)
  - S3: 10 eps (2024, delayed by strikes)
  - S4: 22 eps (2024–25)
  - S5: 22 eps (2025–26)
  - S6: ongoing (premiered Oct 29, 2026)
  - Total aired: 96+ episodes
- **Adaptation**: Developed by Joe Port & Joe Wiseman from BBC One *Ghosts* (2019) by Mathew Baynton, Simon Farnaby, et al.
- **Production**: CBS Studios / Lionsgate Television / BBC Studios LA Productions; filmed in Montreal, Quebec

## Overall Plot Premise
Samantha "Sam" Arondekar (freelance journalist) and Jayanth "Jay" Arondekar (chef) inherit Woodstone Manor, a sprawling Hudson Valley estate in Ulster County, New York, from Sam's great-aunt Sophie. They plan to convert it into a bed-and-breakfast, but the mansion is inhabited by an ensemble of ghosts—people who died on the property across centuries and are bound to the grounds until they resolve unfinished business and "move on" (colloquially "get sucked off"). After a near-death experience caused by the ghost Trevor moving a vase, Sam revives with the ability to see, hear, and interact with them. Jay cannot perceive the ghosts but accepts their existence through Sam. The series follows the daily comedy of cohabitation: the ghosts meddle in Sam and Jay's renovation and B&B venture, Sam fulfills the ghosts' requests to maintain peace, and the ensemble navigates cross-era culture clashes, romantic entanglements, and the mystery of each ghost's death and path to the afterlife.

## World Setting & Ghost Rules
- **Woodstone Manor**: Gilded Age mansion built by the Woodstone family (robber barons). Located in Hudson Valley, Ulster County, NY.
- **Ghost mechanics**:
  - Bound to the property; cannot leave without fading into oblivion (except Pete, who can roam but vanishes if away too long).
  - Invisible/intangible to living people; can phase through walls but not floors or ceilings.
  - Cannot eat, drink, or physically feel; require sleep.
  - Each ghost has a unique "ghost power" tied to their life or death (see Character table).
  - "Walking through" a ghost causes the ghost intense pain; the living feel cold/odor/hallucinations.
  - Ghosts do not age; appear as at moment of death (clothing, injuries).
  - "Getting sucked off" = ascending to the afterlife (sexual innuendo exploited by Trevor).
  - Living children can sometimes see/hear ghosts (e.g., young Hetty saw Thorfinn as imaginary friend "Gordon").
- **Basement cholera victims**: A large group of 19th-century cholera victims who live in the basement; serve as a Greek chorus / labor force; can give non-contagious cholera symptoms for 24 hrs to living they walk through.
- **Shed ghosts**: British Revolutionary War officers (Nigel Chessum, Baxter, Jenkins) occupying the former barracks.
- **Neighbors**: Henry & Margaret Farnsby (adjacent property, initially antagonistic, later allies; their house also haunted).
- **B&B arc**: Renovation → permit battles → soft opening → full operation (S4+); Jay opens a restaurant in a converted barn (S4).

## Key Relationships
- **Sam ↔ Jay**: Married couple; Sam is sole living medium; Jay supports via trust and geek-culture rapport with Pete.
- **Sam ↔ Ghosts**: Only living person who perceives them; negotiates "requests" for peaceful cohabitation; often acts as therapist/mediator.
- **Jay ↔ Ghosts**: Cannot see/hear; communicates via Sam; bonds with Pete over basketball, D&D, 80s pop culture; later runs barn restaurant.
- **Isaac ↔ Nigel**: Closeted gay officer + British Redcoat; multi-season romance; come-out arc assisted by Hetty.
- **Hetty ↔ Elias**: Loveless Gilded Age marriage; Elias cheated; Hetty suicided to protect son Thomas from arrest for Elias's crimes.
- **Hetty ↔ Alberta**: Class tension → grudging respect; Hetty learns she has Irish ancestry.
- **Thorfinn ↔ Flower**: "Viking and hippie" romance (S2+); cultural clash comedy.
- **Pete ↔ Alberta**: Pete's unrequited crush; later kiss (S4); complicated by Pete's loyalty to Carol.
- **Trevor ↔ Sam**: Initial attraction; evolves into protective "bro" dynamic; Trevor uses finger-power to help.
- **Sasappis ↔ Shiki**: Long-distance ghost romance via Sam's phone; dream-walking dates.
- **Basement ghosts ↔ Upstairs ghosts**: Class divide; basement ghosts do manual labor (repairs, cleaning); upstairs ghosts often dismiss them.
- **Farnsbys ↔ Arondekars**: Neighbors; Henry opposes B&B permit (S1) → becomes investor/ally (S2+).

## Cultural Context & Translation Considerations

1. **American historical periods**: Each ghost represents a distinct era — Viking exploration (c.1009), Lenape pre-colonial (1513), Revolutionary War (1777), Gilded Age (1890s), Prohibition/Jazz Age (1920s), Hippie/Cult (1960s), 1980s suburban, Dot-com/Wall Street (1990s). Translations should reflect era-appropriate references without over-annotating.

2. **Jewish coding (Trevor)**: Yiddishisms (oy vey, schmuck, meshugas, kvetch), Wharton/Lehman Brothers references, Jewish mourning customs (shiva, yahrzeit), bagel/lox jokes. Preserve flavor without caricature.

3. **Lenape representation (Sasappis, Shiki)**: Consult Lenape language resources for names/places; avoid "noble savage" tropes; Sasappis explicitly critiques colonial narratives. "Land acknowledgement" episode (S3) requires cultural sensitivity.

4. **LGBTQ+ arcs (Isaac/Nigel, Pete/Alberta tension)**: Isaac's closeted struggle and coming out are central; Nigel is British officer lover. Translate with respect — avoid outdated terms; "closeted" = "深柜/未出柜"; "coming out" = "出柜".

5. **Sexual innuendo ("get sucked off")**: Core running gag. Options: keep double entendre ("被吸走" works both ways), or separate meanings contextually. Subtitle space may favor "升天" for literal, "被吸了" for Trevor's jokes.

6. **Class dynamics**: Upstairs ghosts (wealthy, privileged) vs. basement ghosts (poor, immigrant, disease victims) mirrors US class/immigrant history. Hetty's anti-Irish prejudice → Irish ancestry reveal is key arc.

7. **Pop culture literacy**: Jay/Pete bond over D&D, Star Wars, Ghostbusters, Newhart, Shawshank. Trevor references 90s finance culture. Sam writes for "Ulster County Review." Keep references intact; add TNs only if essential.

8. **Supernatural mechanics as metaphor**: Ghost powers = personality extensions (Trevor's finger = manipulation; Flower's hallucinations = 60s drug culture; Hetty's St. Patrick's = suppressed identity; Sasappis's dreams = storytelling; Thorfinn's electricity = raw power; Pete's roaming = loyalty to family).

9. **Canadian filming / New York setting**: Montreal doubles for Hudson Valley; occasional French signage slips in. No translation impact but good to know.

10. **Adaptation from UK original**: Direct parallels (Captain→Isaac, Pat→Pete, Julian→Trevor, Lady Button→Hetty, Robin→Thorfinn, Mary→Alberta's Alexa power, Kitty→Flower/Sasappis as new). UK terms may appear in fan discourse; clarify "US version" vs "UK version" if needed.
