# Strictly Come Dancing — Background

## Title verification
- Exact title: **Strictly Come Dancing** (common short form: **Strictly**)
- Type: British live-action television dance contest / reality competition
- First aired: 15 May 2004 on BBC One (Series 1; all subsequent series have aired in autumn)
- Broadcaster / production: BBC One, produced by BBC Studios Entertainment Productions; filmed at BBC Television Centre (TC1) in 2004–2012, then at Elstree Studios (George Lucas Stage 2) from 2013 onward; live episodes from the Blackpool Tower Ballroom have recurred since 2009.
- Sister show: companion weekday show _Strictly Come Dancing: It Takes Two_ on BBC Two (hosted by Claudia Winkleman, then Zoe Ball, then Janette Manrara).
- Format exported to 60+ countries as _Dancing with the Stars_.
- A separate Mainland Chinese licence-version, produced by Hunan Television, also bears the Chinese title 舞动奇迹 (2007–2011). The two shows are distinct; this background concerns the UK original.

## Series / episode structure
- 24 regular series + 18 Christmas specials + 19 charity specials; 490 episodes total as of series 23.
- Each main series runs roughly from late September to mid-December and follows the rhythm: Launch Show (pairing reveal) → weekly Saturday-night live show (celebrity–pro couples perform one or two dances, scored 1–10 by each of four judges, maximum 40) → Sunday-night results show (recorded Saturday night, bottom two couples face the judges in a dance-off, eliminated couple revealed) → Grand Final live on a Saturday.
- Viewer phone / online votes are combined with judges' scores (1 point per rank position, summed with judges' ranks) to determine the bottom two on the results show. Judges then save one couple from the dance-off.
- The show holds the Guinness World Record for the world's most successful reality television format (2010).

## World and setting
- Strictly is staged in a purpose-built television ballroom on the BBC One lot; weekly Saturday shows are broadcast live to a UK primetime audience of typically 8–12 million viewers.
- Iconic locations include BBC Television Centre TC1 (2004–2012), Elstree Studios (2013–present), the Blackpool Tower Ballroom (annual Blackpool Week and selected finals since 2009), and the Wembley Arena (special themed weeks 2011–2012).
- Studio atmosphere combines live band, dedicated singers, glitter-ball trophy (the "Glitterball Trophy," 奖杯"水钻球/闪亮球"), on-screen scoreboard, and a judging desk; the dance floor sits in front of the audience.

## Plot premise (overall)
A fixed cohort of British and international celebrities (sportspeople, actors, broadcasters, comedians, politicians, athletes) is paired with professional ballroom and Latin dancers. Each week they learn and perform a different dance — drawn from the ballroom and Latin syllabus (Waltz, Foxtrot, Tango, Quickstep, Viennese Waltz, Cha-Cha-Cha, Samba, Rumba, Jive, Paso Doble), with occasional themed-week additions like the Salsa, Argentine Tango, Couple's Choice, and Charleston. Couples are progressively eliminated through combined judge and public-vote rankings plus a dance-off, culminating in a Grand Final where one celebrity–pro pair wins the Glitterball Trophy. There is no fixed fictional plot; each series is a self-contained annual tournament, with recurring annual traditions (Halloween Week, Blackpool Week, Movie Week, Musicals Week, Icons Week).

## Speaking style and cultural context for translation
- Hosts and judges use warm British primetime entertainment register: polite but playful, with cheeky innuendo (especially Bruce Forsyth in S1–11) and double entendres that subtitle cues should preserve without flattening.
- Judges' feedback is a fast-paced, character-driven mix of British ballroom jargon and personal catchphrases — keep each judge's voice distinct: Craig Revel Horwood is famously sharp withering ("a disaster", "dreadful", pronounced with Australian diction); Len Goodman is avuncular and uses "seven" gestures; Bruno Tonioli is exuberant and Italian-accented; Arlene Phillips is technical and direct; Alesha Dixon is warm and supportive; Darcey Bussell is technical and dancer-focused; Shirley Ballas is technical, anatomically precise; Motsi Mabuse is warm and emotional; Anton Du Beke is insider-pro perspective, fond of cosy British phrasing.
- The Sunday results show is the emotional centrepiece: contestants are regularly tearful, judges deliver verdicts, and Tess Daly / Claudia Winkleman / Emma Willis (S24+) close the show with the formal "lines are now closed" ritual.
- Cultural / national references likely to surface in subtitles: BBC Children in Need (for charity weeks), Royal Albert Hall, Blackpool Illuminations, British sports personalities (rugby, football, cricket, athletics), pantomime tradition, ballroom-dance heritage (Blackpool Tower Ballroom originally hosted Come Dancing from the 1950s–90s), Strictly Live arena tour, "Strictly fever" / "Strictly-mania" media coinages.
- Pre-recorded Sunday results show preserves the dance-off format; finals are broadcast live on Saturdays from Series 14 onward. Subtitle timing cues should expect applause, live band music, and judge deliberation pauses to fill dialogue gaps.
