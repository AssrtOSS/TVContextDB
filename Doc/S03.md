## S03E01

# Doc S03E01

## Plot
The episode opens with Amy Larsen's voiceover: eight years of memory lost to a car accident, occasional flashes, and new seizures that have her on the edge of losing her job. She has refused Gina Walker's offer of TMS, insisting "the past is just a distraction." A new brain scan reveals her old neural pathways are actively rewiring, which is what triggered the seizures in the first place. Gina and Dr. Scherl require daily neuro-cogs, biweekly fMRIs, and daily check-ins before clearing Amy to lead. Joan Ridley's death (between seasons) is the emotional backdrop: Michael Hamda, now Chief of Internal Medicine, opens intern orientation by framing the cohort as Joan's final legacy.

First-day assignments land two cases on Amy. **Case A — Torey Harris, 16:** grand-mal seizure during tech rehearsal of *Romeo and Juliet* (Juliet's suicide scene, gun prop). CT/MRI clean, labs clean, EEG shows diffuse delta-theta slowing. Torey seizes a second time in front of Amy and interns; Graham Barker mistakenly pulls the supply cart instead of the crash cart. **Case B — Mr. Galvin, 38, drama teacher at Torey's school:** chest pain, migraine, dizziness, arm numbness — Jake Heller runs an angio that comes back clean, then Galvin seizes focally. Sonya admits him.

Torey crashes: BP 210/112, HR 48, fixed left pupil — impending herniation. Imaging shows CVST (cerebral venous sinus thrombosis). Ben Grant places an extra-ventricular drain with heparin; Torey survives but is left with a now-chronic blood-clotting disorder requiring lifelong anti-seizure meds. While the OR runs, more students arrive from the theater (dizziness, hand numbness); Josh Fuller seizes in the waiting room. Amy diagnoses mass psychogenic illness (catatonic conversion), citing the Salem "witches" and the Iranian schoolgirls episode as historical analogues.

The case unravels via re-interview: students had a **lockdown / active-shooter drill** that morning. A girl named Heidi had a panic attack during a drill two months earlier; the video went viral. Galvin's choice to give Juliet a gun (for "cool and modern" staging) detonated Torey's accumulated drill trauma, surfacing her dormant clotting disorder. The remaining students' symptoms are conversion reactions amplified by the drama-club group chat.

## S03E02

# Doc S03E02 — Episode Summary

## Plot

The episode pivots on two parallel trauma cases from a deer-versus-car accident that brings the Evans family into Westside Hospital. Anthony Evans (48, father) is impaled through the chest by a deer antler and rushed to surgery under the new Chief of Surgery, Dr. Ben Grant. His wife Jen arrives with rhabdomyolysis, renal failure, and a subdural hematoma; she later develops stroke-like symptoms, fever, and a sudden inability to recognize her own son Theo. Their 12-year-old daughter Harper has a grade 4 liver lac from her seatbelt, a pelvic ring fracture, and DIC. Teenage son Theo, who was driving and just got his license, escaped with scrapes and a mild concussion but carries the guilt of the crash.

The medical crisis drives a confrontation between medicine and surgery. Ben operates on Anthony but massive transfusions push him into fluid overload and heart failure; Amy calls him into surgery mid-procedure to stop and fix the medical problem first. Ben places Anthony on VV ECMO and gives Amy a four-hour window to stabilize him. Amy identifies capillary leak syndrome (the elevated hemoglobin was falsely reassuring), and TJ improvises a plan to hook a dialysis machine into the ECMO circuit to pull fluid off while bypassing the failing kidneys — buying time for Ben to execute a sleeve-resection-style plan (remove the entire penetrated section of antler plus damaged vessels and airway, then re-anastomose artery and bronchus). The surgery succeeds despite an air-in-circuit crisis.

Harper, meanwhile, deteriorates after her liver repair — tachypneic, with a fat embolism confirmed by echo. Ruth catches the early sign, Dr. Fox performs the delayed ORIF on the pelvic ring, and Harper stabilizes. Jen is eventually diagnosed with Brucellosis from the infected deer's blood and started on broad-spectrum antibiotics; her memory loss is stroke-like but from the infection. Amy gets the family together with Anthony in recovery, and Theo finds genuine closure.

## S03E05

# Doc S03E05

## Plot

Three storylines run in parallel and collide at the end.

**Case 1 — Roxie Kevel, 11 (罗克西·凯韦尔).** Brought to Westside after an episode of acute confusion/disorientation during a family gathering. ER workup is clean (labs, imaging), so De Silva and Barker run an infectious-disease panel, vitamin/thyroid workup, then a lumbar puncture (needle angled slightly upward toward the umbilicus). The CSF comes back clear — cloudy would mean meningitis, viscous cancer, yellow a brain bleed — which rules things out without explaining anything. Amy's vaccine-history questions take the family down a path nobody wanted: no one they know had measles, until James reveals that cousin Hazel had measles at about two, shortly after the big family reunion at Leech Lake, and was never vaccinated. Hazel could have infected Roxie before her first birthday, and SSPE (亚急性硬化性全脑炎) is raised out loud. Everything else falls away in turn: toxoplasmosis (negative; the cat litterbox theory, Jocelyn's cat), Lafora disease (negative), anti-NMDA encephalitis (negative), cerebral angiogram (negative), cardio-embolic TIA (ruled out by clean valves, normal EKG, normal head CT). Amy's SSPE hypothesis becomes a diagnosis of exclusion, and a brain biopsy is scheduled; the episode ends on the lab call — the results are not the news the family wanted.

**Case 2 — Mason McCanty (梅森·麦坎蒂).** Swollen jaw, 102°F, warm to the touch after a "slipped barbell." Blood cultures, broad-spectrum IV antibiotics, CT: the infection has invaded bone and there are dozens of hairline fractures in a regular pattern. He did it to himself. The self-inflicted cause is a massage/percussion gun with the guard removed, used to "looksmax" the jaw — callus formation from blunt force, then a squarer jaw by Wolff's Law. Mason refuses surgery because a bone resection would scar his face, so TJ consults and offers needle aspiration plus a stronger IV antibiotic round (ampicillin-sulbactam) with 12-hour monitoring, and Amy takes an informed refusal. He then asks to see his own "research" and pitches looksmaxing as science. The infection spreads (crepitus, spreading erythema), he is finally told he has flesh-eating bacteria, and he consents. Surgery debrides the necrotic tissue; he loses half his jaw and is told reconstruction will take dozens of operations over one to two years.

**Personal threads.** Amy and Gina work out the sperm-donor plan for "our child" (Gina's eggs are viable despite a low ovarian reserve; Marcus, Gina's and Wendy's brother, is the donor — "uncle daddy"). Amy also surfaces a recovered memory of having consulted on a clinical trial four years ago and phoning the investigator with a question; she suspects her brain is trying to warn her, and Michael tells her to go forward, not back. Amy also finally recalls the night Danny died and the funeral.
