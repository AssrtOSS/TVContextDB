## S04E01

# Tulsa King S04E01 — Episode Summary

## Plot

The episode opens with Dwight Manfredi solidifying his legitimate empire in Tulsa while fending off threats from east-coast bosses. Multiple storylines run in parallel: a Montague bourbon distribution push goes nationwide, a sweetheart deal cuts Quiet Ray into the Northeast market, a car-insurance fraud scheme requires fresh DMV paperwork, an interloper named Santiago is muscling into Dwight's ecstasy territory, and Dwight prepares to attend a national mob summit in New York. The episode closes on a cliffhanger: Governor Cal Thresher's plane (carrying Margaret Devereaux) vanishes over the Pacific.

## Key Character Actions

- Dwight holds a "Friedman Enterprises" staff meeting, formally rebranding his crew and ordering everyone to go straight now that the Montague liquor license is active. He decides to deal with Quiet Ray via a profit split rather than violence.
- Dwight negotiates face-to-face with Quiet Ray in New York. Offer opens at 12.5% of Montague profits for Northeast distribution; settles at 25% with both sides agreeing to station "emissaries" at each other's operations. Dwight flatly refuses to let Ray move $150M of aged bourbon to a Jersey warehouse.
- Dwight and his girlfriend Tina ride horses at her ranch; Rudy the blind rescue rooster crows at 2:30 p.m. The two celebrate their "anniversary" (first meeting in a field in November) at Beppe restaurant.
- Governor Thresher signs the Fennario Ranch back to Dwight for $100, in exchange for Dwight flying with him to the Mariana Islands governor's conference.
- Bill Bevilaqua resurfaces after four weeks in federal custody ("Shits Carlton"); he warns Dwight repeatedly that Quiet Ray cannot be trusted.
- Tyson robs Santiago's stash at gunpoint with backup, recovering ecstasy from a safe.
- Mitch Keller walks Dwight through VINs of cars targeted for the swap scheme.
- Quiet Ray publicly announces he is taking over the Invernizzi family at the New York summit and rolls in Turco's severed head in a bowling bag to cow dissent.

## New Names and Terms

- Friedman Enterprises — anagram of "Manfredi"; Dwight's rebranded shell company.
- Montague Distillery — Dwight's legit bourbon brand, now federally licensed, distributing nationwide via Consolidated (west of PA, including AK/HI) and Mid-Atlantic (Eastern Seaboard).
- Bred-2-Buck Cabaret and Casino — Dwight's Tulsa venue, posting its best week ever.
- Even Higher Plane — Dwight's other venture, sales rise when temps drop.
- Mitch Keller's Car City — bankrupt, liquidated; inventory (~123 vehicles, including a 1975 Buick LeSabre convertible with 60K miles) being swapped with matched junkers for insurance burn-and-claim fraud.
- Beppe — Italian restaurant downtown, run by Leonardo Timpone.
- Leonardo Timpone — Beppe proprietor; treats Dwight as a VIP, gifts wine, asks Dwight to charge him only "wholesale."
- 1982 Tenuta delle Colline Velate Nebbia Royale — Piedmont vintage Leonardo delivers to the office; described as "wine royalty."
- Bradley Van Heusen — major donor pushing the AI data farm; Thresher refuses to take farmland for it.
- Margaret Devereaux — Lt. Governor (formerly "Ms. Devereaux," now named in dialogue); runs Thresher's daily office and eyes the top job.
- Amanda Clark — a second Lt. Governor figure introduced at the office; refuses to shake Dwight's hand, citing her oath to uphold state law.
- Wyatt Nix — Thresher's attorney who drafted the ranch transfer.
- Eva — Thresher's aide.
- Paul — DMV insider, reluctant to issue blank Oklahoma car registrations; price escalates from $500 to $1,000 per registration ($150K total for 150 blanks) once Dwight leans on him.
- Bigfoot, Spencer, Joanne — Dwight's crew.
- Tina — Dwight's girlfriend, owns the ranch with Rudy the rooster.
- Diego — Dwight's chemistry contact; still making X ("easier than blueberry muffins") and pesters Dwight to keep baking.
- Tyson — Dwight's enforcer; confronts Santiago at the dance club and later leads the warehouse robbery.
- Santiago — young interloper selling ecstasy on Dwight's turf; backed by older figures but has no heavy muscle.
- W.W.D.D. ("What Would Dwight Do") — Tyson's mantra for deciding how to handle Santiago; conclusion: tax him, don't rob him.
- Turco — Ray's new attack dog, just out of Ossining after 10 years for assault; ex–Canadian mob (Toronto/Montreal). He brings Luca Fiorentino's head in a bowling bag to the summit.
- Vince Antonacci — Invernizzi capo who now reports directly to Quiet Ray.
- Santo Accursio (Louisiana), Dario DeSica (New England), Tommy Abramo (Chicago), Bartomo Alfieri (Cleveland), Frank Zonghi (Vegas), Carmine Yanni (Jersey), Bill Bevilaqua (Kansas City) — regional bosses at the summit.
- Bella, "Ol' Blue Eyes," "Shits Carlton," "Stormtroopers" — slang/aliases deployed for Dwight, federal holding, and the agents who grabbed Bill.
- In the cold open: a flashback voiceover recaps that Dwight did 25 years, "didn't rat, didn't learn," moved west after release and kept killing — bikers, Chickie, and an unspecified spree tied to the Invernizzi line.

## Continuity-Relevant Facts

- Dwight now holds a federal liquor license and a legal bourbon brand; he is publicly pivoting to clean operations, ordering his crew to "be as law-abiding as possible."
- The Invernizzi family is functionally dead: Pete Invernizzi has passed, Chickie is "no longer with us," and Ray has assumed the remaining operation with Vince Antonacci as capo.
- Dwight has formally declared himself head of the "Manfredi family from Tulsa," independent of any Invernizzi obligation.
- Dwight's net worth / scale: $150M of aged bourbon sitting in Tulsa; 7.5-point stake Bill Bevilaqua insists is "worth a lot someday."
- The Fennario Ranch is officially back in Dwight's hands for $100, signed Wednesday with Wyatt Nix as counsel.
- A regional ecstasy war has begun: Santiago is pushing product on Dwight's turf; Dwight's preferred response is taxation, not robbery.
- The car-insurance fraud requires 150 blank Oklahoma car registrations — Paul has now agreed after the price climbed to $1,000 each.
- Bill Bevilaqua was detained by federal agents for four weeks right after leaving Dwight's house, signaling federal heat on the Tulsa operation.

## Unresolved Threads

- Quiet Ray's emissary in Tulsa vs. Dwight's emissary in the Northeast — terms agreed but personnel not named.
- The $150M bourbon stockpile: Dwight refused to let it leave Tulsa, but the underlying dispute with Ray is unsettled.

- Mitch Keller's car swap: 123 vehicles to source, scheme only partly detailed.
- Diego and Bodhi's unresolved begging for Dwight to bake ecstasy despite the new legal pivot.

## S04E03

# Tulsa King S04E03 Summary

## Episode Plot

Dwight's Montague 50 bourbon shipment heads to New York, but the celebration is short-lived. The State Fire Marshal revokes the $3M insurance payout on Mitch's Auto City, and the Oklahoma ABLE Commission raids the distillery, claiming lead contamination from 50-year-old barrel rivets — halting all sales and impounding trucks. Simultaneously, Bodhi's dispensary "Even Higher Plane" is shuttered by OMMA over a property-line measurement technicality (1,190.3 ft building-to-building, but under 1,000 ft property-line-to-property-line), with all inventory seized. Dwight confronts Governor Clark at a $70K/plate fundraiser ("Wranglers of Hope"), publicly accusing her of targeted harassment; she brands him a "felon" and "blight on Oklahoma." Meanwhile, Tyson and Goodie robbed ecstasy dealer "English Dan" (20K pills, $400K), Tyson was shot at, and Dwight orders them to eliminate Dan. The episode ends with Dwight threatening New York boss Ray (Bevilaqua's contact) who demands his 25% despite the bourbon being destroyed.

## Important Character Actions

- **Dwight**: Ships Montague 50; buys thoroughbred Curule (10% of winnings); publicly confronts Governor Clark at fundraiser; threatens Ray with violence if threatened again.
- **Joanne**: Handles ABLE inspection; books are clean but barrels flagged for lead; trucks stopped, sales prohibited.
- **Tyson & Goodie**: Robbed English Dan (ecstasy); Tyson shot at; Dwight orders them to find and "take care of" Dan.
- **Bodhi**: Dispensary shut down by OMMA; entire inventory + safe seized; measurement dispute (property line vs building).
- **Mitch**: Catches ranch hand double-bunking horses; fires him with Dwight's guidance ("he's firing himself").
- **Goodie**: Wins Luger in coin flip; tries to pawn it for $20K; leaves it outside gym; Dwight reclaims it for the hit on English Dan.
- **Governor Clark / Maya Gordon (CoS)**: Orchestrates coordinated shutdowns (liquor, dispensary, casino licenses via "fraudulent" Jimmy the Creek); refuses meeting; fundraiser honors Bradley Van Heusen (Echelon Harvest Technologies).
- **Ray (NY boss)**: Demands 25% of bourbon profits; threatens to send enforcers; Dwight refuses, threatens to "pull you apart piece by fucking piece."

## S04E06

# Tulsa King — S04E06 Summary

## Episode plot
Dwight Manfredi juggles several crises in Tulsa. New enforcer Turco, called "the grim fucking reaper," arrives sent by Quiet Ray to oversee Vince's operations, demands 25% of Dwight's action (minimum $5,000/week), and warns that disrespect is unforgivable — he recounts killing and dismembering the Mayo brothers and dumping the remains at Brooklyn's Fountain Avenue Landfill. Dwight rejects the extortion.

Former Governor Thresher's brand-new jet crashed into the Pacific after a sudden loss of cabin pressure; the NTSB calls it mechanical failure. Dwight doesn't believe it and pressures a federal contact for the maintenance records, which show a clean inspection nine days prior and pressurization tested five weeks prior — but the plane's logbook is down with the wreckage in the deepest part of the Pacific. Dwight suspects a sabotaged mechanic. The crash is revealed to have killed not only Thresher but also Margaret Devereaux, Dwight's girlfriend and Lt. Governor, who was "the other passenger."

At Fennario Ranch, state appraiser Bradley Van Heusen (chairman of Echelon Harvest Technologies) presses eminent domain, revealing the ranch once belonged to Margaret Devereaux and was handed to him by the governor Dwight helped install. The real motive: AI data centers need unlimited water, power, and server acreage. Governor Clark (whom Dwight put in office) confronts Van Heusen, confirms Margaret was Dwight's girlfriend, and refuses to be cowed.

Dwight's Racket Club plans advance: a $100K buy-in Texas Hold'em game for 25 high rollers at the distillery, with a bar, cigar lounge, custom casino-grade chips, and food. Meanwhile Bodhi impulsively spent $2.2 million ($2.6M including commission to Loucadoux) on 276 bottles of "the finest wine France never produced" — a fake-wine scam — at the livestreamed auction. The wine is destined for the distillery game.

A fake Atlanta rap producer, "Liquid Rick" (Blunt Force Records), contacts Tyson through the auction livestream, likes his music, and offers a record deal, demanding Tyson come to Buckhead, Atlanta within 48 hours. Dwight makes Bodhi Tyson's manager and brings Grace along as the "white girl" in the entourage; Tyson promises not to sign anything unless Dwight sees it first.

Racehorse Curule (racing as "Rubric" per Racing Form) is entered Saturday at Anadarko Downs with a $200K purse; at five-to-one odds the crew plots to "arrange" the trifecta by having jockey Marco bring other jockeys to hold back the front-runners.

The episode ends with the Crown Vic used to shoot Bigfoot turning up in a Kansas City abandoned-mall parking garage (fingerprints due in 48 hours). Dwight suspects a wedge being driven between him and Quiet Ray, and orders Nelson to steal another Crown Vic disguised as an unmarked cop car — then reveals he knows Nelson never burned the first car.

## Important character actions
- Dwight: rejects Turco's extortion; pursues the truth behind Thresher's plane crash; confronts Van Heusen and Governor Clark over Margaret's ranch; arranges Tyson's Atlanta trip; organizes the poker game and race fix; pressures Nelson over the Crown Vic.
- Turco: extorts Dwight; tells the Mayo brothers murder story; demands 25%/$5K weekly.
- Bodhi: blows $2.2M on fake wine; becomes Tyson's manager.
- Tyson: receives the Liquid Rick record-deal offer; impulsive spender.
- Van Heusen: runs eminent domain on Fennario; claims Margaret Devereaux was "a very special woman"; exposed by the governor.
- Governor Clark: rebuffs Van Heusen; confirms Margaret was Dwight's girlfriend; won't be intimidated by Dwight.
- Nelson: hired to steal another Crown Vic; caught lying about burning the first.
- Grace: joins Dwight's Atlanta entourage as the "white girl."
