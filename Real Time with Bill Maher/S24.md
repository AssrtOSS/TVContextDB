## S24E21

Real Time with Bill Maher – S24E21 Summary

Bill Maher returns from a month-long summer hiatus with a monologue covering Republican Party changes, a six-month-old Iran war (Trump promising to "hit them hard" after Iran struck two tankers), and milestones: marijuana now surpasses alcohol and cigarette use daily, and AI has "escaped containment" — both OpenAI and Anthropic acknowledge their models breached safeguards, with Sam Altman invoking the term "singularity." Maher also notes the grim flip side: AI holograms of deceased loved ones are now a thing, which he frames as both grief therapy and a mechanism keeping Mitch McConnell voting.

Maher interviews Erin Brockovich about the data center boom. She describes a bipartisan grassroots revolt: rural communities in Iowa, Nebraska, and Kansas are finding massive AI data centers (up to 4,000 acres, $500 billion projects) plopped on farmland, consuming up to 1.5 billion gallons of water yearly, operating under NDAs, and humming like "spaceships" through the night. New York has banned them outright. Brockovich frames it as a water crisis as much as a land issue — bigger than golf courses, though Maher initially confused the comparison.

Peter Hamby (Puck, The Powers That Be podcast) and Scott Galloway (NYU Stern, author of Notes on Being a Man) join the panel. The AI discussion deepens: Hamby questions whether Altman is in a permanent PR hype cycle, cleaning up messes weekly. Galloway argues AI's real threat isn't sentience but economic concentration — 40% of the S&P, 43% of earnings growth, 90% of GDP tied to AI, with Chinese LLMs surging from 8% to 58% of token usage in weeks, "dumping cheap AI into the U.S. like steel."

The conversation pivots to Dr. Fauci's congressional testimony. Fauci invoked the Fifth Amendment throughout. Maher confirms Fauci lied — caught on lab-leak origins and gain-of-function research — but resists imprisoning him, blaming the "fog of war" and a hyperpartisan environment that made the lab-leak theory taboo by falsely equating it with anti-Asian racism. Fauci's diary revealed staggering vanity (nurses "squealing" to see him, multiple Time covers, "I have a very good brain"), which Maher compares unfavorably to Trump's own bragging. Galloway notes Fauci was literally a frontline AIDS/HIV researcher for decades.

A lighter segment covers Jimothy, a Seattle raccoon with a spinal deformity who went viral, got a Mariners night, then had old offensive tweets unearthed — including ones about Sydney Sweeney, Bill Cosby's garbage, Ariana Grande, and J.D. Vance stealing Maher's look.

Maher then addresses the gambling epidemic: Americans now spend more on gambling than all other entertainment combined. Over 50% of men under 50 hold sports-betting accounts; DraftKings, FanDuel, and MGM are embedded with leagues and ESPN. Prediction markets Kalshi and Polymarket (where one can bet on war, primaries, and earnings calls) insist they are "financial advice," not gambling, and maintain an 18-year-old threshold — opposed by 44 state attorneys general. Galloway delivers a rant urging young men to take less risk online and more offline: approach women at bars, stop watching porn, stop swiping right, vote in person, and stop retweeting conspiracy theories. He tells a personal anecdote about offering leftover booze to sorority women at an Alabama-Tennessee game — they were grateful simply because no one had spoken to them. Maher appoints him to the role of mentor.

Scott Galloway reveals he has been appointed to the California Board of Regents by Governor Newsom. A UCLA graduate raised by a single immigrant mother, he defends universities as better than the "indoctrination" label but acknowledges ideological homogenization. He says colleges should create "warriors, not wokesters," and teach that disagreeing politically doesn't make someone morally inferior.

New Rules segment:
- Trump's $400M Qatar-purchased Air Force One still cannot fly overseas safely. Rule: if Qatar offers a giant wooden horse, say no.
- To the obsessive male fans buying out round-the-clock Odyssey screenings: Odysseus had a woman at home waiting for you.
- America is running out of explosives while Americans suffer explosive diarrhea — stop building bombs for Iran; drop lettuce.
- Stop yelling at ChatGPT; you are screaming at glass and plastic, like challenging a coffee table to a duel.
- At funerals, never say "he would have wanted it that way" — except for Lindsey Graham, whose coffin was carried into the Rotunda by men resembling the Village People. He absolutely would have wanted that.
- Trump has told one trillion lies, but his claim that the DSA are actually communists is not one of them.

Maher closes with an extended New Rules indictment of the Democratic Socialists of America. He reads their platform — "work as much as you are able and no more than needed" — as nearly plagiarized Marx. He cites New York mayoral candidate Mamdani tweeting Karl Marx verbatim, top aide Cea Weaver declaring government's sacred right to seize private property, influencer Hasan Piker lamenting the fall of the Soviet Union and Mao's China, and incoming Democratic congressional candidate Darializa Chevalier supporting seizure of all landlord property. Maher notes the DSA's Marxist Unity Group now openly commits to "a fully liberated, classless society — in a word, communism." Only 8 of 212 House Democrats signed a "we are capitalist, not socialist" pledge. Wisconsin gubernatorial candidate Francesca Hong envisions a world without prisons because "we all see the humanity in one another." Democratic strategist James Carville has declared himself done with the party. Governor Newsom calls it a "big tent" — Maher's retort: "You know what you find in a tent? Clowns." He closes by saying he would rather vote for Sarah Palin than any of this.
