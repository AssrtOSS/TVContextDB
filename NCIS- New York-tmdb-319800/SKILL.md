- This entry covers the current CBS series of this title — premiered October 6, 2026, currently in Season 1. It is NOT the unrelated 2004–2013 CBS forensic-police drama "CSI: NY" (zh: 犯罪现场调查：纽约 / CSI犯罪现场：纽约), and not the 2026 film or any other show whose title merely contains the words "NCIS" and "New York". ## Overall premise
NCIS Special Agent Sam Hanna — a former U.S. Navy SEAL, formerly second-in-command of the NCIS Office of Special Projects in Los Angeles — returns to his hometown of New York City to lead a new NCIS field-office team. The unit runs high-stakes missions to defend New York as one of the world's most vital cities and ports. The tone, per LL Cool J's own description, leans on sarcasm, banter, witty repartee, and a distinct New York flavor — not a dry lab-procedural.

## Relationships (per current canon)
- Sam Hanna ↔ Nick Schaeffer: partners; Sam is the steady ex-SEAL lead, Nick is the brash new partner.
- Robyn Wells: their boss (ASAC), running the field office.
- Addy Ross, Wyatt Sellers: fellow NCIS Special Agents on the team.
- Sean Sullivan: technical support / ops specialist on the team.
- Cross-show bridge: Sam Hanna originated in NCIS: Los Angeles; his former partner G. Callen, boss Hetty Lange, and team K-Sensey are part of his backstory. Do not introduce characters from CSI: NY or any other "NY" series.

## World setting
- Contemporary New York City; primary setting is the NCIS New York field office, with missions centered on the city and its harbor (port-security framing).
- NCIS operates as a U.S. federal law-enforcement agency under the Department of the Navy, investigating crimes involving Navy and Marine Corps personnel and matters touching naval/Maritime interests in the NYC area.
- The show sits inside the wider NCIS franchise universe (parent show NCIS; LA spinoff; NCIS: Hawaiʻi, etc.), so franchise-internal references and crossovers are possible but should not invent connections that are not on screen.

## Speaking style & tone
- Procedural dialogue with sharp banter: short, punchy lines; frequent one-liners; New York-flavored wisecracks.
- Nick Schaeffer is written as the "roguish" half of the partnership, so his register is more irreverent and street-smart.
- Sam Hanna carries over his LA-cadence: calmer, military-briefing-style, occasional Arabic / cultural-context asides reflecting his SEAL / Middle-East background.
- ASAC Wells speaks in measured command-bridge tone.
- Heavy use of jargon, ranks, agency acronyms (NCIS, OSP, ASAC, SAC, SEAL, NYPD, FBI, DHS). On first spoken mention in an episode, expand acronym once in subtitle text where screen-readable; after that, use the established Chinese shorthand.
- Profanity and military slang: render at the same intensity; do not soften or escalate.

## Cultural context notes for translators
- 911 in this franchise is the emergency number, not the September-2001 attacks reference used in CSI: NY. Translate as 911 (报警电话) when meaning "call 911"; the September-11 reference does not apply to this show.
- New York City geography: Manhattan, Brooklyn, Queens, Bronx, Staten Island; the Battery / harbor areas for port-security framing. Use established Simplified-Chinese place names (e.g., 曼哈顿, 布鲁克林, 皇后区, 布朗克斯, 史泰登岛).
- Navy / military: keep rank translations standard: 海军 (Navy), 海军陆战队 (Marines), 海豹突击队 (SEALs), 上尉 / 中尉 / 少校 etc. as appropriate to rank stated in dialogue.

## Translation conventions for this title
- Show title: 海军罪案调查处：纽约 (Simplified) / 重返犯罪现场：纽约 (Traditional, Taiwan) — do not translate 零.
- Character names: keep the established Chinese form across the whole season. For names without a fixed Chinese rendering, use a single surname-first transliteration with a middle dot (·) and keep it consistent within the episode and across episodes.
- First-mention acronyms: keep English once on screen, with Chinese gloss in parentheses when subtitle space allows; thereafter, use the Chinese shorthand.
