## S20E01

# Murdoch Mysteries — S20E01 Recap

## Plot

The episode splits into two parallel investigations.

*Malta (Murdoch's storyline):* While visiting Julia at her London hospital, Murdoch learns that the Maltese authorities have arrested a woman he previously suspected of murder — Nigella Fletcher, accused of killing her husband John Mahler. He travels to Malta, where Deputy Constable Frederick Attard (with his falcon Alphonse, his "eye in the sky") greets him. Fletcher has already been convicted by magistrates and awaits hanging; she was found with the knife in her hand beside Mahler's body. She insists two intruders were beating her husband and that she was knocked unconscious and framed.

Mahler had purchased a priceless tapestry once belonging to the Knights of St. John (Knights Hospitaller). Fletcher claims it is a treasure map to riches Napoleon's army stole from the knights and never managed to ship to France. Inspector Oscar Lacroix, who has already declared the case closed, openly resents Murdoch's presence.

Murdoch finds a broken lock on Fletcher's door (why would she break into her own home?), and Fletcher soon escapes her cell — a young woman had visited her earlier, and the guard had been removed. Murdoch tracks Fletcher to her house; rather than recapture her, he agrees to help her find the real killer. Fletcher reveals one of the attackers wore a Knights of St. John cross.

A dying man (her gardener/associate) gasps "Napoleon" before dying, telling Murdoch the tapestry "belongs to us" — implying one faction of knights. Following historical clues in a book titled *Pillaging of Malta*, they trace Napoleon's route through the forts and into a hill cave. Lacroix confronts them inside: he is the killer, descended from one of Napoleon's officers (his great-grandfather served in the army). He murdered Mahler and his Maltese accomplice ("a Maltese dog… my ancestors defeated his") because the accomplice wanted to return the treasure to the Knights. Lacroix draws on Murdoch, but Alphonse the falcon attacks him, allowing Murdoch to disarm him.

Fletcher punches Lacroix — "That's for John!" — and they discover the buried horde of Napoleonic loot. Lacroix is taken into custody by Attard.

*Toronto (Choi's storyline):* Chief Constable Brackenreid finds Albert Choi locked in his office, not at his desk. Brackenreid confides to Dr. Violet Hart that Choi hasn't been himself since being shot by his own father. Hart urges him to get back on the horse. Choi's partner Vivian visits the pub to bring him lunch and reveals two men have come to her shop demanding protection money. Brackenreid orders Constables Higgins and Roberts (Watson and Teddy) to patrol the area; they check in on Miss Moon, whose shop is also being shaken down.

Two extortionists threaten to "teach her a lesson." Choi arrives, announces himself as "Albert Choi, Toronto Constabulary," and — unarmed — subdues both men to protect Miss Moon. Brackenreid congratulates him: "Very strong work, Inspector."

## S20E02

# S20E02 — Plot Summary

## Plot

The episode opens with a brief French-language chase scene, then cuts to Murdoch having breakfast and telling his son Harry they have been summoned to meet Father Matthew, the chaplain at Harry's school (St. Eligius).

Three French Catholic priests — Fathers Jacques Renaud, René Guillard, and Gauthier — are found shot dead at point-blank range in a forced-entry at the Royal Birch Hotel, registered under the alias "John Smith." Their room holds a Toronto map, floor plans of St. Adeline's Cathedral (589 Sumach), a copy of *The Confessions of St. Augustine*, and an ivory carving of the Three Marys. A young Frenchwoman, Mademoiselle Celeste Riveaux (21), travelling with them, is missing. The cathedral is preparing to consecrate the Holy Thorns Reliquary — three thorns from Jesus's Crown of Thorns in a jewel-covered reliquary, recently gifted by the Pope.

Bullets are traced to Bodeo revolvers (Italian Army standard); a witness saw two men in black enter the hotel around 4 a.m. Choi volunteers for night watch at St. Adeline's; a break-in during the watch reveals a hidden compartment in the reliquary. Father John (rector) explains the relic's provenance — Jerusalem → Romans → Visigoths → Knights Templar → chapel at Rennes-le-Château (France) → stolen ~20 years ago → sent anonymously to the Pope → gifted to the Canadian congregation — and reveals the existence of **Verus Dei**, a Rome-based secret Catholic order renounced by the Pope a century ago, accused of extortion, torture, and blood rituals, who see themselves as protectors of papal authority.

Dr. Violet Hart finds Celeste in the morgue searching the priests' belongings; under gunfire, Hart fights off an alley attacker. Celeste says the priests (members of the Fraternité de Madeleine) died protecting her; she was placed in a convent as a child and warned of Verus Dei. She had already taken a coded scroll from the reliquary.

Murdoch and Harry crack the scroll using a Cardan grille (the Three Marys ivory carving as the mask). Harry notices the cipher pattern from a newspaper "Word-Cross" puzzle. The decoded text claims to be Mary Magdalene's testament: she was taken to Gaul after the crucifixion and bore Jesus's child — dated 42 AD, encoded 1527. The reverse is a family tree ending with Celeste's grandfather Alphonse and father Sylvain Riveaux. Conclusion: Celeste is the last living descendant of Mary Magdalene and Jesus Christ.

Harry takes Celeste to a moving picture; she vanishes. Murdoch tracks her to St. Adeline's hospice, where Father Matthew — Harry's school chaplain and Murdoch's old friend — has kidnapped her to force a healing miracle for his dying sister Theresa. Celeste utters the words; Theresa rises from her sick bed. Matthew is arrested; he reveals his Verus Dei oath and that the order has tried to end the secret for 2,000 years. Celeste decides to take the scroll to the Pope in Rome, escorted by the Fraternité.

## S20E03

# Murdoch Mysteries S20E03 — Summary

## Plot

The episode opens on a heavyweight boxing match in Toronto headlined by **Jack Johnson**, the Black world champion. After Johnson easily defeats challenger St. James, his longtime friend and driver **Aaron Willard** is found dead inside the locked luggage compartment of Johnson's automobile — head trauma, hands bound, blood pattern indicating he was killed elsewhere and placed in the trunk.

Murdoch, Brackenreid, and Hart initially build a circumstantial case against Johnson: Willard was last seen heading to meet Johnson; Johnson is a fugitive wanted in the U.S. under the **Mann Act** (crossing state lines with a white woman); and forensic evidence shows a single massive blow with leather/horseshair embedded in the skull — consistent with a gloved fist.

Murdoch's **"Strike-Force-Measuring Device"** (a steampunk force gauge calibrated to the wound) proves no human punch could inflict the damage — the killer wrapped a blunt instrument in a boxing glove to frame Johnson. Willard actually died of **chloroform overdose**, not the head wound.

Two seemingly unrelated deaths run parallel:
- **George Hughes**, a young man training with his cousin Paul (a "physical culturist"), dies after consuming caribou-antler velvet preserved in **formaldehyde** — sold as a strength tonic.
- **Mrs. F. Cameron-Falconet** (Lucille Johnson's mother) arrives in Toronto and hires three thugs to attack Johnson, claiming he kidnapped her white daughter.

Willard had hidden Johnson's **$20,000** cash in the lining of Johnson's fur coat to prevent police seizure. The actual killer is **Emmett Spencer**, Johnson's white business manager, who killed Willard with chloroform and a tripod-wrapped-in-a-glove to extract the money's location. When Johnson wears the coat on stage and is shot (by **Sam Langford**, a rival Black boxer who simply wanted his shot at the title), the sewn-in bills stop a .22-caliber bullet, saving Johnson's life and exposing the hidden cash.

Spencer is arrested at the Starbright; Langford is cleared and released. Johnson and Lucille depart for Montreal en route to Paris.

## S20E05

## Plot

*Camille Gauthier*, a worker at the Ambrose candy factory, is stopped by morality officer Miss Berger outside the factory after hours. A gunshot rings out; factory owner **Godfrey Ambrose** is found shot, carrying the week's receipts (over $1,000) to the bank on his usual Friday run. A carriage blocked the sightline into the alley; a car was idling there; a news photographer working a story on factory conditions believes he captured the gunman moments before the shot. Berger charges Camille with vagrancy.

**Effie Newsome**, a Crown Attorney, is assigned as defence — Camille's first encounter with a female lawyer. Mid-hearing the judge suspends the case and dismisses Effie: the details are "not fit for a lady's ears." Brackenreid and Effie conceive a remedy — a **women's court**, with Effie as magistrate, the first female judge in Canada (and, per Violet Hart, the first in the British Empire).

Murdoch restores the over-exposed photograph with **ferricyanide (Farmer's Reducer)** plus a contrast filter, revealing the masked gunman; Henry notices a **repaired wheel spoke** on the photographed automobile, which leads to a mechanic's logbook, a licence number, and a used-auto lot — the car was bought "last week" in Godfrey Ambrose's name.

The women's court opens on Crown v. Gauthier, but the Crown declines to proceed and instead presents a court order from **Judge Calder of the Superior Court** closing the women's court: under the **British North America Act**, holding elected office or sitting as a judge requires being a "qualified person," and the Act consistently says "he" — so women are not persons.

Meanwhile the abduction is solved: soil analysis (fresh clay + silica dust from brick-forming) and fresh tyre tracks off-road in the Don Valley lead to a shack near the Brick Works where Ambrose is found, struck with a gun that discharged by accident. Mrs. Ambrose then confesses — she bought the car in her husband's name, supplied the jeweller's receipt, hired the gunman, and staged the kidnapping so her husband would be deemed to have "abandoned" her. Her first divorce petition (a year earlier) died for lack of evidence; her second, filed last week, rides on the Detective's own police report confirming infidelity and flight from Toronto. No one was shot; the "blood" and the shot were staging.

Outside the courthouse a man attempts to run Effie down; he is taken in. The court is adjourned for fears of a weapon in the crowd, and the **Supreme Court of Ontario** upholds the "male person" reading of the Act. Effie refuses to quit, and Louise and the Detective argue the loss itself has made a change.

- **Effie Newsome**: defends Camille; is dismissed from the case by the judge for being a woman; founds and judges the women's court; is barred from it by Calder's order; quits the Crown's service, takes on a six-lawyer office and files a lawsuit before the Supreme Court of Ontario; narrowly survives a vehicle attack; continues the fight afterward.
- **Detective Murdoch** (the subtitles credit only "Detective"): restores the photograph with ferricyanide and a contrast filter; spots the repaired spoke; interrogates Camille in the cell and reassures her of confidentiality; acts as **character witness** for her; writes the police report on Godfrey Ambrose's infidelity and flight, which Mrs. Ambrose then submits in her divorce petition; confronts Mrs. Ambrose with the kidnapping; shields Effie after the attack; delivers the law-versus-conscience monologue.
- **Miss Berger** (morality officer, 风化警员): charges Camille with vagrancy; later supports Effie's women's court ("the courts should allow women to be judged and defended by women"); presses the Detective on the sentencing double standard — six months at the Mercer Reformatory and life confinement for a woman with no family to be remanded to, versus a fine for a man.
- **Mrs. Ambrose**: took over her father's failing candy business, consolidated its debts and saved it; purchased the automobile in her husband's name, hired the gunman, and staged the kidnapping to force a divorce and gain control of the company.
- **Godfrey Ambrose**: clears himself as the staged victim; was knocked down by the hired man (whose gun fired by accident); has quit the job and offered an upstairs room for the new legal office.

## S20E07

## Murdoch Mysteries S20E07 — Episode Summary

### Plot
A "tango tea" at the Starbright Theatre brings the famous tango duo **Felicity and Aldo Luna** to Toronto. During a costume change mid-performance, Felicity returns to stage and collapses—stabbed through the heart with an unusually long, ornate **facón** (Argentinian utility knife). The murder occurs in a locked-room scenario: Felicity was offstage for less than ten seconds, the backstage door was unlocked, and no one saw the killer enter or flee.

Murdoch, Crabtree, and Higgins investigate. The knife's sheath bears a distinctive jagged silhouette matching **Mount Fitz Roy in Patagonia**, confirming Argentine origin and pointing to Aldo Luna. Aldo admits the knife is his but denies the murder; a timed re-enactment proves he couldn't have left the stage, killed Felicity, and returned in the available window.

Parallel threads emerge:
- **Woodrow Lawton**, a Pentecostal extremist who condemns tango as "the Devil's music," is caught scratching all the gramophone records backstage. He confesses to vandalism but not murder.
- **Effie Newsome**, a tango student and former Crown Attorney lawyer, arrives in Toronto and forms an instant chemistry with Aldo. She later becomes a shooting target in Aldo's hotel room.
- **Minnie Simpkin** (stage manager) reveals Felicity called the **Tipsy Ferret** (a bar closed the previous year) the night before the murder.
- **Miss Baker**, an ambitious student, is cleared by her neighbour's alibi.

The breakthrough comes when **Miss Hart** spots inconsistencies in Felicity's published biography: she claimed Paris (France), Athens, Berlin—but those cities also exist in **Ontario**. A cobblestone church reference and the impossibility of hearing the Seine from Montmartre confirm Felicity was actually from **Paris, Ontario**, not France. She fabricated her entire past.

The killer is **Miss Marsh**, Felicity's **real sister**, left behind on the family farm while Felicity stole her savings and fled. Marsh came to Toronto, tried to give Felicity a childhood photograph, was rebuffed, and killed her in a rage. She then shot at Effie Newsome (Aldo's presumed new partner) and tried to frame Lawton. Marsh is arrested; the stolen star-shaped necklace (matching Marsh's earrings) is recovered.
