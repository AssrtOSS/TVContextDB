## S09E02

# S09E02 — Brief

## Plot

Undercover NYPD detective Clint Mathis is gunned down at 8:00 AM outside his daughter's school in front of a stolen black Dodge Durango with a busted tail light; weapon is .45 ACP. The murder cuts across three threads that turn out to be one.

- **Burns lead.** Clint had been deputized to a DHS task force probing "active clubs" — MMA gyms that double as white supremacist recruitment fronts. Brooklyn operator Joe-Max Burns is raided on a tip from Clint: 48 unregistered M1911s in .45 ACP. Burns claims ignorance of Clint's cover and flips for a photo taken under the Roosevelt Island Bridge, showing Clint arguing with his old Gang Unit partner Hugo Reyna the night before.
- **Rogue-cop lead.** Reyna (NYPD, 12 years, issued a Glock 21 in .45) is the obvious suspect. Lt. Marcus Warren (Clint's CO) cooperates, sets a meet, and the team arrests Reyna. His alibi — an all-night card game at a Greenpoint lamp store — checks out; ERT clears his Glock.
- **Harkes lead.** Clint had been subpoenaed to testify in the trial of Cobi Harkes, NYC's biggest heroin dealer, after Cobi's 2-kg bust on a traffic stop. Cobi always claimed Reyna planted the drugs; Clint's body cam showed him finding nothing, while Reyna's "malfunctioned." One week later, Clint transferred to DHS, ending the partnership. Reyna suggests Cobi retaliated by ordering the hit, but Cobi — interrogated with his attorney brother Tabias "Tab" Harkes — refuses to break his own three rules and walks.
- **The bear.** Talking to Clint's 12-year-old daughter Ariel, Maggie finds Clint had smuggled her a stuffed bear that morning. Inside its sound module is the memory card from his undercover phone. It contains a security-cam still of Latin King lieutenant Fernando Balboa buying a burner phone on March 10 — the day before the Harkes traffic stop.
- **Balboa takedown.** Team rolls on Balboa's last known; the black Durango with the broken tail light is parked outside. Reyna, having tracked Balboa independently, breaches first and puts a gun on him, shouting that Balboa killed his partner. He's disarmed. Inside, ERT recovers a SIG P220 in .45, recently fired, confirmed as the murder weapon.
- **Tab's play.** Balboa admits he sent the anonymous burner tip that led Reyna and Clint to the BMW, but refuses to name his backer ("y'all gonna get my family killed too"). Ian digs into Balboa's priors: a dropped 3-strike weapons case, defended *pro bono* by Tab Harkes. The FBI theory: Tab defended Balboa as a favor, then called it in by having Balboa tip off the BMW so the 2 kg of Latin King heroin would be planted in his own brother Cobi's trunk, framing him and removing the only obstacle to a Harkes–Kings truce and partnership.
- **Coup at 26 Federal.** Maggie and the team bring Cobi and Tab in separately, play them against each other. Cobi walks out into the parking lot, offers Tab a ride; they have a long, cold brotherly talk. Tab returns and confesses to conspiracy to murder a police officer. AUSA takes him upstairs. Cobi is released.

## Parallel: the Vorpe operation

A side thread, set in bookend scenes, follows a deep-cover operative inside Anna Vorpe's Strike Team meeting covertly with a female FBI handler (addressed as "ma'am," almost certainly SAC Isobel Castille). The Strike Team's counter-surveillance defeats standard bugs; Ian has been building an induction bug on a low-frequency magnetic loop. The handler delivers the finished device and tells the operative to use it to take Vorpe down and "close the circle" before more people are read in. Last beat of the episode: the handler warns "You were followed" — operative denies it — "Somebody want to tell me what the hell's going on?" Cover status unresolved.

# S09E05 — Brief

## Plot

An inmate-transport van is ambushed 10 minutes after leaving the Metropolitan Detention Center (MDC), and eight women convicts scatter into Brooklyn. The case turns into a hunt for two fugitives who turn out to be running opposite stories.

- **The ambush.** The van's tires are blown by hollow **caltrops** (a trick the military used overseas to stop suicide bombers from reaching outposts), then the van is rammed in a camera blind spot between two CCTV units. Attackers — 3–4, medium height/build, masked — fire tear gas through the windshield, zip-tie the crew, and take the keys. A 911 call puts orange jumpsuits entering an auto mechanic six blocks west. That's the tactical signature the team recognizes: planned, professional, with the van stopped exactly where the attackers wanted it.
- **The two names.** U.S. Marshals and the FBI mop up six of the eight. Remaining at large: **Genesis Perez**, 35, senior member of the **Inwood Kings** street gang (her husband **Luis Perez** is #2 in the Kings and was about to be hit with a RICO charge — she may have been extracted for him), and **Laila Sarbani**, 38, an Afghan-American "model prisoner" with five years for embezzlement and wire fraud. Two caught inmates finger Genesis as mastermind; Genesis herself says she had an SUV waiting and drove off with Laila. The van driver tells Scola the opposite: Laila's crew cut the chain, dumped her at the roadside, and drove off with Laila. Genesis and Laila were allies for exactly as long as it took Laila to be useful.
- **The padre.** Laila had no visitors, no calls, no letters at MDC, and spent her time in the chapel — which sits outside every security camera's line of sight. The only regular visitor was **Father Andrew Discano**, a visiting priest spending ~10 hours a week with her, who left her notes on a bench in **McGolrick Park**. Ramos works him: he fell in love with her, and she used him. He agreed to help after she promised no one would get hurt; the "her friends" who could free her were the ambush crew.
- **The call.** Discano waits at the bench; Laila calls, warns him that whoever is forcing him is listening, says he "helped me find my path," and leaves him with her forgiveness before hanging up. Call traces only to a 550-yard radius around a Greenpoint cell tower.
- **The money drop.** Ian's audio-ID of the alarm on her call matches a **Sentinel Onyx** series alarm — critical-infrastructure grade, sold to military facilities, casinos, banks. Trust National Bank on McGuinness has just tripped its internal alarm. The crew robs it, dumps ~$465,000 into the street in the neighborhood with the highest concentration of Afghan refugees, in a banner reading "Take what is owed" in English and Pashto. ERT recovers only a fifth. A crowd phones video the money fall; one man on a balcony is looking up *before* anything falls — he knows in advance. That face goes to the JOC.
- **The balcony man.** **Idris Sherazi**, Afghan refugee who came over after Kabul fell in 2021, no record, married to **Zohal**, daughter sick. He was a U.S. military interpreter attached to Laila's unit. He is tracked via pole cams from an E-train station in Queens to **Triborough Hospital**, where the civilian hit in the getaway was taken. He had gone there to leave money for her. Arrested on the hospital floor; when pressed for names he says "I have principles," then lays out the grievance: U.S. visa promises never came through, no one will hire an undocumented Afghan refugee, his daughter needs medicine, and the Taliban is still in power. Immigration will now review the family's refugee status.
- **The trap.** JOC finds the empty cash positions of Brooklyn and Queens branches: the crew hit a *small* bank to scare the system, which drove all the cash into one target — **First Central Savings Bank** in Brooklyn, roughly $4 million in the vault, with cash deliveries frozen. The team massed there; the crew had already taken ~25 people hostage and built a **human barricade** to kill the sniper's line of sight.
- **The negotiation.** The bank manager **Jenn Heidlemann** won't answer Scola's call. The robbers send one demand: "Send the guy who was just talking. No guns." It is a 15- or 16-year-old girl, and Scola goes in alone and unarmed. Laila insists she wants the money and five hostages; **Rus** (Rustam) does not answer to her and holds the real authority. The command side — with Eva Ramos' prosecutorial read that these people believe the money is owed to them — offers the deal: leave the hostages unharmed, take the cash, go. Laila says yes. Then she takes the bank manager as insurance and runs, because she does not believe a hollow American promise. "She didn't believe you, and now they're running."
- **The chase.** North up **Franklin Street**, past Kent; NYPD shuts Oak Street for construction to box them in. At the roadblock the car stops, keys go out the window, and Laila and her partner surrender; the freed hostages come out with them. But **Rustam and Tamim were never in the van** — they stayed inside the bank disguised as hostages. Rustam is found and taken; **Tamim slips away** with the money, lost by the cameras for a few seconds before the JOC realizes he had a getaway car a couple of blocks down. Laila, on being asked what they did with it: "It is now where it belongs."

The thief: **Bruce Medina**, 42, Staten Island high school English teacher, no record. He stole the cesium to protest **R913**—a city council–approved data center project at **Freshkills Park** (former world's largest landfill) on Staten Island. Bruce saw it as Manhattan dumping pollution on Staten Island again.

Bruce deposited what he thought was cesium into Central Park Pond—but the tank was empty. **Lewis Mitchell**, Bruce's radicalized accomplice (mechanic, unemployed ~1 year, DV history with ex-wife, supports Staten Island secession movement), had swapped the cesium and built a **dirty bomb** at **Whitehall Terminal** (the ferry link between Staten Island and Manhattan). Lewis used Jessica (his girlfriend, under coercive control) to stall the ferry, creating a bottleneck at Whitehall for maximum casualties.
