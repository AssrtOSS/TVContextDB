# Sheriff Country — Subtitle Translation Background

## Title & Show Verification

- Series title: Sheriff Country
- Chinese rendering (provisional, no established subtitles below ID 800000): 《警长国度》or 《治安官之乡》— maintain consistency with whatever the translator's project standardizes. Treat "County Sheriff" as "县治安官/警长" (not "警长国家" — that would misread "Country" as the noun).
- Genre: Police procedural / action drama / crime drama
- Broadcaster: CBS (United States); streams on Paramount+. In Canada it airs on Global.
- First aired: October 17, 2025 (Season 1, Pilot). Season 2 premiered October 9, 2026.
- Structure: anthology of weekly procedural cases of the week, layered over serialized family-relationship arcs. Season 1 has 20 episodes (Oct 17, 2025 – May 22, 2026). Season 2 ordered Dec 3, 2025; premiered Oct 9, 2026.
- Created by: Joan Rater, Tony Phelan, Max Thieriot. Showrunner: Matt Lopez. Production: Jerry Bruckheimer Television, CBS Studios. Filmed in the Toronto/Hamilton/Halton region (Canada), not in the Northern California setting.
- Franchise: first spin-off of Fire Country (《火境救援》/《野火国度》). The parent show's character-name conventions carry over (see crossover characters).

## Overall Plot Premise

Straight-shooting sheriff Mickey Fox patrols the small rural town of Edgewater, in the fictional Edgewater County, Northern California, balancing law-enforcement cases with messy personal ties: her ex-con father Wes, her newly sober daughter Skye, her ex-husband Travis (a county ADA), and her deputies. Early plotline: the murder of Skye's ex-boyfriend, Brandon, a recovering addict, kicks off a season-long mystery involving framing, the wealthy Fraley family, and a serial case ("Blood Moon Killer") tied to the annual Blood Moon Festival. Season 1 finale reveals Wes's late wife Maria is alive (now "Deputy Director Santos," a federal agent) and pulls Mickey into a joint Bulgarian-cartel investigation. Season 2 begins with "Cyclone" (Oct 9, 2026), introducing a major weather/disaster arc.

## Key Relationships (preserve clarity in subtitles)

- Mickey ↔ Sharon: former step-sisters; reconciled after Sharon blamed Mickey for Bode's imprisonment.
- Mickey ↔ Wes: father–daughter, strained; Wes lives off-grid on the mountain growing marijuana.
- Mickey ↔ Travis: ex-spouses (married ~20 years, divorced ~5); coparents Skye; still emotionally connected.
- Mickey ↔ Boone: professional second-in-command and slow-burn romantic partner.
- Mickey ↔ Skye: mother–daughter; Skye is newly sober after fentanyl addiction.
- Mickey ↔ Nathan "Boone" Boone ↔ Nora: estranged marriage subplot.
- Cassidy ↔ Travis: dated; Cassidy's missing sister Zoey is the emotional driver of her arc.
- Wes ↔ Sharon: stepfather–stepdaughter (Wes was previously married to Sharon's mother).
- Bode is Mickey's nephew; Skye is Bode's cousin.
- Fraley family wealth: Travis and Miranda are siblings; most of Edgewater resents the Fraleys.

## World Setting

- Location: Edgewater, Edgewater County, Northern California — fictional small rural town, county seat vibe. Surrounded by wilderness, prone to wildfires. Real filming: Greater Toronto/Hamilton/Halton area, Ontario, Canada. Production deliberately moved 2,100 miles east so lead Morena Baccarin could be near her family in New York.
- Institutions: Edgewater County Sheriff's Office (ECSO); Cal Fire (California Department of Forestry and Fire Protection); Edgewater County DA's Office; Edgewater County Fire vs. PD annual baseball game; Fraley Estate (old-money family compound); local landmarks include the Blood Moon Festival site and Deadwater (a remote area where bones have been found).
- Time-period flavor: contemporary small-town America; ranching, off-grid marijuana cultivation, quinceañeras, quince, deer/boar hunts; reflects real rural Northern California culture.

## Speaking Styles & Cultural Context

- Mickey: terse, dry, straight-shooting; speaks like a working sheriff — short clipped sentences, no-nonsense, occasional sarcasm. Often addresses deputies by surname or rank.
- Boone: measured, ex-big-city cop, methodical.
- Wes: laconic mountain man; informal, folksy; uses rancher/pot-grower slang; occasionally evasive.
- Skye: sharp, restless, Gen-Z inflection, references addiction/recovery vocabulary ("sober," "sponsor," "relapse," "fent-laced").
- Travis: polished, lawyer-speak; county-boy accent underneath; uses legal jargon ("motion," "discovery," "client").
- Cassidy: determined, gritty, blue-collar deputy voice.
- Miranda: upper-class, boardroom cadence; manipulative politeness.
- Cultural layer: heavy Northern California rural flavor (fire season, marijuana grows, ranching, Latino community presence at quinceañeras, county politics, sheriff-vs.-fire rivalry); Spanish terms should be kept in Spanish with parenthetical glosses where helpful.
- Recurring franchise crossovers use Fire Country's tone — emergency-radio chatter ("Engine 42, Engine 42, copy"), dispatch codes, all-hands-on-deck rescues.

## Translation Notes (style guidance for subtitles)

- Keep rank/title before surname in Chinese: 警长米基, 副警长布恩, 检察官助理特拉维斯, 中尉布恩.
- When characters say "Sheriff" to Mickey, render as "警长" or "头儿" depending on tone.
- "County" in Edgewater County translates as "县"; do not confuse with American "county" ≈ Chinese "县."
- "Fire Country" the parent series — use a stable render throughout (e.g., 《火境救援》); first mention spell out, subsequent mentions may shorten.
- Spanish-origin terms (quinceañera, etc.) — keep in italics or romanized original with footnote on first use.
- Place names: Edgewater, Deadwater, Three Rock — keep English transliteration on first use, then use as established.
- "ADA" — first mention "助理地区检察官（ADA）", then "检察官" or "助理检察官."
- Avoid overly literary Chinese; match procedural-genre register (functional, brisk).
- Episode titles: translate literally where possible (e.g., "Pilot" — 《试播集/首播》; "The Sixth Man" — 《第六人》; "Death & Taxes" — 《死亡与税》; "Crucible" — 《熔炉/试炼》; "Mexico" — 《墨西哥》; "Cyclone" — 《气旋》; "Family Trust" — 《家庭信托》; "Ulysses" — 《尤利西斯》).
