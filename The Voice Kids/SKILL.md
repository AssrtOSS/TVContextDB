# The Voice Kids — Background for Subtitle Translation

## Series Identity
**Title:** The Voice Kids  
**Franchise Origin:** Netherlands (original: *The Voice Kids*, RTL 4, premiered 27 January 2012)  
**Creator:** John de Mol Jr.  
**Format Owner:** Talpa Media / ITV Studios  
**Genre:** Reality singing competition (children's spin-off of *The Voice* franchise)  
**Target Contestants:** Ages 6–15 (varies by version: typically 7–14 or 8–15)  
**Broadcaster (Original):** RTL 4 (Netherlands)  
**First Air Date:** 27 January 2012 (Netherlands)  
**Global Rollout:** 30+ international versions (Germany, France, UK, Poland, Spain, USA/Telemundo, Brazil, Indonesia, Kazakhstan, Poland, etc.)

## World Setting & Premise
A televised talent search where child singers compete through a structured multi-stage format. The "world" is a purpose-built TV studio with a live audience; the narrative frame is a mentorship competition. There is no fictional diegesis — the show presents itself as a real contest with real stakes (scholarships, recording contracts, cash prizes). Each national version adapts the format to local language, culture, and broadcast standards, but the core mechanics are franchised and nearly identical.

## Overall Plot Premise
Children audition off-camera (producers' auditions). Those who advance perform **Blind Auditions** on-camera: coaches sit with backs turned; if a coach likes a voice, they press a button to turn their chair. If multiple coaches turn, the child chooses their coach. Coaches build teams, then mentor them through **Battle Rounds** (trios/duels singing the same song, coach picks one to advance), **Sing-Offs / Knockouts** (solo performances, coach narrows to finalists), and a **Live/Pre-recorded Final** where the public votes for the winner. The winning child receives a prize package (scholarship €10k–€25k / $50k, recording contract option, family holiday) and the winning coach earns bragging rights.

## Season / Episode Structure (Franchise Standard)
- **Seasons:** Annual cycles (some countries run 2 cycles/year). Original NL: 10 seasons (2012–2021) + returning 2026.
- **Episodes per Season:** 8–14 (NL: 96 episodes over 10 seasons; Germany: ~14 seasons, 120–150 min each; UK: 41 episodes over 7 series).
- **Phase Breakdown (typical):**
  1. Blind Auditions — 4–6 episodes
  2. Battles — 2–3 episodes
  3. Sing-Offs / Knockouts — 1–2 episodes
  4. Semi-Final (some versions) — 1 episode
  5. Final — 1 episode (live or pre-recorded with televote)

## Character Archetypes (Recurring Roles)
Since each season has new child contestants, there are no continuing "characters" across seasons. The recurring *roles* are:

| Role | Function | Speaking Style / Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Head Coach / Veteran Coach** | Often a pop star with long tenure (e.g., Lena Meyer-Landrut DE, Pixie Lott UK, Patrick Fiori FR) | Warm, encouraging, uses music-industry jargon ("breath support," "phrasing," "stage presence") |
| **New / Guest Coach** | Rotating panelists (singers, rappers, duos) | Fresh energy, sometimes more critical or playful |
| **Duo Coaches** | Two artists sharing one chair (e.g., Nick & Simon, The BossHoss, Deine Freunde, Michi & Smudo) | Banter, dual feedback, "good cop / bad cop" dynamic |
| **Main Host** | Guides the show, announces results, comforts children | Empathetic, clear, upbeat; often a TV personality (Emma Willis UK, Thore Schölermann DE, Martijn Krabbé NL) |
| **Backstage Host** | Interviews contestants pre/post performance, families | Gentle, protective, draws out emotions |
| **Child Contestant** | Ages 6–15; varied backgrounds | Nervous excitement, raw talent, sometimes multilingual/multicultural stories |
| **Parents / Family** | Shown in backstage cutaways | Proud, tearful, supportive; often speak in native language/dialect |

## Cultural Context & Translation Sensitivities
- **Child Protection:** All versions operate under strict child-labor and safeguarding laws. Subtitles must never sensationalize vulnerability; avoid language that could be read as exploitative.
- **Multicultural Casting:** Especially in Germany, France, Netherlands — many contestants are first- or second-generation immigrants. Names, heritage references, and code-switching (e.g., Turkish-German, Arabic-French) appear frequently. Preserve names faithfully; transliterate only if no established Chinese form exists.
- **Prize Framing:** Prizes are educational (scholarships) not commercial jackpots. Translate "scholarship" as 奖学金, not 奖金.
- **Emotional Beats:** Blind audition chair turns are high-emotion moments. Chinese subtitles often use exclamatory particles (哇、好厉害、转椅了) to mirror studio audience reactions.
- **Music Terminology:** Keep song titles in original language (or official Chinese release title). Coach feedback uses terms like "tone," "control," "interpretation," "dynamics" → 音色、气息控制、诠释、强弱变化.
- **Humor / Banter:** Coach banter (especially duo coaches) is culturally specific. Translate intent, not literal wordplay. UK version: dry British humor; DE: direct, warm; FR: expressive; NL: down-to-earth.
- **Junior Eurovision Tie-in:** Several national winners go to JESC. If mentioned, use 青少年欧洲歌唱赛 (official Chinese name).

## Speaking Style Guidelines for Subtitle Translation
1. **Hosts:** Clear, measured pace; standard broadcast register. Use polite forms (您) when addressing coaches/contestants.
2. **Coaches (to children):** Warm, encouraging, simple vocabulary. Avoid overly technical music jargon unless the child demonstrates advanced knowledge.
3. **Coaches (to each other):** Casual, colloquial. Duo coaches overlap, interrupt, tease.
4. **Children:** Natural child speech — incomplete sentences, repetition, emotional outbursts. Preserve authenticity; don't "adultify."
5. **Parents:** Regional accents/dialects sometimes audible. If subtitled, note in brackets [方言] or use standard Chinese with emotional tone markers.
6. **Song Lyrics:** If performed in English (common in non-English versions), subtitle lyrics verbatim. If in local language, translate meaning concisely (one line per subtitle).
