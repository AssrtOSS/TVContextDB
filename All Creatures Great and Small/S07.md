## All Creatures Great and Small S07E01 Summary

### Episode Plot
James Herriot returns to Darrowby after giving a lecture at the veterinary college in Glasgow. The practice faces competition when a new vet, **Granville Bennett**, opens a clinic in nearby Brawton with flashy advertising ("Free consultations") and a red sports car. Meanwhile, **Geoff Hatfield's cat Alfred** falls ill with mysterious weight loss and lethargy—X-rays and blood tests show nothing. James suspects a hairball too far down to show on X-ray or cause retching. After Geoff requests a second opinion from Bennett, James performs an exploratory laparotomy and finds a massive hairball lodged in the stomach and intestine. Alfred recovers. At Heston Grange, **Helen** persuades **Siegfried** to buy Friesian cows (six, though Siegfried counters with four in exchange for five Jerseys), marking a shift from their traditional Jersey herd. **Jimmy** starts school after initial reluctance.

### Important Character Actions
- **James**: Lectures at Glasgow vet college; diagnoses and operates on Alfred (laparotomy); mentors Jimmy about school
- **Siegfried**: Confronts Bennett at The Drovers over advertising; gets charmed by Bennett's flattery and drinks; agrees to Friesians at Heston
- **Tristan**: Helps Jimmy practice shoelaces; moves to attic suite; assists in Alfred's surgery
- **Helen**: Manages Jimmy's school anxiety; pushes for Friesians at Heston; supports Dorothy's return
- **Mrs Hall (Audrey)**: Transforms Tristan's old room for Jimmy & Rosie; mediates Siegfried/Dorothy dynamic
- **Granville Bennett**: Opens Brawton practice with aggressive advertising; publishes papers on eye surgery & intramedullary pinning; specialises in small animals; apologises and amends ad after Siegfried's visit
- **Geoff Hatfield**: Runs sweet shop; deeply attached to Alfred (cat got him through the war); requests second opinion
- **Jimmy**: Reluctant to start school ("I'm already a vet"); comforted by Helen & Tristan

## S07E02

## S07E02 Summary

**Episode Title (implied):** The Friesian inheritance / A matter of survival

### Plot

James's family has acquired four Friesian cows and is struggling to make the transition work. The episode follows multiple threads:

- **The cows:** The new Friesians have lost significant weight and are showing signs of ketosis (slow fever). James diagnoses the condition — they're burning fat instead of glucose because they're not eating enough. Despite being fed plenty of hay, it's unclear whether the grass alone is sufficient. James prescribes a glycol drench and recommends swapping the Friesians to an outside field while the Jerseys stay in the top field.
- **Harriet the sow:** Mrs Hollins calls about their sow Harriet, who has started farrowing but won't let the piglets feed. She's aggressive and agitated. James sedates her with a "new" sedative (which he admits is really an improved formula of the old one, not truly new). She's large and strong — nearly bites off Grandad's hand during reintroduction attempts.
- **Tricki (Mrs Pumphrey's dog):** His arthritis has flared up again with stiffness and swelling. Siegfried prescribes new tablets, and Mrs Pumphrey reveals Tricki has been sleeping on her sofa because he cried when left alone upstairs. Siegfried also advises against carrying him too much.

### Important Character Actions

- **James** diagnoses ketosis in the Friesians, prescribes glycol drench, and insists the farm needs to modernize or die. He visits Harriet to sedate her.
- **Siegfried** deals with Tricki's arthritis, debates with James about sedative orders and magazine choices, and firmly asserts his authority over the practice ("This is my practice, and what I say goes").
- **Richard (James's father)** resists the idea of feed cake and is emotionally distressed about selling the farm. He eventually opens up about his fears — not just for their future but for the legacy of previous generations. James reassures him they'll work it out together.
- **Mrs Hall** proposes a community pantry in the church hall storeroom to help elderly parishioners who can't afford proper meals, inspired by Mrs Pumphrey's suggestion about food parcels. The vicar agrees.
- **Mrs Pumphrey** contributes tinned confit de canard to the food pantry and brings Tricki in for his appointment.
- **Grandad Hollins** insists Harriet won't farrow for another week, but she ends up delivering early.

### Newly Established Names/Terms

- **Anabella-Sue** — one of the new Friesian cows, named by the kids (Rosie doodled on the drawing).
- **Community pantry** — the informal name for the food-sharing initiative set up in the church hall storeroom.
- **Ketosis / slow fever** — introduced as a diagnostic term for the cows' condition.
- **Glycol drench** — the treatment James administers.
- **"Mrs Porter"** — the name of one of the cows; she's also the recipient of James's drawing showing himself and her in the milking stall.

### Continuity-Relevant Facts

- The Friesians require more hay than Jerseys and are more prone to health issues.
- Feed cake is expensive but may be necessary for adequate yield — a major point of contention between James and Richard.
- The new sedatives are "new and improved" but James and Siegfried both note the formula is essentially the same with a slightly higher dosage.
- Siegfried's waiting room magazines are a recurring bone of contention (James wants to add women's magazines/fashion; Siegfried objects to them replacing veterinary publications).
- The concert on Monday (Rachmaninoff programme) is discussed — both Siegfried and Mrs Hall had planned to attend but may skip due to tensions.
- Fred Dobson offered to take the Friesians off their hands if they decide to sell.
- Mrs Pearce's canary was prescribed muscle relaxants by Siegfried (for horses only) — she was upset about it.
- Mrs Hollins mentions elderly parishioner **Mr Slavens** collapsed during Evensong and had been giving his meat rations to his dog — he's now receiving food parcels.

### Jokes and References

- **"Humpty Dumpty"** — Grandad objects to the name; when told one of the calves might be named that, he quips "Humpty Dumpty's an egg!" — the episode ends on this joke, implying Rosieposie (the baby) will be named something equally absurd.
- **Beer for the sow** — a recurring folk remedy; James refuses ("a drunk sow would be no easier to handle"), but Siegfried counters with the old saying "Nothing settles a sow like beer."
- **"Like a tart's bloody boudoir"** — Siegfried's outburst about the waiting room furniture being too soft.
- **Rachmaninoff vs Gilbert and Sullivan** — Siegfried finds Rachmaninoff overrated and Mrs Hall is passionate about G&S, with plans for Siegfried to play "Yum-Yum's aria."
- **"Scrooge"** — Mrs Hall jokes Siegfried's bookkeeping is "like doing the books for Scrooge himself."

### Unresolved Threads

- **The farm's future** — Richard and James agree to consider feed cake and costings, but the debate is far from settled. Fred Dobson may buy the Friesians. The question of whether to sell remains open.
- **Harriet's temperament** — She's sedated but remains aggressive; her ability to nurse piglets is still uncertain.
- **Friesian milk yield** — Not yet resolved; the family is still figuring out whether the breed will be profitable enough.
- **Mrs Pumphrey and Siegfried's relationship** — There are hints of romantic interest ("someone who'll be pleased about that" / "we do seem to be having quite a nice time together"), but nothing confirmed.
- **Tricki's weight** — Siegfried wants him to lose weight, but Mrs Pumphrey keeps indulging him (silk eiderdown, pillow softness, carrying him).
- **The concert** — Both couples planned to attend but the episode ends with the implication they may skip due to Siegfried and Mrs Hall's argument.
- **Community pantry** — Set up but still needing volunteers and publicity; the vicar hasn't formally endorsed it yet.

# Episode S07E04 — Summary

## Plot

The episode opens with the practice caring for Mr Bushell's elderly horse Old Benjy and his pregnant cow Bella, while Siegfried and Mrs Hall share tea at the manor where Tricki has just gotten a rose thorn. Tristan arrives on foot, having abandoned the Rover up the road after another engine failure; Siegfried blames the previous mechanic and vows to fix it himself rather than pay another callout.

Back at Skeldale House, Jimmy struggles with writing the letter J and is rewarded by James with stories about animal surgery. A stray Pekingese — a female — wanders in; Mrs Hall quickly identifies her as Tallulah, belonging to Evie Rogers's sister, who has just moved into a nursing home. Evie asks the household to find her a new home. Helen and Mrs Hall approach Mrs Pumphrey, but she categorically refuses, insisting Tricki is "unique" and that "lineage" matters more than breed.

Learning that Edward has cancelled his visit for her birthday, Mrs Hall's family and friends secretly plan a birthday tea. Dorothy prepares vol-au-vents; Mrs Pumphrey happens to drop by about the community pantry and, after learning it's Mrs Hall's birthday, stays to keep her company, even grooming Tallulah and noting the badly matted fur. Siegfried eventually pulls off his improvised fix on the Rover.

James and Siegfried perform their first bovine Caesarean on Bella at Bushell's farm. The calf is enormous; James accidentally cuts into the rumen instead of the uterus, contaminating the abdomen. They improvise, work by touch, exteriorise the uterus as best they can, then haul out a huge live bull calf by the hock. Bella is given sulphanilamide, but peritonitis is a serious risk. Mr Bushell is grateful regardless.

Mrs Pumphrey correctly diagnoses a matted pad on Tallulah as hiding a torn-off claw that had been causing the dog pain; Mrs Hall removes it.

Audrey's birthday tea is a warm success: paper chains, place cards, toast to "Mrs Hall," and Dorothy's surprise of Eccles cakes from Clitheroe — Audrey's nostalgic favourite.

That night, Siegfried tells James that he and Mrs Hall should find their own place after the wedding so as not to disrupt the household — especially Audrey's home. Early the next morning, Mrs Pumphrey arrives unannounced: she and Tricki have decided to take Tallulah after all. Tricki acts like a puppy again, finding a "new lease of life."

The episode centers on Siegfried and Dorothy's wedding day, which ultimately doesn't happen.
