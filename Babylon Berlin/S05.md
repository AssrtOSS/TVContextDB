## S05E01

# S05E01 — Summary

## Plot

Prologue: **November 1918, Pasewalk Reserve Field Hospital.** A staff surgeon treats a blinded soldier (Adolf Hitler) with hypnosis and willpower rhetoric — "every man has a god inside him," "tear down the walls of ordinariness." The patient reports seeing "a glow" / "the flame." The case file is filed under *Admission of Adolf Hitler*.

**Berlin-Schöneberg, 30 Jan 1933, 10:45.** Radio station introduces Fred Jacoby as "the new voice of Germany." A live broadcast tracks Hitler's appointment as Reich Chancellor; new elections are called for 5 March.

**11:40, Police HQ "Rote Burg."** Charlotte Ritter takes an anonymous tip: a murder in an office building, the suspect — Dr. Edmund Forster — was seen fleeing in haste; the "file" on the doctor is also missing.

**Press offices.** Samuel Katelbach is sent back to work. A rival paper's editor Heymann fields a call from a man identifying himself as *Edmund Forster*, invoking their shared *Dahlem Circle* university past. The caller claims to hold "extraordinarily explosive" information on the new Chancellor and insists on a public meeting — the press ball.

**Dr. Forster's Institute for Suggestive Therapy.** Forster telephones a "Professor": a man named *Strelow* (SA brigade leader) came to collect "the parcel" (the Hitler file), but was accompanied by his entire SA brigade, so Forster couldn't hand it over.

**Opernplatz, 14:15.** Charlotte and Reinhold Gräf compare notes — Forster is the main suspect; newspapers have been tipped. Reinhold handles a tooth extraction. (Side note: Hitler is said to have negotiated his chancellorship by demanding fresh elections, not wanting to rule "by the grace of Hindenburg.")

**Press Ball, Unter den Linden, 18:40.** Charlotte tells Gereon the doctor "disappeared" from the crime scene. They spot Forster in the gallery; Charlotte flanks left, Gereon right. Gereon tells Forster "this gentleman is in danger of his life"; Forster replies, "No, they want to silence me." Both vanish into the crowd.

**Liebenberg Castle, 21:15.** Industrialist Alfred Nyssen hosts a victory dinner — waiters hold "A HITLER REICH CHANCELLOR" placards, Göring is on hand, but Hitler is late. Nyssen reminds everyone that the Führer was "in the position we put him in" and demands to know how his *ten-point plan* will now be implemented. Göring, pressed to speak, frames the Republic as a "pigsty" to be "destroyed and extinguished."

A Nyssen aide reports to headquarters: Rath was seen at the press ball with Forster "and someone else," then both vanished. Rath did not bring Forster in.

Two political rallies follow:
- **KPD cell** (Dr. Völcker speaking): Stalin and Thälmann reject escalation; calls for mass mobilisation, votes over street violence, framing the fight as a five-week election sprint.
- **Nazi command meeting** (Göring presiding): announces (1) all Reich police chiefs will be replaced with loyalists; (2) *Rear Admiral (ret.) von Levetzow* as new Berlin Chief of Police; (3) political police separated from administration and reborn as a *Secret State Police Office* under *Councilor Wendt*; (4) SA units assigned as auxiliary police; (5) officers who use firearms "will have my personal backing."

Charlotte arrives at the press ball looking for Gereon — "we arranged to meet and I was late" — and slips away when asked what she's picking up.

## Important Character Actions

- **Dr. Edmund Forster** — 1918 hypnotherapist to Hitler; now on the run with the original Pasewalk file. Tipped off an SA brigade leader (Strelow) wanted to seize it; Forster refused to hand it over while armed. Reaches out to a "Professor" and to Gereon for extraction.
- **Gereon Rath** — receives Forster's plea, agrees to extract him at the press ball, and disappears with him mid-evening; later fails to show at the rendezvous with Charlotte.
- **Charlotte "Lotte" Ritter** — takes the anonymous murder tip, coordinates the surveillance on Forster at the ball, and ends the episode unable to locate Gereon.
- **Reinhold Gräf** — exchanges intel with Charlotte at Opernplatz; handles paperwork/photo duplication for the press.
- **Samuel Katelbach** — dispatched from the radio station back to newsroom work; flagged by *Blank* as someone the Führer is "relying on personally."
- **Fred Jacoby** — introduced at the radio station as "the new voice of Germany"; holds press-ball tickets.
- **Alfred Nyssen** — hosts the patron dinner at Liebenberg; openly treats Hitler as a client ("position we put him in") and demands accounting for his ten-point plan.
- **Hermann Göring** — banters through the dinner, then delivers the hard-line "destroy and extinguish" line.
- **Dr. Völcker** — KPD speaker at the communist gathering, counsels restraint over street action.
- **Colonel Gottfried Wendt** — named to lead the new Secret State Police Office.

## Newly Established Names / Terms

- **Pasewalk file** — Hitler's 1918 admission record documenting his hysterical blindness and suggestive-therapy treatment; the MacGuffin of the season.
- **Institute for Suggestive Therapy** — Forster's clinic, both 1918 origin and 1933 location.
- **Strelow** — SA brigade leader tasked (or self-tasked) with retrieving the file.
- **Blank** — Nazi operative at the radio station; Führer's man on the ground.
- **Dahlem Circle** — old university network Forster uses to vouch for himself with Heymann.
- **Ten-point plan** — Nyssen's conditions/demands given to Hitler, now to be redeemed.
- **Rear Admiral (ret.) von Levetzow** — slated as new Berlin Chief of Police.
- **Secret State Police Office** (pre-Gestapo nomenclature) — political police spun out of the administrative branch under Wendt.
- **SA as auxiliary police** — newly formalised auxiliary role.

## Continuity-Relevant Facts

- Date anchor: Hitler's appointment (30 Jan 1933) and the 5 March election calendar drive the entire season.
- Nyssen's financial patronage of Hitler is restated explicitly; his mother Annemarie is on hand at Liebenberg.
- Göring's pre-Gestapo design (police restructuring, firearm immunity for officers) is laid out before the audience, foreshadowing the terror apparatus.
- The Pasewalk incident — Hitler treated for war blindness — is canonised as in-universe historical fact.
- Forster's 1933 identity is *psychiatrist who once treated the Führer*, exactly the leverage that makes him dangerous.

## Jokes / References Useful for Later Episodes

- The 1918 hypnosis scene uses theatrical phrasing ("there's a god in every man," "flame") that the show later rhymes with the Nazi self-mythology of will and awakening — useful for spotting thematic callbacks.
- "We need to destroy and extinguish, that's all" is Göring's dinner line and reads as a thesis statement for the season.
- Katelbach's "I'll be at the *Café Mutzbauer* for the afterthought" — sets a recurring meeting place.
- The editor's quip about Fritz Lang's *Testament of Dr. Mabuse* (released 1933) signals the show's self-aware nod to Lang/Nazi-cinema history.
- "Tempo editorial office" + Heymann identifies the rival paper and its editor — useful when leaks cross newsrooms.
- The press ball functions as the recurring cover-meeting venue for the whole season.

## Unresolved Threads

- The murder victim in the building next to Forster's office — who is she, and is Forster actually guilty?
- The current location of the Pasewalk file (still with Forster, or seized by Strelow?).
- Strelow and the SA brigade — are they acting on Führer orders, Göring's, or independently?
- "Blank" — his full role and reach inside media.
- Forster's "Professor" — identity and reliability as an ally.
- Rath's extraction destination and his failure to meet Charlotte.
- Charlotte's errand at the press ball — what is she picking up, and for whom?
- The pace of Nyssen's ten-point plan against Hitler's actual policy rollout.
- Von Levetzow's tenure as Berlin Chief of Police and his relationship with Wendt's new secret police.
- Whether the KPD's election strategy holds, given Völcker's private doubts ("all this here will take a lot longer than most of us think").

## S05E06

# Babylon Berlin S05E06 — Summary

## PlotThe episode weaves four parallel threads in late January–February 1933:

1. **Political salon (SPD–KPD women).** At Elisabeth Behnke's apartment, Samuel Katelbach and Hans Litten orchestrate a clandestine meeting between **Mrs. Helfers** (presented as Rosa Luxemburg's niece, an SPD-aligned left-wing leader) and **Dr. Mrs. Völcker** (the KPD-aligned doctor from earlier seasons). Behnke serves hostess while Katelbach stalls the women with doughnuts and family chatter. The two women clash over tactics — banning the NSDAP, nationalizing Nyssens/Krupps/Quandts, the Soviet economic model — but eventually agree to keep talking. The same salon in another session produces a heated three-way argument between two female delegates and a third woman (probably Marie-Luise Seegers) who accuses both parties of stubbornness and warns the Left will be blamed for failing to stop Hitler.

2. **Lotte at headquarters.** Gennat pressures Lotte Ritter to close two cases before lunch: the Forster file and the disappearance of suspended Inspector Rath. Lotte types up a one-line "Rath is missing" closure report, then confronts Gennat for abandoning one of his best men. Gennat secretly passes her a lead: District Attorney **Dresse** is in Berlin, trying to flee to the US, and will meet her at **3 p.m. on February 27 at the Potsdam ferry**. Dresse knows where the Hitler medical file is.

3. **Nyssen–Schleicher pact, then Hitler's visit.** Former Chancellor **Schleicher** tries to recruit Alfred Nyssen into a right-wing nationalist anti-Hitler alliance (industrialists, DNVP, "reasonable" NSDAP members, Reichswehr). Nyssen is intrigued. That evening at the Nyssen villa, Annemarie (the mother) and Helga Rath receive **Adolf Hitler** himself — Helga engineered the invitation through Göring; it is Hitler's first accepted private invitation since becoming Chancellor, fixed for **February 27**. Hitler demands the Pasewalk 1918 medical file. Nyssen insists he sent Forster to Paris for his own protection, not to deliver the file. Hitler warns Nyssen he has now made himself a personal enemy of the Reich Chancellor.

4. **The concentration-camp breakout.** Katelbach and other prisoners (Brock, Mandel, Eckhart, Mühlensiefen, Heymann) are processed into **Langenberger north camp**. Detectives Czerwinski and Henning, on Gennat's orders, arrive in a police van to extract them. A prisoner named **Matern** has a PTSD breakdown ("Grenadier, pull yourself together!"). A shootout erupts. Katelbach and Behnke make it back to her apartment, but Katelbach is fatally wounded and dies in her arms. Behnke herself is injured. Survivors report there are more camps just north of the city limits.

## Important character actions

- **Lotte Ritter**: resists closing Rath's case; secures the Dresse meeting.
- **Gennat**: maintains public cover of case closure while feeding Lotte the Dresse lead.
- **Katelbach**: dies from wounds sustained escaping Langenberger.
- **Behnke**: wounded, presses Helfers and Völcker to unite quickly in Katelbach's memory.
- **Litten**: organizes the women-of-the-left unity meeting.
- **Schleicher**: pitches a right-wing anti-Hitler front to Nyssen.
- **Nyssen / Helga / Annemarie**: host Hitler at home; Helga's outreach to Göring secured the invitation.
- **Hitler**: refuses to bargain; brands Nyssen a personal enemy.
- **Czerwinski & Henning**: lead the prisoner extraction; ambushed at the camp.
- **Rath**: reappears at Lotte's door accompanied by a stranger named **Döbrich**.
- **Lotte (again)**: lies to uniformed police about the post-shootout noise by claiming she was running a bath.
- **Doris** (Lotte's friend): newly hired as a radio presenter for "**Women for Hitler**."

## Newly established names/terms

- **Dresse** — District Attorney, fleeing to the US, holder of intel on the Hitler medical file; meeting fixed Feb 27, 15:00, Potsdam ferry.
- **Döbrich** — man accompanying Rath on his return (apparent rescuer/ally from the mission).
- **Doris** — Lotte's friend, now working for the Nazi women's radio program.
- **"Women for Hitler" (Frauen für Hitler)** — the Nazi women's organization; the radio job is framed as a "proper job."
- **Langenberger north camp** — a newly introduced concentration/work camp outside Berlin.
- **Potsdam ferry** — covert meeting point for the Dresse handoff.
- **Mrs. Helfers / "Luxemburg"** — SPD-aligned woman leader; plays the Luxemburg family connection to legitimize her standing.
- Prisoners **Brock, Mandel, Eckhart, Mühlensiefen, Heymann, Matern** — first names used as roll-call at Langenberger.
- Cover signal **"the spuds / potatoes"** — prearranged code used by the extraction team.

## Continuity-relevant facts

- Rath was suspended and missing; he is now back, accompanied by Döbrich, having apparently blown something up — but he did not warn Lotte in advance, fueling her anger and her refusal to be an unconsenting accomplice.
- Forster is confirmed alive in **Paris**; he was not arrested, but Rath was.
- The Hitler medical file remains the season's central MacGuffin: Nyssen claims he never had it; Hitler demands it; Dresse knows where it is.
- **February 27, 1933** is now triple-booked: Dresse handoff at Potsdam ferry, Hitler dinner at the Nyssens', and (historically) the Reichstag fire — converging in the finale.
- Göring is the channel through which Helga reached Hitler; Papen is being leaned on by Göring to squeal.
- Lotte's loyalty is established with Rath; the bath cover-up shows she is willing to perjure herself for him, despite her fury.

## Jokes and references useful for later episodes

- "**Lottchen**" — Doris's diminutive for Charlotte, used only by old friends.
- Doris recruitment pitch: "Your rejection comes from your mind. But this is about feelings, about a sense of security and about love." Lotte's retort: "Doris, even when you talk nonsense, I love you."
- Doris's parting line: "Sooner or later, you, too, will find faith in a national revival deep in your heart" — seeds Lotte being publicly tested later.
- Annemarie Nyssen to Alfred: "You're letting her take the reins, son." Alfred: "On the contrary, I'm taking them back" — continuation of the mother-son power struggle.
- The Luxemburg quotation deployed as a rhetorical ice-breaker ("People do not shape their own history of their own free will…") — flag for later political-speech scenes.
- Behnke's bath-water alibi becomes a callback motif: ordinary domestic noise as cover for violent acts.
- "The spuds" as code word — pay off later if the team uses the same signal.

## Unresolved threads

- What exactly Rath and Döbrich blew up, where, and what intelligence they obtained.
- Whether Lotte and Rath can rebuild trust — he returned without warning, she covered for him anyway.
- Will Helfers and Völcker actually deliver an SPD–KPD women's pact?
- Outcome of the **Feb 27 Dresse meeting** and the **Feb 27 Hitler dinner at Nyssens'** — the same day as the Reichstag fire.
- Where Forster is hiding in Paris, and whether anyone will reach him before the Nazis do.
- Number, location, and inmates of the additional camps north of Berlin.
- What happened to **Matern** after his breakdown at Langenberger.
- Fate of Czerwinski and Henning after the shootout — last seen with guns pointed at them.
- Göring's plan to "pin Papen against the wall."
- Katelbach's death and its political fallout (the salon was his project).
- The strategic endgame of the Schleicher–Nyssen anti-Hitler pact.

## S05E02

Babylon Berlin S05E02 — 1933, days after Hitler's inauguration. Two investigations advance while the new regime consolidates power.

Plot
- Opens on a press-ball night: an anonymous tip-off sends Lotte to Opernplatz at 14:30 with intel on the new Reich Chancellor. At the ball she and Gräf spot Dr. Forster, then both he and Gereon vanish.
- At HQ, Lotte briefs Dept A without Rath: Forster's receptionist Sibylle Nachtwey was killed at 201 Kurfürstendamm (blunt force); the calendar page for 30 January — the day of the murder — has been torn out. Böhm has already pulled the Cheruskerstrasse SA/police double-homicide from the political police (the case has at least two perpetrators).
- A parallel storyline introduces Doris Przybyszewski, new archive clerk at the Broadcasting House, learning to splice Reichstag recordings on 3:30 discs under the prickly Mr Przybylski.
- At a "birthday-Sacher-torte" editorial meeting, Katelbach pushes an Urgent Appeal to force SPD (20.4%) and KPD (16.9%) into a left front (37.3%) against the NSDAP's 33.1%, to be posted on Litfass columns before the 5 March election — the "last opportunity."
- Lotte interviews Mrs Forster: the doctor is a bundle of nerves, his trauma tied to Pasewalk. She points to marked passages in Mein Kampf, where Hitler calls his hospitalisation a "dark interlude" — but never names Forster.
- SA thugs in Tempelhof raid intellectuals carrying the Appeal; one is dragged to solitary. Meanwhile a new Nazi-aligned Detective Superintendent takes over HQ, declaring "the system of democratic aberration is behind us," deploying 5,000 new personnel, ending roll-calls with "Heil Hitler." An SA Sturmführer Döbrich is attached to the Homicide Division as "assistance."
- Gräf is sent to the Institut für Sexual Sciences: Hirschfeld's report on a patient is missing, and Hirschfeld himself is preparing to relocate to Zurich.
- Lotte tries Karlshorst army archives for Pasewalk patient files — Grundmann tells her viewing is now forbidden.
- The episode closes on the Mikado Bar (Moka Efti orbit): Gräf and Fred Jacoby are stopped in the street under §175-era harassment, Hirschfeld's transvestite licence waved at them. Inside, Mary the cabaret act has bolted to Paris; Lotte is dragooned onstage as "Carlo Cavaliere," singing a number built on the Hirschfeld-circle poem "And yet most are proud that they are cut from a different cloth" — a defiant "we are children of other worlds" set against the new brown air.

Important character actions

- Gräf: presses Hirschfeld's institute for Forster's missing report; gets publicly ID'd alongside Fred; recruits Lotte to save the show.
- Katelbach: drafts the Urgent Appeal, recruits literary signatories (Thomas Mann, Heinrich Mann, Arnold Zweig — he insists on Arnold over Stefan), and reads the appeal aloud.
- Litten: agrees to be the appeal's public face, gets the cake bill, walks Katelbach out past waiting brownshirts.
- Heymann: toasted as new editor-in-chief.
- Mrs Forster: hands over the Mein Kampf volume with marked lines, names Pasewalk.
- New Detective Superintendent: opens with a Nazi-aligned pep talk, deploys 5,000 personnel.
- Sturmführer Döbrich: SA "assistant" planted in Homicide.
- Fred Jacoby: warns Gräf that the new regime will not tolerate them; performs onstage with Lotte.
- Doris Przybyszewski: first day at the Broadcasting House archive.

Newly established names / terms
- Sibylle Nachtwey (victim), Paul Strelow (SA victim), Officer Hoffmann (police victim), Döbrich (Sturmführer), Doris Przybyszewski, Mr Przybylski, Grundmann (Karlshorst archivist), Heymann (new editor-in-chief), Franzi, Gustav.
- Locations: 201 Kurfürstendamm, Cheruskerstrasse, Pasewalk field hospital (west of Stettin), Karlshorst central army archives, SA barracks Berlin-Tempelhof, Alexandrinenstrasse boarding house, Dobrin (eatery), Broadcasting House, Institut für Sexual Sciences, Mikado Bar.
- Items: Hirschfeld transvestite licence; 3:30-minute Reichstag shellac discs and splicing protocol; Mein Kampf marked copy; the "Urgent Appeal" (Dringender Appell) calling for SPD–KPD electoral alliance.
- Vote shares stated: NSDAP 33.1 %, SPD 20.4 %, KPD 16.9 %, joint left 37.3 %.
- Slang: "friends of Dorothy," "brown air," "democratic aberration," "Carlo Cavaliere," "different from the rest" song.

Continuity facts worth carrying forward

- The torn 30 January page in Forster's calendar — the same day Hitler became Chancellor — is now the case's keystone.
- Wendt is flagged as compromised; Böhm is the working transfer point, the Superintendent has authorised Lotte to bypass political police "for now."

Jokes / references useful later

## S05E08

# S05E08 — Series Finale

## Plot

Set during the nights around the Reichstag fire, the finale cuts between four converging threads.

At the **Nyssen estate** the industrialist dinner goes ahead. Alfred Nyssen installs himself as honorary chairman of the "German Reich AG" and introduces Hitler to the assembled board (Quandt, Flick, Bosch, Krupp, Porsche, Merck, Opel, Siemens, Papen, Hugenberg), then walks Hitler through his ten-point plan — state-led economy, food self-sufficiency, rocket technology, overseas colonisation, the Defence Ministry in his own hands, Krupp as Economic Minister. Gereon, Charlotte and Helga are inside as part of the assassination plot; the bomb is supposed to be disarmed after the seating swap puts Annemarie Nyssen above Hitler, but the defusing code fails — the time fuse is still live. Wegener tries to escort Helga out; she resists. Alfred discovers the bomb under his mother's chair, argues with her, then chooses to stay with her. They die together in the explosion.

At the **Broadcasting House**, Fred Jacoby goes on air and announces Hitler's death as a manifesto — a generational confession by Weimar ghosts who killed one of their own to stop the alliance of capital and fascism. The broadcast is cut off as police and SA storm the building. Przybylski is with Jacoby inside.

A short **Gereon scene** follows the bomb: a voice (Forster's therapy cadence) guides him through breathing exercises while Charlotte calls his name.

The **official Nazi radio** then overwrites Jacoby's broadcast: Hitler is "safe and sound"; the Reichstag is on fire; communist arsonists must be hunted down.

A historical coda (titles over black) lists the real fates of Hans Litten, Marinus van der Lubbe, Ernst Gennat, Rosa Helfers, Ernst Thälmann, Otto Wels, Karl von Ossietzky, Kurt von Schleicher, Rudolf Diels, Edmund Forster, and the unaccounted-for German economic elite.

The episode closes on the **German-Swiss border, March 1933**, with Charlotte's voice-over farewell to Gereon — Berlin, "our Berlin," is gone.

## Important Character Actions

- Alfred Nyssen: presents the ten-point plan as conditions for his patronage; refuses to evacuate with his mother; dies with Annemarie in the bomb.
- Annemarie Nyssen: discovers the ticking bomb, begs Alfred to leave, accepts his decision to stay; dies with him.
- Gereon Rath: inside the estate during the plot; helps extract Helga; later guides or witnesses Hitler's rescue; remains in Berlin at episode's end.
- Charlotte Ritter: evacuates the Broadcasting House with Gereon; reaches the Swiss border alone.
- Helga Rath: physically resists Wegener's extraction before being pulled from the room.
- Georg Wegener: Nyssen family lawyer; escorts Helga out as planned.
- Adolf Hitler: survives — extracted alive from the bombed hall by Anno (or by Gereon alongside Anno).
- Anno Rath: revealed alive, present at Hitler's extraction.
- Fred Jacoby: reads the resistance manifesto on air; is overrun by police at the Broadcasting House.
- Przybylski: helps Jacoby in the booth; is locked inside with him.
- Potocnik: Broadcasting House colleague caught in the raid.

## Newly Established Names / Terms

- Anno Rath: Gereon's missing brother, alive and on the rescue team.
- Wegener (Georg Wegener): Nyssen family lawyer, already known, now active as a field operative in the extraction.
- Przybylski, Potocnik: Broadcasting House staff on the night of the broadcast.
- "German Reich AG": Nyssen's framing of Germany-as-corporation, with Hitler installed as board chairman.
- Industrialist board named on screen: Günther Quandt, Friedrich Flick, Carl Bosch, Alfred Krupp, Ferdinand Porsche, Karl Merck, Wilhelm von Opel, Hermann von Siemens, Franz von Papen, Alfred Hugenberg.
- Enabling Act, Night of the Long Knives, Röhm Putsch, "Thousand-Year Reich": namechecked in the closing coda.
- Historical figures appearing in the coda: Ernst Weiss (novelist of "The Eyewitness," based on Forster), Buchholz labour-education camp, Dachau.

## Continuity-Relevant Facts

- The Nyssen-MacGuffin estate bomb kills both Alfred and Annemarie; their patronage plot and the mother's revived authority both end here.
- Hitler survives; the assassination attempt fails. The Pasewalk file is no longer recoverable through the Nyssens.
- The resistance broadcast is aired long enough to be heard nationally before being silenced.
- The Reichstag fire begins the same night, giving the regime the cover narrative for the arrest of communists, socialists, and dissenters.
- Charlotte exits to Switzerland; Gereon stays behind — the lovers are separated.
- Anno Rath, long presumed dead, is revealed alive.
- Charlotte refers to "my brother" in the closing voice-over (the show has previously only named her sister Toni; this is a new on-screen relation or honourary figure worth flagging).
- Helga's fate after Wegener's extraction is not shown.
- The coda locks the fates of: Hans Litten (Dachau 1938), van der Lubbe (guillotined 1934), Gennat (stays on, dies 1939), Helfers (Gestapo target, post-war SPD rebuilder), Thälmann (shot 1944), Wels (Paris exile), Ossietzky (prison death), Forster (suicide August 1933), Schleicher (Night of the Long Knives), Diels (hands Gestapo to Himmler).

## Jokes / References Worth Carrying Forward

- "We are just the broken robots of the last war. And now we step back into the darkness." — Jacoby's broadcast becomes the show's final political thesis line.
- "Our Berlin" / "The city of Berlin that you showed me" — Charlotte's farewell anchors the series' central place-myth.
- "Miracles wait until the end" / "To ashes, to dust … but not quite yet" — closing song motifs; useful if any spin-off or epilogue material references the series' final image.
- The coda itself reads as a referential table for downstream historical fiction: every Weimar character named gets a real-world analogue named alongside.
- The Przybyszewski / Przybylski spelling gag from S05E02 lands here as Przybylski on the inside during the Jacoby broadcast — a small callback worth noting if the surname recurs.

## Unresolved Threads

- Helga's destination after Wegener pulls her out of the estate (and Moritz's fate, given his HJ enrolment in earlier seasons).
- Fred Jacoby's arrest, trial and fate after the broadcast is cut.
- Przybylski's and Potocnik's fates post-raid.
- Whether the Jacoby broadcast reached a national audience before being silenced, and its political effect.
- What becomes of the Pasewalk file now that the Nyssens are dead and Hitler is alive.
- Whether Charlotte's "brother" (named only in voice-over) survived and joined her in Switzerland.
- The longer-term fates of Böhm, Czerwinski, Henning, the SA-Sturmführer Döbrich, and Detective Superintendent Wendt inside the Nazi-ified Rote Burg.
- Charlotte and Gereon: whether separation is permanent or whether either attempts to cross back.
- Whether the SPD–KPD women's pact from S05E06 (Helfers / Völcker / Litten) survives once Litten is arrested.

## S05E04

# Episode S05E04 — "Mein Kampf" (or equivalent title)

## Plot

The episode opens with a 1918 flashback to the Pasewalk field hospital where Dr. Edmund Forster treats hysterical blindness in a private using suggestive therapy — the patient is Adolf Hitler; the therapy "cures" him, and Forster keeps the compromising psychiatric report.

The present begins on **January 30, 1933**, hours after Hitler becomes Chancellor. Rath and Dr. Forster are fleeing Berlin. Editor-in-Chief Heymann warns Rath that an arrest warrant is out for him and that no German outlet will publish the story; he urges Forster to reach Paris by express train and meet Dr. Ernst Weiss. The train to Paris is jammed with refugees; SA men board to check IDs. Rath uses his police rank ("official trips are vis major") to secure window seats and impersonate a prisoner escort.

On board a Prussian cop demands papers from a Jewish couple from Krakow (Eleanor and Erwin). Rath points out no Prussian–Thuringian border exists; the cop backs down. Rath and Forster detrain in Cologne for clothes and papers; Mrs. Hülsenbeck and an old schoolfriend disguise them.

At the Cologne address Gereon's father, **Engelberth Rath**, refuses to violate medical confidentiality for an old patient (his son Anno) and refuses to drag Mayor Adenauer into a matter whose background he doesn't fully understand. Gereon threatens him: he will remind Adenauer of the unsavoury pre-election affair that originally sent him to Berlin. Engelberth then tells him to find Adenauer during **Rose Monday** at the **Pänz Brewery**. At the brewery Adenauer ("Kölle Alaaf") agrees: a morning flight to Paris the next day, return by evening, in time for an election.

In Paris they are intercepted at the airfield by **Eduard von Kittlitz** (German Embassy), who carries a Göring/Reich Interior Ministry directive. Kittlitz insists on checking papers; Rath deflects, they bolt into the city's catacombs, and reach Dr. Weiss (Fürst / Fiquet's address in the 5th arrondissement, the German expatriate haunt). Weiss at first refuses to deal with a German police officer; Rath invokes his brother Anno, who studied under Forster. Weiss confirms: **Anno Rath is dead** — but, in voiceover correction, "It's complicated." Forster finally reveals the whole case to Weiss: Hitler's 1918 Pasewalk admission as Private Hitler, blistered by a British mustard-gas grenade on his own account, **hysteric** by Forster's diagnosis; the autosuggestive "cure". The scars on Anno came from an attack for this knowledge; Anno changed his name and was meant to be presumed dead. The psychiatric evaluations contradict Hitler's own myth-making.

Weiss agrees to break it through French dailies and takes charge of Forster from then on; Rath is told to go.

The closing scene is a satirical cabaret sketch before the new Chancellor: Hitler (in a knockabout turn with Otto Regensburger) wants to address the audience, is heckled about being "a circumcised guy," and insists on reading from *Mein Kampf*. Forster and Rath watch, in the audience. A call goes out to "SA Barracks in Ihrefeld, hurry" — SA men arrive at the airfield late. Adenauer, en route back, must make the Schnüsse Tring meeting in eight hours.

## S05E05

S05E05 of Babylon Berlin — "Episode 5" (titled by subtitles as "Communists – Urgent Appeal")

Episode plot
- Opens with a "Previously on…" recap: mentions Forster, the "suggestive therapy institute," Rath being told to take the express to Paris, contact with Dr. Ernst Weiss, and a press plan to expose Hitler.
- Spicheren, Moselle, 729 km from Berlin: two ex-servicemen at a drinking spot — "Mandel" (stitches up a comrade) and "Dr. Kornfeld." A lance sergeant named Franz Krajewski is recognized from a police visit; he shows a service book from the Grand Ducal Mecklenburg 90th Fusilier Regiment. He is later found stabbed to death at a Spicheren address "THE LADY / H. Schiele Hairdressing Salon." Rath and Gräf are on the scene. Rath orders Gräf to photograph everything and tells him not to mention the find to the homicide squad; Rath suspects Wendt's men (political police) will hijack the case, and wants Gräf at the autopsy because he doesn't trust the new pathology staff.
- At Behnke's boarding house, Elisabeth Behnke tells Katelbach she's been to the hairdresser and is meeting "Mrs. Luxemburg" — Clara Luxemburg, sister of Rosa Luxemburg. A flashback / parallel café scene: Clara, billed as the "youngest of the six Luxemburg siblings" (Anna, Maximilian, Mikolai, Jozef, Rozalia), born in Warsaw while the others were born in Zamość, fostered under the name Kasimirovska after her parents died. She produces a note Rosa jotted for her KPD founding convention speech (agenda item three, after Paul Levi, before Hugo Eberlein). She reveals Rosa's "deepest wish": a People's Front alliance of SPD and KPD against reactionary forces, suppressed under pressure from the all-male SPD/KPD leadership. Clara (called "Mrs. Luxemburg") tells two SPD women the KPD will only accept the merger if the SPD "unconditionally" joins them. She gives 24 hours to decide, sets a follow-up at Bayreuther Strasse 25 at 10 a.m., and claims powerful press and judiciary contacts are calling for the merger.
- Interspersed phone calls: someone (apparently a Nazi official in Paris) tells an operative that the Paris photo shows Rath and that "an enemy of the movement" must "disappear for good." A detective superintendent at the Red Castle reports to a "Councilor" that Rath missed his check-in after a failed arrest of Forster's murdered assistant's killer, and is now reported missing abroad — Paris, unauthorized leave. The Chief of Police has suspended Rath on suspicion of high treason. The same councilor (Wendt) then threatens Lotte Ritter: if she knows anything about Rath's "illegal activities" and withholds it, she is committing a crime and he will prosecute her.
- Meanwhile, the SA (brownshirts) raid the KPD agitation headquarters in Wedding under the slogan "For work, bread and freedom — a hand to a friend, a fist to an enemy!" Plainclothes CID officers take names at a registration table, including Max Matern (Camp Mitte), and Heymann of KPD leadership is brought in to identify comrades. Rath himself wakes up in Tempelhof SA barracks after being beaten and stitched up — "In purgatory." He sees Henning and Czerwinski, his homicide colleagues, among the arrested. The plan hatched: prison transport to Oranienburg; one or two guards in the cab, two armed brownshirts in back; escape "on the road" by overpowering the guards and seizing the cab. Brock (a small, poor-sighted, German-speaking comrade introduced by Heymann) joins the cell. Brock and another inmate volunteer to stab the two rear guards simultaneously while the rest take the cab; Matern (Rath) will lead. The escape is set for the next morning.
- Outside the Red Castle, Rath and Lotte meet. Lotte reports Krajewski stabbed just like Strelow, his service book shows Pasewalk, "like Wosniak and Forster, and Hitler." She concludes someone is eliminating anyone who saw Hitler at the Pasewalk field hospital, to destroy contradictory evidence — including medical files and photos. They agree to gather facts carefully before telling Gennat, since "it's about the Reich Chancellor." Lotte also presses Rath about a special French brandy he took from her and asks for it back.
- In a parallel political scene, an enormous SPD rally (police estimate ~200,000) is held against the new chancellor. Otto Wels, SPD chairman, gives a speech ("we are the Social Democrats … Berlin stays red … free men, free women … prevent the barbarians from ruling"). Rath/Lotte, watching from above, see Jacky and Lotte is called "a pinko." The KPD did not join; a wit notes "a camel through the eye of a needle" is likelier than a communist joining an SPD march — except Dr. Völcker and her circle. Song "When we walk side by side … a new era is dawning."

Rath unveils the real target: the Reichstag — "very well guarded; we won't get in unless we climb up."

Two men interrupt and force him to Wendt's office. He and Hans Litten are detained on suspicion of hiding documents; SA men are intimidating Litten at his door.
