## S03E01

## 剧情概述

- 玛姬、尼根和赫谢尔在爆炸后重逢。玛姬因尼根曾被自己交给克罗地亚人、又在金妮之死后试图杀死他而与他激烈争执；她承认自己从背后捅过尼根。
- 新巴比伦控制了甲烷罐，克罗地亚人担心这项技术落入对方手中。爆炸摧毁了大部分罐体，但路易斯藏有一个甲烷原型及制造图纸。
- 克罗地亚人返回蕾娜塔和她的家人所在据点，强迫急救学员丘乔抢救一名伤者，并以“杀掉正确的人来让其他人服从”的方式处决丘乔，进一步展示其残酷统治。
- 伊薇特提前两个月临产。为了让早产婴儿使用保温箱，玛姬、尼根、赫谢尔、路易斯和其他幸存者冒险穿过尸群，前往医院寻找甲烷设备。
- 尼根起初想独自留下，但在玛姬请求帮助后改变决定。众人成功启动保温箱并接生，婴儿暂时获得生机。
- 事件后，玛姬与赫谢尔的关系有所修复。尼根提出让玛姬及其人马留在曼哈顿堡垒，双方也可能与“砖块”合作。玛姬开始考虑留下，并计划与尼根一起寻找达玛。
- 结尾显示达玛仍然活跃。她从中城废墟中救出受伤的幸存者，宣称“每个帝国都始于破碎”，准备重新建立自己的势力。

## 重要角色行动

- **玛姬**：承认自己仍受格伦之死影响，却决定停止被愤怒支配；为救早产婴儿主动承担风险，并请求尼根留下协助。
- **尼根**：在争执中首次较直接地道歉；救下克罗地亚人，并参与寻找甲烷、保护婴儿；随后提出与玛姬共同经营曼哈顿据点。
- **赫谢尔**：坦白曾受达玛影响，甚至一度想杀死玛姬；回忆尼根曾保护自己的经历后，选择放下部分怨恨。
- **克罗地亚人**：继续用“家园”“未来”和资源交换包装暴力控制；杀死丘乔以震慑众人，但随后身受重伤，生死状态未明。
- **路易斯**：掌握甲烷原型和制造图纸，为救婴儿及曼哈顿未来提供关键技术。
- **蕾娜塔**：拒绝接受兄弟帮强加的保护协议，明确维护家人与社区的自主权。
- **达玛**：接管残局，搜集幸存者，继续推动建立新帝国。

## 新名称与术语

- **Mason W. Dillard（梅森·W·迪拉德）**：独居酒吧居民，利用大楼构建“闭环垂直食物链”（Closed Loop Vertical Food Chain，CLVFC），靠尸体、蛆虫、青蛙、火鸡和郊狼维持生态循环。

- **Renata（蕾娜塔）**：丘乔的家人及社区核心成员。
- **Yvette（伊薇特）**：早产孕妇。
- **Chucho（丘乔）**：接受急救训练、试图救治伤者的医生学员。
- **Luis（路易斯）**：掌握甲烷设备秘密的幸存者。
- **Mira（米拉）**：医院助产现场的成员。
- **San Lazaro**：圣拉撒路，希望与慈悲的守护圣人；象征“选择战斗或选择光明”。

## 连续性关键信息

- 金妮已经死亡并被尼根埋葬；赫谢尔亲眼见过墓地，也曾观察尼根是否会继续留下。
- 玛姬与尼根的关系从互相利用、仇恨和罪责，逐渐转向带有共同重建目标的脆弱合作。
- 尼根背部仍有玛姬留下的刀伤，需要路易斯处理。
- 甲烷技术不只关系到一罐燃料，还包括稳定供电、农业、狩猎和曼哈顿长期独立发展的可能。
- 达玛的控制方式会利用人们的恐惧、需求与归属感；赫谢尔已意识到自己曾被她操纵。
- 克罗地亚人留下的权力真空可能引发兄弟帮内部争夺，也可能让达玛直接接管其残余势力。

## 笑点与参考

- 尼根多次重复“我真该捅你了”，玛姬则以“你本来不会捅我”回应，成为两人关系中带有黑色幽默的固定玩笑。

## S03E02

# S03E02 — Episode Summary

## Plot

The episode opens in Negan's bar, where the eccentric Dillard lives among walkers he treats as pets, singing along to "Manhattan" by Lee Wiley. Maggie and Renata run their hospital community under strict secrecy — red-light warnings, cameras, window panels — terrified that outsiders will kill them to steal it. Armstrong returns from New Babylon with supplies, says goodbye, and heads home to reform the Federation from within now that its army is gone.

Negan and Maggie spot two of the Dama's people scavenging for food. They track them down and confront the Dama, who quotes Shakespeare (*Henry V*) and claims she "fixes" broken minds. A fight breaks out; Negan wants to kill her, but Armstrong intervenes and the Dama escapes. She is later captured and locked in the hospital basement, where she begins a hunger strike.

Maggie argues for rehabilitation — she didn't kill Negan, so she believes people can change. Negan counters that the community's values are "bullshit" if they keep a murderer alive, and that nothing survives without brutal pragmatism. He declares he lives in "Fuck-you-ville," not their community.

Meanwhile, Negan bonds with Dillard, teaching him video games and showing unexpected patience. Renata and Maggie debate opening the hospital to newcomers: Maggie wants to triple the population, plow Central Park, and train more doctors; Renata fears desperate outsiders. Renata ultimately refuses, but Maggie decides to send out a broadcast anyway. Two groups of survivors arrive. Only one can stay. They will "play a game" to decide.

The Dama is left alone with a knife and a choice: eat and try to change, or starve.

## Important Character Actions

- **Maggie** overrides Renata and sends the broadcast to attract survivors, betting on hope over secrecy.
- **Negan** wants the Dama dead, argues with Renata about the old world, and admits he cannot live in the hospital because he cannot kill who needs killing there.
- **Armstrong** leaves for New Babylon to change the Federation, telling Maggie she can now tell people about the hospital.
- **Renata** refuses to open the hospital but is overruled; she fears Negan's lingering darkness.
- **Dillard** tends his walker "pets," plays video games with Negan, and worries about him.
- **The Dama** refuses food, testing whether Maggie will let her die or kill her.

## Newly Established Names / Terms

- **Dillard** — oddball survivor living in Negan's bar; talks to walkers, plays video games, deeply desires connection.
- **Chaz** — Dillard's friend/bar mate.
- **Titicaca** — one of Dillard's walker "pets" he believes communicates with him.
- **Cockanspiel** — another bar regular; plays "fuck, marry, kill" with Dillard.
- **"Fuck-you-ville"** — Negan's term for his self-imposed exile outside the community.
- **The food chain** — Dillard's bar ecosystem: maggots on walkers → frogs → turkeys.
- **Renata's baby niece** — her motivation for wanting security.
- **The Game** — the method to decide which survivor group stays at the hospital.

## Continuity-Relevant Facts

- Armstrong's army contingent was wiped out; New Babylon's power structure is in flux.
- Hershel is implied to be under the Dama's influence ("You stopped Hershel. But you didn't stop me.").
- Negan still believes his family (Annie, Joshua) is better off without him.
- The hospital has electricity, weapons, food, and one doctor (Luis).
- Bruegel is dead; his coffee stash is now a tradeable commodity.

## S03E03

## S03E03

**Plot**

Recap context: Maggie wants to plow Central Park and bring in more people; Negan doubts "good people" still exist. In the episode, the Dama's suicide is confirmed — Negan admits he doesn't know what he expected to feel, while Maggie says the old hag "did you a favor." Negan refuses Maggie's request to help clear Central Park before the frost, saying he has business of his own. At the bar he confronts Dillard over "playing patty-cake with two flesh sacks" (the walkers he talks to) and forces the issue of trust; when Dillard refuses to kill one, Negan kills it himself.

Fabian's flare sends Maggie and Negan to the river, where survivors from Belleville arrive: their settlement was burned at dawn by a crew from Newark after years of raids, and they followed the light of the Statue of Liberty — lit as Negan's "welcome sign" — on a gamble it still meant hope ("Give me your tired, your poor, and your huddled masses"). Newark survivors wash up too, each side accusing the other of starting the fire. Maggie confiscates both groups' weapons and treats enemies side by side at the hospital, where Renata insists on deciding who stays only once she has all the facts. Supplies run short — the last morphine goes, and Negan reluctantly surrenders the bar's whiskey for use as a sedative. Hershel, guided by Luis, performs his first cricothyrotomy on Ray and succeeds. Negan stages a "Piñata game": Dillard swings a bat at the two tied-up leaders (Bauer and Claudia) until one volunteers to leave Manhattan, while the hospital's corpse-fueled power fails and all four ventilators drop — the group hand-bags patients until Maggie's counting rhythm brings the power back. Patients who were bitten turn, and Negan and Dillard fight walkers through the flooded basement.

Negan proposes letting the two groups fight it out; Maggie rejects the idea, arguing that she, Negan, and Renata were once enemies too, and that the refugees need something to fight for together. Renata offers the groups scythes — to use on each other or to turn the parkland into farmland ("Here, survival is not enough. Here, together, we live"). In the end, 146 of 148 refugees choose hope; only the two leaders, Bauer and Claudia, can't move past their war. Maggie and Renata toast with anything but champagne, and Maggie gives Hershel an heirloom that belonged to Glenn and his father before him.

**Important Character Actions**

- Maggie: defends Renata against Negan's doubts, confiscates weapons, splits and guards both refugee groups, keeps the methane tanks full, and holds the line that there must be a choice beyond killing — then quietly tells Hershel he has Glenn's goodness in him.

- Hershel: performs his first solo medical procedure (cricothyrotomy) under Luis's guidance and helps hand-bag patients during the outage; Maggie calls him a star.
- Luis: the hospital's only doctor, guides Hershel, loses a patient to complications from a broken arm, and tells the family with Maggie that it could have been an embolism or something unrelated.
- Dillard: refuses to kill his walker "family," gets dragged into the Piñata game, fights walkers beside Negan, and later distills bathtub gin with a Belleville survivor.
- Bauer and Claudia: the two feuding leaders, forced into the Piñata game, are the only two of 148 who can't accept the new order.

## S03E04

## Plot

Maggie and Luis's trade mission and Negan's construction work run in parallel, then collide in a disaster cliffhanger.

Cold open: Chris, a refugee from Maryland who saw the Statue of Liberty lit up and "decided to take a chance," arrives at the hospital with a sprained ankle, offering a bottle of Johnnie Walker Blue for pain relief. He tells of a woman at a cabin near Beacon (upstate) who can get her hands on anything for something of value. Renata and Luis vet his story separately; it checks out.

The deal: The hospital is out of morphine, suture kits, saline, IV bags, penicillin, and antiseptics. Maggie overrules Renata's objection to sending away their only doctor — Luis is the only one who can verify traded meds are legit — and sets off with him. At the Trader's outpost, Maggie offers blueprints for a process that turns the dead into energy ("as valuable as the sun"). The Trader keeps the van overnight to fetch the supplies; Maggie and Luis stay unarmed at her camp — a Presbyterian church family camp of 62 people (counting Genevieve's baby), including Gerald and his granddaughter Maylene. At the full-moon celebration, Gerald's community politely declines the technology: "Up here, we found connection. We let go of what was and found out who we wanted to be." The Trader returns with the meds: "It was a fair trade. This is gonna change everything... Maybe for the world."

On the road and at the camp, Maggie and Luis bond: Luis reveals his wife Marisol left at the outbreak to find her parents and never returned — he hasn't driven since. They talk about starting over; Maggie answers who she wants to be: "Someone who stops wearing death like it's the only thing she's got left... Someone who lets herself live." After dancing, she imagines kissing him ("a little smooch out of the deal") but pulls back and insists they leave.

Negan at the construction site: helping fortify the pantry/home against walkers with Fabian, Bucky, Dillard, Hershel, and Renata. A cage incident nearly kills Fabian; Negan saves him. Renata then offers Negan the last empty apartment, admitting she knows he murdered the Dama — she'd eaten some of her tray's tuna casserole, so why kill herself? — and lays down the rule: "no more killing," keys on the mantle.

Maggie returns with supplies and says "Same old, same old" about the trip. An earthquake hits (Dillard: a sinkhole swallowed half a city block on 8th Avenue a few years back). They scramble to save the pantry; the building collapses; Maggie's ring is lost in the rubble and she must be dragged out. The episode ends with them trapped among "hundreds of walkers": "Open the gates." — "You open the gates, we all die."

## Important Character Actions

- Maggie: overrides Renata to send Luis on the trade, accompanies him herself, gives away the dead-to-energy blueprints, admits she no longer knows who she is without protecting Hershel, imagines kissing Luis and cuts it short, then risks everything to recover her ring as the building falls.
- Negan: saves Fabian from the walkers, jokes with and praises Hershel ("You are a sparky little bastard. Kinda proud of you"), sees through what he thinks is Renata's blackmail offer (using the Dama's murder as leverage), and ends the episode facing the "open the gates" decision.
- Luis: opens up about Marisol for the first time in years, mentors Maggie through her fresh-start talk, and completes the trade; the commentary confirms he and Renata are siblings from East Harlem.
- Renata: vets Chris's story, opposes sending Luis away, reveals she knows Negan killed the Dama but offers him a home anyway, and orders the evacuation during the quake.
- Hershel: trades banter with Negan (counting game, "Half-Pint" nickname), helps at the site, and is frustrated they escaped with almost no food.
- The Trader: sizes up Maggie ("You don't want this trade. You need it. Self-protection in the end. Always."), takes the van overnight, delivers the goods, and warns of "recompense" if played.
- Gerald: hosts the full-moon celebration, declines the tech for his community ("The dead, they're dangerous... those old things, TVs, radios, phones, just noise").
