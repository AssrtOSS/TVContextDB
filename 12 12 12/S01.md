## S01E01

# S01E01 Summary — 12 12 12

## Episode Plot
Christmas Eve in Zurich. A master thief known as **"The Holy Ghost"** (Paul Galvin) has spent 12 months planning a heist to execute in 12 hours, with a 12‑day escape window. The target: **Lindenhof Bank** (L‑Hof), holding 800 million in 1,000‑CHF notes. FBI Special Agent **Jack** (demoted after a failed Newark takedown) has tracked Paul to Switzerland, believing the hit is seven days away (New Year’s Eve). Paul’s younger brother **Bobby** resurfaces, in deep debt to dangerous men who’ve taken him hostage and threaten Paul’s pregnant fiancée **Severine** and her father **Serge**. Paul discovers the heist is actually launching *tonight*, not in a week. He agrees to run the job to protect his family.

## Important Character Actions
- **Paul Galvin**: Reveals the 12‑12‑12 framework; confronts Bobby about stolen money (hidden at mother’s grave); agrees to the heist for Severine and their unborn child.
- **Bobby Galvin**: Admits he burned through Newark money on bad investments/poker; brought hostile creditors to Paul; reveals money location under duress.
- **Jack (FBI)**: Spars with Swiss Inspector **Thalmann**; deduces the Lindenhof’s faulty alarm (archaeological dig vibrations) is a deliberate desensitization tactic; realizes the heist is *now*; pursues Paul in a stolen patrol car.
- **Severine**: Confirms pregnancy; pushes Paul toward an honest life; becomes leverage for Bobby’s creditors.
- **Serge**: Blesses Paul and Severine’s marriage; warns Paul about fatherhood (“born in a stable doesn’t make you a horse”).
- **Alberich**: Discovers a **mercury tilt switch** (old alarm trigger) beneath the vault table — vibrations from drilling will set it off.

## S01E02

## Episode Plot
The episode intercuts the Zurich bank heist with its immediate aftermath. Paul’s crew targets Crédit Bern/Lindenhof, where roughly 800 million Swiss francs in cash (linked to Russian clients, the Zakatov family) is being consolidated in underground vaults. During the break-in, the team encounters an undocumented mercury tilt-switch alarm; Alberich disables it with remote guidance from an expert in Montreal, allowing drilling and extraction to proceed. Kira reroutes trains for the cash move, while decoy helicopters draw police toward Italy.

The job succeeds in stealing the money, but the escape fractures: Serge is captured by FBI agent Jack Strickland during the pursuit, one crew member is dead in the tunnel, and another is injured. The remaining crew regroups at a safe house as Paul becomes fixated on rescuing Serge, who is being held under guard at University Hospital. Paul and Bobby infiltrate the hospital, but Serge, terminally ill and unwilling to be used as leverage, jumps from a window and dies. By episode’s end, Swiss authorities track a microchipped security gun to a mountain location near the border and prepare a SWAT response, while a devastated Severine learns of her father’s death and Swiss officials ask Jack to work with them.

## Important Character Actions
- Paul Galvin: Leads the heist and insists on control and no casualties, but abandons the group’s escape plan to attempt Serge’s rescue; arms himself for the hospital operation.
- Bobby Galvin: Supports the heist and extraction, then joins Paul’s risky hospital rescue despite doubting the plan.
- Jack Strickland (FBI): Pressures Serge in hospital interrogation, reveals knowledge of Serge’s cancer, and frames his pursuit of Paul as both professional redemption and personal revenge (“I want my halo back”).
- Serge: Allows himself to be taken/cornered in the aftermath, speaks with Jack about family and belief, and ultimately dies by jumping from the hospital window.
- Alberich: Identifies and neutralizes the mercury tilt-switch alarm under extreme pressure, enabling the vault breach to continue.
- Gunda: Manages security-system constraints during the job and later confirms she followed Paul’s instructions to go dark.
- Kira: Manipulates Zurich transport/train routing to support the crew’s extraction.
- Ayah: Separates from the crew after providing bank codes/access help, raising concern about exposure.
- Kommissar Meier / Swiss authorities: Bring Jack into the post-heist investigation and later move on gun-tracking intelligence toward the border.

## Newly Established Names / Terms
- Crédit Bern: The Swiss bank/vault target holding the consolidated cash.
- Zakatov family: Russian clients liquidating accounts and moving funds into vault anonymity.
- Kleinmatz: Accountant/source who provides access and schematics tied to the bank setup.
- Mercury tilt switch / mercury alarm: Hidden legacy vault trigger that threatens to expose or destroy the operation.
- Frau Erlinger (Gunda Erlinger): Bank security officer revealed to be aligned with/extracted by the crew.
- Burkhalter: Crédit Bern bank manager interviewed during the investigation.
- Kommissar Meier: Swiss Federal Police official coordinating with Jack.
- Kira: Crew associate handling train/transport logistics.
- Ayah: Inside/helper who supplied bank codes and then splits off.
- Letzigrund Helipad / Gewerbeschule: Locations tied to the decoy extraction and recovery of kidnapped officers.
- H wing / University Hospital: Where Serge is held under guard before the rescue attempt.

## Continuity-Relevant Facts
- The operation follows the show’s 12-12-12 framework (12 months planning, 12 hours execution, 12-day window), with Jack explicitly citing the FBI “12 days” principle after the robbery.
- Jack’s fixation on the Galvins is tied to Newark and the death of Sarah; he sees capturing Paul as reclaiming his lost standing.
- The stolen funds are framed as nearly one billion dollars in Swiss francs, tied to Russian mafia-linked interests seeking anonymity ahead of legal changes on January 1.
- The heist relied on long-term preparation: tunneling through bedrock, a fake archaeological dig cover, repeated alarm desensitization, and inside access.
- Post-heist losses are severe: a body is found in the tunnel, Serge is captured and later dies, an injured crew member is mentioned, and Ayah has gone her own way.
- Police were initially misdirected by helicopters sent toward the Italian border; the crew also had inside access at Zurich Transport.

## Jokes / References Useful for Later Episodes
- “12 Days of Christmas” as a dark echo of the 12-day escape window.
- Jack’s “clown car” jab at Paul references their prior chase.
- “John Wayne” as a recurring label for Jack’s lone-cowboy style.
- Bobby’s repeated flippancy (“Kira-baby,” “sugar dumpling”) contrasts with the crew’s escalating stakes.
- Serge’s “born in a stable doesn’t make you a horse” ethos carries forward into the father/family theme (echoed in his and Paul’s values).

## Unresolved Threads
- Serge’s death leaves Severine shattered and creates immediate fallout for Paul and Bobby’s next moves.
- The mountain-border gun trace may be a real lead or another misdirection; SWAT mobilization raises the risk of capture.
- Ayah’s departure is unresolved: she could disappear, be leveraged, or expose the crew.
- The crew remains split and damaged (dead, missing/captured history, injured, and now grieving), with trust and cohesion under strain.
- Jack is being pulled into formal cooperation with Swiss authorities while still pursuing a personal vendetta against Paul.
- The stolen billion and the Zakatov/Russian connection remain dangerous loose ends, with powerful owners likely to respond.
