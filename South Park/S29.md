## S29E01

## S29E01 — E-Bike Chaos in "South America"

**Plot Summary**

Cartman manipulates his mom Liane into buying him a $2,000 Class 3 e-bike by claiming he's being bullied at school. He immediately uses it to torment the other boys, demanding everyone "suck his dick." Stan wants one too but his parents refuse — Randy is a militant traditional cyclist who despises e-bikes, comparing them to "slaves" that do the work for you. Stan, Kyle, and Kenny pool Stan's $200 from Grampa to buy three cheap Chinese e-bikes from City E-Bike, whose owner makes them sign absurd waivers (92% chance of exploding in the garage, "wink, wink"). Butters independently buys a used e-bike from Tolkien for $400 and hides it from his parents with a face-covering. Cartman's mom later buys him a "speed unlock dongle" to go even faster.

Randy and other adult cyclists — the "South American Cycling Club" — grow furious at kids dominating bike lanes, forming gangs, popping wheelies, and "probably doing drugs." They try to bribe police with cash and cigars (as Google instructed), but cops say the government won't act. The adults take matters into their own hands and attack the Chinese bike shop (Mr. Kim), a second Chinese bike business, and Walmart's e-bike section. Meanwhile Cartman appears on local TV news with voice distortion as gang leader "El Gordo," bragging that everyone has to "suck his dick" now.

Kenny brokers a peace meeting between the kids and the adult cyclists, but immediately afterward his Chinese e-bike explodes and he dies. The town unites in grief, and the mayor announces the **Kenny McCormick E-Bike Ordinance**, banning e-bikes.

**Important Character Actions**

- **Cartman** — lies to Liane about bullying to get an e-bike; terrorizes town demanding oral sex; becomes anonymous gang leader "El Gordo" on TV; gets a speed unlock dongle upgrade

## Plot

Girls want anti-aging face cream (Wendy's birthday request), kids use AI apps that rate facial symmetry, and boys obsess over jawlines, mewing and mogging. Ike Broflovski becomes a top aura farmer, beating Noah Bailey on the "South America Glazing" livestream hosted by Peter Mullen (sponsor: Sephora — "We belong to something beautiful"). Fourth-grader Chase Chadwick — the only perfect score on Chadmaxx — challenges Ike for recess.

Kyle forbids Ike from battling, which backfires: the school decides Ike is only "aura farming" and calls him cringe when he doesn't show. Meanwhile Stan takes Wendy into Sephora, where time dilates (17 hours inside = 13 minutes outside). Randy, who claims he only waited outside while taking Shelley, comes home visibly de-aged — so much that Sharon makes him enroll at the elementary school as the "new kid." He drinks Cocoa Puffs and beer, takes a shot of Jäger in Humanities, and hits on Bebe.

Cartman (absent from class all episode) takes Ike to Sephora for "samples"; in the back rooms the manageress ruins Ike's face with de-aging cream. Kyle and Stan break in to find them, get restrained by the sample-obsessed woman ("You like that on your face?"), and barely escape. Kyle consoles Ike — his face will normalize "in time" — and vows to back him. Ike finally appears; Kyle covers by claiming Ike was perfecting a "rizz face." ## S29E02

# S29E02 — "The Billionaire Problem" (working title from content)

## Plot

Garrison runs a "happy place" share in class. Peter presents the **South America Mall**; Butters says his happy place is Dr. Pimple Popper on YouTube, then pivots into a rant: data centers built by "weenie billionaires" in local backyards keep draining the town's electricity. His parents, Stephen and Linda Stotch, dismiss him as a "libtard" and note they already grounded him for it the night before. They have also been using AI to remodel every part of the house.

Cut to the Stotch backyard: a literal miniature town of billionaires — **Weenietown** — is operating there, complete with a mayor, a train ("Now arriving at Weenietown!"), and the anthem "We're in the Money." The Mayor of Weenietown holds an etiquette class for new billionaires: jets and yachts and drugs are fine, but going on the Joe Rogan podcast, posting on Instagram, having public opinions, or arguing with Mark Ruffalo / Macklemore is forbidden. The point is for billionaires to stay invisible.

An exterminator visits the Stotches. He confirms the billionaires are a "highly aggressive non-native species," sprays them, and warns that once they take hold "it's pretty much it for the town." He refuses to do more because "it's South America. Nobody really gives a shit."

Butters canvasses neighbors. Nobody cares — they are all fixated on **Grand Theft Auto VI** releasing in November and just want to play without the power cutting out. Butters convinces a local news team that billionaire data centers could wipe out GTA VI save points and tank the launch. The news goes live with an "invasive billionaire" exposé. Mid-broadcast, however, the coverage flips: the Mayor of Weenietown has bought the local media, and now the billionaires are officially "good for our lakes and wildlife." One new billionaire blows it by getting into a public fight with **Macklemore**. The Mayor decides to call in **Miss Ladypenis** to relocate the problem billionaires.

Meanwhile, Stephen and Linda's marriage collapses over Linda's secret decision, months earlier, to get Butters a measles booster — she forged Stephen's signature on the school forms because Stephen (a podcast listener) had refused. Linda tearfully tells Stephen she "just really wanted him to be able to go to school." Stephen calls the FBI to report "bestiality" with a "blind sheep" — actually Linda — and the FBI director ends up on the line as Linda confesses.

Butters receives a prerecorded message from Miss Ladypenis: if he helps relocate the problem billionaires, Weenietown will move out of his backyard. He sets a trap — pretending he wants to hear billionaires' opinions, drawing them in like moths — and rounds them up while singing a parody of "Loo, loo, loo, I've Got Some Puddles." Weenietown agrees to relocate to "the next backyard over."

Final shot: a Trump-shaped figure delivers a campaign speech ("demolish the deep state," "expel the warmongers," "rout the fake news media," "liberate America") while standing in Stark's Pond, farts, splashes, and the "We're in the Money" reprise plays.

- Garrison: hosts the "happy place" share; tries to redirect Butters off the billionaire tangent.
- Peter: opens the share with his "happy place" — the South America Mall.
- Local news team (Tom + anchor): initially runs the invasive-billionaire story after Butters pitches the GTA VI angle, before being overridden by the Mayor's media purchase.
- Exterminator: treats billionaires like an invasive pest; sprays them, declines to do more, blames local apathy.
- Trump: cameo at the end in Stark's Pond, delivering campaign rhetoric.

## S29E06

## S29E06 — "City Ending" / The Kenny-Corruption Reveal

### Episode Plot
The town is still reeling from rolling power outages just as Grand Theft Auto VI finally releases in November. Angry residents confront the mayor, who blames aging infrastructure while dodging questions about billionaire data centers draining the grid. The crowd jumps to the conclusion that the blackouts are Chinese cyberattacks, so "there must be a Chinese spy in town," and they fixate on Mr. Kim, who now runs a "City Massage" parlor out of the former Chinese e-bike shop.

Meanwhile Kenny has returned from the dead in spectral form, drifting through town, playing GTA VI and casually shooting people. Stan, Kyle and Stan deduce his soul cannot rest and that he is trying to point them toward the truth behind the outages. Following the trail to Butters' house, they find he has been granted endless wishes and a remodeled home, and learn he cut a deal with the billionaires. Butters helped round up the "problem" billionaires, dumped some in Nashville, and relocated the rest into Peter Mullins's backyard.

The investigation leads to City Massage, where agents offer Mr. Kim $5,000 hush money to keep his mouth shut, and to the real data center in Peter's never-entered backyard. When the boys explain the corruption chain to a "sir," he feigns sympathy, then tricks them with a "humid / your dick sticks to your balls / freeze!" bit, orders them tied up and thrown in the lake, and reveals a deeper conspiracy: Kenny's death "fulfilled a need," and whoever "ruined November" means more kids must die. Randy admits "we all did" it. The mayor publicly confesses at a town meeting that he took bribes and made the billionaire deal, framing it as necessary to beat China in the AI race while insisting it must not change who they are. The billionaires then "help" the town by shooting Mr. Kim into space, delivered as a musical number.
