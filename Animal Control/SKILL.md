## Premise & World Setting

**Setting**: Seattle municipal Animal Control department (filmed in Vancouver). The precinct handles wildlife calls, pet complaints, exotic animal incidents, and animal welfare enforcement.

**Core Premise**: "Animals are simple, but humans are not." A group of Animal Control officers navigate absurd animal calls while their personal lives — relationships, bureaucracy, career ambitions, immigration issues — are far messier.

**Tone**: Workplace comedy with heart. Deadpan absurdity (cougar in a hot tub, ostrich chase, python around a neck) balanced with character-driven arcs: Frank's cynicism vs. hidden competence, Shred's optimism tested, Emily's rule-following vs. chaos, Victoria's immigration struggle, Patel's family dynamics.

**Recurring Elements**:
- Dispatch radio calls ("Animal Control dispatch, we have a…")
- Episode titles = the two animals featured that week
- Frank's "Slim Jim" habit / animal-reading "superpower"
- Inter-precinct rivalry with Templeton's unit
- Union politics, budget cuts, "essential worker" status debates

## Speaking Styles & Translation Notes

### Frank (Joel McHale)
- **Voice**: Deadpan, sardonic, verbose but precise. Literary references, obscure facts dropped casually.
- **Patterns**: "Here's the thing…" / "Let me explain…" / Animal behavior monologues delivered with authority.
- **Chinese**: Use 老练、毒舌、书卷气 tone. Classical idioms (成语) for his "well-read" flex. Short, punchy retorts.

### Shred (Michael Rowland)
- **Voice**: Earnest, slightly nervous, optimistic, asks "dumb" questions that aren't dumb.
- **Patterns**: "So…" / "Wait, really?" / Tries to follow protocol, Frank derails him.
- **Chinese**: 老实、略显笨拙但真诚. Polite register (您/请) vs. Frank's casual.

### Emily (Vella Lovell)
- **Voice**: Cheerful but anxious, over-prepares, rule-citing, "team building" speak.
- **Patterns**: "Okay, team…" / "Per protocol…" / Spirals when things go off-script.
- **Chinese**: 职场管理腔、略带焦虑的热情. Formal "按照规定" vs. actual chaos.

### Patel (Ravi Patel)
- **Voice**: Pragmatic, tired-dad energy, dry wit, family references.
- **Patterns**: "My wife…" / "That's not in the budget" / Union jargon when relevant.
- **Chinese**: 务实、生活化、偶尔吐槽. "老婆大人" references.

### Victoria (Grace Palmer)
- **Voice**: Sharp, no-nonsense, competent, occasional vulnerability re: immigration.
- **Patterns**: Direct commands on calls. "Patel, handle the owner." / Quiet frustration re: green card.
- **Chinese**: 干练、利落、偶尔脆弱. Minimal filler words.

### Templeton (Gerry Dee)
- **Voice**: Aggrieved, competitive, petty, "I could do it better" energy.
- **Patterns**: "In my precinct…" / "That's not how we do it…" / Basketball trash talk.
- **Chinese**: 酸溜溜、爱较劲、官僚腔.

## Cultural Context for Translators

1. **US Municipal Animal Control**: Not police; limited authority (no guns, cannot arrest). Handle stray/wild animals, bite reports, neglect, licensing. Seattle setting → rainy, outdoorsy, tech-worker demographic jokes.

2. **Fox Broadcast Sitcom Conventions**: 22-min episodes, cold open → title card → three acts. Network TV standards (no F-bombs, sexual innuendo okay). Laugh-track-free single-cam.

3. **American Workplace Dynamics**: "At-will" employment, union representation (Frank is union rep), HR complaints, "essential worker" pandemic-era debates (S2E1).

4. **Immigration Subtext**: Victoria's green-card marriage (S2) reflects real US immigration pathways. Citizenship test = civics + English. Translate with gravity, not comedy.

5. **Animal References**: Species names must be accurate (cougar ≠ mountain lion ≠ puma; hellhound = slang for aggressive dog; sturgeon = fish). Episode titles are poetic pairings, not always the main animals.

6. **Canadian Filming / Seattle Setting**: Vancouver doubles for Seattle. Occasional Canadian spelling/props slip in. Don't over-localize to Chinese cities — keep Seattle references.

7. **Recurring Gags**: Frank's Slim Jim, Shred's nickname origin mystery, Frank's dad Jimmy, Templeton's basketball grudge, Dolores/Frank hookup ambiguity.
