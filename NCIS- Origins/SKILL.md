# NCIS: Origins — Background for Subtitle Translation

A police-procedural drama on CBS and the sixth installment in the NCIS franchise, set in 1991 at the fledgling NIS (Naval Investigative Service) field office at Camp Pendleton, California. The series premiered on October 14, 2024, on CBS in the United States and streams on Paramount+; internationally it airs on Global/StackTV in Canada and AXN Asia in Southeast Asia. Season 1 ran 18 episodes (Oct 14, 2024 – Apr 28, 2025); Season 2 ran 18 episodes (Oct 14, 2025 – May 5, 2026); Season 3 (10 episodes) is scheduled to premiere Oct 6, 2026, on CBS. As of this writing 36 episodes have aired. The series is a prequel to the flagship NCIS (2003–present) and is created by Gina Lucita Monreal and David J. North; Mark Harmon narrates as an older Gibbs and appears in the Season 3 lineup.

Chinese title (mainland convention): 海军罪案调查处：起源. An alternate Chinese subtitle, 海军罪案调查处：溯源, also circulates on Chinese streaming pages; translators should follow the platform-specific title for that release but may use either 起源 or 溯源 as the subtitle word. Both 大陆/百度百科 and the 海军罪案调查处 franchise use 罪案调查处 (not 犯罪调查处) as the established rendering; Taiwan's rendering is 重返犯罪现场.

## World setting

1991, post-Cold War Southern California. The Camp Pendleton office of the Naval Investigative Service (NIS), the forerunner agency to NCIS (renamed in 1992), is a small, under-resourced, ragtag unit led by veteran agent Mike Franks out of a converted 1990s set built at Fort MacArthur in San Pedro. The team investigates crimes involving U.S. Navy and Marine Corps personnel in the Camp Pendleton / San Diego area — murders, drug rings, espionage, fraud, sexual assault, missing weapons — and cooperates with the San Diego County Medical Examiner's Office, the FBI (Fornell), local sheriff's departments, the Veterans Administration, and the Daly's Tavern neighborhood bar. Daily life is colored by 1990s technology (car phones, fax, no smartphones), the just-ended Gulf War, post-Tiananmen / Soviet-collapse geopolitics, and the era's rock radio (Metallica's "Enter Sandman" is the premiere-episode motif and the show's recurring visual/sonic shorthand for Gibbs' post-trauma state).

## Overall plot premise

A grief-stricken young Leroy Jethro Gibbs joins Franks' Pendleton NIS team as a probationary special agent months after the murder of his wife Shannon and daughter Kelly. Each episode is framed by an older Gibbs (Mark Harmon) in voiceover, reflecting on how the cases, mentors, and personal losses of 1991 forge the rules, loyalties, and silences he carries into the later NCIS era. Recurring arcs include Gibbs' trauma and Rule #12 ("Never date a coworker") origin, his second marriage to Diane Sterling, Lala's fight to be taken seriously as a Latina ex-Marine agent, Franks' rebellious streak against NIS headquarters in D.C., and the team's tense relationship with their SAC Cliff Wheeler. Season 2 picks up after a Season 1 car-crash cliffhanger and follows Gibbs maturing into a field leader while the team handles Cold War residue, gang violence (Manny), and new NIS-vs-NCIS transition politics.

## Speaking styles and cultural context

Dialogue is workplace-procedural American English with period flavor: 1990s military and law-enforcement shorthand, profanity-moderated banter, and a hard-boiled dry wit that keeps the trauma-driven plots from sliding into melodrama. Franks speaks with veteran-grizzled, rule-bending authority ("probie", "squid", "jarhead"); Lala mixes street-Spanglish with Marine discipline; Ducky's cameos carry British-flavored eloquence and forensic aphorisms; Kowalski uses Korean War POW-era gravitas; Wheeler speaks in D.C.-bureaucrat register; Gibbs himself is mostly laconic, often answering in single sentences or head-tilts, with occasional fourth-wall-feel monologues delivered as Harmon's voiceover.

Cultural references for translators: military ranks and pay grades (E-1 through O-5 etc.), U.S. Navy/Marine Corps terminology (PO, JAG, brig, liberty, liberty call, MASH, line, CMC), law-enforcement ranks (Special Agent, SAC, ASAC, GS scale), Coronado / San Diego-area geography, 1991 pop culture (Metallica, Nirvana, Gulf War, Soviet collapse, Magic Johnson, Operation Desert Storm), and the pre-smartphone era's reliance on payphones, fax machines, and car phones. Payómkawichum and Kumeyaay tribal references around Tish and Témet should be rendered with the established tribe name (帕奥姆卡维楚姆族 / 库梅亚族) rather than generic "Native American."
