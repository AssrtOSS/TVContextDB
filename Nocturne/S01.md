## S01E04

## Episode plot
- News coverage escalates around Rebecca Mendel’s disappearance and links it to other missing residents; public scrutiny grows over Detective Sam Mendel’s absence and silence.
- Jonah flies in to tell Saga in person that Sam is dead. Saga, already reeling from Rebecca’s disappearance, is convinced Jurek Walter is responsible for both Rebecca and now Sam.
- At the facility where Saga is held, she endures medication routines and escalating conflict with staff (Esther, Lloyd, and others). After confrontations and a failed attempt to force her way out with a stolen badge, she is subdued and restrained.
- In the RHU, nurse Karl Anders becomes increasingly rattled by news of Sally Gifford’s disappearance. Jurek Walter manipulates Karl’s fear and dependence, denying involvement while framing himself as Karl’s only connection and “lifeline.”
- FBI/local leadership identify Karl as the likely messenger after Saga flags him leaving the RHU agitated. Meanwhile, investigators revisit a coded Cyrillic letter (“Release Michael”) addressed to “FW Proctor,” determined to be a dead-drop location rather than a lawyer.
- To provoke Walter into contacting his network, Quinn and Lynn pressure Rola to allow an unauthorized transfer ruse: Walter is told he will be moved to Wasatch Ridge Secure Psych Unit in Utah. The bluff appears to work.
- Warned of the imminent move, Walter urgently sends Karl to deliver a message to his contact, claiming there is no time to mail it. Surveillance teams track Karl to the drop site, but the operation goes badly: the drone spooks the courier, Jonah/Lynn pursues, shots are fired, and the messenger escapes. The team recovers the letter, but the person delivering it gets away.

## Important character actions
- Saga: Receives confirmation of Sam’s death from Jonah; voices certainty that Walter is behind Rebecca’s and Sam’s fates; resists facility control, clashes with staff, and makes an unsuccessful escape attempt.
- Jonah: Delivers the news about Sam to Saga in person; later participates in the field operation tracking the messenger.
- Jurek Walter: Manipulates Karl emotionally, probes his doubts, pressures him to act as courier, and reacts strategically to the threatened Utah transfer.
- Karl Anders: Serves as Walter’s nurse/contact in the RHU; shows visible agitation; ultimately agrees to deliver Walter’s letter despite fearing for his job.
- Quinn: Coordinates the investigation, identifies Karl as the likely messenger, and helps push the fake transfer plan to force Walter’s hand.
- Detective Lynn: Works with Quinn on the transfer bluff and leads the pursuit at the dead drop, losing the suspect but securing the letter.
- Rola: Reluctantly allows the unauthorized transfer ploy after being pressed on the urgency of finding Felicia.
- Chief Dave Ellison: Manages public pressure and fallout from renewed fear around Walter and the missing persons cases.
- Esther / Lloyd: Enforce control over Saga in the facility; Lloyd is identified by a patient as abusive, contributing to the volatile ward environment.

## Newly established names/terms
- Karl Anders: RHU nurse identified as Walter’s probable outside messenger.
- FW Proctor: Addressee on the coded letter; not a real attorney but tied to a surveillable dead-drop location.
- Wasatch Ridge Secure Psych Unit (Utah): The facility named in the fake transfer threat used to pressure Walter.
- Sally Gifford: Former nurse connected to Walter; reported missing along with her family.
- Michael Frost: Missing person who has unexpectedly returned after three years, reigniting attention on the case.
- Felicia: Still-missing person whose possible survival drives urgency in the investigation.
- RHU (Restricted Housing Unit): High-control area where Walter is held and where patients lose privileges.
- Code gray: Facility alert called during Saga’s escape attempt in the female ward.

## Continuity-relevant facts
- Public narrative now ties Rebecca Mendel’s disappearance to a broader pattern of missing residents and intensifies focus on Sam Mendel’s absence; Jonah confirms Sam is dead.
- Saga remains institutionalized under strict medication/control protocols and is explicitly threatened with transfer downstairs to restricted housing if she continues resisting.
- Sally Gifford disappeared abruptly, leaving behind passport, jewelry, and purse; investigators suspect a burner/mobile was used by an anonymous caller.
- A Cyrillic-coded message reading “Release Michael” was previously found and linked to the FW Proctor drop point.
- Walter can still reach the outside through at least one staff intermediary (Karl), validating fears that confinement has not fully severed his influence.
- The FBI/local plan succeeds in forcing communication but fails tactically: the letter is recovered, yet the courier escapes, preserving uncertainty about Walter’s wider network.

## Jokes or references useful for later episodes
- Esther’s dry line to Karl, “You ever just do flowers?” about his disturbing drawing, underscores the bleak humor and tension in the facility.
- Josie’s post-release fantasy: eating diri djon djon and watching “Love Is Blind.”
- Karl and Walter’s repeated refrain, “It’s the little things,” becomes a pointed motif around small comforts, manipulation, and control.
- Jonah/Saga exchange includes a dark aside about Walter (“Someone should bury him”), reflecting how personal grief is curdling into fixation.

## Unresolved threads
- Who Walter’s escaped messenger/contact network beyond Karl is, and whether Karl can still be leveraged or has fully compromised himself.
- The contents and full implications of the recovered letter from the FW Proctor drop.
- Felicia’s fate and location, and whether Walter’s forced response brings investigators any closer to her.
- The truth behind Sally Gifford’s disappearance and her family’s fate.
- The significance of Michael Frost’s return after three years and any connection to Walter or “Release Michael.”
- The consequences of Saga’s failed escape attempt and escalating conflict with staff inside the facility.
- The status and fallout of Detective Sam Mendel’s death within the larger investigation.
