# Not Going Out S15E03 — Episode Summary

## Plot
Lee is babysitting Olivia, a 10-year-old girl (a friend/neighbour's daughter), while Lucy is out at a restaurant. Olivia has been suspended from school for graffiti — "Life Is Poo", a poo drawn in marker on the head teacher's car bonnet. She defends herself with "art isn't about effort, it's a simple idea that gets a strong reaction", and shows Lee a photo of fresh graffiti in a derelict warehouse at the end of her road that "looks like a Banksy". Lee, spotting a chance to be the man who discovered an original Banksy, goes to the warehouse that night and removes the entire door with his credit-card toolkit screwdriver, taking it home. Lucy is horrified: he dragged it through the street past dozens of doorbell cameras. When Lee returns to take it back, he accidentally kicks a hole in the artwork and is caught by a night security guard who — because Lee's face is covered and he's holding genuine Banksy spray cans he pocketed — becomes convinced Lee IS Banksy. The guard blackmails him: stay anonymous and Lee gets off, but he wants an original Banksy of his own, made specially for him, by morning. Lee spends the night forging one using his theory of "juxtaposition", producing absurd concepts (Dalek Firing Out A Party Popper, Margarita with extra mushroom...cloud, Tooth Fairy with a pneumatic drill, Nun with a stun gun, Angela Rippon versus Predator) before settling on a childish scribbled poo, which the guard — claiming it's satire about AI-era art — delightedly accepts and asks him to sign. Lee signs it "Banksy". Lucy predicts the guard will try to sell it and get exposed as a forger, unravelling everything. Lee goes to confess, but the guard has already called a press event at the warehouse to unveil "his" Banksy. Cornered in front of the press, Lee blurts out the truth — and Olivia, who has followed him, reveals SHE painted the original door artwork as a prank about being punished for graffiti while Banksy gets famous, using a stencil bought from Amazon. The press devours the story; Lee's role as the gullible middle-aged man who stole, damaged, impersonated, and forged Banksy is publicly mocked. He leaves to get milk (they still have none — running gag).

## Important Character Actions
- **Lee**: Tries an attention-check fake news headline on Olivia (Taylor Swift arrested for drowning a ferret); steals the door; kicks a hole in the artwork; impulsively claims to be Banksy to the guard; forges a poo artwork and signs it "Banksy"; confesses at the press conference.
- **Olivia**: Suspended for graffiti; pranks the art world with a fake Banksy (Amazon stencil); publicly exposes the whole hoax; earlier told Lee "art isn't about effort" — the line Lee later reuses to justify his forgery.
- **Lucy**: The exasperated voice of reason — pushes Lee to return the door, predicts the forgery will unravel ("Of course he's going to sell it"), delivers the "wacky-twat-ometer" ratings and the Lassie line ("You're Lassie trying to clear up after it crapped on the lawn").
- **The security guard**: Star-struck Banksy fan; blackmails Lee into painting him an original; dreams of being "the man who put the security into security guard"; promptly tries to sell the piece to the press for "six zeroes — with at least a one at the front".

## Newly Established Names / Terms
- **Olivia** — the 10-year-old girl Lee babysits; her mother is a friend of Lucy's.
- **"Life Is Poo"** — Olivia's graffiti artwork (and the episode's title-style motif; Lee's forged Banksy is also a poo, his self-declared "poo period" to match Picasso's blue period).
- **Banksy** — central reference; the anonymous street artist, described as a "nondescript, boring, middle-aged northern bloke".
- **Wacky-twat-ometer** — Lucy's scale (1–10) for rating how badly Lee has messed up.

## Continuity-Relevant Facts
- Lee and Lucy are married, living together, empty-nesters (post-S14 countryside move); Lee babysits for friends.
- Lee is established as 37 ("said local man, Lee, 37" — and corrected as such later).
- Lee owns a credit-card toolkit (a gift from Lucy) he usually uses for cleaning his toenails.
- The couple still has no milk — gag opens and closes the episode.
- Lee denies being, variously: Keyser Soze, The Masked Singer, Jack the Ripper, The Stig, Spider-Man, Lord Lucan, the Honey Monster.

## Unresolved Threads

## S15E05

# Not Going Out S15E05 — "The Craft Market" (Dream Episode)

## Episode Plot
Lee and Lucy spend the day at an alternative craft market where Lucy is trying to sell her handmade dreamcatchers. Lee relentlessly mocks the "witchy-woo-woo" crowd — crystals, horoscopes, customers named "Moondust" — while Lucy struggles to make a single sale in three hours. Lee buys a £45 pyrite stone ("attracts wealth") to support her, then tries to return it five minutes later when a scratchcard loses. The stallholder Saffron refuses, claiming Lee "flooded it with negative energy." Lee reveals he's a trading standards officer; Saffron counters she prosecuted pyramid schemes in ancient Egypt. Lee explodes, calls the whole scene "horse shit," upsetting Lucy. He tries to make amends by buying more stock and a £50 "lucky salt stone of the Atacama Desert," claiming a clever plan to boost sales. The market turns hostile when Lee accidentally implies Lucy is incontinent (misreading her wave-design sign as "bright yellow piss"). A mob chases him screaming "Burn him!" — at which point Lee wakes up in bed. Lucy reminds him they're going to the market today. He dismisses the dreamcatchers as "a piece of old crap." A "wise woman" voice intones: "You're still asleep." Lee screams. Theme song plays.

## Important Character Actions
- **Lee**: Mocks spiritual market culture; buys pyrite stone to help Lucy; tries to return it after 5 minutes; reveals trading standards officer identity; accidentally outs Lucy as incontinent to entire market; claims a "clever plan" involving salt stone; wakes up dismissing dreamcatchers as rubbish.
- **Lucy**: Vulnerable about her craft; hurt by Lee's patronising "support"; cries when Lee destroys her confidence; sign misread as incontinence advertisement.
- **Saffron** (stallholder): Claims past life as ancient Egyptian trading standards officer prosecuting pyramid schemes; refuses refund on "spiritually damaged" goods; sells £50 Atacama salt stones for "elderly goats" charity.
- **Moondust** (customer): Self-named crystal believer; reads Lee's "negative energy."
- **Other stallholder**: Collaborates with Lee on reciprocal purchase scheme ("tit for quite literally tat").

## Newly Established Names/Terms
- **Moondust** — customer's chosen name at market
- **Saffron** — rival stallholder, claims past-life Egyptian prosecutor
- **Atacama Desert lucky salt stone** — £50 "healing/rejuvenating" rock salt
- **Elderly goats charity** — Saffron's claimed cause for stall profits
- **Wise Woman** — dream-figure who breaks fourth wall at episode end

## Continuity-Relevant Facts
- Lee is a **trading standards officer** (previously unmentioned in this context).
- Lucy's dreamcatcher business is a new venture; she's been up all night worrying.
- The couple are in their **empty-nest countryside phase** (S15 setting).
- Lee's scepticism toward alternative therapies is consistent (cf. S1 Kate's health food).

## Jokes/References for Later Episodes
- **Dreamcatcher logic**: "Net catches bad dreams, good dreams filter through feathers" — Lee: "Unless you're the pigeon. Bet his mum's having nightmares."
- **Horoscope takedown**: "Being Pisces only tells us your mum and dad had it off roughly in May."
- **Reciprocal purchase scheme**: "Tit for quite literally tat" — buying from each other's stalls to fake sales.
- **Pyrite stone**: £45, "attracts wealth" — fails on scratchcard test.
- **Atacama salt stone**: £50, "healing/rejuvenating" — Saffron's proof: "I'm 46."
- **Incontinence sign gag**: Lucy's golden wave sign misread as bright yellow urine stream; Lucy: "I'm raising money for a Louis Vuitton handbag!"
- **Meta ending**: Entire episode is a nightmare the dreamcatchers were meant to prevent; Wise Woman: "You're still asleep" — possible double-dream structure.

## Unresolved Threads
- Whether the "wise woman" final line implies a second dream layer or is purely a comedic button.
- Whether Lucy actually goes to the market in reality (episode ends at wake-up).
- Lee's trading standards officer role — one-off joke or new recurring detail?

## S15E04

# Not Going Out S15E04 Summary

## Episode Plot
Lee becomes obsessed with winning a local radio competition ("Make me filthy rich right now" — £10,000 prize), entering ~100 times via premium-rate texts. Two women from the local church (Olivia and Lauren) knock on the door to proselytise. They get drawn into Lee and Lucy's argument about money: Lucy accuses Lee of being tight (he stopped during sex to answer the phone; he spent £100+ on competition entries), while Lee accuses Lucy of materialism (essential oils, wanting a hot tub). The church women quote Bible verses (1 Timothy 6:10 — "love of money" misquote; Proverbs 29:11; Luke 12:15; Hebrews 13:5; James 1:19; Ecclesiastes 5:10) to mediate. Both Lee and Lucy reveal childhood roots: Lee grew up poor (hiding from bailiffs, got a shaved-head Barbie with Biro beard instead of Action Man); Lucy grew up comfortable but controlled (holiday home near Avignon, forced skiing holidays, Clarks "sensible" shoes, grade-6 violin). They reach mutual understanding and hug — just as the radio station calls. Lee ignores it (ten rings). The call was for **Lauren**, who wins £10,000 live on air and announces she'll buy a hot tub. Lucy screams for the letter opener (referencing the Hugh Grant film *Heretic* mentioned earlier).

## Important Character Actions
- **Lee**: Enters radio competition ~100× (£1/text + network rate); stops during sex to answer phone; hides competition spend; reveals traumatic poverty childhood; finally ignores winning call to hug Lucy.
- **Lucy**: Confronts Lee's money obsession; reveals her own controlled upbringing; buys essential oils ("it says essential in the name"); wants hot tub for "health benefits"; reaches truce with Lee.
- **Olivia (church visitor)**: Quotes Scripture accurately; mediates with "quick to listen, slow to speak"; notes they were "sent" by Sandra (rota organiser).
- **Lauren (church visitor, new)**: Misquotes Bible (Pet Shop Boys lyric); hasn't read past Genesis 3; wins £10k on radio; immediately spends it on a hot tub.

## Newly Established Names/Terms
- **Radio Roar** — local station running the "Make me filthy rich right now" competition (00444 444).
- **Josh** — competition caller (cold-caller mistaken for radio).
- **Sandra** — church rota organiser who "sent" Olivia and Lauren.
- **Chamrousse** — French ski resort Lucy was forced to visit at 16.
- **Avignon** — location of Lucy's family holiday home (15 miles outside).

## Continuity-Relevant Facts
- Lee's childhood poverty: father hid him from landlord/bailiffs; sofa repossessed; Oxfam Barbie modified into Action Man.
- Lucy's childhood: middle-class but controlling parents (horse riding, ballet, private tutors, grade-6 violin, sensible Clarks shoes, forced holidays).
- Lucy's hot tub desire persists from earlier seasons (referenced as ongoing "banging on about it").
- 18 scatter cushions on the bed (Lee's complaint).
- Lee's father Frank mentioned indirectly (rent collector visits).
- House in Surrey (Walton-on-Thames era, post-S14 country move).

## Jokes/References for Later Episodes
- **"Make me filthy rich right now"** — competition catchphrase becomes running gag.
- **Hugh Grant / *Heretic* confusion** — church women think *Paddington 2* is the Hugh Grant film where he kidnaps religious women and stabs one with a letter opener; letter opener returns as final prop.
- **"Tight as an earwig's whoopsie" / "looser than a hippo's whoopsie"** — new vulgar similes for stingy/generous.
- **Lauren the church visitor winning £10k** — ironic reversal: the "money is root of evil" proselytiser becomes the greediest.
- **"Winner, winner, chicken dinner"** — misattributed to Bible (Ecclesiastes 5:10 actually quoted).
- **Ave Satani (*The Omen* theme)** — plays on Lauren's phone when she wins.

## Unresolved Threads
- Lauren now has £10,000 and a hot tub coming — will she reappear?
- Lee's competition obsession "cured" but phone rang exactly 10 rings — temptation remains.
- Lucy's letter opener demand — physical comedy setup unresolved.
- Church women's "policy of not turning anyone away" — may return for follow-up.
