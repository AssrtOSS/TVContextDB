## S01E01

# S01E01 Summary — The Drop: A Snowfall Saga

## Episode Plot
Los Angeles, 1990 — four years after *Snowfall* ends. **Wanda Bell-Simmons** works at a used-car lot by day, hustles to break into the music industry by night. She discovers **Lamar Kinsey** (her 19-year-old cousin), a bedroom producer with raw talent, and **Artillery**, a Bloods-affiliated rapper who just dropped “Officer Blackman,” a track about a cop killing that has police and his own gang gunning for him. Wanda strong-arms her internship boss **Darryl “DG” Grant** at Macroscope Records into hearing Lamar’s beat for Artillery. The episode culminates in a tense Bloods-house visit where Wanda and Lamar play the track for a recovering Artillery; he greenlights it, giving Wanda a week to deliver a finished record.

## Important Character Actions
- **Wanda**: sells a car with a killer pitch (the “Alpine sound system” closer), breaks into Leon’s apartment to steal a rare vinyl sample, confronts DG for a partner credit on the Artillery record, talks Lamar into defying his grandparents and James to pursue the beat.
- **Lamar**: plays Wanda his demo (news-montage sample over atmospheric hip-hop), resists James/Big Daddy/Mama G’s push toward church band/army, secretly meets Wanda at his bedroom studio, collaborates on the horn-loop arrangement, sneaks into Bloods territory to play the beat for Artillery.
- **Leon Simmons**: works the free legal clinic, nearly shoots Wanda climbing through his window, reveals he once agreed to fund Wanda’s label but reneged; their breakup traced to Africa trip aftermath.
- **DG / Jenny**: label meeting exposes Macroscope’s “safe” roster; DG pitches West Coast dance/funk crossover, Wanda counters with Artillery’s “edge.” Jenny demands a demo first, then a production deal.
- **Artillery**: hospitalized after a BY (Bloods subset) hit; family fakes his death. From his bedroom he hears Lamar’s beat, orders a rewind, vibes hard — “Yeah, Blood.”
- **James Kinsey**: refuses army enlistment to stay with Lamar; gets shut down by Big Daddy, then warns Lamar the Bloods house is suicide.
- **Big Daddy / Mama G (grandparents)**: church-and-work path for the boys; Mama G sees Wanda as “selling matches to a tinderbox.”

## Newly Established Names / Terms
- **The Drop** — double entendre: “drop a record” / “drop product” (street slang).
- **Macroscope Records** — struggling indie label Wanda interns at; run by Jenny Favreau, DG as VP.
- **Artillery** (Simmie “Buddy” Sims III) — Bloods rapper, “Officer Blackman” track sparked heat.
- **Dara** — Artillery’s manager/sister(?), gatekeeps access.
- **BY** — Bloods subset that shot Artillery.
- **80s / Leimert High Crips / Tower Crips** — active gang sets; Lamar avoids 108th St. to dodge Tower Crips.
- **Beaumont House** — Lamar/James’ school (lets out 3:15).
- **First AME** — church offering Lamar a band seat (turned down).
- **Pastor Rodney** — James’ employer (10% tithe joke).
- **Hamburglar** — Wanda’s nickname for DG’s sneaky vibe.
- **Ward Cleaver** — James’ “square” dress-code reference.
- **Miriam Makeba** — Lamar’s “weird” taste marker.
- **Tip-Toe** — Bloods OG who once gave James a pass.

## Continuity-Relevant Facts
- Set **1990–early 1991**; Rodney King beating verdict (“not guilty on all counts”) already aired — riots loom.
- **Franklin Saint** (Snowfall protagonist) dead/absent; Wanda cites him and the “Family” trauma as why grandparents smother Lamar.
- **Leon & Wanda** were married/partners; split after Africa; Leon now runs legal clinic, Wanda in music.
- **Big Deon** (Bloods OG from Snowfall) confirmed returning.
- **DG** ex-D-boy turned label exec; uses judge-payoff leverage.
- **Lamar’s first words: “Quincy Jones.”** Musical lineage: James Sr. → mother → James → Lamar.
- **Wanda’s sobriety** framed as “God lifted me up out of my body” — spiritual angle to her hustle.

## Jokes / References for Later Episodes
- “You ain’t liked a new song since 1973” (Skip the record-store clerk).
- “Fuck Seattle / Fuck grunge” — DG’s anti-Nirvana rant; time-capsule 1990 industry blindness.
- “Hamburglar” / “Ward Cleaver” / “Tom and Jerry” — recurring insult shorthand.
- Pastor Rodney’s “10% = you can fly” — satirical prosperity gospel.
- “Blue mark five” (Chevy) / “Isky cam” / “Edelbrock” — car-culture shorthand for the lot scenes.
- “M.A.A.D. City” (Kendrick/MC Eiht) needle-drop during Lamar’s bus chase — anachronistic but thematic.
- “Life Is… Too Short” (Too $hort) plays during Wanda’s car sale.

## Unresolved Threads
1. **Artillery’s survival** — still critical; BY and police both want him dead.
2. **Wanda’s partner demand** — DG said “I can break you off, but I can’t make you…” — deal not closed.
3. **Leon’s legal clinic funding** — Wanda wants that money for her own label; tension unresolved.
4. **James’ army papers** — torn up; Big Daddy’s reaction pending.
5. **Lamar’s grandparents** — “different side” threatened if he pursues music.
6. **Dara’s agenda** — “Ain’t no moves with me, Wanda” — she controls Artillery access.
7. **Rodney King verdict fallout** — city “hot”; gang curfews, police retaliation implied.
8. **The sample** — Wanda stole a Swedish 78 (“Five Sharps”) from Leon; clearance/legal risk.
9. **DG/Jenny power struggle** — DG’s “we take heat, y’all get money” vs. Jenny’s caution.

## S01E02

# S01E02 Summary — The Drop: A Snowfall Saga

## Episode Plot
Wanda secures a recording session between her cousin Lamar (producer) and Artillery (Bloods-affiliated rapper) at Lamar's grandmother's house, capturing a breakthrough moment. She leverages this to negotiate a deal with Macroscope Records (Jenny/DG), obtaining the head of A&R position and getting Lamar signed. Meanwhile, Big Daddy (Lamar/James's grandfather) sells Lamar's "guardianship" to DG for $30,000. Dara (Artillery's manager) reveals she was beaten/robbed by Bloods associates (Keith, her uncles) after the session. Wanda and Leon broker a truce at Louis's in Compton: Dara becomes Artillery's official manager on paper, Bloods stand down. The episode ends with Leon—who's tried leaving street life for a legal clinic—fielding a client asking for "niggas handled," signaling his pull back into that world.

## Important Character Actions
- **Wanda**: Orchestrates the Lamar–Artillery session; negotiates A&R role + Lamar's signing at Macroscope; brokers truce with Dara/Bloods; confronts James over endangering Lamar.
- **Lamar**: Produces the beat/session; signs Macroscope contract; resists James's protectionism; asserts independence from Big Daddy.
- **James**: Physically ejects Artillery from grandmother's house (Crip vs Blood tension); later admits he "can't do it without" Lamar musically.
- **DG**: Pays Big Daddy $30K for Lamar; threatens Dara's attackers; warns Wanda he'll arm enemies if Artillery leaves or Dara crosses him.
- **Leon**: Refuses to have lawyers review music contract (clinic doesn't do that work); tells Wanda "welcome back" after truce; takes meeting with client seeking violent "handling."
- **Dara**: Survives assault/robbery by Bloods; agrees to formal manager role + truce; warns Wanda about DG's pattern of using women to set up artists.
## Newly Established Names / Terms
- **Black Banner Music** — Jenny's label (Macroscope Records) referred to by this name in the deal; Chinese context uses 宏景唱片.
- **A&R head role** — Wanda's official title; DG initially refuses, Jenny insists.
- **Louis's** — Neutral-ground diner in Compton where truce is brokered.
- **Big Daddy (Preston)** — Grandfather figure; sells Lamar's "guardianship" for $30K; warns Wanda about Artillery's arrogance.
- **Drop** — Big Daddy's nephew, introduced as local heavy.
- **Keith / Dara's uncles** — Bloods who assaulted/robbed Dara; names DG demands.
- **Amped 992** — Hip-hop radio station (DJ Block).
- **"Red rags"** — Bloods slang used by DG when demanding names.

## Continuity-Relevant Facts
- **Wanda's past**: James reveals she talked 12-year-old Lamar out of Christmas presents and sold them for crack — James refuses to let her back in his life.
- **Music lineage**: Lamar traces family music from Big Daddy → Mom → James → Lamar → himself.
- **Leon's legal clinic**: Struggling (donations drying up, losing cases); colleague says "you can't win paying for appeals on bad cases."
- **DG's supply chain**: Leon warns DG "I know who on the other side of that dope… you don't wanna meet them."
- **Artillery's song about killed cop** (referenced in E01) confirmed as the track that brought heat.
- **Macroscope financial trouble**: Jenny's dad will push her out when contract ends; label driven "into the ground."

## Jokes / References for Later
- **Honey bun story**: Big Daddy's nephew recounts 20-year-old grudge over stolen honey bun, beats man, buys honey bun with his money — becomes running motif.
- **"Welcome back, Lee"** — DG's line to Leon mirrors gang re-entry; echoed at episode end.
- **K-Tel Record Selector commercial** — period-authentic ad playing during Wanda/Leon phone call.
- **"I Get Lifted" / "Everybody Wants to Rule the World" / "I Only Have Eyes for You"** — needle drops marking emotional beats.
- **Quincy Jones reference** — Big Daddy: "Since you was a kid, you wanted to be Quincy Jones."

## Unresolved Threads
1. **Leon's client** — "niggas need handling" request; will he return to violence?
2. **DG's threat** — If Artillery leaves or Dara talks sideways, DG arms their enemies.
3. **Dara's uncles / Keith** — Names not yet delivered; DG wants "every single soul."
4. **James vs. Wanda** — James explicitly rejects Wanda; Lamar caught between.

## S01E03

## S01E03 — "The Drop: A Snowfall Saga"

## Episode Plot

The episode revolves around a failed photo shoot and escalating tensions over creative control. Macroscope's Enzo slashes the development budget by half ($150K) and reallocates it to alternative/grunge, leaving DG desperate to push Artillery's single "Rydah" to radio. Wanda orchestrates a photo shoot, but Artillery is a no-show — Dara says he won't come because cops are still hunting him. Lamar, who secretly finished the "Rydah" mix days ago, withholds it, wanting Jenny to hear his new track (sampling Chaka Khan's "Ain't Nobody") instead. When Lamar tries to pitch it at the shoot, Jenny dismisses him. Wanda eventually discovers the deception and retrieves the masters from DG by pointing him to the reels. Separately, Lamar and James venture into Crip territory (the "blue apartments") to find Artillery and get him to record a verse on the new track. A fight breaks out with a Crip named Trig; James defends Lamar. A gang sweep ("One time!") results in both brothers' arrest. Leon bails them out using a connection to the deputy DA. Back home, Big Daddy softens toward James, telling him he'll inherit the family business — but pulls James off Wanda's music venture to focus solely on managing Lamar. The episode ends with Wanda revealing she already sent "Rydah" to radio, and Lamar accusing her of betraying him by telling DG where the masters were hidden.

## Important Character Actions

- **Wanda**: orchestrates the photo shoot, shuts down Lamar's pitch to Jenny, retrieves the "Rydah" masters via DG, reveals she already serviced the single to radio, accused of leaking Lamar's reel location.
- **Lamar**: finishes "Rydah" mix but withholds it to push his new "Ain't Nobody"-sampled track; goes to Crip territory to find Artillery; gets arrested in the gang sweep; confronts Wanda over the betrayal.
- **James**: accompanies Lamar to the blue apartments, fights Trig to protect Lamar, gets arrested, coaches Lamar on what to tell police about gang affiliation; Big Daddy tells him the family business will be his.
- **Big Daddy**: learns Lamar is working with Artillery (the dead-cop-song rapper), confronts James, pulls James off the business to manage Lamar, softens after the arrest and promises James the business.
- **Artillery**: remains in hiding at the blue apartments (Crip territory where he lived before his grandmother died); records a verse on "There Goes the Neighborhood" with an OG; refuses to come to the photo shoot.
- **DG**: pressures Wanda to deliver "Rydah" masters, retrieves them once Wanda points him to the reels.
- **Jenny**: vents to Wanda about fighting her father's legacy ("Mario Favarella's inept daughter"), warns "no surprise songs," declares "Rydah" is their "life raft."
- **Leon**: bails out James and Lamar using a deputy DA connection (she's coming to his house to "watch the Laker game"); implies he can get gun charges and gang enhancements dropped.
- **Dara**: tells Wanda Artillery isn't coming; later calls Wanda from the blue apartments saying she's taking Lamar there regardless; brief romantic tension with Lamar ("You fuck like [you're back in the streets]").

## Newly Established Names / Terms

## S01E04

## S01E04 Summary

### Episode Plot
After Macroscope Records pulls Artillery's track from radio (Cal's maneuver behind Jenny's back), Wanda and Lamar bypass the label entirely. They adopt a street-level distribution model — "first hit's for free" — pressing cassettes of "Back to the Hood" and moving them through neighborhood spots (car washes, barbershops, strip clubs) using Crips and Bloods networks. The strategy works: 250 copies sell in four days, demand forces re-presses, and the track catches fire at Last Kings club and on KRZO's "Block Party" with DJ Block. Meanwhile, Wanda confronts Leon for seed money; their argument exposes the still-raw wound of their lost baby. The episode ends with Mama G finally agreeing to tell Lamar the full truth about his mother, Rochelle.

### Important Character Actions
- **Wanda**: Launches independent distribution ("Black Banner Music A&R"), strong-arms street placement, pitches Leon for investment, confronts Macroscope's betrayal.
- **Lamar**: Executes the ground game, celebrates the win, begins to hear his song on radio/club.
- **Leon**: Refuses to invest, brutal fight with Wanda over past trauma, later appears at club — tacit support.
- **Jenny**: Stripped of new-artist authority at Macroscope; Cal Harris now controls all new initiatives.
- **DG**: Warns Wanda against "stupid" moves, buys back the masters money Wanda took.
- **Mama G**: Breaks 20-year silence, initiates the truth conversation about Rochelle with Lamar.
- **Artillery**: "Back to the Hood" becomes the breakout hit; "Motherfuck the masters" lyric lands.
- **Dara**: Openly pursues Leon at the club.

### Newly Established Names / Terms
- **Black Banner Music** — Wanda's new A&R venture
- **KRZO / ANT 99.2 FM** — Radio station; DJ Block's "Block Party" mix show
- **Last Kings** — Club/venue where the track breaks
- **"Back to the Hood"** — Breakout single (Lamar/Artillery)
- **Toddy Tee's "Batterram"** — Reference for cassette manufacturer credibility
- **Mass-cassette manufacturer** — Wanda's contact for scaling physical product

### Continuity-Relevant Facts
- Wanda & Leon's lost baby = core unresolved trauma driving their dynamic
- Macroscope legal dept. pulled Artillery's song; old man wants to sell label
- Jenny demoted; Cal Harris now gatekeeps all new artist initiatives
- Mama G & Big Daddy's 20-year standoff over telling Lamar about Rochelle
- Leon's gang-intervention work (James Sr.) mirrors Liberia/Ghana conversation — freed slaves returning and replicating oppression
- Wanda's "first hit free" model explicitly mirrors Leon/Franklin's crack distribution playbook

### Jokes / References for Later
- "Checkers beats chess" (DG) — street tactics over corporate strategy
- "Mexican popcorn" — Macroscope's dismissive slur for Wanda's street sales
- "Motherfuck the masters" — Artillery lyric, double meaning (label ownership + slavery)

## S01E05

## S01E05

### Plot
Present-day Big Daddy (also called Big Deon) opens by warning Wanda to stop pushing Lamar toward rap, invoking the legal family tree ("family comes first") and promising it is time Lamar learned the truth. The episode then braids three layers:

1. A late-1970s church "Portrait Day" where Big Daddy, then a pastor, publicly puts his pregnant 15-year-old daughter Gertrude out of fellowship and out of the home.
3. A present-day scene where Mama G (Gerty), now raising Lamar and James, finally breaks her promise to Big Daddy and tells Lamar the real story of his mother.
4. A Thanksgiving flashback, the only one the family ever had, in which Mama G invites her estranged daughter Rochelle and Rochelle's ex-manager/husband Maurice to a "perfect" dinner so Mama G can prove to her own parents that her life is not ruined.

At dinner Rochelle refuses to record a new track Maurice has already lined up with a famous white rapper. The argument turns violent; Maurice puts his hands on her; Rochelle stabs him; Maurice drives off bleeding and crashes. Rochelle dies. In the present Lamar says the truth out loud, tells Mama G he intends to keep making music ("I'm not stopping"), accuses her of being the one "like his daddy," and storms toward the door. James tells him, "deep down, you the reason for all this." Big Daddy, Mama G, Wanda, Lamar, and James pose for the portrait Lamar grew up believing was real — the same photo that turns out to have been staged in a single weekend.

### Important character actions
- Big Daddy / Big Deon — orders Wanda off Lamar's case; reveals he was once a pastor who expelled teenage Gerty; agrees the secret must end; later stops Maya by physically blocking Lamar from leaving.
- Mama G (Gerty / Gertrude) — breaks the family pact, narrates Rochelle's full death, admits she brought Maurice back solely for the staged portrait ("I just want one happy memory"), and absorbs Lamar's accusation that she, not Wanda, is the new predatory pusher.
- Wanda — sits in on the telling as Big Daddy's invited witness; is openly labeled "A&R" by Lamar in the present; declines to defend herself when Mama G retorts that she is "like his daddy."
- Lamar — takes the truth, commits to continuing music, identifies with Rochelle ("you're not like him, I am"), tries to leave the house, is stopped.
- James — has known the truth since age 13; introduces himself to Big Daddy as "Black" after crashing through a window bleeding from a dog bite while fleeing "Nazis" / "One Time," and tells Lamar the family secret was always about covering for Rochelle's gift rather than protecting Lamar.
- Rochelle (deceased) — refuses to be re-signed; stabs Maurice in self-defense; dies in the car crash.
- Maurice — former manager / husband of Rochelle; now an auto-assembly foreman at the South Gate plant; physically assaults Rochelle at the dinner.

### Newly established names and terms
- Rochelle ("Aunt 'Chelle," "Big Daddy's daughter") — Lamar and James's mother, a working singer.
- Maurice — Lamar and James's father; Rochelle's ex-manager and ex-husband.
- Gerty / Gertrude / "Mama G" — Big Daddy's expelled daughter, Lamar and James's grandmother and guardian.
- Portrait Day — the annual church-and-family photo ritual Big Daddy instituted; the Thanksgiving photo Lamar remembers was actually shot in one weekend with outfit changes.
- "Dangerous Obsession" — Rochelle's signature song, performed at the fatal dinner.
- "Sparkle" — film Rochelle auditioned for, against an actress named "Irene something."
- Maverick's Flat — the club where 16-year-old Rochelle had her first big audition.
- South Gate plant — where Maurice works as a foreman.
- "Two-bit snake oil nigga" — Big Daddy's stock insult for predatory music managers; reused against Maurice.
- "Black" — James's street name (used when he addresses Big Daddy).
- "Nazis" / "pigs" / "One Time" — present-day street slang for LAPD, used by James.

### Continuity-relevant facts
- Rochelle died on Thanksgiving; the family's refusal to celebrate the holiday is now revealed to be cover, not politics. Big Daddy's "Thanksgiving makes white people feel good about killing Indians" line is exposed as a lie told to Lamar.
- Gerty was expelled at 15 for being pregnant by a "street thug"; her own father (Big Daddy) was the one who cast her out. This is the originating family trauma the rest of the arc rests on.
- Maurice began managing Rochelle when she was 16; he was 26–27. They married, had three children, and he later left her for a "bigger" white manager — Rochelle's bitterness is rooted in that abandonment.
- The police report on the crash contained: Rochelle's knife, Maurice's blood, and Rochelle's song lyrics in the car. Mama G frames the official account as "their version," implying the LAPD narrative concealed the stabbing and self-defense.
- Lamar has been in "the bottle" (alcohol) for years; he tells Mama G he is "done with that shit." This is the first explicit acknowledgement of his drinking.
- Wanda is now openly called Lamar's A&R by Lamar himself, confirming her industry role inside the family.
- Big Daddy's pastoral identity coexists with his Snowfall-era OG identity — the episode establishes that the church and the PJ Watts Crips background belong to the same man at different life stages.

### Jokes / references useful for later episodes
- The unnamed "white boy" on Maurice's private plane — "one of the biggest white boys out," "the whole world's heard of him," "laying his rap down, talking about love," unable to be shaken off. In 1990 LA-rap context this reads as Vanilla Ice (To the Extreme, Nov 1990). Rochelle left him, not the other way around; this is the seed of the West Coast distrust of outside-mediapropping white managers and is the kind of reference later seasons can cash in when real white-rappers enter the timeline.
- "Sparkle" audition opposite "Irene" — a direct wink at Irene Cara, who starred in the 1976 film Sparkle. Use as a cultural time-stamp for late-80s / early-90s Black Hollywood aspirations; may recur.
- "60th Street" is named in Lamar's demo as his home block — likely to recur as South Central geography.
- "Maverick's Flat" is a real South Central venue name and is treated in-show as the club that launched Rochelle; useful as an established venue for future music scenes.

# S01E06 Summary — The Drop: A Snowfall Saga

## Episode Plot

The episode opens on a street robbery where T-Lane (18th Street Crip) gets stripped of his watch and chain by a crew that includes Keith. "Back to the Hood" is now a city-wide hit: DJ Block interviews Artillery on radio, callers from Inglewood fawn over him, and one prank caller threatens him with gang politics. Lamar admits to Wanda he hasn't produced a song in three weeks and is panicking about the Black Banner meeting tomorrow; Wanda tries to keep him focused while moving back home. At the clinic, Leon tells Wanda he respects her hustle but the legal work is his future, not hers. The Black Banner sit-down goes well — 250,000 copies already pressed, full album deal approved — but DG tells Lamar he's "green" and forces Wanda to compile a list of experienced producers for Artillery's record. A drive-by shooting at the Macroscope lot exposes Artillery; DG's street sidekick Vance (the Cobra Clutch specialist) handles the aftermath, bristling that Wanda is doing things by the book. DG uses the chaos to pitch a predatory "advance" structure (small stipends, interest on interest, perpetual debt) that he brags is "pimping 101." Jenny tries to co-opt Wanda as her inside spy on DG with a side compensation package; Wanda pushes back, insisting Lamar's name and voice must lead the album. In parallel, Leon quietly collects DG's contracts through a lawyer and meets DG on a basketball court to probe how DG really operates — Leon sees legit music getting tainted by old street faces and wants to protect his family without rejoining the life. Lamar and Artillery clash in the studio when Lamar pitches a beat ("Exit Wounds") that he feels needs to channel his late mother's singer spirit; Artillery rejects the personal angle, demands "Blood shit / gangster shit," and at one point shreds Lamar's producer credit. Leonard, James, and a graffiti crew called Blur take Lamar out for a midnight bombing run to reset his head — Blur's late legend Artis is invoked as the template for making a name through graffiti, the "fourth pillar of hip-hop." Armed with new resolve, Lamar walks into the next label meeting and pitches the album himself: "rebirth and return," the journey through danger, drugs, parties, broken homes, ending in awakening ("when everything is destroyed, you are reborn"). He also reveals his new alias, Nex / DJ Nex. The episode closes on a corner where Big Deon oversees young runners and warns one of them: "You should die with your gun in your hand."
