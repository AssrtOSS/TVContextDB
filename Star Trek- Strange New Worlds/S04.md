## S04E01

## 剧情概述

企业号调查伊萨克苏斯恒星附近罕见的十级星际风暴，却被风暴拖入6500万年前的太阳系。飞船严重受损、缺少铱燃料，只能前往史前地球寻找资源，并被来自火星的战斗指挥官卡塞尔及其舰队扣押。

卡塞尔的文明正与多尔德姆交战。她误以为企业号属于敌对势力，夺走反物质燃料，企图摧毁太阳系第五颗行星——多尔德姆母星。派克试图以联邦的合作理念和沟通技术说服她，却未能阻止行动。最终，企业号没有发射鱼雷，而是迎着爆炸冲击波前进，借由反物质爆炸与行星核心爆炸产生的能量返回原本的时代。

史波克推断，多尔德姆行星碎片后来成为希克苏鲁伯小行星，并撞击地球、导致恐龙灭绝；行星消失也改变了火星轨道，推动地球环境变化与人类演化。企业号的介入本来就是历史的一部分。

## 重要角色行动

- **克里斯托弗·派克**：连续执行十五次任务后仍担心船员压力；拒绝晋升准将，选择继续留在企业号。他在关键时刻拒绝发射鱼雷，最终凭直觉选择驶入爆炸冲击波。
- **乌娜·钦-莱利**：晋升为指挥官，距离舰长和拥有自己的星舰更近一步；她与派克共同承担指挥压力。
- **史波克**：确认企业号身处6500万年前，并解释量子纠缠、时间锚点及返回机制；继续以过度严谨的方式纠正乌乎拉的瓦肯语。
- **拉安·努宁-辛格**：发现自身潜藏的增强基因似乎被激活，力量、反应和身体协调性进一步提升，但她对这种变化感到困惑。
- **尼奥塔·乌乎拉**：开始指导一名来自瓦肯家庭、倾向瓦肯科学学院的年轻学员。
- **斯科蒂与佩利亚**：临时修复企业号系统，并将殖民船上的铱燃料舱接入企业号。
- **卡塞尔**：为拯救火星文明，不惜牺牲自己的飞船和船员，试图摧毁多尔德姆母星。

## 新建立的名称与术语

- **卡塞尔（Cassell）**：火星战斗指挥官。
- **多尔德姆（Dol'drm）**：生活在太阳系第五颗行星上的蛛形纲物种；被卡塞尔的文明视为吞噬行星的敌人。
- **十级星际风暴**：罕见的时空异常，将企业号送回远古时代。
- **火星第五行星**：后来消失的多尔德姆母星；其残骸构成小行星带的一部分。
- **希克苏鲁伯小行星**：多尔德姆行星碎片，最终撞击地球并导致恐龙灭绝。
- **相位技术**：卡塞尔一方能够穿过企业号舱壁和地板的技术。
- **受控解体功能**：企业号新改装的相位枪功能，但本集因时间禁忌未被用于恐龙身上。

## 连续性相关事实

- 企业号此次任务形成了一个闭合时间循环：他们收到自己在6500万年前部署的求救浮标，并制造了将自己送回过去的风暴。
- 多尔德姆行星的毁灭既保存了已知历史，也促成地球、人类和现代火星的形成。
- 卡塞尔的文明虽然消失，卡塞尔的DNA却已经存在于银河系许多物种之中，说明部分火星人成功扩散并延续。
- 派克继续留任企业号舰长；乌娜晋升后更接近独立指挥星舰。
- 企业号全体船员必须对这次时间旅行和人类起源相关事实保密，官方记录只描述为与企业号短暂失联。

## 笑点与可回收引用

- 史波克认为数字数据比亲眼观看星际风暴更有价值；派克则把风暴比作剧院。
- 斯科蒂给奥特加斯的驾驶岗位增加持续蜂鸣声，作为“让她的工作更难”的报复。
- 斯科蒂坚持使用带有苏格兰口音的“小家伙”称呼恐龙，奥特加斯则纠正它应为“小姑娘”。
- 卡塞尔把“胡萝卜与大棒”理解为武器，史波克和派克只好解释胡萝卜是奖励。
- 斯科蒂为保护恐龙而使用麻醉，最后因躲避攻击徒手打断自己的手；同伴随后反复调侃他“揍了恐龙”。
- 结尾引用《火星公主》，派克感叹小说对真实远古火星文明的意外预示。

## 未解决线索

- 拉安增强基因的激活原因、能力上限及其后续影响尚未确定。
- 卡塞尔本人是否在多尔德姆行星毁灭中幸存，仍无明确答案。
- 派克如何处理自己对过去、现在与未来的执念，仍会继续影响他的指挥选择。
- 乌娜晋升后何时获得自己的星舰，以及她与派克的指挥关系将如何变化，尚待展开。

## S04E02

### Episode Plot
- James T. Kirk, La’an Noonien-Singh, and Spock investigate the long-missing USS Griffin after detecting its distress signal.
- The Griffin appears abandoned, yet retains gravity and briefly reactivates its failing systems. Its crew of 300 vanished without escape logs, leaving behind empty body bags and evidence of mass killings.
- The boarding party discovers that Chief Security Officer Rose Harper murdered or expelled the crew after becoming convinced that an unknown force was taking control of them.
- The ship manifests as a psychic, reality-warping phenomenon: each visitor experiences personalized hallucinations based on guilt, fear, grief, and unresolved relationships.
- Kirk sees his son David and the death of his brother Sam; Spock sees T’Pring; La’an encounters Harper and is pressured to abandon her identity; Una, Ortegas, and others are also manipulated.
- The phenomenon attempts to trap the Enterprise crew aboard the Griffin, but Spock uses a Vulcan poem and logic as an anchor, while the crew rescues one another through shared trust and emotional recognition.
- Kirk’s brother Sam is killed by the entity’s manifestation, though the real Sam is safely away visiting his family. Kirk, La’an, Spock, and the others are ultimately recovered by Enterprise.
- The Griffin is taken into custody by the USS Thorn and transferred to Division 12 for investigation. The true nature of the entity remains unexplained.

### Important Character Actions
- Kirk prioritizes investigating the Griffin despite feeling guilty about missing time with Carol and David.
- La’an takes command during the crisis, resists Harper’s attempt to exploit her trauma, and helps keep Kirk and Spock focused on escape.
- Spock identifies the phenomenon as psychotropic and uses a private memory of his kahs-wan ordeal and Ka’nina’s poem, “It will guide you home,” to distinguish reality from illusion.
- Spock admits that T’Pring’s image was emotionally powerful because it came from his own memories and beliefs, not because the entity understood her independently.
- Uhura and the Enterprise crew improvise a transporter rescue. Ortegas is accidentally trapped in the transporter buffer after the Griffin manipulates Uhura.
- Una nearly gives in to the fantasy of removing her Illyrian identity and escaping Starfleet’s restrictions.
- Harper is revealed as both the Griffin’s killer and another victim of the ship’s influence.
- Pike chooses to classify the incident as an unexplained survival event rather than expose the crew to further danger or speculation.

### Newly Established Names and Terms
- USS Griffin: A Federation ship missing for nine years.
- Captain Taggert: The Griffin’s captain.
- Rose Harper: The Griffin’s Chief Security Officer and sole apparent survivor.
- Dr. G’har: The Griffin’s doctor.
- Division 12: A Starfleet group that investigates unexplained phenomena.
- USS Thorn: The ship assigned to tow the Griffin to Division 12.
- Ka’nina: A revered Vulcan poet.
- Hurk’ah: The location of a past trip taken by Spock and T’Pring.
- Epsilon-5: The outpost where Carol and David are located.
- Kahs-wan: A dangerous Vulcan survival ordeal from Spock’s childhood.
- The Griffin’s phenomenon: An unidentified psychic or extradimensional force that uses personal memories to manipulate visitors.

### Continuity-Relevant Facts
- Kirk has a son, David, with Carol; their relationship is strained by his Starfleet absences.
- Kirk’s brother Sam is alive and visiting his family during the episode. The Griffin’s vision of Sam’s death is only an illusion.
- Sam has two sons, Peter and David, and ends calls with them by saying, “Good night, I love you, and I’ll see you in your dreams.”
- Kirk’s emotional conflict centers on survivor’s guilt and his belief that he must save everyone, especially his brother.
- Spock and T’Pring’s engagement has dissolved, but Spock still carries strong memories of their relationship.
- Spock’s Vulcan identity and logic help him resist the Griffin, rather than functioning as weaknesses he must discard.
- Una remains deeply affected by the legal and personal consequences of being Illyrian.
- Ortegas survives but is temporarily trapped in the transporter buffer.
- Pike acknowledges that Starfleet has referred six cases to Division 12; four received scientific or alien explanations, while two remain unresolved.
- The incident reinforces that some phenomena encountered in deep space may be impossible to explain with current Starfleet science.

### Jokes and References
- Kirk jokes about being a former Boy Scout and repeats “Be prepared.”
- Spock is mocked as a “back seat sensor reader.”
- Kirk’s inability to operate the Griffin’s computer correctly becomes a running gag.
- Spock’s literal, scientific response to “ghosts” contrasts with the crew’s growing belief that the ship is haunted.
- Kirk jokes that, if freed from his fate as a captain, he could become the Griffin’s head chef.
- Chapel jokes that Spock is still “pointy” and emotionally difficult after his recovery.
- Pike’s story about a supposedly haunted barn parallels the Griffin: unexplained terror may leave real scars even when no evidence remains.
- Pike uses freshly made cookies to coax Kirk into discussing the trauma, referencing the idea that refusing cookies indicates madness.

### Unresolved Threads
- What exactly is the Griffin’s entity or phenomenon, and why does it need visitors?
- Why did it select Harper as the first apparent host or agent?
- What happened to the Griffin’s crew after their deaths or disappearance?
- Can the phenomenon affect ships, systems, or personnel beyond the Griffin?
- What will Division 12 discover when it studies the Griffin?
- How will Kirk process the false vision of Sam’s death and the real tensions in his family life?
- Will Ortegas experience lasting effects from being trapped in the transporter buffer?

## S04E03

## S04E03 — "Shore Leave" (working title)

### Episode Plot
The *Enterprise* is en route to the Rodaxian system for peace negotiations in a civil war. With two weeks before the talks, Pike grants Alpha shift shore leave on **Cesnar Grand**, a resort planet directly on their flight path. The planet’s unique atmosphere induces a natural, harmless intoxication that wears off an hour after departure — a “tech-free zone” marketed as personalized relaxation. The crew’s tensions (Ortegas vs. Scotty, La’an’s pent-up aggression, Spock’s emotional avoidance) flare under the influence. A prank “dragon bomb” (actually an airborne relaxant vapor) is knocked over by Ortegas and Scotty, who inhale a concentrated dose. They black out, lose the shuttle, and wake with no memory — discovering a **real** Quadrium-based bomb hidden aboard the shuttle by a Rodaxian militant intent on assassinating both faction leaders during the talks. The crew races to defuse it; Spock and Scotty hold the reactive casing steady while Spock attempts a zeptosecond-before-detonation transport. The bomb turns out to be the **Ancient Rodaxian Formal Rite of Peace** — a centuries-unseen ceremony requiring Quadrium — and its “detonation” honors both sides, launching the talks successfully.

### Important Character Actions
- **Ortegas & Scotty**: Escalate their professional rivalry (warning lights vs. “backseat driving”), drunkenly steal/hide their own shuttle, blame each other, eventually reconcile and admit mutual envy (Ortegas failed Impulse Mechanics; Scotty crashed seven shuttles in his only piloting class). They apologize, work together to track the shuttle via photosynthetic security plants, and end the episode collaborating on intermix-chamber power routing.
- **Spock**: Hallucinates **James T. Kirk** (residual from their mind meld) as a “human best friend” to process vulnerability — fears, hopes, dreams. Kirk is later revealed to be *actually* on Cesnar (vacationing), confirming the hallucination was real contact. Spock also holds the live bomb casing with Scotty, calculating a zeptosecond transport window.
- **La’an**: Channels rage into an **Anbo-jyutsu tournament** (“to the death”), takes over a local thief gang by out-negotiating them, and retrieves the shuttle (hidden behind a billboard).
- **Pike**: Delivers the formal kickoff speech (from memory, while intoxicated), unknowingly invokes the Ancient Rite by “detonating” the Quadrium device, securing peace.
- **Dendru (Cesnar host)**: Orchestrates the crew’s “journeys” — including the kidnapping — as therapeutic interventions. Reveals he once shared Ortegas/Scotty’s anger-driven mindset.

### Newly Established Names / Terms
- **Cesnar Grand** — resort planet with intoxicating atmosphere (planetary gases cause personalized inebriation; wears off ~1 hr post-departure; tech-free zone).
- **Quadrium** — rare particle once native to Rodaxian system; component of the Ancient Formal Rite of Peace (“dragon bomb”).
- **Ancient Rodaxian Formal Rite of Peace** — generations-unseen ceremony; danger/expense/effort to obtain Quadrium signals deep honor.
- **Photosynthetic technology** — Cesnar’s security network runs on plant-based computing (Spock mind-melds with a plant to access it).
- **Zeptosecond** — 10⁻²¹ second; Spock’s transport timing precision.
- **Beto** — Ortegas’s brother (one of six; four adopted), target of her displaced blame growing up.

### Continuity-Relevant Facts
- **Spock–Kirk connection**: The mind meld (S04E01/E02?) left a psychic tether; Spock hallucinates Kirk as emotional outlet. Kirk confirms he was *physically* on Cesnar, validating the link.
- **Ortegas’s backstory**: Failed Impulse Mechanics (intro course) at the Academy; wanted to be an engineer but lacked aptitude for intermix chambers; pivoted to helm. Seven brothers (four adopted); mother said “hearts are infinite.”
- **Scotty’s backstory**: Crashed seven shuttles in his only piloting class; avoided helm thereafter; mother also said “hearts are infinite.”
- **Rodaxian system**: Civil war; two factions. Quadrium is native here. Peace talks moved forward two weeks unexpectedly (saboteur’s doing).
- **Enterprise timeline**: Stardate 2011.7. Two weeks until original negotiation date.
- **Uhura’s party**: Missed by Ortegas/Scotty; referenced as a recurring social anchor.

### Jokes / References for Later Episodes
- **“Shushle”** — drunken mispronunciation of “shuttle” (Ortegas/Scotty).
- **“Dragon bomb”** — Dendru’s name for the relaxant vapor canister; later becomes the Quadrium device.
- **Ortegas’s tattoo** — teased (“deep Earth meaning” = favorite shirt); Chapel wants to see it; crew refuses.
- **Una singing “Total Eclipse of the Heart” in Klingon** (15 in a row) — “Warrior’s songs.”
- **Scotty’s “Monty” hatred** — insists on “Montgomery.”
- **“Big-up high-fives”** — drunken sealing of the kidnapping deal.
- **Intermix chambers are “overrated”** — Ortegas/Scotty bonding line.
- **Kirk’s “invisible/cloaked”** gag — Spock’s hallucination vs. reality.

### Unresolved Threads
- **Spock’s emotional processing**: He shared “many, many secrets” with Kirk (hallucinated and real); Kirk says they’re safe with him. Spock’s vulnerability arc continues.
- **Ortegas/Scotty dynamic**: Truce reached, but underlying envy/resentment pattern acknowledged — likely to resurface.
- **Rodaxian peace**: Talks *begin* successfully, but durability of a Quadrium-forged peace is untested.
- **Dendru’s methods**: His “journey” manipulation (kidnapping, memory gaps) skirts ethics; could reappear.
- **Kirk’s presence**: He was on Cesnar *on vacation*; his path crossing the *Enterprise* again is inevitable.

## S04E04

### Episode Plot
The Enterprise is ordered to recover the Federation probe **Angelo-1** after it drifts across the demarcation line into Klingon territory near **Mat'Aar 12**. Pike assigns Una — newly promoted to Commander — her first solo command: covertly retrieve the probe via shuttle while Enterprise waits outside sensor range. The away team (Una, M'Benga, Uhura) discovers Mat'Aar 12 is a fully occupied world: Klingon forces control the cities alongside a native Mat'Aaran population, protected by an advanced electromagnetic atmospheric barrier that vaporizes any ship breaching it.

Una finds she has an exact doppelgänger on the planet: **Nevette**, a high-ranking collaborator who secretly runs the resistance. Una impersonates Nevette to move freely. Meanwhile on Enterprise, La'an and Spock discover Angelo-1's data chip was wiped during transport — someone aboard tampered with it. Scotty has meanwhile rigged a clever navigational buoy by locking a relay signal onto the orbiting D7 cruiser, giving Erica a tracking fix through the visual interference.

On the surface, Una learns Nevette was murdered (Borgia plant alkaloid toxin) and the resistance was planning an EMP strike on the Klingon **Neodymium Munitions Facility** using a proximity detonator (trigger frequency **34.6 MHz**). The collaborator **Roman** betrays Una to **Commander Kor** (a direct descendant of Kahless). Una turns the tables by planting the detonator on Roman; when he reports to the Klingons, he crosses a checkpoint and triggers the EMP, disabling the D7 (80% systems down) and dropping the atmospheric barrier. The away team escapes in the chaos. Una refuses to stay as the resistance's figurehead, telling Ja'lem (who has a daughter to protect) that they already have their leader.

Back on Enterprise, La'an traces the probe tampering to Scotty. He wiped Angelo-1's log because it recorded the **USS Yang** — a Starfleet medical ship whose CO Scotty served with — crossing into Klingon space unauthorized on a humanitarian mission. La'an chooses discretion over protocol and does not report him; Pike implicitly backs her call.

### Important Character Actions
- **Una Chin-Riley**: First mission as Commander; impersonates her doppelgänger flawlessly; leads the resistance operation; plants the detonator on the traitor Roman; inspires the resistance but walks away, affirming her place is on Enterprise.
- **La'an Noonien-Singh**: Runs the probe-tampering investigation; discovers Scotty's involvement; chooses friendship over security protocol; continues weekly therapy with Chapel for residual aggression/paranoia from her Vulcan episode.
- **Spock**: Assists La'an; analyzes the hidden transmission with a cadet's help; acknowledges his own lingering paranoia.
- **Joseph M'Benga**: Accesses Nevette's database; shares his own past (Ülget hospital siege, killing loyalists to save patients) and connects with Una over living hidden lives to protect others.
- **Nyota Uhura**: Uses linguistic skill to understand the rare **T'Gal dialect**, learning of the 24-hour military takeover and vaporization-barrier upgrade; adopts alias **Nyota Nakuru** (first name + friend's surname).
- **Montgomery Scott**: Creates improvised navigational buoy from D7 relay; wiped probe data to protect a friend; bonds with Ortegas over Cuban sandwiches and mustard.
- **Erica Ortegas**: Tracks D7 position for EMP targeting; shares lunch with Scotty.
- **Commander Kor**: Klingon outpost commander; tests Una's loyalty; plans total lockdown (vaporization on entry/exit, removal of native leaders).
- **Roman**: Collaborator bartender who betrays Una; receives the detonator and is vaporized.
- **Ja'lem**: Resistance member, father of a daughter; becomes the movement's new leader.

### Newly Established Names / Terms

- **Mat'Aar 12 / Mat'Aarans** — Occupied planet and native people
- **Nevette** — Una's doppelgänger, resistance leader (deceased)

- La'an's post-Vulcan-experience symptoms (aggression, paranoia) persist; weekly Chapel sessions continue.

## S04E05

# Star Trek: Strange New Worlds — S04E05 Summary

## Episode Plot
A long-range transporter experiment by Lt. Scott goes wrong, turning the entire *Enterprise* crew (except Spock) into felt puppets — a "Level 5 transporter accident." While the crew struggles to operate the ship in puppet form, Orion pirates (Captain Ch'Rol of the *Vo'Nee*) attack, exploiting the ship's vulnerability. Spock, the only unaffected crew member, is overwhelmed by simultaneous demands. With guidance from T'Pring (aboard as a passenger), Spock learns to prioritize and delegate. Pike and the puppet crew embrace their new nature — discovering they don't need oxygen, can fit through small spaces, and can survive in vacuum — and launch a chaotic counterattack. They retake the bridge, disable life support to knock out the boarding party, and beam the Orions to the brig. In the end, Scotty reverses the transporter effect. A final twist reveals Spock's entire puppet ordeal was a vivid dream during unconsciousness after a minor shuttle accident; the crew was never actually transformed.

## Important Character Actions
- **Scotty** — pushes long-range transporter past 27,000 km (targeting 30,000 km) to beat rival Declan Milder's record; causes the accident with "Little Ricky" (his childhood puppet) in the buffer; later builds an electro-pulse glove to operate consoles and reverses the effect.
- **Spock** — sole unaffected crew member; paralyzed by overwhelming demands until T'Pring slaps him back to focus; uses mind meld to share Pike's battle plan; executes the bridge retake.
- **Pike** — revels in new biometric chair; realizes puppet physiology grants vacuum survival; leads "puppet logic" counterattack; turns off life support from chair to knock out boarders.
- **T'Pring** — arrives to guide Spock through Kal Rekk principles; provides tactical insight (Orion feint); uncharacteristically emotional outburst demonstrates "puppet advantage."
- **La'an & Una** — execute stealth extraction of Spock from Orion captivity.
- **Pelia** — chopped in half by Orions; casually sewn back together; demonstrates puppet resilience.
- **Uhura** — comms fail due to unrecognized fingers; Spock enables audio-only response.

## Newly Established Names/Terms
- **Level 5 transporter accident** — whole-crew transformation classification.
- **Quantum polyfill** — missing variable in Scotty's reversal calculation.
- **Electro-pulse glove** — Scotty's workaround for biometric lockout (mimics human skin conductivity).
- **Kal Rekk** — Vulcan day of solitude and atonement (passed two weeks prior).
- **Vo'Nee** — Orion pirate ship; **Captain Ch'Rol** — its commander; **Ejachi** — Orion lieutenant.
- **Witmere** — Federation ship 29,647 km away (Declan Milder's posting).
- **Declan Milder** — Scotty's Academy valedictorian rival; holds long-distance transport record; "legacy student."

## Continuity-Relevant Facts
- Pike's biometric chair (Pelia-designed, Starfleet first) becomes decisive weapon.
- Spock's struggle with logic/emotion balance continues post-"ghost ship" (S03 finale); T'Pring's mentorship resumes.
- La'an and Spock's friendship deepens — she rescues him; he confides anxiety about being sole functional crew.
- Scotty's competitive streak with Declan Milder established; Pelia favored Declan ("same kind of bananas").
- Puppet physiology: no blood, no oxygen needed, vacuum-survivable, self-sewing, pain reduced to "tickles."
- Orion society: gender-neutral control; females not inherently submissive (Ch'Rol's lieutenant challenges stereotype).

## Jokes / References for Later Episodes

## S04E10

S04E10 episode summary:

## Episode Plot

Enterprise is stationed at a white hole to collect scientific data. A music signal emanates from inside, then unknown attackers strike the ship, disabling warp core and tethering it with a tractor beam. Uhura, just returned from a Starfleet Protocols Refresher course, experiences a "temporal incident" -- she hears events that haven't happened yet: Scotty screaming a Mayday, an energy burst. She contacts La'An, who recognizes the symptoms of a temporal event but warns her not to interfere with the timeline.

Uhura discovers she can communicate with herself from one year in the future through the anomaly. Future-Uhura reveals that in her timeline, Scotty has been dead for a year -- killed when his console exploded during the original white hole mission. The two Uburas collaborate across time, enlisting Spock (who spent nearly a year solving a quantum entanglement equation) and synthesizing a dilithium diamond as a quantum condensate for a reverse pulse. Their first attempt fails and swaps the two Uburas between timelines -- past-Uhura ends up on Kirk's Enterprise in the future, while future-Uhura returns to Pike's era.

They ultimately realize the anomaly is not just a white hole or black hole but both -- two points connected through time. The pulses are "contractions": an alien entity is being born through the anomaly, and it is in breech. "Midwives" -- beings who exist outside linear time -- had disabled Enterprise to prevent it from harming the child. Uhura manually repairs a drone inside the black hole to help turn the baby, working with haptic gloves and coordinated with her other-self on the opposite side. The Midwives then restore both Uburas to their correct timelines; only they remember what changed.

## Character Actions

- **Uhura** drives the entire plot: discovers the temporal link, coordinates with her future self, solves the musical cipher (Dryk'tee mural notes = half a frequency, combined with the anomaly's signal), and manually enters the black hole to save the breech baby.
- **Kirk** (now Captain of Enterprise) deduces past-Uhura is a time traveler and gives her space to act, referencing his own time-travel experience. He recalls teaching Uhura poker at Starbase Six during a five-day solar storm lockdown.
- **Pike** (now Admiral) recognizes something is off about Uhura, addresses her as "Chris" when she slips, and provides cryptic guidance without revealing the future.
- **Scotty** provides antimatter and engineering knowledge for the dilithium diamond synthesis; asks Uhura out (awkwardly).
- **Spock** has been on Enterprise under Kirk for the past year; solves the quantum entanglement equation; is visibly annoyed Uhura didn't come to him sooner.
- **Chapel** is engaged to Roger Korby but tells past-Uhura she wouldn't trade her time with Spock for anything.
- **Ortegas** is now a Captain, stationed at Bindar-12 as an instructor.
- **La'An** exists in the future timeline; Kirk asks about her whereabouts.
