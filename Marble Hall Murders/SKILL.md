# Marble Hall Murders — Subtitle Translation Background

## Series Overview
- **Title**: Marble Hall Murders (大理石厅谋杀案 / 马布尔厅谋杀案)
- **Format**: 6-episode miniseries, 1 season, ~60 min/episode
- **First air**: 6 September 2026 (PBS / MASTERPIECE, US); BBC One (UK)
- **Broadcasters**: PBS (US), BBC One (UK)
- **Creator/Writer**: Anthony Horowitz
- **Director**: Rebecca Gatward
- **Executive Producer**: Jill Green
- **Production**: Eleventh Hour Films, Salt Films, BBC Studios, PBS Masterpiece
- **Source**: Third novel in Susan Ryeland series (2025), following *Magpie Murders* (2016) and *Moonflower Murders* (2020)

## Core Premise
Dual-narrative metafictional mystery. **Real world (2026 London)**: Editor Susan Ryeland is hired to edit a continuation novel *Pünd's Last Case* by troubled author Eliot Crace, grandson of famed children's writer Miriam Crace. When Eliot dies in a hit-and-run, Susan is framed for murder. **Novel world (1955 Corfu)**: Fictional detective Atticus Pünd, terminally ill, investigates the death of Lady Margaret Chalfont at a Corfu villa, aided by Albanian police lieutenant Achilles Vassilikos. The real-world killer's identity is hidden inside the fictional manuscript.

## Speaking Styles

- **Susan Ryeland**: Sharp, professional, weary-but-determined editor's voice; educated British middle-class; dry wit under pressure.
- **Atticus Pünd**: Precise, formal, methodical; 1950s golden-age detective diction; slightly archaic, German-surname precision; "slightly unhinged" heightened tone in novel world.
- **Miriam Crace / Lady Margaret Chalfont**: Manipulative, condescending, authoritative; literary grande dame.
- **Eliot Crace**: Defensive, troubled, pretentious literary affectations; masking insecurity.
- **Ian Blakeney**: Police procedural, skeptical but fair; grounded contemporary London copper.
- **Achilles Vassilikos**: Greek-Albanian accent, pragmatic, eyepatch-wearing sidekick; clipped military bearing.
- **Crace family**: Upper-class British with repressed tensions; double-meaning dialogue.

## World Setting & Cultural Context

- **Dual timeline**: Present-day London publishing world ↔ 1955 Corfu (post-WWII, Ionian Sea, azure backdrop, ouzo/coffee, Nazi-looted art legacy).
- **Metafiction**: Homage to Agatha Christie / golden-age whodunits; "book-within-a-book" structure where fictional clues solve real crimes.
- **Publishing industry**: Continuation novels (cf. Sophie Hannah's Poirot), editor-author dynamics, rights acquisitions, adaptation deals.
- **British class dynamics**: Aristocratic decay (Chalfonts), literary dynasty (Crace family), old money vs. new media.
- **PBS MASTERPIECE brand**: Prestige British drama for US audiences; follows *Magpie Murders* (2022) and *Moonflower Murders* (2024).
- **Filming locations**: London, Dublin, Corfu.

## Recurring Narrative Devices

- Anagrams as clues (e.g., "Robert Waysmith" → "it was my brother")
- Dual-role casting (actors play real-world and novel-world counterparts)
- Manuscript chapters intercut with investigation
- "Tiny" third roles for some actors (Easter egg)
- Literary allusions (Christie's *Evil Under the Sun*, Conway's pastiches)
