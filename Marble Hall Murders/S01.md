## S01E01

**Marble Hall Murders — S01E01 Summary**

**Episode Plot**  
The premiere establishes the dual-narrative structure. In **2026 London**, freelance editor **Susan Ryeland** (苏珊·赖兰德) is approached by Causton Books (科斯顿出版社) to edit a continuation novel, *Pünd's Last Case* (《品德的最后一案》), written by **Eliot Crace** (埃利奥特·克雷斯) — grandson of famed children's author **Miriam Crace** (米里亚姆·克雷斯). Susan has a fraught history with Eliot (he once showed up to a meeting an hour late, covered in blood, unwashed for a week). She reads the first 10,000 words, set in **1955 Corfu** (科孚岛), where fictional detective **Atticus Pünd** (阿提库斯·品德) arrives at **Villa Belmar** (贝尔玛别墅) to visit dying **Lady Margaret Chalfont** (玛格丽特·查尔方特夫人) — Miriam's novel-world counterpart. Lady Chalfont, married to antiquities dealer **Elmer Waysmith** (埃尔默·韦史密斯), seeks Pünd's advice about something she overheard concerning her husband. Meanwhile, her son **Jeffrey** (第七代查尔方特伯爵) and daughter **Lola** (洛拉) bicker over money and a crumbling hotel project at **Pontikonisi** (蓬蒂科尼斯) run by **Harry Lyttleton** (哈里·利特尔顿). The episode ends with Lady Chalfont choking to death during a 4 PM meeting with solicitor **Hector Lambrose** (赫克托·兰布罗斯) about her will.

In the real world, Susan meets **Elaine** (伊莲), wife of **Charles Clover** (查尔斯·克洛弗) — the publisher who murdered **Alan Conway** (艾伦·康威) and tried to kill Susan in a prior case. Elaine reveals Miriam was a cruel, controlling "monster" who destroyed Eliot's childhood; the family signed NDAs. She begs Susan to take the job to support Eliot, who has fallen out with his brother **Roland** (罗兰). A flashback shows young Eliot, Roland, and sister **Julia** (朱莉娅) at **Marble Hall** (大理石厅 / 迪韦齐斯) reciting the **Sator square** (播种者，阿雷波，掌控，运转，车轮) and vowing to kill Grandma — mirroring the novel-world poison talk in the cold open.

**Important Character Actions**  
- Susan reluctantly agrees to read Eliot's manuscript; meets Elaine, learns the full truth about Charles and Miriam.  
- Eliot delivers his draft, admits he needs the book to succeed, reveals nervousness.  
- Pünd accepts Lady Chalfont's dinner invitation; notes her illness and the family's tensions.  
- Harry Lyttleton confronts unpaid workers at his hotel site; admits he has no money left.  
- Robert Waysmith (罗伯特·韦史密斯) clashes with father Elmer over catalogue tone and the **calyx krater** (花萼口陶罐) provenance (linked to **Eric Verner** / 埃里克·维纳).  
- Edward Crace (爱德华·克雷斯) tells Eliot he's divorcing his mother and must sell the London house, leaving Eliot homeless.  
- Lady Chalfont summons Lambrose to finalize her will; dies before signing.

**Newly Established Names / Terms**  
- *Pünd's Last Case* (《品德的最后一案》) — continuation novel title  
- Villa Belmar (贝尔玛别墅) — Chalfont summer home on Corfu  
- Pontikonisi (蓬蒂科尼斯) — hotel development site near Corfu town  
- Calyx krater / Euxitheos & Euphronios (欧克西托斯与欧弗罗尼奥斯) — 6th-century BC vase, potential provenance issue  
- Eric Verner (埃里克·维纳) — Elmer's partner, source of antiquities  
- Marble Hall / Devizes (大理石厅 / 迪韦齐斯) — Crace family estate near Salisbury  
- Tiny People (微小一族) — Miriam's children's series (3-inch / 5-cm family)  
- Parkinson (帕金森访谈秀) — BBC talk show Miriam refused  
- NDA (保密协议) — signed by anyone near Miriam  
- Sator square (萨托尔方阵) — palindrome recited by the Crace children as a ritual

**Continuity-Relevant Facts**  
- Charles Clover murdered Alan Conway (bestselling Pünd author) and left Susan in a burning building; he is in prison. Elaine still visits and loves him.  
- Susan edited the original Pünd novels; her previous involvement with Charles cost her reputation and retirement.  
- Eliot's prior project (*Doctor Gee* / 乔治·吉尔医生 series) failed commercially; Charles commissioned it, not Susan.  
- Miriam Crace sold 300 million copies; the Crace name is a "huge brand."  
- The Crace children (Eliot, Roland, Julia, Leila/Lola) were raised under Miriam's tyranny at Marble Hall.  
- Frederick Turner (弗雷德里克·特纳) — Miriam's adopted son — maps to **Achilles Vassilikos** (阿基里斯·瓦西利科斯) in the novel world.  
- Dual-role casting: Miriam / Lady Chalfont (Patricia Hodge), Edward / Elmer (Mark Bonnar), Roland / Robert (Harry Lloyd), Leila / Lola (Yasmine Al Massri), Frederick / Achilles (Zubin Varla).

**Jokes / References for Later Episodes**  
- Sator square chant ("Sator, arepo, tenet, opera, rotas") — appears in both timelines as a childhood ritual.  
- "Clark Kent" glasses joke: Miriam mocks Julia's new frames; Julia retorts "He was Superman."  
- "Greek" used as an insult by Harry: "You don't have to be so... Greek."  
- "Jeepers creeper" / "Two Girls in a Gondola" — Jeffrey's wife's terrible song, played for comic tension.  
- Poison list in cold open (wolfsbane/aconite, funeral bell mushrooms, morphine, deadly nightshade, magnesium, arsenic) — foreshadows murder methods.  
- "Inevitability of crime" — Susan's published theory: two people's chemistry can lead to murder.

**Unresolved Threads**  
1. **Who killed Lady Chalfont?** (Poison? The will? Elmer? Jeffrey? Lola? The overheard secret?)  
2. **What did Lady Chalfont overhear about Elmer?** (She said it "shocked her to the core.")  
3. **Will Susan accept the editing job?** (She hasn't formally agreed.)  
4. **What is in the *Pünd's Last Case* manuscript that mirrors the real-world murder?** (The book-within-a-book clue structure.)  
5. **Who killed Miriam Crace?** (The children's pact: "We all know what has to be done to Grandma... we will do it... but we must never ever tell.")  
6. **Eliot's future hit-and-run death** (teased in series overview) and **Susan being framed** for it.  
7. **Harry Lyttleton's hotel finances** — where will the money come from?  
8. **The calyx krater's true provenance** — Robert's suspicion ("Maybe it was [in a museum]").  
9. **Edward and Miriam's divorce** — why now, after decades?  
10. **Roland and Eliot's falling out** — is it truly about the book, or something deeper?

## S01E02

**S01E02 — Marble Hall Murders**

**Episode Plot**
The novel-world investigation begins. Police Lieutenant Achilles Vassilikos visits Atticus Pünd at his Corfu hotel to inform him Lady Margaret Chalfont has died, suspected poisoned. Pünd reluctantly agrees to investigate after Athens applies pressure. At Villa Belmar, the family gathers: son Jeffrey (the Earl), daughter Judith Lyttleton, Judith's husband Harry, Lola (Jeffrey's wife), Robert Waysmith (Elmer's son), and young Cedric (Lola and Jeffrey's son). Maid Maria Kapodistria describes how someone knocked on the kitchen window — possibly Luan the gardener — as a distraction while she prepared tea, after which she found the lid on Lady Chalfont's teapot removed. A service staircase connects the kitchen to the first-floor bedrooms; any family member could have slipped down to poison the tea. Lab results later confirm Lady Chalfont was killed by aconitine. Solicitor Hector Lambrose reads the will: the bulk of a £900,000 estate goes to Elmer; the children receive token amounts (£20,000 each, Robert only £10,000). The family erupts in fury, accusing Elmer of murder. A physical brawl breaks out between Jeffrey/Harry and Robert/Elmer before Vassilikos restores order.

In the real world, Susan reads deeper into the manuscript and spots Eliot's hidden references — "Belmar" is an anagram of "marble" (Marble Hall), and Margaret Chalfont shares Miriam Crace's initials. Eliot admits Elmer is based on someone he knows and that the entire family hated Miriam. Susan visits Marble Hall, now a public museum, and meets Frederick Turner, Miriam's adopted son, who still manages the property. Frederick reveals he was orphaned at birth, adopted from St Ambrose Children's Home in Salisbury, worked as the family accountant for 15 years, and lives in the eaves. Susan then meets retired Dr. Lambert, who drops a bombshell: young Eliot was caught stealing medicine from the doctor's bag days before Miriam's death. Lambert insists Miriam died of mitral stenosis (heart disease) and there was nothing suspicious — but the timing is striking.

**Important Character Actions**
- Pünd accepts the investigation under pressure from Athens; questions each family member about their movements at 4 PM.
- Vassilikos identifies the service staircase as the likely poisoner's route — any family member could have used it.
- Elmer reveals he knows the will's contents and hints the family will be "in for a big surprise"; later admits he helped draft it.
- Robert is cowed by Elmer, obeying him slavishly even when dismissed.
- Judith describes Elmer's first wife's suicide (threw herself under a train at Grand Central Station).
- Pünd spots a toxicology book (*Erskine's Toxicology: A Study of Poisons*) on Elmer's shelf, recently read.
- Kristos shows Pünd photographs of a calyx krater stolen from the Vathy Museum in Ithaca by Nazis in 1944; Pünd declares he can now "make his move."
- Susan identifies anagram clues in the manuscript (Belmar → marble; Miriam Crace / Margaret Chalfont = same initials).
- Susan meets Frederick Turner, who warns her not to damage Miriam's reputation.
- Dr. Lambert reveals Eliot's childhood medicine theft and insists Miriam's death was natural (mitral stenosis).
- Lambrose reads the will; the family turns violently on Elmer.
- Vassilikos ends the episode musing: "I wonder who will be murdered next."

**Newly Established Names / Terms**
- Maria Kapodistria — maid at Villa Belmar, from Ithaca; makes Lady Chalfont's tea daily
- Luan — the gardener; possibly the one who knocked on the kitchen window as a diversion
- Mr. Lambrose — Chalfont family solicitor, summoned about the will
- Sofia Androulakis — Lambrose's bilingual private secretary, engaged to Antonis (hotel worker), about to leave the practice
- Dr. Lambert — retired Crace family doctor, lives in Urchfont near Marble Hall; treated Arthur Crace and caught Eliot stealing medicine
- Erskine's Toxicology: A Study of Poisons — book on Elmer's shelf, recently read
- Aconitine — the confirmed poison (from wolfsbane/aconite, foreshadowed in E01's cold open)
- Calyx krater — 6th-century BC vase stolen from Vathy Museum, Ithaca, by Nazis in 1944; Pünd connects it to Elmer
- Votsalo — restaurant in Corfu old town where Elmer and Robert had lunch
- St Ambrose Children's Home and Orphanage — institution in Salisbury where Frederick Turner grew up
- Mitral stenosis — the official cause of Miriam Crace's death (heart disease)
- "Tiny People" song — "We are the Tiny People / We're tiny / That's why we're coming for you / Cos Tiny People matter" — plays at Marble Hall museum

**Continuity-Relevant Facts**
- Elmer's first wife died by suicide (Grand Central Station, New York); he inherited her fortune — establishes a pattern of wives dying and Elmer profiting.
- The service staircase allows any first-floor resident to reach the kitchen unseen — means anyone in the house had opportunity.
- Only Elmer knew the will's contents before the reading; the family's outrage gives everyone motive.
- Frederick Turner was adopted from an orphanage, never called Miriam "Mother," worked as accountant then house manager — the novel-world Achilles maps to him (dual-role casting confirmed).
- "Darnley" is an anagram of "Ryeland" — Eliot uses real people's names as anagrams, just as Alan Conway did. Susan calls him out on it.
- Eliot's childhood medicine theft occurred days before Miriam's death; he had a "morbid fascination with poison and disease."
- Miriam died on April 12, 2004; her funeral was at Westminster Abbey; the Queen sent a letter of condolence.
- Marble Hall is now a museum, open to the public (£12 admission); guided by "Brenda."

**Jokes / References Useful for Later Episodes**
- Susan asks why Vassilikos speaks perfect English; Eliot writes in an explanation (father was a translator at the British embassy) mid-episode — a meta joke about the writer fixing plot holes in real time.
- "Don't you think it's a bit League of Nations?" — Susan teasing Eliot about the trilingual Corfu setting.
- "Tiny People" theme song and "You can always stop at teatime!" — the stopwatch stopped at 4 o'clock, the recurring Miriam catchphrase.
- Cedric's bluntness: "Did you kill her?" to Lola, and "Cos she wouldn't give you the money for your play" — comic relief that also plants motive seeds.
- Jeffrey's Paddington Station quip ("Where did you expect it to be? Paddington Station?") when discussing funeral location.
- "I sometimes think about going back just to burn it down" — Eliot on Marble Hall, echoing the children's pact from E01.
- Netflix has bought rights to make a new Tiny People series — mentioned by Brenda the tour guide, a meta-commentary on IP exploitation.
- Arthur Crace's taxidermy hobby and his chemical exposure (treated with dimercaprol) — may become relevant if poison methods are traced.

**Unresolved Threads**
1. Who poisoned Lady Chalfont with aconitine? Everyone had opportunity (service staircase) and now motive (the will).
2. What did Lady Chalfont overhear about Elmer that "shocked her to the core"?
3. What is Pünd's "move" regarding the stolen calyx krater — and how does it connect to Elmer?
4. Did Miriam Crace truly die of mitral stenosis, or was her death unnatural? Eliot's medicine theft and the novel's poisoned matriarch are suspicious parallels.
5. What is the significance of Eliot writing Elmer based on "someone I know" — who exactly?
6. The stolen Nazi vase and Elmer's antiquities dealings — is there a crime ring?
7. Sofia Androulakis leaving Lambrose's practice — coincidence or will she become a loose end?
8. Frederick Turner's warning: "We will not allow you or Eliot to damage her name" — what is he protecting?
9. The NDA the Crace family signed — what are they hiding about Miriam?
10. Eliot's coming hit-and-run death and Susan being framed (teased in series overview, not yet advanced).

## S01E03 — Marble Hall Murders

**Episode Plot**

The investigation deepens on both timelines. In the real world, Susan meets **Jonathan Crace** (乔纳森·克雷斯), Eliot's uncle and controller of the entire Tiny People estate, who warns her off Eliot's "defamatory" claims about Miriam. Jonathan is furious about Eliot's upcoming **Front Row** radio appearance and threatens Susan through lawyers. Susan then has lunch with **Roland Crace** (罗兰), who confirms Marble Hall was a "creepy, horrible place" and Miriam was indeed a monster — but insists Eliot is wrong about the poisoning, claiming the children made a potion as a game inspired by Roald Dahl's *George's Marvellous Medicine* that never reached Miriam.

Eliot delivers the next 10,000 words and reveals he's going on Front Row despite Susan's warnings. Susan meets Roland's fiancée **Dave**, an investment fund manager, at dinner — he's painfully shy and remembers almost nothing about Eliot's life. Meanwhile, Jonathan pressures Roland to keep Susan under control, calling her "trouble."

Eliot goes on **Front Row** with **Samira Arvid** (萨米拉·阿尔维德) and drops a bombshell: he publicly accuses Miriam's killer of being in the audience, claims he saw someone enter her room the night she died, and reveals he's hidden a secret message in *Pünd's Last Case*. The family is horrified. Later, **Gillian Crace** (吉利安·克雷斯) reveals to Susan that she had an affair, is now pregnant (Eliot is infertile), and Eliot stormed out with a knife. Susan finds Eliot at **Boone's** club in Notting Hill; he admits he intended to use the knife on Gillian, not himself, and fires Susan as his editor in a drunken rage.

At Jonathan's 20th anniversary party for Miriam, Susan meets **Julia Crace** (朱莉娅·克雷斯) — Eliot's sister, a geography teacher at a Pimlico school who changed her name to escape the family legacy. Jonathan makes a speech about the Tiny People and the Netflix deal. Eliot crashes the stage, publicly declares Miriam was murdered by someone in the room, fires Susan on the mic, and is thrown out by security. Jonathan orders Susan removed too. **Leila Crace** (莱拉), Jonathan's wife, quietly asks Susan to call her — hinting she wants to talk privately. Eliot drives off into the night.

In the novel world, Pünd and Vassilikos track down **Yorgos Leonidas** (尤尔戈斯·莱奥尼达斯), an 84-year-old herbalist at the Kassiopi market, who confirms selling aconitine on the morning Lady Chalfont died. The buyer wore a white suit, straw hat, sunglasses, and had white hair — he smelled of surgical spirit and claimed it was for a patient. They trace him to **Hotel Poseidon**, run by English-born **Valerie Androlakis** (瓦莱丽·安德罗拉基斯), who recalls a white-haired man ("John Ford") who booked two nights, never slept in the bed, but showered and left. In his room: an empty tin of **Spatz** shoe polish under the bed. Pünd suffers a medical episode and Vassilikos reveals his war wound — lost an eye at the **Metaxas Line** in April 1941, hit by a hand grenade. Pünd confesses he is terminally ill and spent the war in a prison camp for speaking out against the Nazis. The two bond over shared wartime trauma. Pünd reconstructs the suspect's timeline and finds a critical gap: the man was at the Kassiopi market at noon and at a lunch in Kerkyra by 1:00 PM — an impossible journey by the single coastal road on market day. The white suit and suitcase vanished; a shower was taken; shoe polish was used. Something doesn't add up.

- Jonathan confronts Susan and warns her off; controls the estate and the Netflix deal.
- Roland admits Miriam was a monster but says Eliot's poisoning theory is fiction — a childhood game.
- Eliot goes on Front Row and publicly accuses Miriam's killer, reveals the hidden-message clue in his book, then fires Susan.
- Gillian confesses her affair and pregnancy; Eliot leaves with a knife, admits he intended violence against her.
- Jonathan invites Susan to the party to "keep her on the inside"; Roland reluctantly complies.
- Leila Crace quietly approaches Susan, requesting a private conversation.
- Pünd and Vassilikos trace aconitine to a disguised buyer at Kassiopi and Hotel Poseidon.
- Pünd and Vassilikos share personal histories — both imprisoned during WWII on opposing sides.
- Pünd reconstructs an impossible timeline that undermines Elmer's alibi.

**Newly Established Names / Terms**

- Jonathan Crace (乔纳森·克雷斯) — Eliot's uncle, estate controller
- Julia Crace (朱莉娅·克雷斯) — Eliot's sister, geography teacher at a Pimlico school, changed her name to avoid the Miriam legacy

## S01E04 — Marble Hall Murders

**Episode Plot**
Cold open: Susan wakes to a nightmare blending the party audio (Eliot: "She was poisoned... This is your fault! You know who you are!") with an intrusion at her flat — voices chanting Tiny People rhymes ("Eenie, meenie, miney, mo / Tiny People never go"), calling her "jolly annoying," spelling out M-Y-O-B (mind your own business) before attacking her.

Real world: DI Blakeney and DC Wardlaw (沃德劳) tell Susan Eliot was hit by a car on Maynard Street leaving Jonathan's party and is in a coma on life support. It was a hit-and-run on an empty street 10m from the exit — deliberate. Eliot's drunken speech claimed Miriam was poisoned 20 years ago, that he'd seen the killer, and had named them in his book. The detectives suspect Susan (fired, publicly humiliated): her MG shows radiator-grill damage, a fabric snippet matching Eliot's jacket, and possible blood — evidence Susan insists was planted. The car is impounded under the Road Traffic Act 1988 reg. 2005; she's asked to surrender her passport. Pünd's voice frames the theme: "The fly caught by the spider must examine every strand of the web."

Susan visits the hospital, meets Edward Crace — Eliot has brain trauma, broken ribs, spinal injury; the next 24 hours are critical. Edward calls Eliot a wild child who blames him for everything and points Susan to Charles Clover, who worked on *Tiny Angels* and *Tiny World* and "was more of a father to Eliot than I ever was." At Eliot's house Roland turns on Susan, demanding she drop the novel ("that novel's not happening"); Susan deduces Roland is the father of Gillian's baby — the real reason the brothers fell out. Roland throws her out. Susan then persuades Elaine to arrange a prison meeting with Charles Clover.

Prison: Matthew Flynn (马修·弗林) reveals Causton won an auction for a new Tiny People book by Michael Morpurgo (迈克尔·莫波戈), and secretly hired Eliot for the new Atticus Pünd continuation — "you chose the wrong Crace." Jonathan was furious; Susan was hired as a babysitter, not an editor. *Pünd's Last Case* is effectively dead: "Eliot isn't even dead yet." / "But the book is."

Novel world: Harrison Carter (哈里森·卡特), American professor and wartime "Monuments Man" (MFAA, 古迹卫士), tells Pünd he tracked a calyx krater stolen from the Vathi Museum on Ithaca via Austria to Eric Werner's (埃里克·韦尔纳) Geneva gallery — Werner is Elmer Waysmith's business partner. His confrontation with Elmer two weeks earlier was overheard by Lady Chalfont (open windows beneath her bedroom) — her husband accused of thief, possibly war criminal — and she immediately called her solicitor, presumably to change her will. Yet she died before the 4 PM Lambrose meeting, having tea instead with her son and Harry Littleton. Pünd finds her forgetting the lawyer "strange."
