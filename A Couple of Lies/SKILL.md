## Character Relationships

- **Carl & Helene**: Married couple from Stockholm archipelago; attempting to repair strained marriage through the home swap.
- **Adam & Scarlett**: Married couple living in Sicily; similarly using the swap to address marital issues.
- **Cross-couple dynamic**: Strangers linked only by the home-exchange arrangement; each couple occupies the other's home simultaneously, creating parallel surveillance/mistrust narratives.
- **Neighbours / locals**: Secondary characters in both locations (Stockholm archipelago community; Sicilian villa surroundings) who contribute to the uneasy atmosphere.

---

## World Setting

- **Primary locations**:
  - **Stockholm archipelago (Skärgården)**: Carl & Helene's modern architectural home; isolated, affluent, tight-knit island community with nosy neighbours.
  - **Sicily, Italy**: Adam & Scarlett's sun-drenched villa; presented as romantic idyll but reveals discrepancies upon arrival.
- **Tone**: Nordic noir meets Mediterranean gothic — clinical Swedish minimalism contrasted with sun-bleached Sicilian decay.
- **Timeframe**: Contemporary; the swap spans several weeks.

---

## Overall Plot Premise

Two wealthy, marriage-strained couples — Carl & Helene (Stockholm) and Adam & Scarlett (Sicily) — arrange a holiday home swap via a premium exchange service. Each couple hopes the change of scenery will rekindle their relationship. Instead, both discover the other's home doesn't match its listing: locked rooms, mismatched interiors, odd neighbour behaviour. As they investigate the strangers now living in their own homes, long-buried secrets, betrayals, and psychological manipulation surface. The swap becomes a duel of surveillance and gaslighting, where neither couple can trust the other — or even their own spouse.

## Speaking Styles & Dialogue Notes

- **Carl (Fares Fares)**: Measured, controlled Swedish male lead; dialogue often terse, internalised frustration; switches between Swedish and English (Sicily scenes).
- **Helene (Frida Gustavsson)**: Sharp, observant, increasingly suspicious; Swedish with educated cadence; emotional restraint cracking under pressure.
- **Adam (Jakob Oftebro)**: Charismatic but opaque; Norwegian actor speaking English with Scandinavian inflection; charming surface masking calculation.
- **Scarlett (Emma Appleton)**: British actress playing a character in Sicily; English with posh/upper-class markers; performative warmth concealing agenda.
- **Language mix**: Primary dialogue in Swedish (Stockholm) and English (Sicily/cross-couple); occasional Italian (Sicilian locals). Subtitles should flag language shifts.
- **Tone**: Restrained, subtext-heavy; silences and glances carry weight. Avoid over-explanatory translation — preserve ambiguity.

---

## Cultural Context for Translation

- **Swedish social codes**: *Jantelagen* (don't think you're special), conflict avoidance, privacy obsession — characters violate these by inviting strangers into their intimate space.
- **Stockholm archipelago culture**: Summer-house tradition, island insularity, neighbour surveillance as social norm.
- **Home-exchange culture**: Premium platforms (HomeExchange, etc.) imply trust-based community; the series subverts this.
- **Nordic noir conventions**: Cold palette, procedural undertones, family secrets, institutional distrust — all present but refracted through domestic thriller lens.
- **Sicilian setting**: Not tourist postcard; heat, decay, isolation, Catholic imagery, *omertà*-adjacent silence from locals.
- **Class markers**: Both couples are affluent professionals; dialogue reflects educated, cosmopolitan registers — avoid colloquial flattening.
- **Gender dynamics**: Mutual gaslighting; neither wife nor husband is purely victim/perpetrator. Translation must preserve moral ambiguity.
