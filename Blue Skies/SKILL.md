## Overall Plot Premise

After her mother's death and the end of her Military Police service, **Jodi Larsen** returns to her fictional hometown to serve as a criminal investigator with the National Park Police in **Crystal Ridge National Park** — a 5,000-square-mile wilderness that draws332 million visitors a year and is staffed by just 40 rangers, a small support unit, one tech specialist, and three criminal investigators. On arrival, Jodi picks up an unexpected partner: **Blue**, a stray Labrador (or yellow Lab–type dog) with tracking instincts sharper than most trained K9s. The series alternates two layers — self-contained weekly wilderness cases (missing persons, environmental crime, suspicious deaths) and a serialized personal arc in which Jodi slowly reconciles with her father Bill, revisits old feelings for SAR volunteer Ryan Miller, and investigates the suspicious circumstances of her mother Claire's death at a place called Whispering Pines. Season 2 expands the threats into a wider conspiracy.

## World Setting & Cultural Context

- **National Park Service (NPS)** — US federal agency managing national parks. The fictional Crystal Ridge draws on archetypes of Yellowstone / Glacier / Olympic / Canadian Rockies.
- **National Park Police** — real federal law-enforcement branch within the NPS; jurisdiction over park lands.
- **Military Police (MP)** — US Armed Forces law-enforcement branch; Jodi transitions out of MP into NPS.
- **Retired military working dog** — Blue is a former US Army K9 (call sign Maverick), handler Randall, then adopted by Colonel Andrews after Randall's medical discharge; lost after Randall's death.
- **Faith-friendly network** — UPtv / UP Faith & Family is a Christian-affiliated family brand. The show foregrounds hope, healing, family reconnection, and teamwork; minimal coarse language; no graphic violence.
- **Filmed in Vancouver, BC** — coastal temperate-rainforest vegetation stands in for high-alpine US park scenery; viewers may notice the visual mismatch.
- **Serialized arc** — begins S1 (Claire's death) and escalates S2 into threats "beyond the park borders."

## Speaking Styles & Character Voices

- **Jodi Larsen** — clipped, professional, dryly witty; police/Military jargon fluent; starts guarded and solitary, gradually softens.
- **Bill Larsen** — gruff, terse, regretful; long silences; measured sentences; old-school cop cadence.
- **Ryan Miller** — warm, community-oriented; defuses tension with gentle humor; uses a calm, steady tone.
- **Liza King** — formal, by-the-book, calm; rank-conscious speech; rare warmth leaks through.
- **Alex Meeks** — sardonic, embittered, sharp-edged; complaint and challenge dominate.
- **Nora Esposito** — warm, emotionally intelligent; casual banter; supportive rather than combative.
- **Tyson Bernard** — socially awkward; tech jargon heavy; rapid, occasionally shy delivery.
- **Blue** — nonverbal; barks, whines, body cues drive plot. Closed-caption labels read [DOG BARKS], [DOG WHINES], etc. → render as [狗吠声] / [狗呜咽声].

## Translation Notes for Subtitles

- **No prior ASSRT subtitles exist for this series.** All renderings above are first-established. Stay internally consistent across S1 and S2.
- **Title ambiguity**: the show title "Blue Skies" coincides with the dog's name. Translate the show as 《蓝天》or 《碧空》; keep the dog as 布鲁 to keep them distinct. Both meanings can be honored by rendering Jodi's S1 quote "blue skies, fresh air, and snow-capped mountains" as 蓝天、纯净空气、雪山环抱.
- **Faith network register**: avoid gratuitous profanity; the show uses minimal coarse language; favor a clean, warm subtitle register.
- **Rank precedence**: when Liza King is addressed formally, use 警监; informal speech may use 主管. Bill Larsen, as a retired captain, is sometimes referred to as 老警监 (retired captain) — keep that as 比尔老警监 or just 比尔, depending on speaker.
- **Episode-title capitalization** matches the source exactly (Title Case in English). Chinese subs should follow the show's title-card format.
- **Standard caption SFX** (e.g., [MUSIC PLAYING], [WIND HOWLING], [DOG BARKS]) — keep bracketed, translate only the descriptive phrase.
