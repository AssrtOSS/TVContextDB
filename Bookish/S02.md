## S02E02

### Plot

Harold Sneed, a psychic medium, is stabbed through the heart during a séance at his home. All six participants sat in a circle with hands joined at the time, making it a locked-room mystery — no one could have reached Sneed without breaking the circle. Gabriel Book, who knew Sneed from wartime intelligence work, inserts himself into the investigation alongside Inspector Bliss.

### Important Character Actions

- Gabriel Book visits Mrs Dredge under the pretence of friendship to assess her dependence on Sneed, then attends a séance to observe his methods firsthand.
- Book confronts Sneed privately after the séance, exposing his cold-reading techniques and revealing he knows Sneed worked for British intelligence during the war, identifying Nazi sympathisers like Harrison Hopper and Sir Gideon Fay.
- Trottie Book partners with Jack to deploy the "pattern book" — tracking suspects' spending habits at local shops to build behavioural profiles.
- Jack receives a startlingly specific message in German from his supposedly dead father Felix during the séance, triggered by a photograph Jack provided.
- Inspector Bliss initially fixates on the two people sitting beside Sneed (Professor Chopra and Miss Mercer), but fingerprint analysis upends this.

### Newly Established Names and Terms

- **Summerland** — spiritualist term for the afterlife.
- **Grief leech** — Book's caustic label for mediums who exploit the bereaved.
- **Pattern book** — Book and Trottie's investigative method: analysing regular spending habits to map a person's routine.
- **Harrison Hopper** — executed Nazi sympathiser (Blackshirt, interned, escaped to Germany, arrested 1944, hanged early 1946) whose evidence came from Sneed.
- **Felix von Bode** — Jack's father, German, supposedly died in a sanatorium in 1935.
- **Crossed seven** — continental European handwriting habit (7 with a horizontal stroke), spotted on the back of Jack's photograph.

### Continuity-Relevant Facts

- Sneed was co-opted by British intelligence during the war; Book was also "pressed into service."
- Jack's father Felix: death certificate says 1935, but a photograph received last year is dated 5 December 1936 — he was alive after his reported death.
- Book and Trottie are actively "pulling strings" to get into Germany to investigate Felix.
- Mrs Dredge's son Alf died of accidental morphine overdose; his fingers were ink-stained at death (he had been writing, but no pen or paper at bedside).
- Professor Chopra's daughter Lakshmi was killed in a hit-and-run in Scotland; the driver was never traced.
- The Plumchute sisters lost brother Teddy in WWI (Western Front); they have attended Sneed since 1928.

### Jokes and References Useful for Later Episodes

- **Great Expectations**: Book calls Jack "Pip to my Joe Gargery."
- **Rider Haggard**: "She who must be obeyed" — Ayesha's name origin.
- **Poe**: Amontillado sherry / *The Cask of Amontillado* joke at breakfast.
- **Book-trade quiz**: duodecimo, bastard title, half-cloth binding — establishes Jack's apprenticeship.
- **Political quip**: "Bugger Attlee!" / "Bring back Winston" from Colonel Winters.
- **Empire metaphor**: Book's crumbling flat — "Like the empire."

### Unresolved Threads

1. **Who killed Sneed?** Fingerprints on the dagger match Harrison Hopper — a man executed months earlier. The episode ends on the impossible: "Harold Sneed was murdered… by a dead man."
2. **Is Sneed's mediumship genuine?** The German message to Jack ("Trauerst nicht, mein Sohn") was too specific for cold reading — the crossed-seven on the photo back is the only clue Book finds.
3. **What happened to Felix von Bode?** Alive in Dec 1936, not 1935. Who sent the photo? Why?
4. **Lakshmi Chopra's hit-and-run driver** — never identified.
5. **Book and Trottie's Germany operation** — still in motion, strings being pulled.
6. **Book's own war service** — hinted at but not detailed.

## S02E03

## S02E03 Summary

### Episode Plot
A 75-year-old tailor, **Max Kleinmann**, claims he killed an intruder (**Martin Eden**) in self-defense at midnight. Inspector Bliss doesn't believe him — Kleinmann is too frail. The victim's identity card reads "Martin Eden," which Gabriel Book recognizes as the protagonist of Jack London's novel: a fictional alias. The dead man was dressed for the City at midnight, had bruised knuckles from throwing a punch, and died from a single blow to the throat. A broken bottle of cleaning solvent lies at the scene; only Kleinmann's prints are on it. Crucially, the body vanishes — it never reaches the morgue.

Parallel threads converge: **Ted Craik**, a chess master and "Man Who Wants to Know" columnist, has gone missing after a trade mission to Moscow. His wife Lily reports he called from the airport saying "I've been used" and mentioned a "pattern." Craik's suit went missing in Moscow; the Soviet Council of Industry lent him a spare. Meanwhile, **Jack Blunt** receives a free pinstripe suit from Kleinmann, then gets picked up by a glamorous woman (**Bunty Chapman**'s friend) for a party at the **Chalcot Hotel**. Trottie and Reggie tail him, fearing a setup.

### Important Character Actions
- **Gabriel Book**: identifies the fake identity; finds Russian editions of *Martin Eden* in his shop; hints at his own wartime intelligence past ("I've been part of it myself"); deduces a "secret world" is operating.
- **Inspector Bliss**: investigates the Kleinmann case while concealing a pending promotion (likely into intelligence); knows the Kleinmanns personally; insists on doing things "by the book."
- **Trottie Book**: protects Jack; recruits Reggie to follow him to the Chalcot; spots the suit's origin.
- **Jack Blunt**: accepts the suit; goes to the Chalcot with a stranger; ends up cornered in a hotel room (trousers demanded).
- **Max Kleinmann**: confesses to protect his family (wife Ruth, granddaughter Hannah); reveals he made Craik's Moscow suit; knows Craik well.
- **Hannah (Krasinski)**: returned from Warsaw/Berlin/Paris/Dieppe via Red Cross; mother was English, father Polish; speaks perfect English.
- **Nora**: locates the Russian editions in the shop.

### Newly Established Names / Terms
- **Martin Eden** — fictional alias (Jack London novel) used by the victim.
- **Hannah Krasinski** — granddaughter's real name; Polish father, English mother.
- **Ted Craik** — chess master, *Chronicle Diary* columnist ("The Man Who Wants to Know"), Anglo-Soviet trade delegate, now missing.
- **Soviet Council of Industry** — lent Craik a replacement suit in Moscow.
- **The Chalcot Hotel** — Knightsbridge venue for the party.
- **Bunty Chapman** — hostess of the birthday party.
- **Kleinmann's** — tailor shop off Savile Row.
- **"The Man Who Wants to Know"** — Craik's newspaper column.

### Continuity-Relevant Facts
- Kleinmann naturalized in **1933** ("a very bad year"); Jewish family history (Warsaw, "married out," paper signatures meant to stop persecution).
- **Ted Craik** was at the **relief of Belsen**; voted Labour ("voted for Clem"); "dresses to the left."
- Craik's Moscow trip was ostensibly for **Anglo-Soviet friendship**.
- Gabriel and Bliss fought "on the same side in the last war"; Gabriel admits past involvement in a "secret world" (intelligence).
- **"The old war" vs "a new one beginning"** — explicit Cold War framing.
- Bliss's unexplained promotion points to a move into intelligence work.
- Russian editions of *Martin Eden* in Gabriel's shop suggest a deliberate trail.

### Jokes / References for Later
- Bookish headline puns: **"Terror at the Tailor's Shop," "The Bespoke Murder," "Double-Breasted Death."**
- **"On me Jack Jones"** — Cockney rhyming slang for "on my own."
- Latin banter: *Et in Arcadia ego* / *Mors vehementi ictu*.
- **"Dead as a turbot"** — period slang.
- **"Without tea, I'm a kind of Arizona"** — dry desert.
- **"Softly, softly"** — police caution, immediately violated by Trottie barging into the Chalcot.
- Lavender marriage dynamic: Gabriel and Trottie's dry, protective rapport.

### Unresolved Threads
1. **Who killed the man** (and who he really was)?
2. **Where is the body** — and who took it?
3. **Ted Craik's fate** — what "pattern" did he see? Why did the Soviets swap his suit?
4. **The Russian editions** of *Martin Eden* — planted or pre-existing?
5. **Bliss's promotion** — into what role?
6. **Gabriel's wartime "secret world"** role — what did he do?
7. **Jack's predicament** at the Chalcot — who is the woman, and what do they want?
8. **Hannah's full story** — her mother's death, her survival route.
9. **The Kleinmanns' deeper secrets** — what else are they hiding?

## S02E05

**S02E05 — Dead Man's Whistle**

**Plot**  
Gabriel Book and Jack travel to Schloss von Bode in Germany, posing as antiquarian bookseller "Sebastian Melmoth" and assistant "Jack Blunt" to buy a Shakespeare Second Folio from Luther von Bode. Their real aim: investigate the 1936 death of Jack's father, Felix, Luther's son. During the Feast of Saint Nicholas, Luther's longtime physician Dr Kaufmann is murdered—choked with coal and covered in soot, mimicking the Knecht Ruprecht legend (Saint Nicholas's punitive companion). Inspector Bliss, coincidentally present, investigates. Soon after, the butler Duttweiler is also killed (barbiturate overdose, stuffed in a sack). Gabriel and Jack are expelled after Luther discovers Jack's true identity.

**Important Character Actions**  
- **Jack** reveals he is Luther's grandson (Felix's son), blowing their cover.  
- **Gabriel** deduces the Knecht Ruprecht motif, links the murders to folklore, and discovers Luther paid for *two* sanatorium patients (Felix + an unnamed 1912 admission).  
- **Bliss** secures the scene, interviews guests, and realizes the "official story" of Felix's death is false.  
- **Luther** admits he considered Felix's homosexuality a "perversion" and "disease"; confesses he would have "put Felix down like a sick animal."  
- **Duchess Alberta (Yamauchi)** blames Kaufmann for ignoring her husband's fatal peritonitis.  
- **Irma Queisser** (Luther's ward, father suicided) takes secobarbital—the same drug used to kill Duttweiler.

**Newly Established Names/Terms**  
- **Schloss von Bode** — the von Bode family castle in Germany.  
- **Knecht Ruprecht** — Germanic folklore figure who accompanies Saint Nicholas, punishes naughty children with coal, sack, and birch rods; becomes the killer's signature.  
- **Second Folio (1632)** — the Shakespeare volume Luther offers for sale; withdrawn after the confrontation.  
- **Sanatorium patient #2** — unnamed, resident 1912–1946 (ended two months ago); paid for by Luther.  
- **Secobarbital** — barbiturate Irma uses for sleep; murder weapon for Duttweiler.  
- **Frau Yamauchi / Duchess Alberta** — Japanese noblewoman, widowed, holds grudge against Kaufmann.

**Continuity-Relevant Facts**  
- Felix died (or "died") 5 Dec 1936; Luther claimed brain tumour, but Felix was photographed at the Christmas party that day.  
- Luther has no friends, "only people who can be useful."  
- Gabriel & Trottie's lavender marriage reinforced; Trottie calls to check on Gabriel, warns "walls have ears."  
- Jack's parentage confirmed (Felix's son, Luther's grandson); Luther initially denies, then sees resemblance.  
- Gabriel's alias "Sebastian Melmoth" recognised as Oscar Wilde's exile pseudonym and the gothic novel *Melmoth the Wanderer*.

**Jokes / References for Later**  
- "The butler did it" — Gabriel's quip when Duttweiler becomes suspect, then victim.  
- Knecht Ruprecht nursery rhyme recited twice (full text in subtitles) — becomes leitmotif for the killings.  
- "White and snowy lies the land…" — rhyme bookends the episode.  
- Gabriel's "Anastasia" comparison — Jack's return likened to the Romanov impostor.  
- Duchess Alberta's "Olympic ideal / Aryan boys" Munich 1936 memory — hints at her past with Luther.

**Unresolved Threads**  
- Identity of the 1912 sanatorium patient (paid until two months ago).  
- Who is the Knecht Ruprecht killer? (Motives: Alberta/Kaufmann grudge, Kaufmann–Duttweiler feud, Irma's access to secobarbital, Luther's secrets.)  
- What really happened to Felix in 1936 — murder? concealment?  
- Luther's reaction to expulsion — will he cooperate or double down?  
- Gabriel & Jack stranded without the Folio or Luther's trust; must investigate from outside.
