## S01E02

# The Blame — S01E02 Summary

## Plot
The case against Kyle Frasier, Sophie's skating coach, seems airtight: he admits he developed feelings for Sophie, made an advance in his car, then dropped her from the team citing poor performance. Sophie used a burner phone to dial 999 at 5:26pm Thursday — exactly when Frasier claims he was home alone, yet his car was caught on camera; the fatal blow came from a left-handed assailant, and Frasier is left-handed. A search of his home turns up a jewellery receipt for a woman's necklace bought 24 hours before the murder, and the matching necklace is found in a make-up bag at the rink. Under interview Frasier pivots: the necklace was a gift for 18-year-old skater Amalie Rafkins, whom he is secretly dating; she photographed them together on his sofa that night (his real alibi), and his mother had borrowed his car. His account of the make-up bag (it belongs to Amalie, not Sophie) contradicts what Amalie told police, but no proof either way. Emma abruptly leaves the interview mid-flow to chase a different lead; Frasier is later released without further action on the murder, with the team planning to pursue him under Section 16 so he can never work with young people again.

In parallel, Emma has been quietly building a case against her own partner and secret lover, Tom. She has digital forensics (Neil Shergill) isolate the background audio of Sophie's 999 call: the distant chirping is a police radio — Sophie was with a police officer when she died. She also notices Tom's habitually filthy car is now spotless, suggesting a professional clean. She used the Frasier house search to probe DC Lewis Brimah, who confirmed he alone stayed late on the CID floor last Thursday (the night of the "Keep Our Streets Safe" event at Mountgrove Square) and never saw Tom, despite Tom's claim he returned for his wallet. Emma takes it all to DCI Walker, who arrests Tom in person.

An extended thread shows the old Vogel case: Alan (at one point "Stanley") Vogel, a calm, forensically aware serial predator, is in custody for the rape and murder of Amber Dodds. With no semen (he used a condom), no witnesses, and texts the defence will call flirting, CPS will never file rape charges — so Emma and Tom agree to make the murder charge stick "together," the collusion behind Emma's buried secret.

In the cell, Tom vents that his career is over (36, no education, nothing but detective work), assumes Emma will now tell Walker about the Vogel falsification, and hears her say "Because I love you. And I never want to see you again." He then gives his real account: after the safety event, Sophie approached him wanting to privately report an abuse of power at Wakestead station — no specifics, and he only learned of the video when they watched it together. He drove while she talked, offered to help her come forward, she asked for time to think, and he dropped her minutes from her home — the last time he saw her. He admits cleaning the car (he'd driven a murder victim) and lying out of fear.

Shergill then reports there is no digital link whatsoever between Tom and Sophie (no calls, texts, DMs; she never even searched his name; Tom's phone never pinged the Dankworth Forest mast at the time of death). Emma visits Tom's cell and lays out the real theory: dozens of Wakestead officers were at the event, any of whom could have seen Sophie get into Tom's car; the WhatsApp group chat (which included material about teenagers) would mean prison time for its members if exposed — "Someone at Wakestead killed Sophie Madsen." Proving it clears Tom's name. "So what do we do?" — cliffhanger.

## Important character actions
- Emma: covertly investigates Tom (999 audio, car cleanliness, Thursday alibi via Lewis), hands everything to Walker including the dashcam video, continues leading the case, recruits Lewis as her new partner.
- Tom: arrested for Sophie's murder; maintains innocence; explains his secret contact with Sophie and why he lied.
- Walker: green-lights the arrest despite Emma expecting him to defend Tom; briefs the team that Crane shared the video; bans press contact; calls senior management about next steps.
- Lewis: unknowingly corroborates Emma's suspicion, then works out he was chosen for the search team precisely because of his Thursday late shifts — and covers for her rather than exposing it.
- Frasier: reconstructs an alibi (Amalie, sofa photo, mum borrowing the car) that gets him released on the murder.
- Stevens and James: shown slacking (slurping drinks) — part of the toxic-boys-club texture.

## S01E01

## S01E01 Summary — *The Blame*

### Episode Plot
The series opens in Wakestead police station where male officers (Stevens, James, Drummond) trade misogynistic banter about a local girl, Sophie Madsen. DI Emma Crane and DI Tom Radley — trusted partners with growing romantic tension — are assigned by DCI Walker to investigate 16-year-old Sophie’s disappearance after a truncated 999 call traced to Dankworth Forest.  

Emma and Tom learn Sophie was a figure skater kicked off her team two months prior by coach Kyle Frasier. The parents reveal Sophie bought a burner phone after losing hers. Visiting the rink, they discover Frasier drove Sophie home alone from a competition; PC Drummond had knocked on the car window but dismissed it as “teenage drama.”  

Chloe, Sophie’s friend, calls Emma: Sophie had formally reported Frasier for sexual assault at the station. The interview recording — found by Emma in James’ drawer — shows Stevens and James belittling Sophie, forcing her to re-enact the assault on camera, then taking no action. Horrified, Emma wants to expose the station’s culture immediately; Tom argues it will derail the murder inquiry and their careers. They compromise: arrest Frasier first, then come forward together.  

ANPR proves Frasier lied about his alibi (his car spotted near the forest 15 minutes before Sophie’s 999 call). Frasier voluntarily attends interview, admits sexual contact with Sophie (he was her coach since age 10), and is arrested. At a birthday dinner for Emma’s mother Ava, romantic tension between Emma and Tom deepens. Later, Emma steals Walker’s phone and uncovers a WhatsApp group shared by Walker, Stevens, James, Drummond, and others — containing photos of women, rape jokes, and casual misogyny. Tom admits he knew of the group but stayed silent to protect his career. They reaffirm their pact to report it after the case.  

Dashcam footage from a Smithal Brewery lorry (routed past the south car park) arrives; they begin reviewing it. The episode ends with press ambushing Frasier’s arrest at the rink — someone leaked his suspect status.

## S01E04

## S01E04 Summary — *The Blame*

### Episode Plot
DI Tom Radley is arrested for Sophie Madsen's murder; IOPC arrives to investigate the station. Emma Crane, convinced Tom is innocent, pursues the "serial predator" lead: Theo Asaro (Sophie's boyfriend) reveals Sophie knew a Wakestead police officer was raping women outside the station and had evidence (printed photos) of at least one other victim. The team traces two survivors — Kacy Denson (domestic abuse complainant) and Marissa Andrei (sex worker assaulted at a brothel) — both drugged with Rohypnol and raped after their cases were dismissed ("no further action" signed by Walker). The pattern: vulnerable women report crimes → cases buried → details shared on the station WhatsApp group → a predator contacts them posing as a helpful detective → drugs and assaults them. Sophie's home printer yields three "trophy photos" of unconscious women; one is **Amber Dodds**, raped and murdered — a crime pinned on **Stanley Vogel** (Alan Vogel), who is innocent. ## S01E05

## S01E05

### Plot
The episode opens with Emma and Tom processing the case in his cell: the trophy photos came from Sophie's printer, and Tom hides the fact that Sophie was with a police officer when she died. A flashback shows16-year-old Sophie competing in a figure-skating event; afterward, in a tearful conversation with Chloe, she reveals that her coach Kyle Frasier "fancies" her, touched her in his car after the competition, then dropped her from the team. Chloe urges her to tell someone.

In the present, an anonymous tip leads officers (the whole search team, including Stevens and James) to Tom's grandfather's house. They find a burner phone matching the make and model of the one found on Sophie. Emma immediately suspects a frame-up by someone at Wakestead and tells Tom, who is horrified; his father visits him and pushes a "police harassment" narrative that Tom bitterly rejects. Emma confronts DCI Walker with the trophy photos and the two corroborating survivors, Marissa Andrei and Kacy Denson. Walker refuses to accept that any of his officers are responsible and signs off the interview with a "snowball" warning. She then tells Walker she will interview Tom herself and shows him the WhatsApp group chat to identify the predator's number.

Lewis tracks the burner phone: using the IMEI, he traces a purchase through CCTV. A homeless man, "Cheeky Pete," was paid by a South Asian man to buy the exact model. The man is identified as Kapil Manek — Tom's former partner — who was at the station shortly after Tom's arrest and could have overheard the phone's details. Lewis learns from Stevens, James, Drummond, and Kat Shaw that Kapil was relentlessly mocked by the group for his awkwardness around women, called "uppity" and a "virgin"; Stevens admits Tom never joined in but never stopped it. Kapil eventually left the force because "he can't function around a woman." Lewis is finally shown the WhatsApp group thread and reads aloud its toxic content while Phil Collins's "In the Air Tonight" plays.

Walker personally interviews Tom, who is openly hostile and sarcastic; Walker presses him on a supposed romantic approach to Sophie, and Tom fires back that the force needs a scapegoat and he is "not one of the boys." The interview is terminated at 5:14. Emma privately confronts Tom, telling him that her perjury over Amber Dodds is the reason Vogel can never be re-investigated. Tom vents that his life and career are already destroyed, regardless of charges. Emma secures Marissa's attacker's phone number from the threatening message she received; the number does not appear in the Crime Management System, and a reverse-subscriber search is filed but will take days. Lewis and Emma plan to confront Kapil the next day.

In the closing sequence, Emma is shown a flashback reconstruction: a young woman — clearly Sophie — appears at a door and says, "I almost didn't [come]… But you'll help me, right?" Emma replies, "Of course I will. Come on in." The episode cuts to black.

### Important character actions
- **Tom**: Refuses the "frame-job" narrative his father pushes; warns Emma that Walker would bury anything Sophie told him; confronts Walker in interview with contempt; rejects Emma's confession that her Vogel perjury blocks re-opening the case.
- **Emma**: Spots the phone-planting scheme immediately; pressures Walker to view the survivor photos; identifies the predator's MO; secures Marissa's attacker's number; asks Walker to show her the WhatsApp group.
- **Walker**: Personally interviews Tom; defends Wakestead culture as banter that "sometimes goes a little bit too far"; insists "nobody's bad" and that the predator theory is not an isolated event but a snowball he will not let roll over his department; calls Kapil "uppity" in recounting the bullying.
- **Lewis**: Conducts the CCTV/IMEI trace identifying Kapil; interrogates Stevens, Drummond, Kat Shaw, and James about the bullying; reads the WhatsApp group thread aloud and confronts Stevens/James over their behaviour toward Kapil and victims.
- **Kapil Manek** (off-screen but identified): Paid Cheeky Pete to buy the burner phone matching Sophie's, then made the anonymous tip to trigger the search of Tom's grandfather's home.
- **Stevens/James**: Mocked Kapil for years; in the locker scene, are caught laughing about female officers; Stevens admits Tom neither joined in nor stopped the bullying.
- **Marissa Andrei**: Confirms her attacker messaged her the day Emma visited, warning that he is watching both of them.

## S01E06

Episode 6 of *The Blame* resolves the Sophie Madsen case while detonating the show's central moral bomb: Tom Radley, Emma's partner and lover, is the killer.

Plot
- Open: Lewis backs up Emma's long-standing claim that Vogel is innocent of the Amber Dodds murder. The predator photos in the evidence bag are Polaroids. Just as Emma resolves to investigate the anonymous mobile number properly, a second anonymous threat arrives on that same number.
- Emma goes off-book to Neil (digital forensics). He admits being on the group chat and lurking to "gather intel" rather than act — a small, sharp bit of complicity she'll have to live with. She forces him to trace the number outside legal channels.
- The predator pattern emerges: a Wakestead officer uses his station access to identify vulnerable women, contacts them off-duty, drugs and rapes them. Victims don't remember him and don't return to file complaints. Sophie figured it out and gathered evidence — and that, Emma concludes, is exactly why she was killed.
- Neil confirms the number traces to a Wakestead officer who also threatened a victim today. They dial the number together; it just rings. Emma begins to feel physically unwell — Neil has drugged the wine.
- Cut to Sophie Madsen's final hours (flashback framing continues). Tom, working the case, meets Sophie at a StreetSafe event. She tells him she was drugged and raped at a police station by two detectives. Tom tells her to come forward; she agrees. Later, alone with him in his car, she says it's much worse — she's identified a serial rapist who took photos of victims at his house. Tom recognises the implication immediately and tries to talk her out of going to the press. He reframes her evidence as "creatable from scratch," warns about victim-blaming questions, and pivots to her skating coach as the real target. Sophie walks out on him.
