## Key Relationships
- **Founding trio:** Allura, Liberty, Emerald — left a male-dominated "boys' club" firm to form Grant, Ronson & Greene, accepting only female divorce clients.
- **Mentor:** Dina Standish — veteran attorney, guiding figure.
- **Rival:** Carrington "Carr" Lane — left behind at the old firm, scorned, actively plotting to destroy the new firm.
- **Internal dynamics:** Shifting allegiances, personal betrayals, professional rivalry, and friendship tested by high-stakes cases and personal crises.

## World Setting & Overall Plot Premise
A glossy, camp-inflected legal dramedy set in the world of ultra-high-net-worth divorce in Los Angeles. The series follows the attorneys of Grant, Ronson & Greene as they negotiate messy, high-money splits for elite clients — while the real power plays unfold inside the office. Each episode centers on a new case; the serialized arc tracks the firm's survival, the founders' personal lives unraveling, and Carr's vengeance campaign. Themes: gender and power in law, wealth and status display, female friendship and rivalry, ageism, beauty standards, fertility, dating, and professional ethics stretched to breaking point.

## Speaking Styles
- **Allura (Kim Kardashian):** Measured, media-savvy, brand-conscious; mixes legal precision with influencer cadence.
- **Liberty (Naomi Watts):** Sharp, brittle, emotionally guarded; British-inflected diction, dry wit.
- **Emerald (Niecy Nash-Betts):** Warm, boisterous, plain-spoken; Southern US rhythms, comedic timing, heartfelt outbursts.
- **Dina (Glenn Close):** Imperious, elegant, cutting; old-school patrician delivery, layered menace beneath politeness.
- **Carr (Sarah Paulson):** Intense, volatile, theatrical; rapid-fire sarcasm, emotional swings, Murphy-camp monologues.
- **Milan (Teyana Taylor):** Street-smart, confident, contemporary AAVE-inflected slang.
- **Overall:** Heavy profanity (fuck, shit, bitch, cunt, etc.), sexual innuendo, legal jargon woven into personal arguments, pop-culture and luxury-brand name-drops, Ryan Murphy "camp" one-liners.

## Cultural Context
- **Ryan Murphy universe:** Ensemble female leads, glamour-as-armour, camp sensibility, celebrity casting, meta-commentary on fame.
- **Legal drama tropes subverted:** Focus on divorce (not crime/corporate), all-female firm, clients are women wronged by powerful men.
- **Wealth porn:** Lingering shots of Bentley, Chanel, Birkin, Tiffany — visual language of excess.
- **Gender discourse:** "Taking only female clients" as feminist mission statement; explored with irony.
- **Contemporary US divorce culture:** Prenups, postnups, asset hiding, custody battles, reputational warfare.
- **Diversity:** Cast spans age (20s–70s), race, sexuality; show acknowledges but often undercuts with humor.
